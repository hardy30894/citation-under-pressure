The District Court's decision to invoke 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the clear statutory mandate of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Section 1407(a) explicitly requires that each action transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel" to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The language of the statute is unequivocal, leaving no room for interpretation that would permit the transferee court to retain the case for trial.

The Ninth Circuit's decision in the instant case, while denying mandamus relief, acknowledged that the district court's order may not be clearly erroneous as a matter of law due to existing case law, such as Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), and In Re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation is not cited because it was decided after 1970, however,  In re Plumbing Fixture Cases, 298 F. Supp. 484 (J.P.M.L. 1968), demonstrates that courts have previously recognized the importance of remanding cases to their original districts after pretrial proceedings.

However, a closer examination of the statutory text and relevant case law reveals that the district court's actions were not justified. The plain meaning of § 1407(a) dictates that cases be remanded after pretrial proceedings, and the use of the word "shall" emphasizes the mandatory nature of this requirement. As seen in United States v. Munsingwear, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), where the Court held that a statutory mandate must be followed unless it leads to an absurd result, the directive in § 1407(a) to remand cases is clear and must be respected.

Furthermore, the legislative history and purpose of § 1407 support the conclusion that cases should be returned to their original districts for trial. The statute was enacted to facilitate the efficient management of pretrial proceedings in multidistrict litigation, not to permit transferee courts to assume jurisdiction over trials. Allowing a transferee court to invoke § 1404(a) to retain a case for trial would undermine this purpose and contravene the statutory text.

In light of the clear statutory language and relevant case law, it is evident that the District Court erred in assigning the transferred case to itself for trial. The decision should be reversed, and the case remanded to the Northern District of Illinois for trial, as mandated by § 1407(a).
