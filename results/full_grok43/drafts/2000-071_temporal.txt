The school district's community use policy creates a limited forum confined to social, civic, and recreational activities pertaining to community welfare. Religious instruction and prayer fall outside that forum. In McCollum v. Board of Education, 333 U.S. 203 (1948), the Court held that public-school premises may not be used for sectarian religious teaching during school hours, because such use "aid[s] . . . religious groups . . . in propagating their faith." Id. at 210. The same principle bars after-hours access when the proposed activity is "the equivalent of religious worship . . . rather than the expression of religious views . . . on a secular subject matter."

The Club's meetings consist of prayer, memorization of scripture, Bible stories, and invitations to accept Christ as personal Savior. These practices are not merely a viewpoint on morals; they constitute religious instruction itself. Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421 (1962), condemned state-sponsored recitation of a prayer composed by government officials because it "officially establish[es] the religious beliefs embodied in" the prayer. Id. at 430. Permitting the Club's program would produce the identical establishment. Abington School District v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203 (1963), likewise invalidated daily Bible reading and recitation of the Lord's Prayer, observing that "the place of the Bible as an instrument of religion cannot be gainsaid." Id. at 224.

The district's refusal to open its facilities for these activities therefore rests on a content distinction, not viewpoint discrimination. Everson v. Board of Education, 330 U.S. 1 (1947), recognized that the Establishment Clause "was intended to erect 'a wall of separation between church and State.'" Id. at 16 (quoting Reynolds v. United States, 98 U.S. 145, 164 (1879)). Allowing sectarian worship on school property would breach that wall, while the district's neutral policy preserves it.

Because the exclusion prevents an establishment violation, it satisfies the First Amendment. The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
