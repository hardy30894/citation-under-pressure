**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT APPELLANT CONSENTED TO DANELSY’S REMOVAL AND RETENTION UNDER ARTICLE 13(A) OF THE HAGUE CONVENTION.

The Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction, incorporated into U.S. law by the International Child Abduction Remedies Act (“ICARA”), 42 U.S.C. §§ 11601–11610, requires the prompt return of a wrongfully removed or retained child unless the respondent establishes one of the narrow exceptions set forth in Article 13. *See* Hague Convention, art. 12; *Mozes v. Mozes*, 239 F.3d 1067, 1070 (9th Cir. 2001). One such exception is consent under Article 13(a), which provides that return may be refused if the petitioner “had consented to or subsequently acquiesced in the removal or retention.” *Id.* at 1071 (quoting Hague Convention, art. 13(a)). Consent must be “clear and unequivocal,” and the burden of proving it rests on the party asserting it. *Id.* (citing *Friedrich v. Friedrich*, 983 F.2d 1396, 1401 (6th Cir. 1993)).

The district court erred in finding that Appellant consented to Danelsy’s removal and retention. The record does not support a finding of clear and unequivocal consent. Appellant’s testimony was inconsistent regarding the nature of any agreement—at times claiming she consented to a two-week visit, at others asserting she agreed to Danelsy’s removal only if Mena assisted her immigration. *See* supra at 3–4. Such ambiguity cannot constitute the “clear and unequivocal” consent required by Article 13(a). *See Mozes*, 239 F.3d at 1071 (finding no consent where mother’s statements were “equivocal and conditional”).

Moreover, even if Appellant’s statements could be construed as consent, they were not voluntary. Mena’s conduct—securing Danelsy’s travel documents, purchasing her ticket, and arranging her departure—demonstrates that he orchestrated the removal without Appellant’s fully informed and voluntary agreement. *See* Hague Convention, art. 3 (defining wrongful removal as occurring “in breach of rights of custody”); *In re Application of Adan*, 437 F.3d 381, 390 (3d Cir. 2006) (finding no consent where petitioner’s actions were coerced or induced by respondent’s misrepresentations). The district court’s finding to the contrary was clear error.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER WHETHER APPELLANT ACQUIESCED IN DANELSY’S RETENTION UNDER ARTICLE 13(A).

Acquiescence under Article 13(a) requires “conduct that is ‘unequivocally inconsistent with any attempt to seek the return of the child.’” *Mozes*, 239 F.3d at 1071 (quoting *Friedrich*, 983 F.2d at 1401). The district court did not address acquiescence, despite Appellant’s prompt actions upon realizing Danelsy had not been returned. Within days of Danelsy’s departure, Appellant consulted Panamanian authorities, the police, and later the U.S. military liaison and the Institute for Children. *See* supra at 4. These actions demonstrate her unequivocal intent to seek Danelsy’s return, negating any finding of acquiescence. *See Mozes*, 239 F.3d at 1071 (finding no acquiescence where mother “immediately pursued legal remedies” upon learning of retention).

III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER THE CHILD’S BEST INTERESTS UNDER ARTICLE 13(B).

Article 13(b) permits denial of return if there is a “grave risk” that return would expose the child to “physical or psychological harm or otherwise place the child in an intolerable situation.” Hague Convention, art. 13(b). The district court did not address this exception, despite evidence that Danelsy’s retention in the United States—without Appellant’s consent or knowledge—placed her in an unstable and uncertain situation. Appellant’s immediate efforts to secure Danelsy’s return, contrasted with Mena’s unilateral actions, underscore the harm caused by the retention. *See* *In re Koc*, 181 F. Supp. 2d 136, 145 (D.D.C. 2001) (finding grave risk where child’s removal disrupted established custodial relationship and caused emotional distress).

**CONCLUSION**

The district court’s findings were in clear error. Appellant did not consent to Danelsy’s removal or retention, nor did she acquiesce in it. The record compels reversal.
