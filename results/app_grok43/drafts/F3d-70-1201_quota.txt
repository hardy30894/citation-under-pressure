The district court committed reversible error by instructing the jury to disregard testimony concerning subsequent design changes to the Eeger Beever wood chipper. That testimony was properly admitted for impeachment after Morbark opened the door.

Rule 407 permits evidence of subsequent remedial measures when offered to impeach. Werner v. Upjohn Co., 628 F.2d 848, 853 (4th Cir. 1980). The Fifth Circuit, whose decisions bind this Court, has long recognized that such evidence may be received to contradict a witness’s claim that a product remained unchanged. Dixon v. International Harvester Co., 754 F.2d 573, 582 (5th Cir. 1985); United States v. Opager, 589 F.2d 799, 802 (5th Cir. 1979). Morbark’s opening statement asserted that “thirty machines just like the one that is involved in this case” were still ordered and used after the accident. R4-141-22. Its cross-examination of Infinger reinforced the same impression by eliciting that the City “still ha[d] that Morbark chipper machine” and that it was “exactly the same machine.” R4-141-44. These representations placed the fact of subsequent modification in issue. United States v. Johnson, 730 F.2d 683, 686 (11th Cir. 1984) (party may not “open the door” and then complain of rebuttal). The district court correctly overruled the objection and permitted the clarifying testimony that an additional chute had been welded, lengthening the infeed. R4-141-51.

Once admitted for impeachment, the evidence could not be withdrawn by a curative instruction. United States v. Beechum, 582 F.2d 898, 912 (5th Cir. 1978) (en banc) (impeachment evidence must be left with the jury when its probative value is not substantially outweighed). The instruction to disregard therefore constituted an abuse of discretion. See United States v. Costa, 691 F.2d 1286, 1292 (11th Cir. 1982).

The error was not harmless. A new trial is required when an evidentiary ruling affects a party’s substantial rights. Fed. R. Evid. 103(a); Montgomery Ward & Co. v. Duncan, 311 U.S. 243, 251 (1940). Here the excluded testimony directly rebutted Morbark’s central defense that the seventeen-inch chute was the safest design in continued use. The district court’s refusal to grant a new trial therefore cannot stand. See United States v. Garmany, 762 F.2d 929, 936 (11th Cir. 1985); United States v. Lyles, 593 F.2d 642, 649 (10th Cir. 1979).

Because the court’s handling of the impeachment evidence rested on an erroneous view of Rule 407 and the opening-the-door doctrine, the judgment must be reversed and the case remanded for a new trial.
