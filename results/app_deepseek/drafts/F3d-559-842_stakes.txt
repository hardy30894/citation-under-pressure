**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment Because Genuine Disputes of Material Fact Exist as to Whether Rand’s Disclosures Violated TILA and HOEPA, Triggering a Three-Year Rescission Period.**

Under TILA, a borrower may rescind a consumer credit transaction secured by a principal dwelling within three business days after the later of the transaction’s consummation or delivery of the required disclosures. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a). If the lender fails to deliver material disclosures or a proper notice of the right to cancel, the rescission period extends to three years. *Id.* § 1635(f); *see also Jesinoski v. Countrywide Home Loans, Inc.*, 574 U.S. 259, 262 (2015). HOEPA imposes additional disclosure requirements for high-cost mortgages, including a three-day advance disclosure period before consummation. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b); 12 C.F.R. § 226.31(c)(1) (2005). Failure to comply extends the rescission period. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1639(j); *Harris v. Option One Mortg. Corp.*, 296 F.3d 645, 648 (8th Cir. 2002).

**A. Rand Failed to Provide Material Disclosures Required by TILA and HOEPA.**

The Mouas presented evidence that they did not receive a three-day advance HOEPA disclosure before the April 22, 2005, closing. *See* Appellants’ Statement of Facts ¶ 12. Rand’s sole defense—that the Mouas signed a handwritten waiver of the three-day review period—is disputed. Manisy Moua testified she did not understand the statement she was asked to copy, and she was not informed of its significance. *See* Appellants’ Statement of Facts ¶ 15. A waiver of the HOEPA disclosure period must be “knowing and voluntary” and made “in response to a bona fide personal financial emergency.” *See* 12 C.F.R. § 226.31(c)(1)(iii) (2005); *Johnson v. Fleet Nat’l Bank*, 638 F. Supp. 2d 1102, 1110 (D. Minn. 2009). Whether the Mouas’ waiver was knowing and voluntary is a quintessential question of fact for the jury. *See * *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 255 (1986). Moreover, the district court improperly resolved this factual dispute in Rand’s favor. *See Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Prods., Inc.*, 530 U.S. 133, 150 (2000).

**B. Rand Failed to Provide Clear and Conspicuous Notice of the Right to Cancel.**

TILA requires that the notice of the right to cancel be “clearly and conspicuously” disclosed. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a); 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(b) (2005). Rand provided two versions of the Notice of Right to Cancel. One version included a “Confirmation” section that the Mouas signed on the same day as the closing—April 22, 2005—purporting to certify that more than three business days had elapsed since the transaction. *See* Appellants’ Statement of Facts ¶ 18. The “Confirmation” directly contradicts the express three-day rescission period and misleads borrowers into believing they have no right to cancel. Such a provision is inherently deceptive and violates TILA’s requirement that the notice be “clear and conspicuous.” *See * *Rodriguez v. EMC Mortg. Corp.*, 2011 WL 3274050, at *4 (D. Minn. July 29, 2011) (unpublished) (similar “Confirmation” language rendered notice invalid). Because the Mouas were not given a valid notice of their right to cancel, the rescission period extended to three years. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1635(f); *Williams v. Wells Fargo Home Mortg., Inc.*, 410 F.3d 1077, 1081 (8th Cir. 2005).

**C. Rand’s Prepayment Penalty Violates HOEPA, Extending the Rescission Period.**

HOEPA prohibits prepayment penalties on high-cost mortgages. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c)(1)(A). The promissory note here did not explicitly state a prepayment penalty, but the loan documents included a “Prepayment Penalty Rider” that imposed a penalty. *See* Appellants’ Statement of Facts ¶ 20. The inclusion of a prohibited prepayment penalty constitutes a material disclosure violation, triggering the extended rescission period. *See * *In re Silvas*, 2010 WL 1610107, at *5 (Bankr. D. Ariz. Apr. 19, 2010) (unpublished) (prepayment penalty in HOEPA loan violates § 1639(c), extending rescission under § 1635(f)). The district court erred by failing to consider this independent violation.

**II. Conclusion**

Genuine issues of material fact preclude summary judgment. The Mouas are entitled to a trial on whether Rand’s defective disclosures extended the rescission period to three years. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
