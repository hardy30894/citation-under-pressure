**Argument**

I. The search warrant affidavit supplied no nexus between the alleged offenses and the parents’ residence. Probable cause requires “a fair probability that contraband or evidence of a crime will be found in a particular place.” Illinois v. Gates, 462 U.S. 213, 238 (1983). The affidavit recited decades-old complaints and SJ’s account of 1980s abuse; it contained no fact showing that photographs, videos, or computers were then located at the searched home. See United States v. Ribeiro, 397 F.3d 43, 49 (1st Cir. 2005) (affidavit must link evidence to specific premises). The officer’s generalized training-and-experience statement cannot bridge that gap. See United States v. Feliz, 182 F.3d 82, 87 (1st Cir. 1999).

II. Admission of uncharged molestation testimony was an abuse of discretion. Federal Rule of Evidence 404(b) bars propensity evidence unless it is offered for a non-propensity purpose and its probative value is not substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice. See United States v. Varoudakis, 233 F.3d 113, 118-19 (1st Cir. 2000). The government offered no permissible purpose beyond showing that Joubert “engaged in a pattern of abuse”; the resulting prejudice requires reversal. See Old Chief v. United States, 519 U.S. 172, 180 (1997).

III. The Commerce Clause does not authorize prosecution of purely intrastate, noncommercial conduct under 18 U.S.C. §§ 2251(a) and 2252A(a)(5)(B). Congressional power extends only to activities that “substantially affect interstate commerce.” United States v. Lopez, 514 U.S. 549, 559 (1995). The indictment alleged no movement of images across state lines and no commercial element; application of the statutes to this conduct exceeds the enumerated power. See United States v. Morrison, 529 U.S. 598, 617-18 (2000).

IV. The 480-month sentence is substantively unreasonable. A sentence must be “sufficient, but not greater than necessary,” to achieve the purposes of 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a). Gall v. United States, 552 U.S. 38, 50 (2007). The district court imposed the statutory maximum on each count despite the absence of any charged production or distribution after 2004 and without individualized consideration of Joubert’s age and health. The resulting sentence is greater than necessary and therefore unreasonable. See United States v. Rivera-Moreno, 613 F.3d 1, 10 (1st Cir. 2010).
