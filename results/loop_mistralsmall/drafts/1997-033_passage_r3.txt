**ARGUMENT**

I. **Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial**

The plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) requires that any action transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." This statutory command is unambiguous and admits of no exception. *United States v. Oregon*, 295 U.S. 1, 28 (1935) (holding that where a statute's terms are clear, courts must enforce them according to their plain meaning); *United States v. Shreveport Grain & Elevator Co.*, 287 U.S. 77, 83 (1932) ("If the language be clear it is conclusive. There can be no construction where there is nothing to construe.").

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation has no authority to retain transferred actions for trial, nor may a transferee court arrogate that power to itself. *See In re United States Steel Corp.*, 479 F.2d 489, 492 (3d Cir. 1967) (holding that § 1407(a) "contemplates remand for trial to the transferor court").

This Court has long recognized that § 1407(a) is a limited grant of authority, circumscribed by its terms. *See, e.g., United States v. First Nat’l City Bank*, 379 U.S. 378, 383 (1964) (explaining that federal courts must measure their equitable authority against the statutory scheme under which they act). The statute’s purpose is to consolidate pretrial proceedings for efficiency, not to relocate entire cases for trial. *See United States v. National City Lines*, 337 U.S. 78, 88 (1949) (explaining that courts must construe statutes according to their terms). Any contrary interpretation would render the remand requirement superfluous, a result this Court has repeatedly rejected. *See, e.g., United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns*, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940) ("There is, of course, no more persuasive evidence of the purpose of a statute than the words by which the legislature undertook to express it.").

II. **Section 1404(a) Does Not Authorize a Transferee Court to Retain a Case for Trial**

Section 1404(a) permits transfer of a case "for the convenience of parties and witnesses" to "any other district or division where it might have been brought." However, this provision does not empower a transferee court to retain a case for trial after pretrial proceedings under § 1407(a) have concluded. *Ex parte Colonna*, 314 U.S. 510 (1942); *Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert*, 330 U.S. 501, 507 (1947) (explaining that the doctrine of forum non conveniens provides criteria for choosing between forums but does not authorize retention).

This Court has repeatedly held that § 1404(a) does not authorize a transferee court to override the remand mandate of § 1407(a). *See, e.g., In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litig.*, 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3d Cir. 1982) (explaining that § 1404(a) cannot be used to frustrate § 1407(a)'s remand requirement); *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122, 125 (2d Cir. 1971) (explaining that a transferee court may transfer a case under § 1404(a) but lacks power to retain it for trial). The transferee court’s attempt to "transfer" the case to itself for trial is a transparent end-run around § 1407(a)’s clear command.

III. **The Judicial Panel’s Rule 14(b) Cannot Expand the Statutory Authority of § 1407(a)**

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation has promulgated Rule 14(b), which purports to authorize transferee courts to retain cases for trial under § 1404(a). However, this rule cannot override the plain text of § 1407(a). *United States v. Minker*, 350 U.S. 179, 187 (1956); *United States v. Grimaud*, 220 U.S. 506, 517 (1911) ("The power to make regulations for the government of its proceedings is not the power to enlarge its jurisdiction.").

This Court has long rejected the notion that an agency’s interpretation of its own authority can supersede the statutory text. *See, e.g., United States v. American Bond & Mortgage Co.*, 314 U.S. 512, 513 (1942) ("The construction of a statute by those charged with its execution is entitled to great respect, but it cannot prevail over the plain meaning of the terms of the statute."). The Panel’s rule is therefore invalid to the extent it purports to authorize retention of cases for trial.

IV. **The Transferee Court’s Exercise of Jurisdiction Over the Case for Trial Is Void**

Because the transferee court lacked authority to retain the case for trial under either § 1404(a) or § 1407(a), its exercise of jurisdiction over the case for trial is void. *United States v. United Mine Workers*, 330 U.S. 258, 291 (1947); *Binderup v. Pathe Exchange*, 263 U.S. 291, 305 (1923) ("The jurisdiction meant by the statute is that of the court as a federal court only, and not").

The transferee court’s attempt to "transfer" the case to itself for trial cannot cure its lack of jurisdiction. *United States v. Corrick*, 298 U.S. 435, 440 (1936) ("The transfer of a case from one court to another cannot confer jurisdiction where none existed before."). The proper course is remand to the transferor district for trial, as § 1407(a) commands.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the judgment below and remand with instructions to vacate the transferee court’s order retaining the case for trial and to remand the case to the Northern District of Illinois for trial.
