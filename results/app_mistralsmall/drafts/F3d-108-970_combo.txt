**ARGUMENT**

I. **FELTMANN FAILED TO PRESENT SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE TO ESTABLISH A PRIMA FACIE CASE OF SEX DISCRIMINATION UNDER TITLE VII.**

To prevail on a disparate treatment claim under Title VII, a plaintiff must first establish a prima facie case by showing: (1) she is a member of a protected class; (2) she was qualified for the position; (3) she suffered an adverse employment action; and (4) similarly situated employees outside the protected class were treated more favorably. *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792, 802 (1973). Even assuming Feltmann met the first three elements, she failed to demonstrate that similarly situated male employees were treated more favorably.

Feltmann’s reliance on *Phillips v. Martin Marietta Corp.*, 400 U.S. 542 (1971), is misplaced. That case involved an employer’s explicit policy of refusing to hire women with young children, a facially discriminatory practice. Here, by contrast, Plaza Infiniti’s actions were based on legitimate, performance-related concerns—not gender. *See Goostree v. Tennessee, 796 F.2d 854, 859 (6th Cir. 1986)* (employer’s reliance on performance evaluations, not gender, defeats prima facie case).

Moreover, Feltmann’s sales performance was inconsistent. While she was named to the Pinnacle Club in 1992, her gross profits per sale were the lowest among consultants in early 1993. Rich’s criticism of her sales tactics—allowing premature "guest drives"—was not pretextual but reflected legitimate business concerns. *See Furnco Constr. Corp. v. Waters*, 438 U.S. 567, 577 (1978) (employer need not ignore performance deficiencies to avoid liability).

II. **FELTMANN’S RETALIATION CLAIM FAILS BECAUSE SHE DID NOT ENGAGE IN PROTECTED ACTIVITY UNDER TITLE VII.**

Title VII’s anti-retaliation provision protects employees who oppose unlawful employment practices. *See 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-3(a)*. However, Feltmann’s complaint about a vulgar remark—while unprofessional—did not constitute opposition to sex discrimination. *See EEOC v. Crown Zellerbach Corp.*, 720 F.2d 1008, 1013 (9th Cir. 1983) (mere complaints about workplace conduct do not qualify unless tied to protected activity).

Even if her complaint were protected, Feltmann suffered no adverse action. The reprimand of Anzalone was a reasonable corrective measure, not retaliation. *See Hochstadt v. Worcester Found. for Experimental Biology*, 545 F.2d 222, 231 (1st Cir. 1976) (employer’s response to misconduct is not adverse if not materially adverse).

III. **MISSOURI’S HUMAN RIGHTS ACT AND COMMON LAW CLAIMS FAIL FOR THE SAME REASONS.**

The MHRA incorporates Title VII’s standards. *See Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.055*. Thus, Feltmann’s state-law claims suffer the same deficiencies. *See Smith v. Firestone Tire & Rubber Co.*, 875 S.W.2d 822, 826 (Mo. Ct. App. 1994) (state law tracks federal standards).

Under Missouri common law, Feltmann must show she was discharged without good cause. *See Johnson v. McDonnell Douglas Corp.*, 745 S.W.2d 361, 366 (Mo. Ct. App. 1988). However, her termination was based on documented performance issues, not gender. *See id.* (employer’s legitimate, non-discriminatory reasons defeat claim).

**CONCLUSION**

Feltmann failed to present sufficient evidence to establish discrimination or retaliation. The judgment below should be reversed.
