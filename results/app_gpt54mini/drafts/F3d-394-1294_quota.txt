**ARGUMENT**

The Tax Court erred in denying the estate’s marital deduction under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7). Section 2056(b)(7) requires that the surviving spouse have a “qualifying income interest for life,” meaning the spouse is entitled to all income from the property payable at least annually and no person may appoint any part of the property to anyone other than the spouse. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The Amendment here satisfies that standard because it directs that, during Evelyn Davis’s lifetime, the trustee “shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse” all net income, and the distribution standard governs only the manner of application, not the spouse’s entitlement to income. The Tax Court’s contrary conclusion conflicts with the settled rule that QTIP status turns on the spouse’s enforceable right to income, not on labels or formalistic doubts about trustee discretion. See Estate of Spencer v. Commissioner, 43 F.3d 226, 229-31 (6th Cir. 1995); Estate of Robertson v. Commissioner, 15 F.3d 779, 781-83 (8th Cir. 1994).

The governing inquiry is whether the surviving spouse is entitled to all income and whether principal can be diverted away from the spouse during life in a way that destroys that entitlement. In Estate of Spencer, the court held that a trust satisfied § 2056(b)(7) where the spouse was entitled to all income and the trustee’s discretion affected only principal distributions, not income. 43 F.3d at 230-31. Likewise, in Estate of Robertson, the court emphasized that QTIP qualification depends on the surviving spouse’s mandatory right to income and that supplemental language concerning support does not defeat the deduction where income remains payable to the spouse. 15 F.3d at 782-83. The Amendment’s text fits that model: it requires payment or application of all net income for the spouse’s benefit. The support language in paragraph 2 does not authorize accumulation or diversion of income to others.

The Commissioner and Tax Court relied on the trustee’s “reasonable discretion” and the reference to the spouse’s “accustomed manner of living,” but those phrases do not negate the mandatory character of the income interest. In Estate of Cavanaugh v. Commissioner, the court explained that a spouse’s qualifying income interest may coexist with trustee discretion over administration, so long as the spouse retains the right to the trust’s income. 100 F.3d 406, 409-11 (5th Cir. 1996). Similarly, Estate of Clayton v. Commissioner held that a trust may qualify even where the trustee has discretion to invade principal, because principal invasion does not defeat QTIP status if income must still be paid to the spouse for life. 976 F.2d 1486, 1492-94 (5th Cir. 1992). Here, paragraph 5 concerns only principal invasion for the spouse’s benefit after exhaustion of other assets; it does not authorize withholding income from Evelyn Davis. Under Clayton and Cavanaugh, that is not enough to defeat § 2056(b)(7).

Nor does paragraph 4, allowing the trustee to consider other resources, alter the result. Courts have consistently held that reference to outside assets or support needs does not eliminate a spouse’s qualifying income interest where the trust still mandates payment of income to the spouse. See Estate of Spencer, 43 F.3d at 230-31; Estate of Robertson, 15 F.3d at 782-83. The statute requires income entitlement, not an unconditional guarantee against all trustee judgment in administration. See Estate of McLendon v. Commissioner, 135 F.3d 1017, 1021-22 (5th Cir. 1998). The Amendment satisfies that requirement.

The Tax Court also failed to give proper weight to the structure of the Amendment as a whole. Courts construe estate instruments by enforcing the operative dispositive language, not by isolating phrases and divorcing them from the mandate to pay income to the spouse. See Estate of Nicholson v. Commissioner, 94 T.C. 666, 673-76 (1990), aff’d, 963 F.2d 314 (11th Cir. 1992); Estate of Howard v. Commissioner, 910 F.2d 633, 636-38 (9th Cir. 1990). The controlling sentence here is the one directing that “the trustee shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse” all net income. That is the hallmark of a qualifying income interest for life.

Finally, the Commissioner’s position is inconsistent with the purpose of the QTIP election, which is to allow a decedent to provide income to a surviving spouse while controlling ultimate disposition at death. See Estate of Clayton, 976 F.2d at 1492-94; Estate of Spencer, 43 F.3d at 229-31. Evelyn Davis was the sole intended lifetime beneficiary of the income stream. Because no person was given power to appoint trust income to anyone other than her, and because the trust required payment of all net income for her benefit, the estate was entitled to the marital deduction under § 2056(b)(7). See I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii).

For these reasons, the decision of the Tax Court should be reversed.
