The district court erred in concluding that the releases bar appellants’ WARN Act claims. The Act mandates that covered employers “shall not order a plant closing or mass layoff until the end of a 60-day period after the employer serves written notice” on affected workers or their representatives. 29 U.S.C. § 2102(a). DHL’s November 10, 2008 announcement and the ensuing December agreements did not satisfy this command for the hundreds of employees whose layoffs began months earlier and continued through January 2009.

Section 2105 provides that WARN rights “may not be diminished by a collective-bargaining agreement.” Consistent with that prohibition, a union may waive statutory rights only through “clear and unmistakable” language. *Metropolitan Edison Co. v. NLRB*, 460 U.S. 693, 708 (1983). The Effects Bargaining Agreements contain no such language; they merely recite that participating employees release “any claims under . . . the Federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act.” This general release falls short of the “clear and unmistakable” standard required to surrender individual statutory entitlements. *See Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp.*, 525 U.S. 70, 80 (1998).

Even if the releases were otherwise valid, they were executed under time pressure that precluded knowing and voluntary waiver. Drivers were forced to elect the ten-week package by December 11—six days after the agreements were signed—while simultaneously completing rebid forms. Four-week participants received at most thirteen days. Such abbreviated periods, coupled with the absence of any explanation that WARN notice rights were being relinquished, render the releases unenforceable. *See, e.g., Puentes v. United Parcel Service, Inc.*, 196 F.3d 1256, 1260-61 (11th Cir. 1999) (listing factors for evaluating voluntariness of employment releases).

Because the releases cannot extinguish appellants’ WARN claims, the judgment must be reversed and the case remanded for determination of whether DHL provided the required sixty days’ notice.
