**ARGUMENT**

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) possesses jurisdiction under the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970 to issue citations to uninspected vessels such as the *MR. BELDON*, a drilling barge operating in Louisiana’s territorial waters. The Fifth Circuit erred in holding that the Coast Guard’s regulatory authority over seamen’s working conditions on vessels in navigation preempts OSHA’s authority under § 4(b)(1) of the Act. That holding rests on a misreading of the statutory framework and a misapplication of preemption principles grounded in pre-1970 precedent. The Court must reverse.

**I. The Occupational Safety and Health Act does not exclude uninspected vessels from OSHA jurisdiction.**

Section 4(b)(1) of the Act provides that OSHA’s authority “shall not apply to working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). The Fifth Circuit concluded that because the Coast Guard has authority over “seamen on vessels in navigation,” OSHA is barred from regulating such workplaces. But this conclusion ignores the critical distinction between *inspected* and *uninspected* vessels, a distinction that is both statutory and judicially recognized.

The Coast Guard’s authority under 46 U.S.C. § 2 (formerly 46 U.S.C. § 101) extends to “all matters not specifically delegated by law to some other executive department,” including the safety of vessels on navigable waters.
