**ARGUMENT**

**I. An inmate possesses no life or liberty interest protected by the Due Process Clause in state clemency proceedings.**

The Due Process Clause protects only interests in life or liberty that arise from the Constitution itself or from state law creating a legitimate claim of entitlement. *Connecticut Bd. of Pardons v. Dumschat*, 452 U.S. 458, 463 (1981). Clemency decisions fall outside both categories. As the Court explained, “pardon and commutation decisions have not traditionally been the business of courts; as such, they are rarely, if ever, appropriate subjects for judicial review.” *Id.* at 464 (citing *Meachum v. Fano*, 427 U.S. 215, 225 (1976)). Ohio law confers on the Governor “almost unfettered discretion,” confirming that no state-created entitlement exists. *See also Ohio Adult Parole Auth. v. Woodard*, 523 U.S. 272, 280-82 (1998) (plurality).

The Sixth Circuit’s reliance on *Evitts v. Lucey*, 469 U.S. 387 (1985), and *Griffin v. Illinois*, 351 U.S. 12 (1956), is misplaced. Those cases addressed procedures that states had made “an integral part of the system for finally adjudicating the guilt or innocence of a defendant.” *Evitts*, 469 U.S. at 393 (quoting *Griffin*, 351 U.S. at 18). Executive clemency lies at a “great distance” from trial and appeal and has never been treated as part of the adjudicatory process. *Dumschat*, 452 U.S. at 464. Extending due process scrutiny to clemency would contradict the Court’s refusal to do so in *Dumschat* itself.

**II. The option of a voluntary clemency interview does not compel self-incrimination in violation of the Fifth Amendment.**

The Fifth Amendment prohibits only compelled self-incrimination. *Baxter v. Palmigiano*, 425 U.S. 308, 316-17 (1976). Ohio’s regulation makes the interview available solely “if the prisoner requests” it; the inmate “remains free to say as much or as little at the interview as he wishes.” No state compulsion exists. Any hypothetical future use of statements in a new prosecution is “premature” for federal adjudication. *Id.* at 324. The district court therefore correctly rejected the claim without further proceedings.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
