I. Petitioners’ Claims for Injunctive and Declaratory Relief Are Moot.

A student’s graduation “moot[s]” claims for prospective relief against campus policies. *Board of School Commissioners v. Jacobs*, 420 U.S. 128, 129 (1975). Uzuegbunam’s graduation therefore eliminated any live controversy over injunctive or declaratory relief. *See also DeFunis v. Odegaard*, 416 U.S. 312, 319–20 (1974). The revised policies likewise ended Bradford’s claims for such relief. A defendant’s voluntary cessation moots a case when it is “absolutely clear that the allegedly wrongful behavior could not reasonably be expected to recur.” *Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc.*, 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013) (quoting *Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc.*, 528 U.S. 167, 190 (2000)). The district court found that standard satisfied, and petitioners do not contest the finding on appeal.

II. Petitioners’ Request for Nominal Damages Does Not Preserve a Live Controversy.

A claim for nominal damages alone “will not save” an otherwise moot challenge to a superseded policy. *Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248, 1264 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc). That rule controls here. Petitioners never alleged any “actual injury” beyond the abstract violation of constitutional rights. *See Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (compensatory damages in § 1983 actions require proof of “actual injury caused by the defendant rather than on the ‘abstract value’ of the constitutional rights”). Their complaint therefore sought only nominal damages, which *Flanigan’s* holds insufficient to keep the controversy alive. *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1264.

III. Petitioners Identify No Exception to *Flanigan’s*.

Petitioners’ attempt to distinguish *Flanigan’s* on the ground that this case involves an as-applied challenge fails because that distinction was never presented to the district court and is therefore forfeited. *See Access Now, Inc. v. Southwest Airlines Co.*, 385 F.3d 1324, 1331 (11th Cir. 2004). Even if preserved, the distinction would not help them: *Flanigan’s* itself addressed both facial and as-applied claims and held that nominal damages do not prevent mootness in either setting. 868 F.3d at 1264–65. The district court therefore correctly dismissed the action as moot.
