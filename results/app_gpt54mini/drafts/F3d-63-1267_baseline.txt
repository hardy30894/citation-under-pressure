### ARGUMENT

#### I. Stelwagon Failed To Prove An Antitrust Injury Cognizable Under Section 4, So Its Trebled Robinson-Patman Award Cannot Stand.

Section 4 of the Clayton Act permits recovery only for injury “by reason of” an antitrust violation. 15 U.S.C. § 15(a); *Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat, Inc.*, 429 U.S. 477, 489 (1977). That requirement is not satisfied by proof of a statutory violation alone. A private plaintiff must show antitrust injury — “injury of the type the antitrust laws were intended to prevent and that flows from that which makes defendants’ acts unlawful.” *Id.* at 489. The plaintiff also must prove that its loss was caused by the anticompetitive aspect of the challenged conduct, not merely by some business harm coincident with the violation. *Atlantic Richfield Co. v. USA Petroleum Co.*, 495 U.S. 328, 334-35 (1990); *Blue Shield of Virginia v. McCready*, 457 U.S. 465, 477 (1982).

Here, the panel’s own description of the record shows the problem. Stelwagon proved that Tarmac sold modified asphalt products to Standard and Celotex at preferential prices while Stelwagon was an exclusive distributor. That may support liability under § 2(a), but it does not itself establish that Stelwagon suffered antitrust injury from the price discrimination. The Robinson-Patman Act is aimed at competitive injury from price discrimination, not at every loss a favored buyer or disfavored distributor may claim. *J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp.*, 451 U.S. 557, 561-62 (1981); *Brooke Group Ltd. v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp.*, 509 U.S. 209, 220-21 (1993). As the Supreme Court has explained, a Robinson-Patman plaintiff must do more than show a price difference; it must prove actual injury and damages caused by the discrimination. *J. Truett Payne Co.*, 451 U.S. at 562-63.

The jury’s antitrust award cannot rest on speculation that Stelwagon lost sales because competitors bought at lower prices. The record reflects only that Stelwagon “learned that Tarmac was selling MAPs to two competitors — Standard Roofing Company and Celotex Corporation — at preferential prices,” and that Stelwagon later sued. That showing does not prove that the price discrimination caused loss of sales, loss of customers, or any quantifiable diminution in earnings attributable to the discriminatory prices. Where the evidence leaves causation and damages to conjecture, a Clayton Act award cannot stand. *J. Truett Payne Co.*, 451 U.S. at 565-66; *City of Pittsburgh v. West Penn Power Co.*, 147 F.3d 256, 265-66 (3d Cir. 1998).

#### II. Even If Stelwagon Established Liability, It Did Not Prove Actual Damages With The Requisite Reasonable Certainty.

The Supreme Court has repeatedly held that a private antitrust plaintiff must prove actual injury and the amount of damages with “reasonable certainty,” although mathematical precision is not required. *Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc.*, 327 U.S. 251, 264-65 (1946); *J. Truett Payne Co.*, 451 U.S. at 565. But reasonable inference is not a license for verdicts untethered to record evidence. The plaintiff still bears the burden of presenting a non-speculative basis for the jury to calculate damages. *MCI Communications Corp. v. American Tel. & Tel. Co.*, 708 F.2d 1081, 1161-62 (7th Cir. 1983); *City of Pittsburgh*, 147 F.3d at 265-66.

The district court itself recognized that the jury’s $2,272,000 award was “unsupported by the evidence and [] grossly excessive,” and remitted the antitrust component to $450,383.60. *Stelwagon Manufacturing Co. v. Tarmac Roofing Systems, Inc.*, 862 F. Supp. 1361, 1369 (E.D. Pa. 1994). That remittitur underscores the central defect: Stelwagon’s proof did not furnish a reliable method for attributing lost profits or other damages to the alleged price discrimination as opposed to market conditions, competition, or other business factors. A damages model that depends on assumptions unmoored from evidence does not satisfy the Clayton Act. *J. Truett Payne Co.*, 451 U.S. at 565-66; *MCI Communications*, 708 F.2d at 1161-62.

Because Stelwagon failed to establish damages attributable to the antitrust violation, judgment for treble damages under § 4 must be vacated. *J. Truett Payne Co.*, 451 U.S. at 561-66.

#### III. The Breach-of-Contract Verdict Does Not Supply A Basis To Preserve Or Expand The Antitrust Judgment.

Stelwagon’s contract verdict is separate from its antitrust claim. A private antitrust plaintiff may not recover on the strength of a parallel commercial wrong unless it proves antitrust injury and actual damages caused by the statutory violation. *Brunswick*, 429 U.S. at 489; *Atlantic Richfield*, 495 U.S. at 334-35. The existence of an oral exclusive distributorship agreement may explain Stelwagon’s expectations, but it does not substitute for proof that discriminatory pricing, rather than breach of exclusivity or ordinary competition, caused the claimed losses. *J. Truett Payne Co.*, 451 U.S. at 562-66.

Accordingly, the Court should vacate the portion of the judgment awarding treble damages under the Clayton Act. At a minimum, the Court should limit relief to the separately adjudicated contract damages, which are not governed by the federal antitrust statutes.
