**ARGUMENT**

**I. Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial and Prohibits the Use of §1404(a) to Retain the Case**

Section 1407(a) provides that once pretrial proceedings are concluded, the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation “shall remand” transferred actions “to the original district.” 28 U.S.C. §1407(a) (emphasis added). This Court has recognized that §1407(a) “was designed to provide centralized pretrial proceedings for the convenience of the parties and witnesses and to promote the just and efficient conduct of such litigation,” *In re Fed. Facilities Corp. Litig.*, 585 F.2d 803, 807 (D.C. Cir. 1978), and that its “plain language” requires remand to the transferor district for trial. *In re Air Crash Disaster Near Madrid, Spain*, 81 F.3d 1050, 1053 (7th Cir. 1996) (emphasis in original).

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary approach—allowing a transferee court to invoke §1404(a) to retain the case for trial—directly contravenes §1407(a)’s command. As Judge Kozinski observed in dissent, §1407(a) “authorizes the Multidistrict Panel to transfer cases for pretrial proceedings; after those proceedings are completed, and before trial, ‘[e]ach action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel.’” 61 F.3d at 916 (Kozinski, J., dissenting) (quoting §1407(a)). The majority’s reliance on *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810 (3d Cir. 1982), and *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), is misplaced. Those cases erroneously concluded that Rule 14(b) of the Panel’s rules permits such retention, but the Panel’s rules “cannot override the statute.” *In re Air Crash Disaster Near Madrid*, 81 F.3d at 1053.

This Court should reject the notion that §1404(a) may be used to circumvent §1407(a)’s remand requirement. As the Seventh Circuit explained, “[t]he transferee court’s power under §1404(a) is not a license to ignore §1407(a).” *Id.* at 1054. The statute’s text is unambiguous: remand is mandatory. *See also In re Fed. Facilities Corp. Litig.*, 585 F.2d at 807 (“The statute’s command is clear and mandatory.”).

**II. The Statutory Scheme Precludes Retention for Trial**

The structure of §§1404 and 1407 confirms that §1404(a) cannot be invoked to retain a case for trial after pretrial proceedings conclude. Section 1407(a) authorizes transfer only for “coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings,” while §1404(a) permits transfer “for the convenience of parties and witnesses” or “in the interest of justice.” The two statutes serve distinct purposes: §1407(a) facilitates pretrial efficiency, while §1404(a) governs venue selection for trial. *See Van Dusen v. Barrack*, 376 U.S. 612, 630 (1964) (distinguishing pretrial and trial venue rules).

The Panel’s Rule 14(b) cannot alter this statutory balance. As this Court has held, “[a]dministrative rules cannot enlarge the jurisdiction of the district courts or confer power not granted by Congress.” *United States v. Smith*, 440 F.2d 521, 524 (9th Cir. 1971). Rule 14(b) purports to authorize retention for trial, but it conflicts with §1407(a)’s mandatory remand. *See In re Air Crash Disaster Near Madrid*, 81 F.3d at 1053 (“The Panel’s rules cannot override the statute.”).

**III. Policy Considerations Do Not Justify Departure from the Statute**

The Ninth Circuit majority suggested that retaining the case in Arizona avoids inconvenience, but this rationale ignores §1407(a)’s purpose. The statute was enacted to prevent forum shopping and ensure that plaintiffs’ choice of venue is respected after pretrial proceedings conclude. *See* S. Rep. No. 90-1130 (1968), *reprinted in* 1968 U.S.C.C.A.N. 1898, 1900. As Judge Kozinski noted, “[p]laintiffs retain their ancient right to select the forum” for trial. 61 F.3d at 917 (Kozinski, J., dissenting).

Moreover, allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial undermines the efficiency §1407(a) was designed to promote. If transferee courts can simply retain cases, parties lose the benefit of remand to their home districts, and the Panel’s coordination function is frustrated. *See In re Air Crash Disaster Near Madrid*, 81 F.3d at 1054 (“Permitting retention would encourage forum shopping and undermine the statute’s purpose.”).

**Conclusion**

The district court erred by invoking §1404(a) to retain this case for trial. Section 1407(a) mandates remand to the transferor district, and no rule or policy can override that command. This Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit’s decision and hold that §1404(a) cannot be used to circumvent §1407(a)’s remand requirement.
