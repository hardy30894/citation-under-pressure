**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT THE SCHOOL DISTRICT’S FACILITIES WERE A LIMITED PUBLIC FORUM NOT OPEN TO RELIGIOUS SPEECH.**

The Second Circuit’s conclusion that the school facilities were a limited public forum not open to religious speech is contrary to this Court’s precedents. In *Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983), the Court held that when the government opens a nontraditional forum for expressive activity, it must do so on a viewpoint-neutral basis. The District’s policy of excluding religious speech while permitting secular speech violates this principle.

The District’s facilities were opened to a wide range of community groups, including political, social, and recreational organizations. As this Court recognized in *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educ. Fund*, 473 U.S. 788, 802 (1985), such broad access creates a limited public forum. The District’s refusal to allow religious speech while permitting secular speech constitutes viewpoint discrimination, which is impermissible in a limited public forum. *Rosenberger v. Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995).

**II. THE DISTRICT’S EXCLUSION OF RELIGIOUS SPEECH VIOLATES THE FIRST AMENDMENT’S FREE SPEECH CLAUSE.**

The District’s policy of excluding religious speech while permitting secular speech is content-based discrimination that cannot survive strict scrutiny. *Reed v. Town of Gilbert*, 576 U.S. 155, 163 (2015). The District’s justification—that allowing religious speech would violate the Establishment Clause—is insufficient. This Court has repeatedly rejected the notion that the Establishment Clause permits viewpoint discrimination. *Lamb’s Chapel v. Ctr. Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist.*, 508 U.S. 384, 394 (1993) (“The showing of a film series on child-rearing, among other subjects, is a far cry from the ‘prayers, proselytizing, or worship’ that the Court has found impermissible in the public school context.”).

The District’s policy is not viewpoint-neutral. It permits secular discussions of family values but excludes religious perspectives. This is precisely the type of content-based discrimination that the First Amendment forbids. *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 828-29.

**III. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY VIOLATES THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE.**

The District’s policy discriminates against religious speech while permitting secular speech on the same subject. This violates the Equal Protection Clause’s guarantee of neutrality toward religion. *Church of Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah*, 508 U.S. 520, 542 (1993) (“At a minimum, the protections of the Free Exercise Clause pertain[] to[] the freedom to select religious conduct without restriction under the Equal Protection Clause.”).

The District’s policy is not neutral because it singles out religious speech for disfavored treatment. *Estate of Thornton v. Caldor, Inc.*, 472 U.S. 703, 709 (1985) (plurality opinion) (“The First Amendment . . . gives no one the right to insist that in pursuit of their own interests others must conform their conduct to his own religious necessities.”).

**IV. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY DOES NOT VIOLATE THE ESTABLISHMENT CLAUSE.**

The District’s policy is not justified by the Establishment Clause. This Court has held that the Establishment Clause does not permit the exclusion of religious speech from a limited public forum. *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 394. The District’s policy is not neutral toward religion and does not further a compelling interest. *Id.* at 395.

The District’s reliance on *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263 (1981), and *Board of Educ. of the Westside Community Schs. v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226 (1990), is misplaced. Those cases involved student-initiated religious speech in a forum open to student groups. The instant case involves an outside religious organization seeking to use school facilities. The District’s policy is not saved by the Establishment Clause.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the court below should be reversed. The District’s policy of excluding religious speech while permitting secular speech violates the First Amendment’s Free Speech and Equal Protection Clauses. The District’s justification under the Establishment Clause is insufficient.
