The plain language of 28 USC section 1407(a) mandates that the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation "shall" remand any action transferred for pretrial proceedings to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." As the court noted in United States v. American Trucking Ass'ns, 310 U.S. 534 (1940), "There is, of course, no more persuasive evidence of the purpose of a statute than the words by which the legislature undertook to give expression to its wishes." Id. at 543.

The District of Arizona's invocation of section 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the statutory scheme established by section 1407(a). As the court noted in Ex parte Collett, 337 U.S. 55, 58 (1949), section 1404(a) is a general transfer provision that must be read in conjunction with other statutory provisions governing the transfer of cases. In this case, the specific mandate of section 1407(a) takes precedence over the general transfer authority granted by section 1404(a).

The Ninth Circuit's decision in the instant case, as well as other cases that have permitted transferee courts to invoke section 1404(a) to retain cases for trial, are inconsistent with the statutory text and the principles of statutory construction. The Supreme Court has held that "where there is in the same statute a specific provision, and also a general one which in its most comprehensive sense would include matters embraced in the former, the particular provision must control." Ginsberg & Sons v. Popkin, 285 U.S. 204 (1932), at 208. This principle is relevant here, as the specific provision of section 1407(a) governs the transfer of cases for pretrial proceedings.

Furthermore, the practice of allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial under section 1404(a) undermines the policy choice reflected in section 1407(a), which is to limit the transfer of cases to pretrial proceedings and preserve the plaintiff's traditional right to select the forum for trial. See, e.g., Baltimore & Ohio R. Co. v. Kepner, 314 U.S. 44, 52-53 (1941) (emphasizing the importance of the plaintiff's choice of forum).

The Supreme Court has consistently held that statutory provisions should be interpreted in a manner that gives effect to their plain meaning. In Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470 (1917), the Court emphasized the importance of considering the statutory text as a whole. Id. at 485. In this case, the plain meaning of section 1407(a) requires remand to the original district at or before the conclusion of pretrial proceedings.

Other cases have also emphasized the importance of adhering to the statutory text and respecting the policy choices reflected in the statute. See, e.g., United States v. Wiltberger, 18 U.S. 76, 95-96 (1820) (Marshall, C.J.); Ozawa v. United States, 260 U.S. 178, 194 (1922) ("It is the duty of this Court to give effect to the intent of Congress."); United States v. Missouri Pac. R. Co., 278 U.S. 269, 278 (1929) (noting that the statute should be construed to give effect to its language).

In light of these authorities, it is clear that the District of Arizona erred in invoking section 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial. The plain language of section 1407(a) and the principles of statutory construction require that the case be remanded to the original district for trial.
