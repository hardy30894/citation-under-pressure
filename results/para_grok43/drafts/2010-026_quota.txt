The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that Exemption 2 shields the ESQD documents. FOIA requires disclosure of agency records unless they fall within one of nine “explicitly exclusive” exemptions that “are to be interpreted narrowly.” U.S. Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989) (quoting Adm’r FAA v. Robertson, 422 U.S. 255, 262 (1975)); Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S. 352, 361 (1976). Exemption 2 protects only matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The statutory text and Rose confine the exemption to “minor employment matters” such as “sick leave” and “lunch hours,” or to law-enforcement materials whose disclosure would risk circumvention of agency regulation. Rose, 425 U.S. at 363, 369.

The Ninth Circuit’s “predominantly internal” standard, drawn from Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 670 F.2d 1051, 1074 (D.C. Cir. 1981) (en banc), and adopted in Milner v. U.S. Dep’t of Navy, 575 F.3d 959, 970 (9th Cir. 2009), has no footing in the statute or Rose. Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 631 F.2d 653, 656 (9th Cir. 1980), permitted withholding only of “law enforcement materials” presenting a circumvention risk; it did not authorize a broader “predominantly internal” test. Maricopa Audubon Soc’y v. U.S. Forest Serv., 108 F.3d 1082, 1085 (9th Cir. 1997), likewise treated the law-enforcement route as sufficient but not necessary only after first applying the statutory “internal personnel rules” limitation.

The ESQD arc maps and related data are technical planning tools used to site ammunition storage facilities. They bear no relation to “internal personnel rules and practices.” See Schwaner v. Dep’t of the Air Force, 898 F.2d 793, 795 (D.C. Cir. 1990) (information must be “directed toward” agency employees). Because the documents fall outside the statutory language, the risk-of-circumvention inquiry is irrelevant. Morley v. CIA, 508 F.3d 1108, 1124 (D.C. Cir. 2007).

An agency may withhold records only by satisfying its burden under a properly construed exemption. U.S. Dep’t of State v. Ray, 502 U.S. 164, 173 (1991). The Navy’s affidavits describe operational safety data, not personnel rules. Minier v. CIA, 88 F.3d 796, 800 (9th Cir. 1996). Expanding Exemption 2 to encompass any “predominantly internal” document whose release might embarrass the agency would “distort[] Congress’s [ ] balance” and contradict the command that exemptions “must be narrowly construed.” John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp., 493 U.S. 157, 157 (1989); Rose, 425 U.S. at 361. The judgment should be reversed.
