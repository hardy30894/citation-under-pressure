ARGUMENT

The Ninth Circuit erred in concluding that uncounseled tribal-court convictions valid under the Indian Civil Rights Act, 25 U.S.C. §§ 1301-1303, cannot serve as predicates for a prosecution under 18 U.S.C. § 117(a). In Nichols v. United States, 511 U.S. 738 (1994), this Court held that an uncounseled misdemeanor conviction that does not violate the Sixth Amendment may be used to enhance punishment for a later offense. Id. at 746-47 (citing Scott v. Illinois, 440 U.S. 367 (1979)). The Court explained that recidivist statutes “penaliz[e] only the last offense committed by the defendant.” Id. at 747.

Section 117(a) is such a statute. It imposes an enhanced penalty only upon proof of a new domestic assault committed within Indian country by a person who already has two qualifying convictions. The statute therefore does not re-prosecute the prior tribal offenses; it merely takes account of them in fixing the penalty for the federal crime. See also United States v. Rodriquez, 553 U.S. 377, 386 (2008) (“When a defendant is given a higher sentence under a recidivism statute … 100% of the punishment is for the offense of conviction.”).

The distinction drawn by the court of appeals between the guilt and sentencing phases is foreclosed by Nichols itself. There the Court rejected the notion that the reliability concerns associated with uncounseled convictions preclude their use once the conviction itself is constitutionally valid. 511 U.S. at 747-48. Because the Sixth Amendment does not apply in tribal court, United States v. Wheeler, 435 U.S. 313, 323-24 (1978), Bryant’s tribal convictions did not violate that Amendment. They are therefore available for use under § 117(a) on the same terms as any other valid prior conviction.

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary rule, derived from United States v. Ant, 882 F.2d 1389 (9th Cir. 1989), also conflicts with the decisions of the Eighth and Tenth Circuits, which have held that valid tribal convictions may serve as predicates under § 117(a). See United States v. Cavanaugh, 643 F.3d 592, 604 (8th Cir. 2011); United States v. Shavanaux, 647 F.3d 993, 997-98 (10th Cir. 2011). That division leaves the statute inoperative in one circuit while fully operative in two others, undermining Congress’s express purpose to provide “felony-level punishment for serial domestic violence offenders.” 

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
