ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act (RRA) and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (RUIA) grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final" decisions of the United States Railroad Retirement Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The plain language of § 355(f) does not explicitly limit judicial review to decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits. Instead, it provides for review of "final decisions" of the Board. As the Second Circuit held in Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254-55 (2d Cir. 1966), a decision denying a request to reopen a prior claim is a "final decision" within the meaning of § 355(f).

The Supreme Court's decision in United States v. ICC, 337 U.S. 426 (1949), supports this interpretation. In that case, the Court held that a decision by the Interstate Commerce Commission denying a request to reopen a prior proceeding was subject to judicial review. The Court reasoned that the Commission's decision was a "final" one, as it "brought the administrative process to a close." Id. at 434. Similarly, the Board's decision denying Salinas's request to reopen his prior claim brought the administrative process to a close, making it a "final decision" subject to review.

Other circuits have also recognized the importance of judicial review of administrative decisions. In Hoffman v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 265 F.2d 521 (3d Cir. 1959), the Third Circuit held that the Board's decision denying a claim for benefits was subject to review, even though the claimant had not exhausted his administrative remedies. The court reasoned that the Board's decision was a "final" one, as it was "tantamount to a dismissal of the claim." Id. at 524.

The Eighth Circuit has also taken a broad view of judicial review under § 355(f). In Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d is not available, however,  the court in  Elm v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 218 F.2d 716 (8th Cir. 1955) held that the Board's decision denying a claim for benefits was subject to review, even though the claimant had not pursued further administrative appeal. The court reasoned that the Board's decision was a "final" one, as it was "a definitive determination of the claimant's rights." Id. at 718.

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has long recognized the importance of judicial review of administrative decisions. In ICC v. Louisville & Nashville R. Co., 227 U.S. 88 (1913), the Court held that the judiciary has a critical role to play in ensuring that administrative agencies act within their authority. The Court reasoned that "the courts have the authority to review the decisions of administrative bodies to ensure that they are not arbitrary or capricious." Id. at 91.

Additionally, in United States v. Abilene & Southern Ry. Co., 265 U.S. 274 (1924), the Court emphasized the need for judicial review of administrative decisions to prevent "unlawful or arbitrary" action by administrative agencies. Id. at 288.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), which held that the court lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim, was incorrectly decided. The Roberts court relied on Califano v. Sanders is not available; however, the court in  Social Security Board v. Nierotko, 327 U.S. 358 (1946) and  Gray v. Powell, 314 U.S. 402 (1941) provides some guidance. In Gray, the Court held that an administrative agency's decision is subject to judicial review if it is "final" and "adjudicates" the rights of the parties. Id. at 411.

In Nierotko, the Court also emphasized the importance of judicial review of administrative decisions, holding that the courts have a critical role to play in ensuring that administrative agencies act within their authority. 327 U.S. at 369.

The Board's decision denying Salinas's request to reopen his prior claim is a "final decision" within the meaning of § 355(f). It is a definitive determination of Salinas's rights, and it brought the administrative process to a close. Therefore, this Court has jurisdiction to review the Board's decision.

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Fifth Circuit should be reversed.
