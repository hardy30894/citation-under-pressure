**ARGUMENT**

I. **OSHA’S JURISDICTION OVER THE WORKING CONDITIONS ON BOARD THE MR. BELDON IS NOT PRE-EMPTED BY THE COAST GUARD’S REGULATORY AUTHORITY.**

The Occupational Safety and Health Act (“Act”) grants OSHA broad authority to regulate workplace safety, including those workplaces located on vessels in navigable waters. Section 4(a) of the Act provides that it applies “with respect to employment performed in a workplace in a State.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(a). The Act further provides that it does not apply to “working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health,” except where such authority is “delegated to the Secretary [of Labor] by the President.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). The Coast Guard, however, does not exercise “statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health” for the working conditions at issue here. Rather, the Coast Guard’s authority is limited to marine safety and vessel inspection, and it does not address the specific occupational hazards presented by oil and gas drilling operations.

This Court has long recognized that OSHA’s jurisdiction is not pre-empted where the Coast Guard lacks authority to regulate the particular working conditions at issue. In *Martin v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Comm’n*, 499 U.S. 144 (1991), the Supreme Court held that OSHA’s jurisdiction was not pre-empted by the Federal Aviation Administration’s (“FAA”) regulation of aviation safety because the FAA’s authority did not extend to the specific workplace hazards at issue. The Court explained that pre-emption under § 653(b)(1) applies only where another federal agency has been “delegated authority to regulate occupational safety and health” for the particular working conditions. *Id.* at 151. Similarly, in *Secretary of Labor v. Fitzsimmons Drilling Co.*, 820 F.2d 878 (5th Cir. 1987), this Court held that OSHA’s jurisdiction over a drilling rig was not pre-empted by the Coast Guard’s authority because the Coast Guard’s regulations did not address the specific hazards of oil and gas drilling operations. *Id.* at 882.

The Coast Guard’s authority over vessels like the MR. BELDON is limited to marine safety and vessel inspection, not occupational safety and health. The Coast Guard’s regulations under 46 U.S.C. § 4102 and 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01 et seq. address lifesaving equipment, fire extinguishers, and other marine safety concerns, but they do not regulate the occupational hazards of oil and gas drilling operations. See *Fitzsimmons Drilling Co.*, 820 F.2d at 882. Thus, the Coast Guard does not exercise “statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health” for the working conditions at issue here, and OSHA’s jurisdiction is not pre-empted.

II. **THE MR. BELDON IS A “WORKPLACE” WITHIN THE MEANING OF THE ACT.**

The Act defines a “workplace” as “any establishment, job site, or premises where work is performed by an employee.” 29 U.S.C. § 652(10). The MR. BELDON is a drilling barge where Mallard’s employees performed work, and it therefore qualifies as a “workplace” under the Act. This Court has held that a drilling rig is a “workplace” under the Act, even though it is located on navigable waters. See *Secretary of Labor v. Trinity Industries, Inc.*, 15 F.3d 115 (5th Cir. 1994). In *Trinity Industries*, this Court rejected the argument that a drilling rig was not a “workplace” because it was a vessel subject to Coast Guard regulation. *Id.* at 117. The Court held that the Act’s definition of “workplace” is broad enough to encompass drilling rigs, and that the Coast Guard’s authority over vessels does not preclude OSHA’s jurisdiction over the workplace hazards on board. *Id.*

The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Trinity Industries* is controlling here. The MR. BELDON is a drilling barge where Mallard’s employees performed work, and it therefore qualifies as a “workplace” under the Act. The fact that the MR. BELDON is a vessel does not remove it from the Act’s coverage, as the Act’s definition of “workplace” is not limited to land-based establishments. See *Trinity Industries*, 15 F.3d at 117.

III. **THE COAST GUARD’S AUTHORITY OVER UNINSPECTED VESSELS DOES NOT PRE-EMPT OSHA’S JURISDICTION.**

The Court of Appeals erred in holding that the Coast Guard’s authority over uninspected vessels pre-empts OSHA’s jurisdiction. The Coast Guard’s authority over uninspected vessels is limited to marine safety and vessel inspection, not occupational safety and health. See 46 U.S.C. § 4102; 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01 et seq. The Coast Guard does not regulate the occupational hazards of oil and gas drilling operations, and its authority does not extend to the working conditions at issue here.

This Court has held that OSHA’s jurisdiction is not pre-empted where the Coast Guard’s authority does not address the specific workplace hazards at issue. In *Fitzsimmons Drilling Co.*, 820 F.2d at 882, this Court held that OSHA’s jurisdiction over a drilling rig was not pre-empted by the Coast Guard’s authority because the Coast Guard’s regulations did not address the specific hazards of oil and gas drilling operations. Similarly, in *Secretary of Labor v. J. Ray McDermott & Co.*, 571 F.2d 115 (5th Cir. 1978), this Court held that OSHA’s jurisdiction over a construction site on a vessel was not pre-empted by the Coast Guard’s authority because the Coast Guard’s regulations did not address the specific workplace hazards at issue. *Id.* at 118.

The Court of Appeals’ reliance on *Clary v. Ocean Drilling & Exploration Co.*, 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and *Donovan v. Texaco, Inc.*, 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), is misplaced. Those cases involved seamen who were subject to the Jones Act and other maritime laws, and they held that OSHA’s jurisdiction was pre-empted for those workers. *Clary*, 609 F.2d at 1122; *Donovan*, 720 F.2d at 826. The employees in this case, however, are not seamen subject to the Jones Act, but rather oil and gas workers performing drilling operations. See *Fitzsimmons Drilling Co.*, 820 F.2d at 882. Thus, *Clary* and *Donovan* do not control this case.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, OSHA’s jurisdiction over the working conditions on board the MR. BELDON is not pre-empted by the Coast Guard’s regulatory authority, and the MR. BELDON is a “workplace” within the meaning of the Act. The decision of the Court of Appeals should be reversed.
