**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s Decision Not to Reopen Salinas’s 2006 Application Is a Final Decision Subject to Judicial Review Under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).

The Fifth Circuit erred in holding that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s prior benefits claim. The plain text of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) confers jurisdiction on the courts of appeals to review “any final decision of the Board.” *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003). A decision declining to reopen a prior claim is a “final decision” when it definitively resolves the claimant’s rights. *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 107 (1977) (“[A] petition to reopen a prior final decision may be denied without a hearing . . . .”).

The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application was final because it foreclosed any further administrative review. *Lindahl v. Office of Personnel Mgmt.*, 470 U.S. 768, 785 (1985) (holding that a final agency decision is one that “mark[s] the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process”). The Board’s regulations explicitly limit reopening to cases involving new and material evidence or administrative error within four years. 20 C.F.R. § 261.2. By denying Salinas’s request, the Board conclusively determined that his claim could not be revived. This is precisely the type of final decision that § 355(f) contemplates.

II. The Majority of Circuits Recognize That Decisions Not to Reopen Are Reviewable Final Decisions.

The Fifth Circuit’s rule conflicts with the weight of authority. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141 (acknowledging a circuit split but siding with the majority). The D.C. Circuit recently held that the Railroad Retirement Act permits review of Board decisions declining to reopen. *Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016). Other circuits agree. *See, e.g., Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991) (reviewing a refusal to reopen); *Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 253, 254–55 (2d Cir. 1966) (same).

The Supreme Court’s decision in *Califano v. Sanders* does not foreclose review. 430 U.S. at 108 (“[T]he Secretary’s denial of a petition to reopen . . . is a final decision for purposes of judicial review.”). The Court distinguished between the Secretary’s refusal to reopen and the merits of the underlying claim, emphasizing that the former is reviewable. *Id.* at 107–08. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s claim is no different.

III. The Rule of Orderliness Does Not Bar Review.

The Fifth Circuit invoked the rule of orderliness to defer to *Roberts*. *Vaughan v. Anderson Reg’l Med. Ctr.*, 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir. 2017). But that rule applies only when reconsideration would unsettle settled expectations. *Henderson v. Shinseki*, 562 U.S. 428, 439 (2011) (distinguishing jurisdictional rules from prudential doctrines). Here, the conflict with other circuits and the Supreme Court’s guidance in *Califano* counsel against adherence to *Roberts*. *Jimenez v. Quarterman*, 555 U.S. 113, 120 (2009) (permitting reconsideration of precedent in light of intervening authority).

**Conclusion**

The Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is a final decision subject to judicial review under § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary rule conflicts with the text of the statute, the weight of authority, and controlling Supreme Court precedent. This Court should reverse the judgment below and remand for review on the merits.
