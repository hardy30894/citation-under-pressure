The Equal Protection Clause provides that no State shall “deny to any person within its jurisdiction the equal protection of the laws.” U.S. Const. amend. XIV, § 1. This Court has long held that the Clause safeguards against “invidious discrimination” directed at members of a particular class or group. *Brown v. Board of Education*, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954). A plaintiff therefore must allege and prove membership in such a class and differential treatment of similarly situated persons outside it. *See City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 432, 439 (1985); *Plyler v. Doe*, 457 U.S. 202, 216-17 (1982).

The Seventh Circuit’s recognition of a “class of one” claim—resting solely on an assertion that state action was “motivated solely by a ‘spiteful effort to “get” him for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective’”—finds no support in these precedents. The Clause has never been understood to constitutionalize every claim of arbitrary or malicious treatment by government officials. *See, e.g.*, *Snowden v. Hughes*, 321 U.S. 1, 8 (1944) (“[M]ere errors of judgment by officials will not support a claim of discriminatory administration.”); *Oyler v. Boles*, 368 U.S. 448, 456 (1962) (selective enforcement does not violate equal protection absent proof of “intentional or purposeful discrimination”).

Treating every individual as a class unto himself would convert the Equal Protection Clause into “a font of tort law,” *Paul v. Davis*, 424 U.S. 693, 701 (1976), duplicating the office of substantive due process while evading its stricter limits. *See County of Sacramento v. Lewis*, 523 U.S. 833, 846 (1998). Such an expansion lacks any “judicially manageable standards,” *Vieth v. Jubelirer*, 541 U.S. 267, 281 (2004), and would subject routine exercises of governmental discretion to federal constitutional attack.

Because respondent alleged neither class membership nor group-based animus, the complaint fails to state a claim under the Equal Protection Clause. The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
