**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON BURNETT’S FMLA CLAIM BECAUSE HE PROVIDED SUFFICIENT NOTICE OF HIS NEED FOR LEAVE.**

The FMLA requires an employee to provide notice of the need for leave "as soon as practicable." 29 U.S.C. § 2612(e)(1). Notice is "sufficient" if it "giv[es] the employer enough information to reasonably understand that the employee’s condition might qualify for FMLA leave." *Davis v. Michigan Dept. of Corrections*, 563 F.3d 975, 981 (7th Cir. 2009) (quoting *Carr v. GatesHealthcare Servs., LLC*, 518 F.3d 449, 454 (7th Cir. 2008)). The notice need not invoke the FMLA by name or explicitly state that leave is sought. *Id.* Rather, the employee must apprise the employer of the "serious health condition" that may require leave. *Id.* (citing *Carr*, 518 F.3d at 454).

Here, Burnett provided sufficient notice. In December 2003, he informed his supervisor, Sergio Polo, that he had seen a doctor and was suffering from elevated PSA and cholesterol levels. He further disclosed that he had upcoming medical appointments, including a prostate biopsy. This information was enough to put Habitat on notice that Burnett’s condition might require FMLA leave. *SeeDavis*, 563 F.3d at 981 (holding that notice of a "serious health condition" need not be explicit to be sufficient). The district court’s contrary conclusion—that Burnett’s statements were too vague—ignores the FMLA’s notice requirements. *See id.* (rejecting the argument that notice must be "precise or technical").

Habitat argues that Burnett’s notice was untimely because he did not explicitly request leave until January 7, 2004. However, the FMLA does not require an employee to request leave in a particular manner or at a specific time, so long as notice is given "as soon as practicable." *See id.* (holding that notice is sufficient if given "within a reasonable time under the circumstances"). Burnett’s December disclosures were sufficient to trigger Habitat’s obligations under the FMLA, including its duty to inquire further about his condition. *See id.* at 982 (employer’s duty to inquire arises when notice is ambiguous or incomplete).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON BURNETT’S ADA CLAIM BECAUSE HE PROVIDED SUFFICIENT NOTICE OF HIS DISABILITY AND REQUESTED ACCOMMODATION.**

The ADA requires an employee to provide notice of a disability and a request for accommodation. *See 42 U.S.C. § 12112(b)(5)(A)*. Notice is sufficient if the employer is aware of facts that would lead a reasonable person to believe the employee is disabled and needs an accommodation. *SeeEEOC v. Sears, Roebuck & Co.*, 417 F.3d 789, 797 (7th Cir. 2005) (citing *Bultemeyer v. Fort Wayne Cmty. Schs.*, 100 F.3d 1281, 1285 (7th Cir. 1996)).

Here, Burnett provided sufficient notice. In October 2003, he told Polo that he had a "weak bladder" and was seeing a doctor to determine the cause. In December 2003, he disclosed that he had elevated PSA and cholesterol levels and was scheduled for a prostate biopsy. These disclosures were enough to put Habitat on notice that Burnett might have a disability under the ADA. *SeeSears*, 417 F.3d at 797 (holding that notice of a medical condition may be sufficient to trigger the ADA’s accommodation requirements).

Habitat argues that Burnett did not request an accommodation. However, the ADA does not require a formal request for accommodation. *See id.* (holding that an employee’s statements about his condition may suffice). Burnett’s disclosures about his medical condition and need for medical appointments were enough to put Habitat on notice that he might need an accommodation. *See id.* Habitat’s failure to engage in the interactive process further supports Burnett’s claim. *See id.* (holding that an employer’s failure to engage in the interactive process may support an ADA claim).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in granting summary judgment on Burnett’s FMLA and ADA claims. The judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
