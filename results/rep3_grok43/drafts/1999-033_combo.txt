The Equal Protection Clause secures to all persons the right to be free from arbitrary classifications that single out a group for adverse treatment. Strauder v. West Virginia, 100 U.S. 303, 307-08 (1880). It “is essentially a direction that all persons similarly situated should be treated alike.” Barbier v. Connolly, 113 U.S. 27, 31 (1885). The Clause therefore presupposes the existence of a class or group whose members have been denied equal protection of the laws. The Slaughter-House Cases, 83 U.S. 36, 81 (1873).

A plaintiff who alleges only that she was treated differently from others, without identifying any class or group to which she belongs, states no claim under the Clause. Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356, 373-74 (1886) (invalidating ordinance applied “with a mind so unequal and oppressive as to amount to a practical denial by the State of that equal protection of the laws”). The same principle governs regulatory measures that distinguish among individuals or businesses: the distinction must rest on some classification that the State has drawn. Gulf, Colorado & Santa Fe Ry. v. Ellis, 165 U.S. 150, 155 (1897); Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Ry. v. Matthews, 174 U.S. 96, 104 (1899).

This Court has repeatedly rejected equal-protection challenges that rest on nothing more than an assertion of individual disparity. Railway Express Agency, Inc. v. New York, 336 U.S. 106, 112 (1949) (sustaining classification even though it “may not be the best” because it “is at least a rational one”); Williamson v. Lee Optical of Oklahoma, Inc., 348 U.S. 483, 489 (1955) (same). Where no classification appears on the face of the challenged action, the plaintiff cannot invoke the Clause merely by claiming that the action was motivated by personal animus. Morey v. Doud, 354 U.S. 457, 465 (1957); Oyama v. California, 332 U.S. 633, 640 (1948).

The decision below, by permitting a “class of one” suit based solely on allegations of spite unrelated to any group characteristic, departs from these settled principles. Because the complaint contains no allegation of class-based discrimination, it fails to state a claim under the Equal Protection Clause.
