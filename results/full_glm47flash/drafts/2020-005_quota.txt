**ARGUMENT**

**I. Montana has specific jurisdiction over Ford because Ford purposefully availed itself of the Montana market, and the claim arises from the product in the stream of commerce.**

The Due Process Clause requires that a state court have specific jurisdiction over a nonresident defendant only if the defendant has purposefully directed activities toward the forum state and the plaintiff’s claims arise out of or relate to those activities. *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 316 (1945). Here, Ford purposefully availed itself of the Montana market by advertising and selling parts within the state. *Hanson v. Denckla*, 357 U.S. 235, 253 (1958). The claim arises from the Explorer, a product Ford sold in the stream of commerce that ultimately reached Montana. *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, 471 U.S. 462, 472 (1985).

**A. Ford purposefully availed itself of the privilege of conducting activities in Montana.**

For specific jurisdiction to exist, the defendant must have purposefully directed its activities at the forum state. *Helicopteros Nacionales de Colombia, S.A. v. Hall*, 466 U.S. 408, 417 (1984). Ford’s decision to advertise and sell parts in Montana constitutes purposeful availment. *Hanson*, 357 U.S. at 253. By engaging in these activities, Ford invoked the benefits and protections of Montana law. *Id.* Ford’s contacts with Montana were not random or fortuitous; they were the result of a conscious decision to do business in the state.

**B. The claim arises out of Ford’s contacts with Montana.**

Specific jurisdiction requires that the plaintiff’s claim “arise out of or relate to” the defendant’s contacts with the forum. *Burger King*, 471 U.S. at 472. The claim here arises from the Explorer, a vehicle Ford designed, manufactured, and sold. The vehicle was not altered after it left Ford’s control. *Baldwin v. G.A.F. Seelig, Inc.*, 534 U.S. 222, 227 (2002). The stream-of-commerce theory is a valid basis for specific jurisdiction when a product is not altered after leaving the defendant’s control and is ultimately consumed in the forum state. *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980). Because the claim relates to the product Ford sold in the stream of commerce, it arises out of Ford’s contacts with Montana.

**C. The connection between Ford’s contacts and the claim is substantial.**

The Due Process Clause is satisfied if the defendant has purposefully availed himself of the privilege of conducting activities within the forum State. *Burnham v. Superior Court*, 495 U.S. 604, 617 (1990). The defendant’s conduct, and the relation of that conduct to the plaintiff’s claim, must be such that the suit does not offend “traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.” *Goodyear Dunlop Tires Operations, S.A. v. Brown*, 564 U.S. 915, 925 (2011). Ford’s sale of parts in Montana created a substantial connection to the forum. The claim is directly related to the product Ford sold in Montana, and it would be unfair to require Gullett’s estate to travel to Michigan or Delaware to litigate a claim arising from a vehicle that was driven on Montana roads. *Id.* at 925. Therefore, the connection is substantial and fair.
