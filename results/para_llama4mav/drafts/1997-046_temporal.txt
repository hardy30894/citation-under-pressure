The Ohio Adult Parole Authority's clemency process does not implicate a protected life or liberty interest, and the voluntary interview option does not violate an inmate's Fifth Amendment rights.

First, the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment is not triggered by Ohio's clemency process because it does not involve a life or liberty interest protected by the Constitution. As established in Murray v. Hoboken Land & Improvement Co., 59 U.S. 272 (1856), the Due Process Clause is limited to protecting individuals from arbitrary deprivations of life, liberty, or property. Clemency proceedings, being a matter of executive discretion, do not fall within this purview. The Supreme Court has long recognized that pardon and commutation decisions are not subject to judicial review, as they are inherently executive in nature (United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. 128 (1871)). 

Moreover, the voluntary nature of the clemency interview process means that an inmate is not compelled to incriminate themselves. The Fifth Amendment protection against self-incrimination is not implicated when an individual is not coerced into making statements (Twining v. New Jersey, 211 U.S. 78 (1908)). In this case, the Ohio Adult Parole Authority's clemency procedure explicitly allows the inmate to choose whether to participate in the interview and to determine the extent of their participation. As such, there is no compulsion to waive Fifth Amendment rights.

The Due Process Clause does not require the constitutionalization of clemency proceedings. The historical understanding of clemency as an executive function, rather than a judicial one, supports this conclusion (United States v. Klein). The Constitution grants the executive the power to grant reprieves and pardons, and the exercise of this power is not subject to the same due process requirements as judicial proceedings.

In conclusion, Ohio's clemency process, as administered by the Ohio Adult Parole Authority, does not implicate a protected life or liberty interest, and the voluntary interview option does not violate an inmate's Fifth Amendment rights. The Due Process Clause is not triggered, and the Fifth Amendment is not compromised by the voluntary nature of the clemency interview.
