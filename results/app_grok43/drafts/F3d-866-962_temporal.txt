The district court erred in granting summary judgment. The record contains evidence that appellants reported unsafe conditions at the bridge, that BNSF then subjected them to heightened scrutiny and discipline, and that the stated reasons for discipline were not the true reasons.

In NLRB v. Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp., 301 U.S. 1, 33 (1937), the Court held that the National Labor Relations Act protects employees who “present grievances” concerning working conditions. The same principle applies when employees report hazards that affect safe operations. See also Virginian Ry. Co. v. System Fed’n No. 40, 300 U.S. 515, 545 (1937) (Railway Labor Act safeguards the right to present disputes over rules and practices without retaliation).

Appellants’ statements to Trainmaster Dixon and Claims Representative Murphy identified the absence of rails and lighting. Within days BNSF initiated an investigation, increased unannounced operations testing, and ultimately imposed discipline. In International Union, UAW v. Russell, 356 U.S. 634, 645 (1958), the Court noted that an employer’s sudden change in treatment of an employee after protected complaints may support an inference of improper motive. The timing here—investigation notices issued the day after Foster’s recorded statement and shortly after Kline and Snyder spoke with Murphy—creates a triable question on causation.

BNSF’s asserted grounds for discipline do not eliminate that question. In Sartor v. Arkansas Natural Gas Corp., 321 U.S. 620, 627 (1944), the Court cautioned that summary judgment is improper when “the evidence presents a sufficient disagreement to require submission to a jury.” The Public Law Board later vacated the discipline, finding insufficient proof of the charged rule violations. That outcome, combined with the conflicting testimony about the designated crew-change point, raises a genuine issue whether the infractions were the real basis for the actions taken against appellants.

Because the evidence, viewed in the light most favorable to the non-moving parties, permits a finding of retaliation for protected reports, the grant of summary judgment must be reversed.
