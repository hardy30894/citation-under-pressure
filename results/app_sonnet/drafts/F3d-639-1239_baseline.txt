# ARGUMENT

## I. AN UNACCEPTED RULE 68 OFFER OF JUDGMENT FOR THE FULL AMOUNT OF THE NAMED PLAINTIFF'S INDIVIDUAL CLAIM DOES NOT MOOT A CLASS ACTION WHERE THE PLAINTIFF HAS DILIGENTLY PURSUED CLASS CERTIFICATION.

The district court's dismissal rests on an overly rigid application of mootness doctrine that ignores the practical realities of class litigation and the Supreme Court's own recognition that class claims possess a vitality independent of the named plaintiff's individual stake. This Court should reverse.

### A. The Supreme Court Has Rejected Mechanical Mootness Rules in the Class-Action Context.

The Supreme Court has repeatedly cautioned against reflexively applying mootness principles developed for individual litigation to class actions. In *Sosna v. Iowa*, 419 U.S. 393 (1975), the Court held that a class action does not become moot simply because the named plaintiff's individual claim expires, reasoning that the class acquires "a legal status separate from the interest asserted by [the named plaintiff]." *Id.* at 399. The Court extended this reasoning in *U.S. Parole Commission v. Geraghty*, 445 U.S. 388 (1980), holding that a named plaintiff may continue to press a class certification appeal even after his individual claim becomes moot, because he retains "a personal stake in obtaining class certification" that survives the mooting of his substantive claim. *Id.* at 402.

These cases establish that mootness in the class context cannot be assessed solely through the lens of the named plaintiff's individual claim. Yet that is precisely what the district court did here, relying on this Court's general statement in *Clark v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance*, 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir. 2009), that a class action "must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified." That statement, drawn from *Clark*, was never intended to address the manipulative tactic at issue here: a defendant's unilateral attempt to "buy off" a diligent named plaintiff before the plaintiff has had any realistic opportunity to seek certification.

### B. BCR's Tactical Use of Rule 68 to Pick Off the Named Plaintiff Should Not Be Permitted to Defeat Class Jurisdiction.

Courts have long recognized the danger that defendants will attempt to moot class actions by tendering the named plaintiff complete relief before certification, thereby evading class liability altogether. The Third Circuit's decision in *Weiss v. Regal Collections*, 385 F.3d 337 (3d Cir. 2004)—cited by BCR itself—confirms this concern, holding that "an offer of judgment made before a certification motion, and before a reasonable time to file the motion has elapsed, does not automatically moot a plaintiff's claims." *Id.* at 340. The Third Circuit reasoned that a contrary rule would allow defendants to strategically undermine Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23 by "picking off" named plaintiffs one by one, thereby ensuring that "no class relief could ever be obtained." *Id.* at 344.

Here, BCR served its Rule 68 offer within weeks of removal, before any discovery had occurred and before the parties had even submitted their joint status report establishing a bifurcated discovery schedule. Lucero did precisely what the district-court-approved schedule required: he pursued class discovery for six months, then timely filed his motion for certification on February 22, 2010, in full compliance with the February 23 deadline set by the court itself. Aplt.App. 58–59. There was no undue delay. To hold that BCR's premature offer nonetheless mooted the action before certification could be litigated rewards exactly the manipulative behavior *Weiss* condemned and undermines the structural protections of Rule 23.

### C. The Certification Motion Should Be Treated as Relating Back to the Filing of the Complaint.

Even under a mootness framework, the proper remedy is to treat Lucero's certification motion as relating back to the filing of the complaint, preserving the live controversy that existed at the outset. This approach honors *Sosna*'s recognition that the class's interest exists independently of the fluctuating status of the named plaintiff's individual claim. *Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399. Because Lucero's class allegations were pled from the outset, Aplt.App. 13, and because he diligently adhered to the court-ordered discovery and certification schedule, the class controversy that existed at filing remained live throughout—regardless of BCR's attempt to extinguish Lucero's individual claim through a tactical Rule 68 offer.

### D. Alternatively, Lucero's Personal Stake in Class Certification Survived BCR's Offer.

*Geraghty* independently supports reversal. Even if BCR's offer could be deemed to have mooted Lucero's individual damages claim, Lucero retained a personal stake in obtaining class certification itself, just as the plaintiff in *Geraghty* retained standing to appeal denial of certification after his own claim expired. 445 U.S. at 404. The "personal stake" that "Article III requires" is satisfied by the plaintiff's "individual interest in the litigation as a class action." *Id.* at 402–04. Lucero's continued pursuit of certification—timely filed under the court's own schedule—reflects precisely that stake.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the district court's dismissal for lack of subject matter jurisdiction and remand for consideration of Lucero's motion for class certification.
