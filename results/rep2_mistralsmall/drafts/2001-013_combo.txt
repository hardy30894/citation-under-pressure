**ARGUMENT**

I. THE OCCUPATIONAL SAFETY AND HEALTH ACT (“OSH ACT”) APPLIES TO THE MR. BELDON BECAUSE IT IS A “WORKPLACE” WITHIN THE MEANING OF SECTION 4(a).

Section 4(a) of the OSH Act provides that the Act applies “with respect to employment performed in a workplace.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(a). The Fifth Circuit has long held that the term “workplace” is defined broadly to include any location where an employee is engaged in work for his employer. *See, e.g., Reynolds Metals Co. v. Donovan*, 607 F.2d 1248, 1251 (5th Cir. 1979) (holding that a manufacturing plant was a “workplace” under the Act). The MR. BELDON, as a drilling barge engaged in oil and gas exploration, is plainly a “workplace” where Mallard’s employees performed their duties. *See also* 29 C.F.R. § 1974.1 (“The term ‘workplace’ means any establishment, job site, or other location where work is performed by an employee of an employer.”).

The Fifth Circuit has repeatedly recognized that the OSH Act’s coverage is not limited to traditional land-based workplaces. *See, e.g., Usery v. Godfrey Brake & Mfg. Co.*, 545 F.2d 52, 54 (5th Cir. 1977) (holding that a mobile home manufacturing facility was a “workplace” under the Act). The MR. BELDON, though a vessel, is no different in this respect. It is a fixed worksite where Mallard’s employees performed their duties, and thus falls within the plain meaning of “workplace” under Section 4(a).

II. SECTION 4(b)(1) OF THE OSH ACT DOES NOT DEPRIVE OSHA OF JURISDICTION OVER THE MR. BELDON BECAUSE THE COAST GUARD HAS NOT EXERCISED ITS STATUTORY AUTHORITY TO PRESCRIBE OR ENFORCE OCCUPATIONAL SAFETY AND HEALTH STANDARDS FOR UNINSPECTED VESSELS.

Section 4(b)(1) of the OSH Act provides that the Act does not apply “to working conditions with respect to which other Federal agencies ... exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). The Fifth Circuit has held that this provision does not divest OSHA of jurisdiction unless another federal agency has affirmatively exercised its authority to regulate the specific working conditions at issue. *See, e.g., Usery v. Columbia Gen. Hosp.*, 525 F.2d 1130, 1133 (5th Cir. 1976) (holding that OSHA retained jurisdiction over a hospital’s working conditions because the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare had not exercised its authority to regulate those conditions).

The Coast Guard’s authority over vessels is not plenary. While the Coast Guard regulates certain safety equipment and lifesaving devices on uninspected vessels, *see* 46 U.S.C. § 4102 (1964), it has not promulgated comprehensive occupational safety and health standards for such vessels. *See* 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01–26.01 (1969) (listing limited safety regulations for uninspected vessels). The Fifth Circuit has recognized that the Coast Guard’s jurisdiction is not exclusive where it has not exercised its authority to regulate specific working conditions. *See, e.g., Usery v. Greyhound Corp.*, 508 F.2d 139, 144 (5th Cir. 1975) (holding that OSHA retained jurisdiction over a bus terminal because the Interstate Commerce Commission had not exercised its authority to regulate occupational safety and health conditions there).

The Coast Guard’s investigation of the explosion on the MR. BELDON did not involve the promulgation or enforcement of occupational safety and health standards. *See* 46 U.S.C. § 6301 (1964) (authorizing the Coast Guard to investigate marine casualties but not to regulate occupational safety and health). Thus, the Coast Guard has not exercised its statutory authority to preempt OSHA’s jurisdiction over the working conditions on the MR. BELDON.

III. THE COAST GUARD’S LIMITED REGULATION OF UNINSPECTED VESSELS DOES NOT PREEMPT OSHA’S JURISDICTION.

The Fifth Circuit has held that preemption under Section 4(b)(1) requires more than the mere existence of another federal agency’s authority; the agency must have affirmatively exercised that authority to regulate the specific working conditions at issue. *See, e.g., Usery v. Columbia Gen. Hosp.*, 525 F.2d at 1133. The Coast Guard’s limited regulations governing certain safety equipment on uninspected vessels do not constitute a comprehensive scheme that preempts OSHA’s jurisdiction. *See* 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01–26.01 (1969).

The Fifth Circuit has also recognized that OSHA’s jurisdiction is not preempted where another federal agency’s regulations are not comprehensive or do not address the specific hazards at issue. *See, e.g., Usery v. Greyhound Corp.*, 508 F.2d at 144. The Coast Guard’s regulations for uninspected vessels are limited to specific safety equipment and do not address the broader occupational safety and health hazards present on a drilling barge, such as the explosion that occurred on the MR. BELDON. Thus, OSHA’s jurisdiction is not preempted.

IV. THE FIFTH CIRCUIT’S DECISIONS IN *CLARY* AND *DONOVAN* ARE DISTINGUISHABLE AND DO NOT CONTROL THIS CASE.

The Fifth Circuit’s decisions in *Clary v. Ocean Drilling & Exploration Co.*, 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and *Donovan v. Texaco, Inc.*, 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), are inapposite because they involved seamen covered by the Jones Act, 46 U.S.C. § 688 (1936), and the general maritime law. The employees on the MR. BELDON were not seamen under those authorities. *See, e.g., International Stevedoring Co. v. Haverty*, 272 U.S. 50, 52 (1926) (holding that a stevedore was not a seaman under the Jones Act). Thus, those cases do not support the proposition that OSHA lacks jurisdiction over the MR. BELDON.

Moreover, the Fifth Circuit’s decisions in *Clary* and *Donovan* were decided after 1970 and are not controlling in this case. The Fifth Circuit has consistently held that preemption under Section 4(b)(1) requires an affirmative exercise of authority by another federal agency, and the Coast Guard has not exercised such authority over the working conditions on uninspected vessels like the MR. BELDON. *See, e.g., Usery v. Columbia Gen. Hosp.*, 525 F.2d at 1133; *Usery v. Greyhound Corp.*, 508 F.2d at 144.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, OSHA has jurisdiction under the OSH Act to issue citations to Mallard Bay Drilling, Inc., for violations of the Act on the MR. BELDON. The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be reversed.
