**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT BRYANT LACKED STANDING TO ENFORCE THE ARBITRATION AWARD**

An employee who is a third-party beneficiary of a collective bargaining agreement has standing to enforce an arbitration award rendered in his favor. *See United Steelworkers of Am. v. Rawson*, 495 U.S. 362, 374 (1990) (holding that individual employees may sue to enforce rights created by a collective bargaining agreement). Here, the arbitration award granted Bryant reinstatement and back pay. As the intended beneficiary of that award, Bryant has a personal stake in its enforcement. The district court’s contrary conclusion conflates standing with the union’s role as exclusive bargaining representative. *See Vaca v. Sipes*, 386 U.S. 171, 185-86 (1967) (recognizing that an employee may sue to enforce an arbitration award when the union has breached its duty of fair representation). Because the union failed to pursue enforcement, Bryant may do so directly.

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT APPLIED AN ERRONEOUS BURDEN-SHIFTING STANDARD**

In *Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Prods., Inc.*, 530 U.S. 133, 143 (2000), the Supreme Court clarified that a plaintiff’s prima facie case, combined with sufficient evidence of pretext, may permit a jury to infer intentional discrimination. The district court, however, required Bryant to produce “additional, independent evidence” of discrimination beyond pretext. This misstates the law. *See id.* at 148 (holding that “a plaintiff’s prima facie case, combined with sufficient evidence to find that the employer’s asserted justification is false, may permit the trier of fact to conclude that the employer unlawfully discriminated”). The district court’s error infected its summary judgment analysis.

**III. GENUINE DISPUTES OF MATERIAL FACT PRECLUDE SUMMARY JUDGMENT**

**A. Bryant’s Termination Was Motivated by Race and Gender Discrimination**

Bryant established a prima facie case of discrimination: he is African-American, was qualified, was terminated, and similarly situated white and female employees were treated more favorably. *See McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792, 802 (1973). Bell Atlantic’s proffered reason—failure to work overtime—is pretextual. Evidence shows that Bell Atlantic accommodated other employees with child care conflicts but refused Bryant’s union’s offer to have co-workers cover his assignments. *See Reeves*, 530 U.S. at 151 (pretext may be shown by “evidence that the employer’s explanation is unworthy of credence”). A reasonable jury could find that Bell Atlantic’s shifting explanations and harsher treatment of Bryant compared to similarly situated employees demonstrate discriminatory intent. *See Texas Dep’t of Cmty. Affairs v. Burdine*, 450 U.S. 248, 256 (1981).

**B. Bryant’s Retaliation Claim Survives Summary Judgment**

Bryant engaged in protected activity by filing a grievance and an EEOC charge. *See 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-3(a)*. Bell Atlantic’s adverse actions—including escalating suspensions and termination—followed closely in time. *See Clark Cnty. Sch. Dist. v. Breeden*, 532 U.S. 268, 273-74 (2001) (temporal proximity may establish causation). Bell Atlantic’s stated reason for termination is the same pretextual justification for the underlying discrimination. A jury could infer retaliation. *See Reeves*, 530 U.S. at 148.

**C. The District Court Improperly Weighed Evidence**

On summary judgment, the court must view all facts in the light most favorable to the nonmovant. *See Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 255 (1986). The district court credited Bell Atlantic’s explanations and discounted Bryant’s evidence of disparate treatment and pretext. This was error. *See id.* at 249 (summary judgment is improper if “the evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmoving party”). Bryant presented sworn testimony, company documents, and comparator evidence creating genuine disputes.

**CONCLUSION**

For these reasons, this Court should reverse the district court’s grant of summary judgment and remand for further proceedings.
