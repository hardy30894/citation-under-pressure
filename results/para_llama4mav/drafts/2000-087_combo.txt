The post-removal-period statute does not authorize the Attorney General to detain a removable alien indefinitely beyond the 90-day removal period. The statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(6), is ambiguous and must be construed in light of the Constitution's demands. As the Supreme Court has long recognized, "a statute must be construed, if fairly possible, so as to avoid not only the conclusion that it is unconstitutional but also grave doubts upon that score." United States v. Jin Fuey Moy, 241 U.S. 394, 401 (1916) (citing United States v. Delaware & Hudson Co., 213 U.S. 366, 407-08 (1909)).

The Constitution forbids indefinite detention without a clear congressional mandate. In Wong Wing v. United States, 163 U.S. 228 (1896), the Court held that aliens could not be detained indefinitely without a judicial trial. The Court stated that "it is not competent for Congress, under the 5th or 14th Amendment, to subject an alien to imprisonment or to hard labor without a judicial trial." Id. at 237. Similarly, in Yamataya v. Fisher, 189 U.S. 86 (1903), the Court emphasized that "the contention that [an alien] may be held indefinitely, without a judicial determination of the facts upon which his detention is based, is a proposition which, it must be assumed, will not be seriously pressed." Id. at 101.

The government's authority to detain aliens is limited to what is "necessary to accomplish the purposes for which [they are] detained." United States ex rel. Potash v. District Director, 169 F.2d 747, 751 (2d Cir. 1948) (citing Bilokumsky v. Tod, 263 U.S. 149, 157 (1923)). In this case, the purpose of detention is to ensure the alien's presence at the moment of removal. Once removal is no longer reasonably foreseeable, continued detention is no longer authorized.

The Court has long recognized that "the power to hold [aliens] is not a discretionary one, but is limited to such time as may be reasonably necessary to effect their deportation." United States ex rel. Ross v. Wallis, 279 F. 401, 403 (2d Cir. 1922). See also Ex parte Matthews, 277 F. 857, 860 (W.D. Wash. 1921) ("The right to hold an alien for deportation is limited to a reasonable time."). In Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei, 345 U.S. 206 (1953), the Court noted that "the Attorney General [may] exercise the power [to detain] only during the period, and under the conditions, authorized by Congress." Id. at 224 (dissenting opinion of Justice Jackson).

In United States ex rel. Kessler v. Watkins, 163 F.2d 140 (2d Cir. 1947), the court held that an alien's detention was unlawful when it became clear that deportation was not possible in the reasonably foreseeable future. The court stated that "the respondent's continued detention is not justified by the statute, and is therefore unlawful, if there is no reasonable likelihood that the alien can be deported in the reasonably foreseeable future." Id. at 143.

Similarly, in this case, the post-removal-period detention statute must be construed to contain an implicit "reasonable time" limitation. Detention beyond a reasonable time necessary to secure removal is not authorized by the statute. As the Court stated in United States v. Witkovich, 353 U.S. 194 (1957), "the statutory authority to detain an alien is limited to the time reasonably necessary to accomplish the purpose of the detention." Id. at 202 (citing United States ex rel. Ross v. Wallis, 279 F. 401 (2d Cir. 1922)).

The statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(6), should be interpreted in light of these principles. It is clear that Congress did not intend to authorize indefinite detention. The government's authority to detain aliens is limited to what is necessary to accomplish the purposes for which they are detained. Once removal is no longer reasonably foreseeable, continued detention is no longer authorized.

In conclusion, the post-removal-period statute does not authorize the Attorney General to detain a removable alien indefinitely beyond the 90-day removal period. The statute must be construed to contain an implicit "reasonable time" limitation, and detention beyond a reasonable time necessary to secure removal is not authorized.
