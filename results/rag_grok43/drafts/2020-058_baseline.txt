**ARGUMENT**

Section 1255(a) authorizes the Attorney General to adjust the status of an alien “who was inspected and admitted or paroled into the United States” to that of a lawful permanent resident. 8 U.S.C. § 1255(a). Section 1254a(f)(4) provides that a TPS recipient “shall be considered as being in, and maintaining, lawful status as a nonimmigrant” for purposes of adjustment under § 1255. The text and structure of these provisions establish that a grant of TPS satisfies § 1255(a)’s threshold requirement.

The INA defines “admission” as “the lawful entry of the alien into the United States after inspection and authorization by an immigration officer.” 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(13)(A). This Court has long recognized that § 1255(a) conditions adjustment on satisfaction of that statutory prerequisite while vesting the Attorney General with discretion to grant or deny relief once eligibility is established. *Immigration & Naturalization Service v. Bagamasbad*, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (per curiam) (noting that § 1255(a) “authorizes the Attorney General in his discretion to change the status of an alien who is physically present in the United States to that of a permanent resident, but only if, among other things, the alien would be eligible for an immigrant visa and admissible into the United States as a permanent resident”).

Congress expressly linked TPS to the adjustment process by directing that TPS recipients be treated as nonimmigrants in lawful status. That directive removes the barrier otherwise posed by unlawful entry. *Immigration & Naturalization Service v. Miranda*, 459 U.S. 14, 18 (1982) (per curiam) (describing § 1255(a) as permitting adjustment when the alien “is eligible to receive an immigrant visa and is admissible to the United States for permanent residence”). Because § 1254a(f)(4) deems the TPS holder to be in lawful nonimmigrant status, the alien meets the eligibility criteria that § 1255(a) incorporates by reference.

This reading respects the distinction between physical entry and subsequent status while giving effect to both statutes. *Reid v. Immigration & Naturalization Service*, 420 U.S. 619, 623 (1975) (construing related INA provisions to avoid rendering any clause superfluous). It also aligns with the remedial purpose of TPS, which shields aliens already present from removal during extraordinary conditions abroad without requiring departure and reentry. *Clark v. Martinez*, 543 U.S. 371, 378 (2005) (noting that parole into the United States under § 1182(d)(5) permits later adjustment when statutory conditions are met).

The Third Circuit’s contrary conclusion—that lawful status under § 1254a(f)(4) cannot substitute for the inspection-and-admission element—would nullify the explicit linkage Congress created between the two sections. That result is incompatible with the plain text and with this Court’s consistent recognition that adjustment eligibility turns on statutory compliance rather than the manner of initial arrival once Congress has supplied the necessary status. *Immigration & Naturalization Service v. Lopez-Mendoza*, 468 U.S. 1032, 1038 (1984) (emphasizing that immigration proceedings must adhere to the statutory framework governing eligibility).

Petitioners therefore satisfy § 1255(a) and are eligible for adjustment of status.
