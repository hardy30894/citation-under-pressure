**Argument**

**I. The District Court Abused Its Discretion by Enhancing the Fee Based on the Quality of Counsel’s Performance**

The district court abused its discretion by enhancing the lodestar fee based on the quality of counsel’s performance rather than specific evidence of exceptional results. The Supreme Court has not authorized such enhancements, and the district court’s findings were insufficient to support the 1.75 multiplier.

**A. The Supreme Court’s “May Be” Language Does Not Authorize Fee Enhancements Based on Quality of Performance**

The Supreme Court has not established a broad right for district courts to enhance fees based on the quality of representation. In *Hensley v. Eckerhart*, 461 U.S. 424 (1983), the Court established the lodestar method, which presumes the fee to be the product of hours reasonably expended multiplied by a reasonable hourly rate. In *Blum v. Stenson*, 465 U.S. 886 (1984), the Court reiterated that the lodestar is presumed to be the reasonable fee. The *Blum* Court noted that "in some cases of exceptional success an enhancement award may be justified." *Blum*, 465 U.S. at 901, 104 S.Ct. at 1550. However, the *Blum* Court reversed the enhancement below, rendering this language dicta. The Court did not intend to give district judges broad discretion to enhance fees for "superb quality" representation absent specific evidence of exceptional results.

**B. The District Court Lacked Specific Evidence to Support the Enhancement**

The district court failed to meet the *Blum* standard for "specific evidence." *Blum* requires that an applicant "offer specific evidence" to demonstrate that an enhancement is necessary to reflect counsel’s performance. *Blum*, 465 U.S. at 899, 104 S.Ct. at 1549. Here, the district judge relied on his own "substantial experience" and "observation" of counsel's performance. This is not specific evidence in the record. The judge’s subjective assessment of "stellar performance" is not a substitute for the objective record required by *Blum*. The district court’s enhancement was therefore an abuse of discretion.
