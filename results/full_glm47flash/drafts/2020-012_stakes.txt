**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Board’s decision denying the request to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application constitutes a “final decision” under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) and is therefore subject to judicial review.**

The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.* is a narrow interpretation of the statute that conflicts with the broader statutory purpose of ensuring finality in administrative proceedings. Under the plain language of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), the jurisdiction of the federal courts of appeals is limited to the review of Board decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits after administrative appeals have been exhausted. 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003). However, a decision to deny a request to reopen a prior claim is not merely a procedural step; it is a substantive adjudication that definitively resolves the underlying claim. The Supreme Court has emphasized that the need for judicial review is at its greatest when the agency's action is final and conclusive. *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 108 (1977). By refusing to reopen Salinas’s claim, the Board issued a final determination that the claim is barred, effectively ending the administrative process on that specific application.

The D.C. Circuit’s recent decision in *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board* provides a more accurate interpretation of the statute. The D.C. Circuit held that “the Railroad Retirement Act grants the [circuit] Court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations.” 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016). This interpretation aligns with the statutory text and the principle that a negative adjudication on a claim is a final decision on the merits. The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding in *Roberts* creates an unnecessary circuit split and deprives petitioners of meaningful judicial review of final agency actions. Therefore, the Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is a final decision subject to review under § 355(f).
