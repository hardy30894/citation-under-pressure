Argument

Section 1407(a) directs that each action transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded by the panel” at or before the conclusion of those proceedings. This language is mandatory. Anderson v. Yungkau, 329 U.S. 482, 485 (1947). Where Congress has spoken specifically, that command controls over the general transfer authority of section 1404(a). Escoe v. Zerbst, 295 U.S. 490, 493 (1935); Ex parte Collett, 337 U.S. 55, 58 (1949).

Section 1404(a) itself permits transfer only to a district where the action “might have been brought.” Hoffman v. Blaski, 363 U.S. 335, 342-43 (1960). A transferee court therefore cannot assign the case to itself for trial. Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612, 616-17 (1964); Continental Grain Co. v. The FBL-585, 364 U.S. 19, 21 (1960). The statute reflects the settled principle that a plaintiff’s choice of forum governs absent compelling justification. Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501, 508 (1947); Koster v. (American) Lumbermens Mut. Cas. Co., 330 U.S. 518, 524 (1947).

Mandamus lies to correct a district court’s refusal to remand. The writ issues where the petitioner has no other adequate means of relief and the order is clearly erroneous as a matter of law. Roche v. Evaporated Milk Ass’n, 319 U.S. 21, 26 (1943). Supervisory control is warranted to prevent a court from exceeding its jurisdiction or usurping judicial power. La Buy v. Howes Leather Co., 352 U.S. 249, 256 (1957); Bankers Life & Cas. Co. v. Holland, 346 U.S. 379, 383 (1953); Ex parte Fahey, 332 U.S. 258, 260 (1947). Because the Arizona court lacked authority to retain the action for trial, the judgment should be reversed and the case remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
