**ARGUMENT**

I.  The FDCPA distinguishes debt collectors from creditors based on whether the entity collects for its own account or for another, not on the default status of the debt.

The FDCPA defines a "debt collector" as a person who "regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed or due or asserted to be owed or due another." 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). This definition focuses on the relationship between the collector and the creditor at the time of collection. See *Heintz v. Jenkins*, 514 U.S. 291, 293 (1995) (quoting § 1692a(6)). Conversely, a "creditor" is a person to whom the debt is owed. 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(4). When a creditor collects its own debt for its own account, it is not acting as a debt collector. See *Heintz*, 514 U.S. at 293. Here, Santander purchased the defaulted loans from CitiFinancial and subsequently collected the debts for its own account. Therefore, Santander was a creditor, not a debt collector, at the time of the alleged violations.

II.  The statutory structure of § 1692a(6) requires determining if a person fits a definition *before* applying exclusions.

The Appellants attempt to use the exclusions in § 1692a(6) to create a "mutually exclusive" rule, but this misreads the statute. The statute first defines who is a debt collector, and only then excludes certain classes of persons. See *Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A.*, 797 F.3d 1309, 1314 (11th Cir. 2015) ("[W]here a person does not fall within subsection (F) or any one of the six statutory exclusions, he is not deemed a ‘debt collector’ as a matter of course"). The exclusion in § 1692a(6)(F)(iii) applies only to those who are already debt collectors. See *S. Rep. No. 95-382*, at 3-4 (1977) (explaining the exclusion was intended to protect loan servicers). Because Santander collected for its own account, it did not fit the definition of a debt collector, rendering the exclusion inapplicable.

III.  The phrase "debts owed or due another" refers to the time of collection, not the time of origination.

The phrase "debts owed or due another" must be read to apply to the time of the collection activity. See *Paroline v. United States*, 572 U.S. 434, 440 (2014) (quoting *Porto Rico Ry., Light & Power Co. v. Mor*, 253 U.S. 345, 348 (1920)). At the time Santander collected, the debt was owed to Santander, not CitiFinancial. See *Davidson*, 797 F.3d at 1318. Therefore, Santander was a creditor collecting its own debt, not a debt collector.
