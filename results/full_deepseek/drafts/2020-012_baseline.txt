**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Board’s Decision Not to Reopen a Prior Benefits Claim Is a “Final Decision” Subject to Judicial Review Under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).**

The plain language of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) grants courts of appeals jurisdiction to review “any final decision of the Board.” A decision declining to reopen a prior claim is a final decision because it conclusively resolves a discrete issue—whether the prior determination should be revisited—and leaves nothing further for the Board to adjudicate. *See Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 108 (1977) (holding that a refusal to reopen is a final agency action reviewable under the Administrative Procedure Act). Although *Sanders* interpreted the Social Security Act, its reasoning applies with equal force here: the Board’s refusal to reopen “is a final decision” that “terminates the proceeding” and “is not subject to further administrative review.” *Id.* at 108–09.

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding in *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), misreads § 355(f). That court reasoned that jurisdiction is “limited to the review of Board decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits after administrative appeals have been exhausted.” *Id.* at 140. But nothing in § 355(f) restricts review to merits determinations. The statute speaks broadly of “any final decision,” and a reopening decision is final because it ends the Board’s consideration of the request. *See Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 826 F.3d 500, 503 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (holding that “the Board’s decision not to reopen a prior benefits determination is a ‘final decision’ under § 355(f)”). The D.C. Circuit correctly observed that “the Board’s own regulations treat a reopening decision as a final decision for purposes of administrative appeal,” and that “the Board’s decision not to reopen is the last word on the subject.” *Id.* at 504.

**II. The Majority of Circuits That Have Rejected Jurisdiction Rely on an Erroneous Interpretation of *Sanders*.**

The Fifth Circuit, in *Roberts*, joined several circuits in concluding that *Sanders* precludes review of reopening decisions. *See Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141 (citing *Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 198 F.3d 139 (4th Cir. 1999); *Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 63 F.3d 972 (10th Cir. 1995); *Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 918 F.2d 567 (6th Cir. 1990); *Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 708 F.2d 250 (7th Cir. 1983)). But *Sanders* did not hold that reopening decisions are unreviewable; it held only that the Social Security Act’s provision for judicial review did not apply to reopening decisions because the Act’s “final decision” requirement was not satisfied. *Sanders*, 430 U.S. at 108. Critically, *Sanders* then reviewed the reopening decision under the Administrative Procedure Act, finding it final and reviewable. *Id.* at 109. The Railroad Retirement Act’s “final decision” language is broader than the Social Security Act’s, and the Board’s own regulations treat reopening decisions as final. *See* 20 C.F.R. § 261.2 (providing that a reopening decision is “final” for purposes of administrative appeal). Thus, *Sanders* actually supports review here.

**III. The Fifth Circuit’s Rule of Orderliness Does Not Bar This Court from Correcting *Roberts*.**

The Fifth Circuit dismissed Salinas’s petition solely because *Roberts* bound the panel. But *Roberts* is wrongly decided and conflicts with the D.C. Circuit’s persuasive reasoning in *Stovic*. This Court should grant certiorari to resolve the circuit split and clarify that the Board’s refusal to reopen a prior claim is a final decision subject to judicial review under § 355(f). *See Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504 (holding that “the Board’s decision not to reopen is a final decision” and that “the majority of circuits have erred in concluding otherwise”).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit’s dismissal and hold that the Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim is a final decision reviewable under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).
