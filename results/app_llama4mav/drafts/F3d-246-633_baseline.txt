The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's claim of conspiracy in violation of the Railway Labor Act ("RLA"), 45 U.S.C. § 153. The RLA requires that a union fairly represent its members in grievance proceedings. Steele v. Louisville & Nashville R.R. Co., 323 U.S. 192, 204 (1944). A union breaches its duty of fair representation when its actions are "arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith." Air Line Pilots Ass'n, Int'l v. O'Neill, 499 U.S. 65, 67 (1991) (quoting Vaca v. Sipes, 386 U.S. 171, 190 (1967)). Here, Plaintiff presented evidence that UTU representative Earley conspired with CSX representative Emerick and PLB Chair Cluster to deny Plaintiff the thirty shares of CSX stock. Specifically, Plaintiff alleged that Earley argued against Plaintiff's position during the executive session, which was recorded by Plaintiff. The district court should have considered this evidence in determining whether a genuine issue of material fact existed regarding UTU's breach of its duty of fair representation.

The district court also erred in awarding Defendants statutory damages, punitive damages, and attorneys' fees and costs under the Federal Wiretap Act (the "Act"), 18 U.S.C. § 2510 et seq. The Act prohibits the intentional interception of oral communications, 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(a), but provides an exception for recordings made in the "ordinary course of business." 18 U.S.C. § 2510(5)(a)(i). The district court found that Plaintiff intentionally recorded the executive session, but failed to consider whether the recording was made in the ordinary course of the PLB hearing. The PLB members requested an executive session, which is a common practice in PLB hearings. See United States v. McIntyre, 582 F.2d 1221, 1224 (9th Cir. 1978) (holding that a recording made in the ordinary course of a judicial proceeding is not an "interception" under the Act). As the recording was made in a quasi-judicial setting, it is arguable that it was made in the "ordinary course of business." The district court should have considered this exception when determining whether Plaintiff's actions violated the Act.

Furthermore, the district court's award of $250,000 in statutory damages was excessive. The Act provides for statutory damages in an amount equal to the greater of $100 per day or $10,000. 18 U.S.C. § 2520(c)(2)(B). The district court should have considered the actual harm suffered by Defendants when determining the amount of statutory damages. See Reynolds v. Spears, 93 F.3d 428, 435 (8th Cir. 1996) (holding that the district court should consider the actual harm suffered by the plaintiff when determining statutory damages under the Act).

In conclusion, the district court erred in granting summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's RLA claim and in awarding Defendants damages and attorneys' fees under the Act. The judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
