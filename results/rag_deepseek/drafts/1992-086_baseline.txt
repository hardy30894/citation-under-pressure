## ARGUMENT

The judgment below must be reversed because the School District’s policy of excluding religious speech from its facilities—while permitting a broad array of secular expressive activities—constitutes impermissible viewpoint discrimination in a limited public forum, violating the Free Speech Clause of the First Amendment.

### I. The School District Created a Limited Public Forum Open to a Wide Range of Expressive Activity

Under this Court’s forum analysis, a public entity that intentionally opens its property for expressive activity by the community creates a “limited public forum.” *Perry Education Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983). Once such a forum is established, content-based exclusions are subject to strict scrutiny, and viewpoint discrimination is forbidden even in a nonpublic forum. *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 788, 806 (1985).

The record demonstrates that Center Moriches School District regularly permitted a wide variety of community groups to use its facilities after hours: the Boy Scouts, Girl Scouts, civic organizations, adult education classes, musical performances, and even a lecture series on parapsychology. The district court found that these uses “carried out” secular purposes, but that finding only underscores the forum’s openness to diverse expressive content. By admitting groups for cultural, recreational, and educational purposes, the District created a forum that was “generally open for use by” community speakers. *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 267 (1981).

The District’s own regulations permitted use of school premises for “social, civic, and recreational purposes” and for “meetings of a community, cultural, or educational nature.” This broad language, combined with the District’s consistent practice of approving secular uses, established a limited public forum for community expression. The District cannot now claim that religious speech—which addresses the same topics of family and child-rearing that its forum accommodates—falls outside the forum’s boundaries. *See Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 267-68.

### II. Excluding Lamb’s Chapel Because Its Film Series Presented a Religious Viewpoint Is Viewpoint Discrimination

The District did not deny Lamb’s Chapel access because the proposed use would disrupt school operations or interfere with educational purposes. It denied access solely because the film series was “church related” and presented “Christian values.” This is the essence of viewpoint discrimination: the District permitted discussion of family values and child-rearing from secular perspectives but barred the identical subject matter when offered from a religious viewpoint.

This Court has repeatedly held that religious speech enjoys full First Amendment protection. *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 269. In *Widmar*, the Court invalidated a university regulation that excluded religious worship and teaching from a forum otherwise open to student groups, holding that the regulation “discriminates against religious speech on the basis of its content” and could not survive strict scrutiny. *Id.* at 273. The same principle applies here. The District opened its facilities to groups discussing family and societal issues; it cannot then silence groups that address those issues from a religious perspective without advancing a compelling interest.

The District’s claim that it created a forum limited to *secular* speech is foreclosed by *Widmar*. In that case, the University argued that it had not opened its forum to “religious worship or religious teaching,” but this Court rejected that distinction, noting that “religious worship and discussion” are forms of “speech and association protected by the First Amendment.” *Id.* at 269. A forum cannot be defined by carving out disfavored viewpoints. Once the government opens a forum for expressive activity, it cannot exclude speech because of the speaker’s religious perspective. *Board of Educ. of Westside Community Schs. v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226, 250-51 (1990) (plurality opinion) (rejecting argument that equal access for religious groups would violate Establishment Clause and noting that “the First Amendment forbids ‘enility’ in education” is not a proper basis for viewpoint discriination).

### III. Avoiding an Establishment Clause Violation Does Not Justify Viewpoint Discriination

The District contends that excluding religious speech is necessary to avoid Establishment Clause concerns. But *Widmar* explicitly rejected that argument: “The University’s interest in maintaining strict separation of church and state is not sufficiently compelling to justify content-based discriination against religious speech.” 454 U.S. at 271, n.13. This Court has consistently held that equal access to public facilities for religious and secular speakers does not violate the Establishment Clause because the government does not endorse religion by providing a neutral forum. *Mergens*, 496 U.S. at 248-50.

Here, Lamb’s Chapel sought only to use the forum on the same terms as secular groups—after hours, open to the public, and without school sponsorship. Permitting a church to present a film series on family values in a forum already open to secular discussions of family life does not convey government endorsement of religion. It simply ensures that the government does not “discriinate against religious speech.” *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 274.

The District’s policy sweeps more broadly than necessary to avoid any Establishment Clause problem. It excludes *all* religious speech, even speech that is purely expressive and nonproselytizing. Such a blanket exclusion is not narrowly tailored to any legitimate interest. *See Cornelius*, 473 U.S. at 808 (reaonableness standard for nonpublic forum still prohibits viewpoint discriination).

### Conclusion

Because the School District opened its facilities to a broad range of community groups but denied access to Lamb’s Chapel solely on account of the religious viewpoint of its proposed films, the District violated the Free Speech Clause of the First Amendment. The judgment of the Second Circuit should be reversed.
