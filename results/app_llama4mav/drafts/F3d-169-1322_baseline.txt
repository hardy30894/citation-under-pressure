## Argument

The district court correctly granted summary judgment in favor of the appellees, John Burton, Felix Burton, Little Rock Produce Company, and Bobby Hall, as they were not joint employers of the migrant farm workers under the Agricultural Workers Protection Act (AWPA), 29 U.S.C. §§ 1801-1872 (1994).

### I. The Appellees Were Not Joint Employers Under the AWPA

The AWPA defines "employ" as "to suffer or permit to work." 29 U.S.C. § 1802(5). To determine whether an entity is an employer under the AWPA, courts consider factors such as the nature and degree of control over the workers, the degree of supervision over the work, and the power to determine the workers' pay rates or methods of payment. See Antenor v. D & S Farms, 88 F.3d 925, 929-30 (11th Cir. 1996) (citing Aimable v. Long & Scott Farms, 20 F.3d 434, 440-41 (11th Cir. 1994)). 

In this case, the Burtons directed Luxama to specific snap bean fields, and Luxama supervised the harvest. The Burtons observed the workers' progress approximately two to three times a day. However, this level of supervision is not sufficient to establish that the Burtons, or Little Rock and Hall, were joint employers. See Antenor, 88 F.3d at 932-33 (holding that a grower's supervision of farm workers was not sufficient to establish joint employment where the farm labor contractor was responsible for the day-to-day operations).

Moreover, the district court correctly found that Little Rock and Hall were not joint employers, as they did not exercise control over the workers or determine their pay rates. See Charles v. Burton, 857 F.Supp. 1574, 1583 (M.D.Ga. 1994). The contract between the Burtons and Little Rock required the Burtons to obtain labor for the snap bean harvest, and Little Rock supplied the seeds, boxes, and a trailer. However, this arrangement does not establish that Little Rock and Hall were joint employers.

### II. The Appellees Are Not Liable for Luxama's Failure to Comply with the AWPA's Registration and Insurance Provisions

The AWPA requires farm labor contractors to register with the Department of Labor and to carry liability insurance or a bond. 29 U.S.C. §§ 1811, 1841(b). The statute also imposes a duty on agricultural employers to verify a farm labor contractor's registration before utilizing their services. 29 U.S.C. § 1842.

The Burtons failed to check Luxama's certification as an FLC and failed to learn that Luxama no longer carried the required insurance. However, the district court correctly held that the workers were entitled only to statutory damages under 29 U.S.C. § 1854(c)(1) for the Burtons' violation of the registration verification provisions. See Charles v. Burton, No. 92-150-VAL (M.D.Ga. Mar. 12, 1996).

The district court also correctly granted summary judgment in favor of Little Rock and Hall, finding that they had not "utilized" the services of Luxama and his crew within the meaning of 29 U.S.C. § 1842. The statute defines "utilize the services of a farm labor contractor" as "to employ, engage, or contract with a farm labor contractor to supply farm laborers." 29 C.F.R. § 500.20(n). The record shows that Little Rock and Hall did not contract with Luxama to supply farm laborers, and therefore, they did not "utilize" his services.

In conclusion, the district court correctly granted summary judgment in favor of the appellees, as they were not joint employers of the migrant farm workers under the AWPA, and they are not liable for Luxama's failure to comply with the AWPA's registration and insurance provisions.
