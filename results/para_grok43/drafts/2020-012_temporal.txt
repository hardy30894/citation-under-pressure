The Railroad Retirement Act confers jurisdiction upon the courts of appeals to review any final decision of the Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A Board order refusing to reopen a prior claim satisfies that statutory requirement. Once the Board has declined to disturb its earlier determination, “no further administrative proceedings remain to be had,” and the denial therefore constitutes the final administrative act on the claim. Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254 (2d Cir. 1966).

That conclusion follows from the settled principle that an agency’s refusal to exercise its discretion to reopen ends the administrative process and renders the underlying order reviewable. The Second Circuit so held in Szostak, observing that the statutory grant of review extends to “any final decision” and that a reopening denial meets the definition because it “finally disposes of the rights of the parties.” Id. at 254–55. The same reasoning applies here: the Board’s determination that Salinas’s 2006 application lay beyond the regulatory reopening period conclusively resolved his request and left nothing further for administrative consideration.

The text of § 355(f) contains no exception for reopening decisions. Where Congress has wished to limit review to initial benefit determinations, it has done so expressly; the absence of such language confirms that reopening denials fall within the general grant of jurisdiction. Szostak, 370 F.2d at 255. Accordingly, the petition for review should be entertained.
