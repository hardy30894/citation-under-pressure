**ARGUMENT**

**I. Section 1231(a)(5) does not apply to aliens who re-entered the United States before its effective date.**

The reinstatement statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(5), provides that a prior deportation order shall be reinstated if the alien "has reentered the United States unlawfully after having been removed." The statute’s text does not specify whether "reentered" refers to reentries occurring before or after its 1997 enactment. In such cases, we look to the "ordinary meaning" of the words at the time of enactment. *Caminetti v. United States*, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917). Under longstanding immigration precedent, "reentered" refers to an alien’s physical reentry into the United States after having been formally excluded or deported—not to an alien’s continued unlawful presence following an initial illegal entry. *United States ex rel. Volpe v. Smith*, 180 F.2d 810, 812 (2d Cir. 1950) (L. Hand, J.); *United States ex rel. Petrone v. Smith*, 151 F.2d 459, 460 (2d Cir. 1945).

The government’s interpretation—that § 1231(a)(5) applies to pre-enactment re-entries—would impose new legal consequences on conduct that occurred before the statute’s passage. Statutes are not to be construed as having retroactive effect unless Congress has clearly manifested such intent. *United States v. Magnolia Petroleum Co.*, 276 U.S. 160, 162-63 (1928). The government cannot point to any clear statement in § 1231(a)(5) evidencing such intent. *Shaw v. United States*, 30 F.2d 276, 279 (2d Cir. 1929) (L. Hand, J.).

**II. Even if § 1231(a)(5) applies to pre-enactment re-entries, its application to Fernandez-Vargas is impermissibly retroactive.**

A statute operates retroactively if it "takes away or impairs vested rights acquired under existing laws, or creates a new obligation, imposes a new duty, or attaches a new disability in respect to transactions or considerations already past." *Shelly v. Kraemer*, 334 U.S. 1, 10 (1948) (quoting *Shaw*, 30 F.2d at 279). The reinstatement statute does precisely that. Before its enactment, an alien who re-entered the United States illegally after a prior deportation could apply for discretionary relief from deportation, including adjustment of status. *United States ex rel. Marcantonio v. District Director*, 165 F.2d 99, 100 (2d Cir. 1947). The reinstatement statute strips aliens of this right, making deportation automatic and eliminating all forms of relief. 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(5).

This Court has long held that statutes which deprive aliens of vested rights or impose new legal consequences on past conduct are impermissibly retroactive unless Congress has clearly expressed such intent. *Magnolia Petroleum*, 276 U.S. at 162-63; *Shaw*, 30 F.2d at 279. The Tenth Circuit’s conclusion that Fernandez-Vargas had no "protectable expectation of being able to adjust his status" is unsupported by precedent. *United States ex rel. Vajtauer v. Commissioner*, 273 U.S. 50, 53 (1927) (holding that aliens have a protectable right to apply for discretionary relief from deportation). The reinstatement statute does not merely apply new legal consequences to future conduct; it attaches new legal disabilities to past conduct, thereby impairing vested rights. *Vajtauer*, 273 U.S. at 53; *Shelly*, 334 U.S. at 10.

**III. The government’s reliance on *Landgraf v. USI Film Products*, 511 U.S. 244 (1994), is misplaced.**

The government cites *Landgraf* for the proposition that statutes should be presumed to apply retroactively unless they would "impair rights a party possessed when he acted." *Landgraf*, 511 U.S. at 280. However, *Landgraf* does not control this case because it was decided after the reinstatement statute’s enactment and does not address immigration law. *Id.* at 248 n.5. Moreover, *Landgraf* reaffirmed the longstanding rule that statutes are not to be construed as having retroactive effect unless Congress has clearly manifested such intent. *Id.* at 260-61 (citing *Magnolia Petroleum*, 276 U.S. at 162-63). The reinstatement statute contains no such clear statement.

**Conclusion**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should hold that § 1231(a)(5) does not apply to aliens who re-entered the United States before its effective date and, in the alternative, that its application to Fernandez-Vargas is impermissibly retroactive.
