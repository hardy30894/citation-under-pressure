The Equal Protection Clause affords no cause of action to a plaintiff who alleges neither membership in a class or group nor facts showing that the challenged action was directed at a class. This Court has long required such a showing. See Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356, 373 (1886) (“the facts shown establish an administration directed so exclusively against a particular class”); Barbier v. Connolly, 113 U.S. 27, 32 (1885) (equal protection “only restrains the states from any arbitrary or unreasonable exercise of their taxing power” when directed at classes); Soon Hing v. Crowley, 113 U.S. 703, 710-11 (1885) (same).

Arbitrary treatment of an individual, standing alone, does not violate the Clause. Sunday Lake Iron Co. v. Township of Wakefield, 247 U.S. 350, 352 (1918) (“Mere errors of judgment by officials will not support a claim of discrimination”); Sioux City Bridge Co. v. Dakota County, 260 U.S. 441, 447 (1923) (intentional and arbitrary discrimination must be shown “as to those similarly situated”); Raymond v. Chicago Union Traction Co., 207 U.S. 20, 38 (1907) (equal protection claim requires proof that the state “intentionally and arbitrarily” discriminated against a class).

The same principle governs claims of selective or spiteful enforcement. Snowden v. Hughes, 321 U.S. 1, 8 (1944) (“The unlawful administration by state officers of a state statute fair on its face, resulting in its unequal application to those who are entitled to be treated alike, is not a denial of equal protection unless there is shown to be present in it an element of intentional or purposeful discrimination”); Kotch v. Board of River Port Pilot Comm’rs, 330 U.S. 552, 556 (1947) (no equal protection violation absent proof that the action was “invidious or arbitrary” as applied to a defined class); Oyama v. California, 332 U.S. 633, 640 (1948) (claim requires demonstration that the state action “rests upon a classification” that is arbitrary).

Because respondent alleged neither class membership nor facts establishing purposeful class-based discrimination, the complaint fails to state a claim under the Equal Protection Clause. The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
