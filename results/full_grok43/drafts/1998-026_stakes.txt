ARGUMENT

A district court's failure to comply with Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) does not furnish a basis for collateral relief under 28 U.S.C. § 2255 when the defendant knew of his right to appeal and elected not to exercise it. Collateral attack is available only for "a fundamental defect which inherently results in a complete miscarriage of justice." Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424, 428 (1962). The omission of the required advice is not such a defect when the defendant already possessed the information the rule seeks to convey.

This Court has repeatedly declined to grant § 2255 relief for Rule 32 violations absent prejudice. In Hill, the Court held that the sentencing court's failure to afford the defendant an opportunity to speak before imposition of sentence did not justify collateral relief because the defendant "suffered no prejudice." Id. at 429. The same principle governs here. After an evidentiary hearing, the district court found that Peguero "knew of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing." That finding, undisturbed on appeal, establishes that the rule's purpose was fulfilled and that no miscarriage of justice occurred.

Relief is likewise unavailable under the harmless-error framework applied to Rule 32 claims on collateral review. See United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780, 784 (1979) (holding that a Rule 11 violation "is not a fundamental defect" warranting § 2255 relief absent prejudice). Where, as here, the defendant was already aware of his appellate rights, the district court's silence caused no harm. The court of appeals correctly concluded that the violation was harmless.

Peguero's reliance on cases in which defendants were unaware of their rights is misplaced. Those decisions turned on the absence of knowledge; they do not support relief when knowledge is affirmatively established. Because Peguero knew of his right to appeal and chose not to pursue one, the district court's omission supplies no ground for habeas relief.
