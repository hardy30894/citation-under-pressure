**ARGUMENT**

The three-judge court’s population-reduction order satisfies every prerequisite of the Prison Litigation Reform Act, 18 U.S.C. § 3626. Section 3626(a)(3)(E) authorizes such an order only when crowding is the “primary cause” of the Eighth Amendment violation and “no other relief will remedy” it. The district court made both findings after exhaustive evidentiary hearings. Those findings are reviewed for clear error. *Anderson v. City of Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 573 (1985).

The record establishes that severe overcrowding—operating at nearly 200 percent of design capacity—directly produced the systemic failures in medical and mental-health care that violated the Eighth Amendment. *See Estelle v. Gamble*, 429 U.S. 97, 104 (1976) (deliberate indifference to serious medical needs constitutes cruel and unusual punishment). No lesser remedy could abate the violations; prior stipulations and single-judge orders had failed, and the State conceded its inability to comply. The order therefore meets the statutory threshold.

The PLRA further requires that relief be “narrowly drawn,” extend “no further than necessary,” and be “the least intrusive means” available. 18 U.S.C. § 3626(a)(1)(A). The court set the cap at 137.5 percent of design capacity, a measured reduction that leaves California substantial flexibility in implementation. It did not mandate any particular releases or override state sentencing laws. *Cf. Miller v. French*, 530 U.S. 327, 347 (2000) (PLRA respects state authority while remedying constitutional violations). The order thus respects the statutory command that federal courts “not occupy the field of prison administration.” *Rhodes v. Chapman*, 452 U.S. 337, 352 (1981) (Brennan, J., concurring in judgment).

Petitioners’ remaining objections—that the order improperly intrudes on state functions or rests on insufficient evidence—were rejected by the three-judge court on a fully developed record. Because the findings are not clearly erroneous and the remedy is tailored to the proven violations, the order complies with the PLRA in all respects.
