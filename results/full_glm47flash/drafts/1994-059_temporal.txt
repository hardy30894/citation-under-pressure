**Argument**

**I. The Qualifications Clauses of Article I, Section 5 of the United States Constitution are Exclusive and Preempt State Regulation.**

The Qualifications Clauses of Article I, Section 5 of the United States Constitution are exclusive and preclude states from adding additional requirements for holding federal office. The text of the Constitution is unambiguous in this regard. In *United States v. Hartwell*, 73 U.S. (6 Wall.) 384 (1868), the Supreme Court held that "The Constitution fixes the qualifications of Senators and Representatives, and no State can add to them." This principle is rooted in the Founders' intent to prevent a "hodge-podge" of qualifications that would undermine the national character of the legislature. As the Court reiterated in *Powell v. McCormack*, 395 U.S. 486 (1969), "The Constitution fixes the qualifications of Senators and Representatives, and no State can add to them." By engrafting term limits onto the qualifications for Congress, Arkansas Amendment 73 violates this exclusive federal power. The Constitution does not list term limits as a qualification, and thus, the State lacks the authority to impose one.
