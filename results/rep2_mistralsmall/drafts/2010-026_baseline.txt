**ARGUMENT**

I. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE “INTERNAL PERSONNEL RULES AND PRACTICES” UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 2.

The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test for Exemption 2 was erroneous and conflicts with this Court’s precedent. Exemption 2 shields only “matters related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). As this Court has repeatedly emphasized, FOIA exemptions are to be narrowly construed. *Dep’t of Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 361 (1976); *U.S. Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989).

The Ninth Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test improperly expands Exemption 2 beyond its statutory bounds. The statute does not refer to “predominantly internal” materials; it requires that the information be “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” *Id.* The D.C. Circuit’s test conflates the statutory language with a policy concern—risk of circumvention—without grounding in the statute itself. *Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 670 F.2d 1051, 1072-74 (D.C. Cir. 1981). This Court has never endorsed such an expansive reading. To the contrary, in *Rose*, the Supreme Court cautioned that Exemption 2’s scope is limited to “routine matters of no genuine public interest.” 425 U.S. at 369.

Moreover, the ESQD arc maps at issue here are not “internal personnel rules and practices.” They are technical safety calculations used to design and operate ammunition storage facilities. Commander Whitbred’s affidavit confirms that these maps are used for “risk mitigation and enhanced safety,” not for regulating agency personnel. This is quintessential operational, not internal personnel, information. *See Maricopa Audubon Soc. v. U.S. Forest Serv.*, 108 F.3d 1082, 1085 (9th Cir. 1997) (holding that data not “related solely to internal personnel rules and practices” is not exempt under Exemption 2).

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), is misplaced. *Hardy* involved law enforcement manuals used by agency personnel to execute search warrants—clearly internal practices. The ESQD arc maps, by contrast, are not used to regulate agency personnel; they are used to plan and operate a military facility. The Ninth Circuit’s expansion of Exemption 2 to cover operational safety data undermines FOIA’s core purpose: ensuring public access to government information. *See Rose*, 425 U.S. at 360-61.

II. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT DISCLOSURE OF ESQD ARC MAPS WOULD “RISK CIRCUMVENTION OF AGENCY REGULATION.”

Even if the ESQD arc maps were “internal personnel rules and practices,” the Ninth Circuit erred in finding a risk of circumvention. The circumvention risk must be real, not speculative. *See Schiller v. NLRB*, 964 F.2d 1205, 1208 (D.C. Cir. 1992). The Navy has not shown that disclosure of these maps would enable anyone to circumvent safety regulations. The maps are not regulatory documents; they are technical tools used by the Navy to comply with safety regulations.

The dissenting judge correctly noted that the Navy’s failure to classify these maps under Exemption 1 undermines its claim of national security harm. If disclosure truly threatened national security, the Navy would have classified the maps. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(1). The Navy’s inconsistent treatment of comparable maps for the Bangor base further suggests that its withholding is motivated by political, not security, concerns.

III. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER WHETHER EXEMPTION 7(F) APPLIES.

The Ninth Circuit did not address whether Exemption 7(F) applies, despite the Navy’s alternative argument. Exemption 7(F) permits withholding of records “compiled for law enforcement purposes” if disclosure “could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of any individual.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F). The ESQD arc maps are not compiled for law enforcement purposes; they are compiled for safety and operational planning. *See Church of Scientology v. U.S. Dep’t of Defense*, 611 F.2d 738, 748 (D.C. Cir. 1979) (holding that records compiled for “adjudicative or enforcement purposes” qualify under Exemption 7(F)).

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit’s decision expands FOIA Exemption 2 beyond its statutory limits and conflicts with this Court’s precedent. The ESQD arc maps are not “internal personnel rules and practices,” and any risk of circumvention is speculative. The Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand for disclosure of the requested documents.
