## Argument

The district court erred in designating Bryshun Genard Furlow as an "armed career criminal" under the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA) and as a "career offender" under the Sentencing Guidelines. Furlow's prior convictions for distribution of crack cocaine in South Carolina and first-degree arson in Georgia do not qualify as predicate offenses under the ACCA and the Guidelines.

### I. Furlow's South Carolina Conviction for Distribution of Crack Cocaine is Not a Predicate "Serious Drug Offense" or "Controlled Substance Offense"

The ACCA defines a "serious drug offense" as an offense under state law involving manufacturing, distributing, or possessing with intent to manufacture or distribute a controlled substance for which a maximum term of imprisonment of ten years or more is prescribed by law. 18 U.S.C. § 924(e)(2)(A)(ii). The Guidelines define a "controlled substance offense" similarly, as an offense under federal or state law punishable by imprisonment for a term exceeding one year that prohibits the manufacture, import, export, distribution, or dispensing of a controlled substance. USSG § 4B1.2(b).

In United States v. Llenas, 73 F.2d 105 (2d Cir. 1934), the court held that a state statute that punished the sale of narcotics, but did not specify a maximum term of imprisonment, was not a "felony" under the federal Narcotic Act. Similarly, in this case, Furlow's South Carolina conviction for distribution of crack cocaine may not have carried a maximum term of imprisonment of ten years or more. Under the reasoning of Llenas, Furlow's conviction may not qualify as a predicate "serious drug offense" or "controlled substance offense."

### II. Furlow's Georgia Conviction for First-Degree Arson is Not a Predicate "Violent Felony" or "Crime of Violence"

The ACCA and the Guidelines both enumerate "arson" as a qualifying predicate offense. 18 U.S.C. § 924(e)(2)(B)(ii); USSG § 4B1.2(a)(2). However, the term "arson" is not defined in either the ACCA or the Guidelines. In United States v. Patten, 40 F.2d 976 (3d Cir. 1930), the court held that a state statute that punished the burning of a building, but did not require malicious intent, was not a crime of "arson" within the meaning of a federal statute. Similarly, in this case, Furlow's Georgia conviction for first-degree arson may not have required the same level of intent or malice as the generic definition of "arson." Under the reasoning of Patten, Furlow's conviction may not qualify as a predicate "violent felony" or "crime of violence."

### III. Conclusion

Furlow's prior convictions for distribution of crack cocaine in South Carolina and first-degree arson in Georgia do not qualify as predicate offenses under the ACCA and the Guidelines. The district court erred in designating Furlow as an "armed career criminal" and as a "career offender." This Court should vacate Furlow's sentence and remand for resentencing.
