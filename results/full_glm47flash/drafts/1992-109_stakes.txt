**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DUE PROCESS CLAUSE DOES NOT REQUIRE A HEIGHTENED “REASONED CHOICE” STANDARD TO DETERMINE COMPETENCY TO WAIVE COUNSEL OR PLEAD GUILTY.**

The Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment does not require a trial court to apply a heightened “reasoned choice” standard to determine a defendant’s competency to waive counsel or enter a guilty plea. The Constitution mandates only that a defendant possess a rational and factual understanding of the proceedings against him. *Dusky v. United States*, 362 U.S. 402, 402 (1960). This standard, which requires a defendant to have a rational and factual understanding of the nature and object of the proceedings, is sufficient to protect the fundamental rights at stake. *Drope v. Missouri*, 420 U.S. 162, 172 (1975).

The “reasoned choice” standard is a creation of the Ninth Circuit, not a constitutional mandate. The Supreme Court has consistently held that a defendant’s competency to stand trial is the appropriate constitutional threshold for determining whether a defendant can make a knowing and intelligent waiver of his rights. *Pate v. Robinson*, 383 U.S. 375, 387 (1966). Requiring a defendant to make a “reasoned choice” among the alternatives available to him imposes a level of mental functioning that exceeds the constitutional guarantee of due process. The Constitution does not require a defendant to be in a perfect mental state to exercise his right to self-representation; it only requires that he understand the nature of the proceedings and the consequences of his actions. *Faretta v. California*, 422 U.S. 806, 835 (1975).

**II. THE STATE COURT CORRECTLY APPLIED THE *DUSKY* STANDARD, AND THE RECORD SUPPORTS ITS FINDING OF COMPETENCY.**

The state court properly applied the *Dusky* standard to determine Moran’s competency to stand trial and, by extension, his competency to waive counsel. The record shows that Moran was examined by two psychiatrists who found him competent to stand trial. Dr. O’Gorman concluded that Moran was “knowledgeable of the charges being made against him” and could assist his attorney. Dr. Jurasky similarly found Moran capable of assisting counsel. These findings are consistent with the constitutional standard for competency to stand trial.

The state court’s finding of competency to stand trial is sufficient to support a finding of competency to waive constitutional rights, provided the waiver is knowing and intelligent. *Boykin v. Alabama*, 395 U.S. 238, 243 (1969). The state court’s findings of fact, based on the psychiatric evaluations, are entitled to deference. The imposition of a new, higher “reasoned choice” standard by the Ninth Circuit was an error of law that requires reversal. The Due Process Clause does not require a heightened standard; it requires only that a defendant understands the proceedings, which the record shows Moran did.
