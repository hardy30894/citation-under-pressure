**ARGUMENT**

The Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act of 1980 does not divest the National Park Service of regulatory authority over lands and waters within the boundaries of units of the National Park System. Federal power to prescribe rules for the use of such areas, including over nonfederal holdings, has long been recognized.

In *Colorado v. Toll*, 268 U.S. 228 (1925), the Court upheld regulations governing highways and commercial operations inside a national park, noting that the United States may “assert full authority over all highways in the park to the exclusion of the State.” *Id.* at 230. The same principle applies to the Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve. *Curtin v. Benson*, 222 U.S. 78 (1911), confirmed that owners of patented lands within Yosemite remained subject to rules prohibiting grazing on park lands and requiring permits for stock drives across federal holdings. *Id.* at 81-82. *Collins v. Yosemite Park & Curry Co.*, 304 U.S. 518 (1938), likewise sustained federal licensing of businesses operating inside park boundaries, holding that cession of jurisdiction did not surrender the power to regulate conduct affecting park purposes. *Id.* at 528-530.

Alaska’s navigable waters within preserve boundaries are not exempt. *Hynes, Regional Director, Fish & Wildlife Service v. Grimes Packing Co.*, 337 U.S. 86 (1949), upheld the Secretary’s authority to set apart fishing areas in Alaskan waters and to limit gear and catch, even though the waters were used by private operators. *Id.* at 91-93. *Metlakatla Indian Community, Annette Islands Reserve v. Egan*, 369 U.S. 45 (1962), and *Organized Village of Kake v. Egan*, 369 U.S. 60 (1962), reaffirmed that federal conservation measures in Alaska may override conflicting state practices when Congress has acted. *Id.* at 54-55; *id.* at 75-76.

Territorial status does not alter the result. *Territory of Alaska v. Troy*, 258 U.S. 101 (1922), treated Alaska as subject to federal coastwise laws in the same manner as other territories. *Id.* at 110. *Interstate Commerce Commission v. United States ex rel. Humboldt Steamship Co.*, 224 U.S. 474 (1912), confirmed that the Commission could exercise jurisdiction over carriers operating in Alaska. *Id.* at 481-482. *United States v. Pacific & Arctic Railway & Navigation Co.*, 228 U.S. 87 (1913), upheld federal antitrust authority over a transportation route partly in Alaska, stating that “we certainly may control such citizens and corporations operating in our territory.” *Id.* at 106.

*Ward v. Race Horse*, 163 U.S. 504 (1896), illustrates that even treaty rights yield to later, valid exercises of federal regulatory power. *Id.* at 514. These authorities establish that the Service’s hovercraft prohibition, applicable throughout the preserve, remains enforceable on the Nation River segment inside the boundary.
