## Argument

### I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment to the FDIC on the Grounds that the Release Bars Bancorp's Claims

The district court incorrectly concluded that the release executed by Bancorp on June 6, 1988, bars its claims against the FDIC. The language of the release does not cover the claims asserted by Bancorp in the instant action. The release only covers claims "arising from or with respect to the decision of the [OCC] to close the Bank on June 21, 1985." The claims asserted by Bancorp, however, relate to the FDIC's actions as receiver, including its payment of post-insolvency interest to itself, its sale of Bank assets, and its charging of legal and office expenses to the receivership estate. These claims do not "arise from or with respect to" the OCC's decision to close the Bank.

Courts have consistently held that releases are to be construed narrowly, and that they only bar claims that are explicitly covered by their terms. See, e.g., S.E.C. v. Levine, 881 F.2d 1165, 1181 (2d Cir. 1989) (holding that a release "should be read narrowly"). The release at issue here does not explicitly cover the claims asserted by Bancorp, and therefore, it does not bar those claims.

Furthermore, the FDIC's actions as receiver, which are at issue in Bancorp's claims, occurred after the OCC's decision to close the Bank. The release cannot be interpreted to cover claims that arose after the events specified in the release. See, e.g., In re Ivan F. Boesky Sec. Litig., 948 F.2d 1358, 1366 (2d Cir. 1991) (holding that a release does not cover claims that "had not yet accrued" at the time of the release).

### II. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment to the FDIC on the Grounds that the Statute of Limitations Bars Bancorp's Claims

The district court also erred in concluding that New York's six-year statute of limitations for claims of unjust enrichment, breach of fiduciary duty, and corporate waste bars Bancorp's claims. N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 213(7). The statute of limitations does not bar Bancorp's claims because it runs from the termination of the receivership on November 1, 1995.

Under New York law, a claim for unjust enrichment or breach of fiduciary duty accrues when the plaintiff possesses a complete and present cause of action. See, e.g., Kaufman v. Cohen, 307 A.D.2d 113, 124 (1st Dep't 2003). In this case, Bancorp's claims did not accrue until the FDIC terminated the receivership on November 1, 1995. Prior to that date, Bancorp's claims were not ripe, as the FDIC's actions as receiver were still ongoing.

The district court's conclusion that the statute of limitations bars Bancorp's claims is therefore incorrect. Bancorp commenced the instant action on October 31, 1995, one day before the FDIC terminated the receivership, and well within the six-year statute of limitations.

In sum, the district court erred in granting summary judgment to the FDIC on the grounds that the release and the statute of limitations bar Bancorp's claims. The release does not cover Bancorp's claims, and the statute of limitations does not bar them. This Court should reverse the district court's judgment and remand the case for further proceedings.
