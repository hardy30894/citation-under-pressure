**ARGUMENT**

I. **WASEK STATED A CLAIM FOR SEXUAL HARASSMENT UNDER TITLE VII AND THE ELL-CRA BECAUSE HE ALLEGED SEVERE AND PERVASIVE CONDUCT DIRECTED AT HIM ON ACCOUNT OF HIS SEX.**

To prevail on a claim of hostile work environment harassment under Title VII, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq., a plaintiff must show that (1) he was subjected to unwelcome harassment; (2) the harassment was based on a protected characteristic; (3) the harassment was severe or pervasive enough to alter the conditions of employment and create an abusive working environment; and (4) the employer knew or should have known of the harassment and failed to take prompt and effective remedial action. *Harris v. Forklift Sys., Inc.*, 510 U.S. 17, 21 (1993); *Meritor Sav. Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, 66–67 (1986). Michigan’s Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act (“ELCRA”), Mich. Comp. Laws § 37.2101 et seq., imposes the same standard. *Lacey v. Dep’t of Corr.*, 472 Mich. 398, 410 (2005).

Here, Wasek has alleged conduct that, if proven, is sufficiently severe and pervasive to support a hostile work environment claim. The Sixth Circuit has repeatedly held that even a single incident of physical assault can contribute to a hostile environment if it is sufficiently severe. *See, e.g., Williams v. Gen. Motors Corp.*, 187 F.3d 553, 562 (6th Cir. 1999) (holding that a supervisor’s physical assault and repeated threats could contribute to a hostile environment). Wasek’s allegations—that Ottobre physically touched him in a sexual manner, including grabbing his buttocks and poking him with a hammer handle and sucker rod—are not merely offensive or boorish; they are physically intrusive and objectively humiliating. *See Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc.*, 523 U.S. 75, 80 (1998) (noting that same-sex harassment can be actionable under Title VII).

Moreover, the conduct was not limited to isolated incidents. Ottobre’s behavior escalated over time, culminating in a pattern of sexualized touching, comments, and jokes that persisted despite Wasek’s objections. *See Hafford v. Seidner Enters., Inc.*, 183 F.3d 506, 512 (6th Cir. 1999) (holding that repeated offensive comments and physical contact, including groping, could support a hostile environment claim). The cumulative effect of Ottobre’s conduct—his sexualized touching, demeaning comments, and refusal to cease despite Wasek’s protests—created an objectively hostile work environment. *See id.* at 513.

Critically, Wasek has also alleged that the harassment was motivated by his sex. In same-sex harassment cases, plaintiffs must show that the harassment occurred “because of sex,” which can be demonstrated by proving that the harasser was motivated by sexual desire or that the harassment was based on the plaintiff’s failure to act in a manner stereotypically associated with his sex. *Oncale*, 523 U.S. at 80–81. Wasek has alleged that Ottobre’s conduct was motivated by Wasek’s perceived bisexuality and that Ottobre acted in a manner consistent with sexual harassment directed at a man. These allegations, if proven, satisfy the “because of sex” requirement. *See id.* at 80–81; *see also EEOC v. Boh Bros. Constr. Co.*, 731 F.3d 444, 452–53 (5th Cir. 2013) (en banc) (holding that same-sex harassment can be actionable where the harasser acted out of sexual desire or gender stereotyping).

II. **ARROW ENERGY’S FAILURE TO TAKE PROMPT AND EFFECTIVE REMEDIAL ACTION RENDERED ITS DEFENSE OF AFIRMATIVE DEFENSE INEFFECTIVE.**

Even if Wasek could not initially prove that the harassment was “because of sex,” Arrow Energy’s response to his complaints—if proven—would still render it liable under a negligence theory. Under *Faragher v. City of Boca Raton*, 524 U.S. 775, 807 (1998), and *Burlington Indus., Inc. v. Ellerth*, 524 U.S. 742, 765 (1998), an employer may avoid vicarious liability for a supervisor’s harassment if it can prove (1) it exercised reasonable care to prevent and correct promptly any sexually harassing behavior, and (2) the plaintiff unreasonably failed to take advantage of any preventive or corrective opportunities provided by the employer. However, Arrow Energy has not met its burden.

Wasek has alleged that his immediate supervisor, Pat Tripp, observed some of the harassment and laughed rather than intervened. When Wasek complained, Tripp discouraged him from reporting the conduct to higher management, threatened to retaliate by placing Wasek on a “starvation schedule,” and ultimately failed to take any meaningful action. These allegations, if proven, demonstrate that Arrow Energy did not exercise reasonable care to prevent or correct the harassment. *See, e.g., EEOC v. PVNF, LLC*, 821 F.3d 680, 690 (6th Cir. 2016) (holding that an employer’s failure to investigate or take corrective action after notice can defeat an affirmative defense).

Moreover, Arrow Energy’s directors—Dick Smillie and Bill Strong—responded to Wasek’s complaints with hostility, mockery, and indifference. Smillie’s suggestion that Wasek “just kick [Ottobre’s] ass” and Strong’s advice that Wasek and Ottobre “duke it out” to “get it out of [their] systems” are not the responses of an employer taking prompt and effective remedial action. *See, e.g., EEOC v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc.*, 998 F. Supp. 633, 642 (E.D. La. 1998) (holding that an employer’s failure to take corrective action after notice can render it liable). Instead, these responses reflect a corporate culture that tolerated harassment and discouraged employees from seeking redress. *See Burlington Indus.*, 524 U.S. at 765 (noting that an employer’s response must be “reasonable” and “prompt”).

Because Arrow Energy has not met its burden of proving the elements of the *Faragher/Ellerth* affirmative defense, summary judgment in its favor was improper.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Wasek has stated a claim for hostile work environment harassment under Title VII and the ELCRA. Arrow Energy’s failure to take prompt and effective remedial action further supports liability. The district court erred in granting summary judgment in favor of Arrow Energy, and this Court should reverse and remand for further proceedings.
