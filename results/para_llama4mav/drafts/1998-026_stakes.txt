The issue before this Court is whether a District Court's failure to advise a defendant of his right to appeal, as required by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32, provides grounds for habeas relief when the defendant was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The respondent, United States, submits that it does not.

The Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit correctly held that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review. As the court noted, the purpose of Rule 32 is to ensure that a defendant is aware of his right to appeal. See United States v. Deans, 436 F.2d 596, 599 (3d Cir. 1971) (holding that the purpose of Rule 32 is to "advise the defendant of his right to appeal"). When a defendant is already aware of this right, as Peguero was, the rule's purpose is served, and any error is harmless. See Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18, 22 (1967) (holding that a constitutional error is harmless if it is "harmless beyond a reasonable doubt").

The District Court's finding that Peguero knew of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing is supported by the record. As a result, Peguero's claim that the court's failure to advise him of his right to appeal entitles him to habeas relief is without merit. The Court of Appeals' decision is consistent with the principle that "Rule 32(a)(2) is not a constitutional or jurisdictional rule, and its violation does not automatically entitle a defendant to relief." United States v. Drummond, 903 F.2d 1391, 1393 (11th Cir. 1990).

Furthermore, this Court has held that a defendant's knowledge of his right to appeal can render a court's failure to advise him of that right harmless. See Rodriquez v. United States, 395 U.S. 327, 330 (1969) (noting that a defendant's awareness of his right to appeal is a relevant factor in determining whether a court's failure to advise him of that right is prejudicial). In this case, Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal, combined with his decision not to exercise it, demonstrates that the District Court's error did not prejudice him.

In conclusion, the respondent submits that the Court of Appeals correctly held that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review and that Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal renders the District Court's error harmless. Therefore, the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
