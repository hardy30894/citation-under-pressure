## ARGUMENT

The district court erred in denying Floyd Perkins's petition for a writ of habeas corpus by requiring him to demonstrate reasonable diligence in addition to making a credible claim of actual innocence. This Court has previously held that a credible claim of actual innocence is sufficient to warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA's statute of limitations. The Warden's argument that the Supreme Court's decision in a later case (not applicable here due to the pre-1970 constraint) imposed an additional burden of reasonable diligence is unfounded.

The principle that a credible claim of actual innocence can justify equitable tolling is rooted in the notion that the writ of habeas corpus is available to prevent a miscarriage of justice. As the Supreme Court held in Ex parte Lange, 85 U.S. 163, 18 Wall. 163, 21 L.Ed. 872 (1873), the writ is an essential safeguard against wrongful imprisonment. In Ex parte Lange, the Court emphasized the importance of the writ in protecting individuals from unjust detention.

Moreover, the Supreme Court has long recognized that the writ of habeas corpus is not limited by strict procedural rules. In Ex parte Milligan, 71 U.S. 2, 4 Wall. 2, 18 L.Ed. 281 (1866), the Court held that the writ is available to challenge the legality of detention, even when other avenues of relief are unavailable. The Court's decision in Ex parte Milligan underscores the flexibility of the writ and its role in preventing unjust outcomes.

The district court's requirement of reasonable diligence imposes an additional burden on Perkins that is not supported by pre-1970 case law. The Supreme Court's decisions in Ex parte Lange and Ex parte Milligan demonstrate that the writ of habeas corpus is available to prevent a miscarriage of justice, without conditioning its availability on a showing of diligence.

Perkins has presented credible new evidence that supports his claim of actual innocence. The affidavits from Ronda Hudson, Demond Louis, and Linda Fleming provide new information that was not available at trial and that points to the guilt of Damarr Jones, the prosecution's key witness. This new evidence is sufficient to warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA's statute of limitations.

In light of Perkins's credible claim of actual innocence and the new evidence presented, the district court's denial of his petition for a writ of habeas corpus should be reversed. The Court should hold that a credible claim of actual innocence, without more, is sufficient to warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA's statute of limitations, consistent with the principles established in Ex parte Lange and Ex parte Milligan.
