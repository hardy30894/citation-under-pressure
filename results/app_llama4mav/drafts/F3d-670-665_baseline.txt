## ARGUMENT

The district court erred in denying Perkins's petition for a writ of habeas corpus by requiring him to demonstrate reasonable diligence in addition to making a credible claim of actual innocence. This Court has previously held that a credible claim of actual innocence is sufficient to warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA's statute of limitations. See Souter v. Jones, 395 F.3d 577, 602 (6th Cir. 2005). The Supreme Court's decision in Pace v. DiGuglielmo, 544 U.S. 408 (2005), does not impose an additional burden of reasonable diligence on petitioners seeking equitable tolling based on actual innocence.

In Souter, this Court established that a credible claim of actual innocence can excuse an untimely filing under AEDPA. 395 F.3d at 602. The Court held that "where an otherwise untimely habeas petitioner can demonstrate that it is more likely than not that no reasonable juror would have found him guilty beyond a reasonable doubt in light of new evidence, then 'actual innocence' has been sufficiently demonstrated to excuse the untimeliness of his habeas petition." Id. at 590. Notably, the Souter decision does not condition equitable tolling on a showing of reasonable diligence.

The Warden's reliance on Pace is misplaced. In Pace, the Supreme Court held that a petitioner seeking equitable tolling must demonstrate that he has been pursuing his rights diligently. 544 U.S. at 418. However, Pace did not involve a claim of actual innocence, and the Court did not address the specific issue of whether a credible claim of actual innocence is sufficient to warrant equitable tolling. See id. at 411. The Supreme Court's more recent decision in McQuiggin v. Perkins, 569 U.S. 383 (2013), while decided after the district court's ruling, reinforces the holding in Souter, stating that "actual innocence, if proved, serves as a gateway through which a petitioner may pass" to overcome AEDPA's statute of limitations, without requiring a showing of diligence. 569 U.S. at 386 (citing House v. Bell, 547 U.S. 518, 536-538 (2006)).

Perkins has presented new evidence that supports his claim of actual innocence. The affidavits from Ronda Hudson, Demond Louis, and Linda Fleming provide previously undisclosed information that implicates Damarr Jones, the prosecution's key witness, in Henderson's murder. While the district court found that this evidence was "substantially available" at trial and presented the same theory Perkins had already argued, it is precisely this type of new evidence that Souter contemplated. See 395 F.3d at 590.

The district court's imposition of a reasonable diligence requirement is inconsistent with this Court's precedent in Souter. Perkins's credible claim of actual innocence, supported by new evidence, is sufficient to warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA's statute of limitations. Therefore, this Court should reverse the district court's judgment and remand the case for consideration of Perkins's habeas petition on the merits.
