The Railroad Retirement Act provides that “any final decision of the Board” is subject to review in the courts of appeals. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A Board order denying a request to reopen a prior benefits determination is a final decision within the meaning of that provision. See Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (“the Railroad Retirement Act grants the Court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations”); Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991); Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254–55 (2d Cir. 1966).

Section 355(f) contains no textual limitation restricting review to decisions rendered “after a hearing.” The absence of that phrase distinguishes the Railroad Retirement Act from the Social Security Act provision construed in Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99, 108 (1977) (“the opportunity to reopen final decisions and any hearing convened to determine the propriety of such action are afforded by the Secretary’s regulations and not by the Social Security Act”). Because the statutory text here is broader, Califano does not control. See Stovic, 826 F.3d at 504.

The Board’s regulations themselves treat a reopening denial as the culmination of the administrative process. 20 C.F.R. § 261.2. Once that denial issues, no further Board action remains available on the request. The decision is therefore final for purposes of § 355(f). See Stovic, 826 F.3d at 503.

The contrary view adopted in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139, 141 (5th Cir. 2003), rests on an erroneous extension of Califano and should be rejected. The plain language of § 355(f) supplies jurisdiction to review the Board’s refusal to reopen petitioner’s 2006 application.
