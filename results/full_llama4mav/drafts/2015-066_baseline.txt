The Sixth Amendment does not prohibit the use of a valid, uncounseled tribal court conviction as a predicate offense under 18 U.S.C. § 117(a). The Ninth Circuit's holding to the contrary is erroneous.

First, the Sixth Amendment right to counsel does not apply to tribal courts. See Plains Commerce Bank v. Long Family Land & Cattle Co., 554 U.S. 316, 337 (2008). Tribal courts are governed by the Indian Civil Rights Act (ICRA), which provides certain procedural safeguards, but not a right to counsel coextensive with the Sixth Amendment. See 25 U.S.C. §§ 1301-1303. The Ninth Circuit's requirement that tribal courts guarantee a right to counsel "coextensive with the Sixth Amendment right" is an unwarranted intrusion into tribal self-governance. See Santa Clara Pueblo v. Martinez, 436 U.S. 49, 71 (1978).

Second, the Supreme Court's decision in Nichols v. United States, 511 U.S. 738 (1994), permits the use of prior uncounseled misdemeanor convictions to enhance a sentence, as long as the conviction is valid and does not violate the Sixth Amendment. See id. at 746-47. The Ninth Circuit's attempt to distinguish Nichols on the grounds that it involved sentencing rather than an essential element of an offense is unpersuasive. The Court's reasoning in Nichols applies with equal force to the use of prior convictions as predicate offenses under § 117(a).

Third, the use of prior tribal court convictions under § 117(a) is consistent with the principles of comity and respect for tribal court judgments. See Wilson v. Marchington, 127 F.3d 805, 810 (9th Cir. 1997). The Ninth Circuit's holding undermines these principles by imposing a requirement that tribal courts adhere to Sixth Amendment standards, despite being governed by the ICRA.

Finally, the Ninth Circuit's decision creates a circuit split and is contrary to the Supreme Court's precedent. See United States v. Shavanaux, 647 F.3d 993, 997-98 (10th Cir. 2011); United States v. Cavanaugh, 643 F.3d 592, 595, 604 (8th Cir. 2011). The use of prior uncounseled tribal court convictions as predicate offenses under § 117(a) is a valid means of prosecuting repeat offenders and does not violate the Sixth Amendment.

The judgment of the Ninth Circuit should be reversed.
