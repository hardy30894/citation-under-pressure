Argument

The Tax Court erred in disallowing the marital deduction claimed by the estate of Ralph H. Davis under section 2056(b)(7) of the Internal Revenue Code. To qualify for a QTIP deduction, the surviving spouse must have a "qualifying income interest for life" in the trust property. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The Amendment to the Declaration of Trust grants Mrs. Davis a qualifying income interest for life.

The trust instrument provides that the trustee "shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse, in quarter annual or more frequent installments, all of the net income from the trust estate as the trustee, in the trustee's reasonable discretion, shall determine to be proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare of grantor's surviving spouse in accordance with the surviving spouse's accustomed manner of living." This language satisfies the requirement that the surviving spouse be "entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals." I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii); see Commissioner v. Estate of Bosch, 387 U.S. 456 (1967); Estate of Edward E. Bradley, 1 T.C. 518 (1943), aff'd sub nom. Helvering v. Estate of Bradley, 146 F.2d 87 (2d Cir. 1944); Commissioner v. Estate of Field, 324 U.S. 113 (1945).

Although the trustee has discretion to determine what portion of the income is "proper" for Mrs. Davis's benefit, this discretion is not unlimited. The trust instrument requires the trustee to consider Mrs. Davis's "accustomed manner of living," which limits the trustee's discretion and ensures that Mrs. Davis will receive all the income she needs to maintain her standard of living. See Estate of Nathan P. Cutler, 5 T.C. 1304 (1945); Estate of Louis B. Hoffenberg, 22 T.C. 1185 (1954), aff'd per curiam, 233 F.2d 161 (2d Cir. 1956); Commissioner v. Estate of Ellis, 252 F.2d 109 (3d Cir. 1958).

Moreover, the trust instrument does not grant any person the power to appoint any part of the property to any person other than Mrs. Davis during her lifetime, satisfying the second prong of the "qualifying income interest for life" test. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii); see Estate of Allen v. Commissioner, 150 F.2d 304 (3d Cir. 1945); Estate of William J. Higgs, 12 T.C. 280 (1949); Estate of Elmer M. Straight, 7 T.C. 1284 (1946).

The Commissioner's reliance on the trustee's discretion to invade principal only after Mrs. Davis has exhausted her own trust assets is misplaced. The trust instrument's provision regarding invasion of principal is separate from the provision governing the distribution of income, and does not affect Mrs. Davis's entitlement to the trust income. See Estate of Proctor D. Rensenhouse, 27 T.C. 107 (1956), rev'd on other grounds, 252 F.2d 566 (6th Cir. 1958); Estate of William F. Hofmann, 12 T.C. 1024 (1949); Commissioner v. Estate of Awtry, 147 F.2d 1013 (2d Cir. 1945).

In sum, the Amendment to the Declaration of Trust grants Mrs. Davis a qualifying income interest for life, and the estate is entitled to a marital deduction under section 2056(b)(7). The Tax Court's decision to the contrary should be reversed.
