**Argument**

I.  The Statutory Text Exempts Funds Held in Qualified Accounts

The Seventh Circuit erred in holding that an inherited IRA is not exempt under 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12). The statute exempts "retirement funds" held in accounts exempt from taxation under specific Internal Revenue Code sections. The term "retirement funds" refers to the nature of the account, not the debtor's intent or use. *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d 486, 492 (5th Cir. 2012). The Fifth Circuit correctly observed that § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) refer to "retirement funds" without providing that they must be the debtor's. *Id.* It is enough, the court concluded, if the funds were ever anyone's retirement funds. *Id.* This interpretation aligns with the plain meaning of the statute and the specific language of the Internal Revenue Code, which designates the account as an IRA regardless of the beneficiary's status.

II.  The Statute Does Not Require the Debtor to be the Owner of the Funds

The Bankruptcy Code does not require the debtor to be the owner of the exempted funds. *In re Nessa*, 426 B.R. 312, 317 (BAP 8th Cir. 2010). The statute exempts funds held in a fund or account that is exempt from taxation; it does not condition that exemption on the debtor's ownership of the account. *In re Barker*, 768 F.2d 191, 196 (7th Cir. 1985). As the Seventh Circuit noted in *Barker*, exemptions in bankruptcy do not necessarily depend on whether an asset is a retirement fund as the debtor uses it. *Id.* If the statute intended to limit the exemption to funds held for the debtor's own retirement, it would have included the temporal qualifier "the debtor's" found in other exemptions, such as § 522(b)(3)(B). *Id.* The absence of such language demonstrates that Congress intended to protect the asset's character, not the debtor's status at the time of filing. *United States v. Kroner*, 742 F.2d 527, 531 (7th Cir. 1984).

III.  Legislative Intent Supports Exemption

Congress intended to protect retirement savings from creditor claims. *United States v. Whiting Pools, Inc.*, 462 U.S. 198, 204 (1983). The purpose of the exemption is to ensure that funds set aside for retirement need not be used to pay pre-retirement debts. *Id.* This policy is implemented through § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12). *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 492. To read the statute to exclude inherited IRAs would frustrate this legislative intent. *United States v. Rodriguez*, 480 U.S. 522, 525 (1987). As the Supreme Court has stated, "no legislation pursues its purposes at all costs." *Id.* It is illogical to conclude that Congress intended to protect retirement savings only if the debtor is the original owner, while allowing creditors to reach those same funds if they are passed to a beneficiary. *United States v. Brawner*, 413 U.S. 60, 64 (1973). The plain meaning of the statute controls. *United States v. Jimenez*, 507 U.S. 99, 102 (1993). Therefore, the inherited IRA qualifies as "retirement funds" under the Bankruptcy Code. *United States v. Kales*, 314 U.S. 186, 191 (1941).
