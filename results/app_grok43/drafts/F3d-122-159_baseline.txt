ARGUMENT

The Bureau of Prisons exceeded its authority by denying Roussos eligibility for early release under 18 U.S.C. § 3621(e)(2)(B). That statute permits reduction of up to one year for an inmate convicted of a “nonviolent offense” who completes the residential drug treatment program. The governing regulation, 28 C.F.R. § 550.58, incorporates the definition of “crime of violence” contained in 18 U.S.C. § 924(c)(3), which requires either that the offense have “as an element the use, attempted use, or threatened use of physical force” or that it “by its nature, involve[] a substantial risk that physical force against the person or property of another may be used in the course of committing the offense.”

Roussos’s conviction for conspiracy to distribute a controlled substance, 21 U.S.C. § 846, satisfies neither prong. The offense contains no element of force, and its nature does not entail a substantial risk of force. The two-level enhancement imposed under U.S.S.G. § 2D1.1 for possession of a firearm is a sentencing factor, not an element of the offense of conviction. The categorical approach therefore precludes the Bureau from treating the enhancement as transforming the offense into a crime of violence. Taylor v. United States, 495 U.S. 575, 600 (1990) (holding that the statutory language “requires the sentencing court to look only to the fact of conviction and the statutory definition of the prior offense,” not to “the particular facts underlying those convictions”).

Program Statement 5162.02, which directs that any drug offense accompanied by a weapons enhancement be deemed a crime of violence, is inconsistent with both the statute and the regulation. An agency interpretation that contradicts the plain language of the governing statute receives no deference. Chevron U.S.A., Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837, 842-43 (1984). Because the Program Statement redefines “crime of violence” by reference to sentencing facts rather than the offense itself, it is not a permissible construction of 18 U.S.C. § 924(c)(3) and cannot lawfully disqualify Roussos from the early-release benefit Congress authorized.

The district court’s order denying the petition should therefore be reversed.
