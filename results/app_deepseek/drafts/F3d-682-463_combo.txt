**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment Because Wasek Presented Sufficient Evidence of a Hostile Work Environment Based on Gender**

Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(a)(1), prohibits an employer from discriminating against any individual with respect to his compensation, terms, conditions, or privileges of employment because of such individual’s sex. *Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, 63 (1986) (citing *Sprogis v. United Air Lines, Inc.*, 444 F.2d 1194, 1198 (7th Cir. 1971)). A plaintiff establishes a hostile work environment claim by showing that the harassment was “sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of [his] employment and create an abusive working environment.” *Rogers v. EEOC*, 454 F.2d 234, 238 (5th Cir. 1971). Critically, the harassment must be “because of” the plaintiff’s sex. *Id.*

**A. The Sexual Nature of the Harassment Establishes That It Was Based on Wasek’s Gender**

When harassment involves explicit or implicit proposals of sexual activity, it is reasonable to infer that the harasser was motivated by the victim’s gender. *Barnes v. Costle*, 561 F.2d 983, 990 (D.C. Cir. 1977). Here, Ottobre’s conduct—grabbing Wasek’s buttocks, poking him in the rear with a hammer handle and a sucker rod, and making comments such as “you’ve got a pretty mouth” and “you know you like it sweetheart”—was undeniably sexual in nature. Such conduct is “directed at [the plaintiff] because of his sex.” *Id.* at 990 n.55. The Supreme Court has long recognized that “sexual harassment which creates a hostile or offensive environment for members of one sex is a form of discrimination.” *Vinson*, 477 U.S. at 64 (citing *Henson v. City of Dundee*, 682 F.2d 897, 902 (11th Cir. 1982)). Because Ottobre’s actions were sexual, a jury could infer that they were motivated by Wasek’s gender. *See Bundy v. Jackson*, 641 F.2d 934, 942 n.8 (D.C. Cir. 1981).

**B. The Harassment Was Severe and Pervasive**

A hostile work environment exists when the workplace is “permeated with discriminatory intimidation, ridicule, and insult.” *Vinson*, 477 U.S. at 65 (quoting *Rogers*, 454 F.2d at 238). The conduct must be both objectively and subjectively abusive. *Id.* at 67. Wasek subjectively perceived the environment as abusive, as evidenced by his repeated complaints and his ultimate decision to leave his job. Objectively, the harassment was severe: Ottobre physically touched Wasek in a sexual manner on multiple occasions, made degrading sexual comments, and engaged in a campaign of intimidation. This conduct was not isolated; it occurred over several months. *See Henson*, 682 F.2d at 904 (holding that a “continuous pattern” of sexual harassment can establish a hostile environment).

**C. Arrow Energy Is Liable for the Harassment**

An employer is liable for a hostile work environment created by a supervisor or co-worker if the employer knew or should have known of the harassment and failed to take prompt remedial action. *Henson*, 682 F.2d at 905. Here, Wasek reported the harassment to his rig boss, Pat Tripp, who laughed and did nothing. Tripp also threatened Wasek with a “starvation schedule” if he reported the conduct to higher management. When Wasek finally reported the harassment to Director of Operations Dick Smillie, Smillie responded, “Why don’t you just kick [Ottobre’s] ass?” R. 26-1 at 56. When Wasek reported to Bill Strong, Strong told Wasek to stop “whining” and suggested the two men “duke it out.” *Id.* at 57. These responses demonstrate that Arrow Energy failed to take any remedial action. *See id.* at 905 (holding that an employer’s failure to investigate or discipline the harasser constitutes a failure to take prompt remedial action).

**II. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Wasek’s Claim Under the Elliot-Larsen Civil Rights Act**

The Elliot-Larsen Civil Rights Act, Mich. Comp. Laws § 37.2101 *et seq.*, is interpreted consistently with Title VII. *Radtke v. Everett*, 442 Mich. 368, 381 (1993). Because Wasek has presented sufficient evidence to survive summary judgment under Title VII, his state law claim must also survive. *Id.* at 382.

**Conclusion**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the district court’s grant of summary judgment and remand for further proceedings.
