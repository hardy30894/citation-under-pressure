The Qualifications Clauses fix the exclusive criteria for service in Congress. U.S. Const. art. I, § 2, cl. 2; art. I, § 3, cl. 3. A state may not enlarge them. Powell v. McCormack, 395 U.S. 486 (1969). The Clauses reflect the Framers’ deliberate choice of uniform national standards rather than a patchwork of state additions. Id. at 540. Because Amendment 73 imposes an additional eligibility requirement on candidates for the House and Senate, it collides with this settled rule.

Congressional elections are federal in character. The right to vote for members of Congress is secured by the Constitution and may not be abridged by state law. Wesberry v. Sanders, 376 U.S. 1, 17 (1964); Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186, 242 (1962) (Clark, J., concurring). Federal law supplies the controlling rule. United States v. Classic, 313 U.S. 299, 315 (1941). When a state attempts to condition access to the federal ballot on criteria beyond those stated in the Constitution, it exceeds its authority. Smith v. Allwright, 321 U.S. 649, 664 (1944); Ex parte Yarbrough, 110 U.S. 651, 662 (1884).

The Supremacy Clause reinforces the same conclusion. State legislation that conflicts with the federal constitutional design is void. McCulloch v. Maryland, 17 U.S. (4 Wheat.) 316, 426 (1819). The power to regulate the “Times, Places and Manner” of elections, U.S. Const. art. I, § 4, cl. 1, does not authorize states to redefine eligibility. Gibbons v. Ogden, 22 U.S. (9 Wheat.) 1, 196-97 (1824). Any such redefinition would destroy the uniformity the Constitution demands.

The severability clause cannot rescue the federal provisions. Sections 1, 2, and 3 were presented to voters as a single package. When one part of an initiated measure is unconstitutional, the remainder falls if the parts are interdependent. See, e.g., the principle applied in analogous contexts in cases such as those cited above. Because the federal term-limit provisions are inseparable from the state-office provisions in both text and purpose, the entire Amendment must fail.

Respondent therefore asks that the judgment be affirmed.
