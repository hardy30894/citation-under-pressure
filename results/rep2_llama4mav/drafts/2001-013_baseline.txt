ARGUMENT

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has jurisdiction under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (Act) to issue citations to uninspected vessels, which are subject to minimal Coast Guard regulation.

The Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit erred in holding that the Coast Guard's exclusive jurisdiction over the regulation of seamen's working conditions aboard vessels precludes OSHA's regulation under section 4(b)(1) of the Act. Section 4(b)(1) provides that the Act does not apply to "working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health." 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). However, the Coast Guard's authority is not so comprehensive as to preempt OSHA's jurisdiction over uninspected vessels.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Clary v. Ocean Drilling and Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), do not control this case. While these decisions establish that the Coast Guard has exclusive authority over the working conditions of seamen on vessels, they do not distinguish between inspected and uninspected vessels. However, a closer examination of the Coast Guard's regulatory authority reveals that it is limited for uninspected vessels.

The Coast Guard's authority to issue safety regulations for uninspected vessels is restricted to specific areas, such as fire extinguishers, life preservers, and ventilation of engine and fuel tank compartments. 46 U.S.C. § 4102. Although the Coast Guard has issued some safety regulations for uninspected vessels, its regulatory scheme is not as comprehensive as it is for inspected vessels. See 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01 et seq; 46 C.F.R. §§ 26.01 et seq.

In contrast, OSHA's regulations provide a comprehensive framework for ensuring occupational safety and health in the workplace. The Act's purpose is to "assure so far as possible every working man and woman in the Nation safe and healthful working conditions." 29 U.S.C. § 651(b). Allowing OSHA to regulate uninspected vessels would further this purpose and ensure that workers on these vessels are protected.

The Fifth Circuit's concern about overlapping regulation is misplaced. Section 4(b)(1) is intended to prevent redundant control programs, but it does not preclude OSHA's jurisdiction where another agency's authority is limited. Donovan, 720 F.2d at 827. In this case, the Coast Guard's limited regulatory authority over uninspected vessels means that OSHA's jurisdiction is not preempted.

Therefore, OSHA has jurisdiction to issue citations to uninspected vessels, and the Court of Appeals' decision should be reversed. The Occupational Safety and Health Review Commission's order affirming the citation issued against Mallard Bay Drilling, Inc. should be reinstated.
