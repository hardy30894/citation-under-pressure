## ARGUMENT

### I. A credible showing of actual innocence is an equitable exception to AEDPA’s time bar, and diligence is not a separate prerequisite to invoke it.

This Court has already recognized that a credible claim of actual innocence can equitably toll AEDPA’s statute of limitations. *Souter v. Jones*, 395 F.3d 577, 602 (6th Cir. 2005). *Souter* held that a petitioner who presents new, reliable evidence of innocence may pass through the time bar because “the circumstances are extraordinary.” *Id.* at 597-98, 602. That rule tracks the Supreme Court’s long-settled principle that habeas review should remain available to prevent the incarceration of the actually innocent. *Murray v. Carrier*, 477 U.S. 478, 496 (1986); *Schlup v. Delo*, 513 U.S. 298, 324, 327 (1995).

The district court erred by reading *Pace v. DiGuglielmo*, 544 U.S. 408 (2005), to impose a freestanding diligence requirement on an actual-innocence gateway claim. *Pace* addressed a routine equitable-tolling request by a prisoner who alleged confusion about state filing rules; it reiterated that a litigant seeking equitable tolling must show both diligence and extraordinary circumstances. *Id.* at 418. But *Pace* did not involve actual innocence, and the Court has repeatedly treated innocence differently from ordinary tolling doctrines. In *House v. Bell*, 547 U.S. 518 (2006), the Court applied the *Schlup* innocence gateway without suggesting that the petitioner had to prove diligence as an independent element. *Id.* at 539-40. The question is not whether the petitioner acted promptly enough in the abstract, but whether the new evidence shows that “it is more likely than not that no reasonable juror would have convicted him.” *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 327.

That is the governing standard. Actual innocence is an “equitable exception” grounded in the fundamental miscarriage-of-justice principle, not a conventional tolling claim. *McQuiggin v. Perkins*, 569 U.S. 383, 392 (2013). The Court later confirmed that “actual innocence, if proved, serves as a gateway through which a petitioner may pass” notwithstanding AEDPA’s statute of limitations. *Id.* at 386. Nothing in that doctrine makes diligence dispositive. To the contrary, the Court explained that “unexplained delay” is relevant only as “a factor in determining whether actual innocence has been reliably shown,” not as an absolute bar. *Id.* at 399-400; see also *House*, 547 U.S. at 537-38.

### II. Perkins presented new, reliable evidence that requires merits review under the Schlup gateway.

Perkins submitted affidavits from Ronda Hudson, Demond Louis, and Linda Fleming that support the alternative theory that Damarr Jones, not Perkins, killed Rodney Henderson. Evidence need not be admissible in the form presented if it is sufficiently reliable to permit a court to assess the innocence claim. *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 324; *House*, 547 U.S. at 537. The relevant inquiry is holistic: the court must consider all evidence, old and new, and determine whether, in light of the new proof, “no reasonable juror would have found the defendant guilty.” *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 327; *House*, 547 U.S. at 538.

The district court instead minimized the affidavits because they were said to be “substantially available” at trial and because they resembled the defense theory already argued to the jury. That approach conflicts with *Schlup* and *House*. The gateway inquiry does not ask whether the defense previously suspected the same general theory; it asks whether the new evidence is sufficiently reliable and probative to undermine confidence in the verdict. *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 327-28. Even evidence that is cumulative may matter when it materially strengthens the innocence showing. *House*, 547 U.S. at 554-55.

Nor may the court deny gateway review simply because Jones was the prosecution’s eyewitness at trial. Actual-innocence review expressly permits a federal court to revisit the reliability of the evidence underlying the conviction. *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 315-16, 324. Perkins’s affidavits, taken together, point to Jones’s motive, incriminating statements, bloodied clothing, and efforts to clean those clothes. That is precisely the sort of evidence that can cast substantial doubt on a conviction that rested primarily on one witness’s testimony.

### III. Reasonable diligence may affect credibility, but it cannot defeat a proven innocence claim.

The Supreme Court has repeatedly rejected rigid procedural rules that would prevent courts from hearing compelling innocence claims. *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 324-27; *House*, 547 U.S. at 537-38. Delay may be considered, but only as one factor among many. *McQuiggin*, 569 U.S. at 399-400. A diligence prerequisite would invert the miscarriage-of-justice doctrine by making timeliness more important than innocence itself. That is incompatible with the Court’s recognition that “tenable actual-innocence gateway pleas are rare,” but must remain available where they are persuasive. *Id.* at 386, 392.

Here, the district court’s diligence ruling was especially misplaced because the affidavits were executed over time and were not all in Perkins’s possession at once. The proper question is whether the evidence, viewed cumulatively, shows a credible claim of innocence under *Schlup*. *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 327; *House*, 547 U.S. at 538. If it does, AEDPA’s limitations period yields. *McQuiggin*, 569 U.S. at 386.

### CONCLUSION

Because Perkins presented a credible actual-innocence gateway claim, the district court should have reached the merits of his habeas petition. The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for full consideration of Perkins’s claims.
