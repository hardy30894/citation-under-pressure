ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act authorizes judicial review of any “final decision” of the Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A Board order refusing to reopen a prior benefits determination is such a final decision.

In Rochester Telephone Corp. v. United States, 307 U.S. 125 (1939), the Court held that an administrative order is reviewable when it “has the effect of finally determining the rights of the parties.” Id. at 143. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application leaves no further administrative avenue and therefore finally determines his rights with respect to that claim.

Columbia Broadcasting System, Inc. v. United States, 316 U.S. 407 (1942), confirms that review lies where agency action “imposes an obligation, denies a right, or fixes some legal relationship.” Id. at 425. By denying reopening, the Board has denied Salinas the right to have his 2006 application considered on the basis of new and material evidence.

Leedom v. Kyne, 358 U.S. 184 (1958), recognized that courts may review final agency orders even when the governing statute does not expressly provide for review, because “the silence of Congress” does not bar judicial correction of orders that exceed statutory authority. Id. at 190. The Board’s refusal to reopen rests on an asserted lack of authority under its own regulations; that determination is therefore subject to review.

Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967), likewise teaches that “judicial review of a final agency action” is available absent “clear and convincing evidence” of congressional intent to preclude it. Id. at 141. No such evidence appears in the Railroad Retirement Act.

The Fifth Circuit therefore possessed jurisdiction to review the Board’s order.
