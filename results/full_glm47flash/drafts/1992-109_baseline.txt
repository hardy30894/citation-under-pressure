**Argument**

**I. The State Court Applied the Wrong Legal Standard**

A defendant may not waive fundamental constitutional rights unless he possesses the capacity for "reasoned choice" among the alternatives available to him. *Harding v. Lewis*, 834 F.2d 853, 856 (9th Cir. 1987). This "reasoned choice" standard is distinct from the "competency to stand trial" standard, which requires only that a defendant have a rational and factual understanding of the proceedings and be capable of assisting counsel. *Drope v. Missouri*, 420 U.S. 162, 172, 95 S.Ct. 896, 904, 43 L.Ed.2d 103 (1975). The state court erroneously applied the *Drope* standard when it concluded that Moran was competent to waive counsel. The state court relied on psychiatric reports prepared to determine Moran's competency to stand trial, not his capacity for reasoned choice. *Id.* at 856-57. Because the state court applied the wrong legal standard, its finding of competency cannot stand.

**II. The Record Does Not Support a Finding of Competency Under the Correct Standard**

Even under the correct standard, the record does not support a finding that Moran was competent to make a valid waiver. We must indulge in every reasonable presumption against waiver of constitutional rights. *Evans v. Raines*, 800 F.2d 884, 885 (9th Cir. 1986). At the time of the plea, Moran was heavily medicated with four different types of drugs, including Inderal, Dilantin, Phenobarbitol, and Vistaril. *Harding*, 834 F.2d at 856. The state court failed to inquire into the effects of this medication, despite Moran's admission that he was taking drugs. *Id.* Furthermore, Moran’s responses to the court were monosyllabic, and he had recently attempted suicide. *Id.* at 856-57. The state court’s reliance on the *Drope* standard and the psychiatric reports, which focused on Moran's ability to assist counsel rather than his capacity for reasoned choice, was fatal to its conclusion. *Id.* at 857. Therefore, Moran’s waiver of his right to counsel and his guilty pleas were invalid.
