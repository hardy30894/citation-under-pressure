The district court's award of a fee enhancement based on the quality of counsel's representation and the exceptional results obtained is consistent with Supreme Court precedent and should be affirmed.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that a district court has discretion to enhance a lodestar fee award in "rare" and "exceptional" cases. Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984); Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens' Council for Clean Air, 478 U.S. 546, 565 (1986) ("Delaware Valley I"). In Blum, the Court held that an enhancement based on the "quality of representation" and "results obtained" is permissible if the fee applicant offers "specific evidence" to demonstrate that the quality of service rendered was superior to what one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged and that the success was "exceptional." 465 U.S. at 899. The Court reaffirmed this principle in Delaware Valley I, stating that "upward adjustments of the lodestar figure are still permissible ... in certain 'rare' and 'exceptional' cases, supported by both 'specific evidence' on the record and detailed findings by the lower courts." 478 U.S. at 565.

Here, the district court made detailed findings regarding the exceptional quality of counsel's representation and the outstanding results achieved. The court noted that "the superb quality of counsel's representation far exceeded what could reasonably be expected for the standard hourly rates used to calculate the fee." Kenny A. v. Perdue, 454 F.Supp.2d 1260, 1288 (N.D.Ga. 2006). The court also highlighted the "exceptional" results obtained, including the comprehensive settlement agreement that provided significant relief to the plaintiff class. Id. at 1289-90.

The district court's findings are supported by the record, and its decision to enhance the lodestar fee award was not an abuse of discretion. The Eleventh Circuit correctly affirmed the district court's decision, holding that it was consistent with Supreme Court precedent. Kenny A. v. Perdue, 532 F.3d 1209, 1242 (11th Cir. 2008).

Petitioners argue that the district court's use of its own experience and observations to justify the enhancement was improper. However, the district court's findings were not based on unreviewable personal experience, but rather on its observation of counsel's performance throughout the litigation. The court's 58 years of experience as a practicing attorney and federal judge provided a unique perspective on the quality of counsel's representation. See Kenny A., 454 F.Supp.2d at 1290.

Furthermore, there is no circuit split on this issue. The majority of circuits have held that a district court may enhance a lodestar fee award based on exceptional performance and results. See, e.g., Geier v. Sundquist, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co., 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000); Quaratino v. Tiffany & Co., 166 F.3d 422, 425 (2d Cir. 1999).

In conclusion, the district court's award of a fee enhancement based on the quality of counsel's representation and the exceptional results obtained is consistent with Supreme Court precedent and should be affirmed. The district court's detailed findings and the record evidence support the enhancement, and the decision was not an abuse of discretion.
