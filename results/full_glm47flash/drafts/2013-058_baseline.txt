**Argument**

**I. The Bankruptcy Code Exempts Only Funds Held for the Debtor’s Own Retirement.**

The Bankruptcy Code protects "retirement funds" to ensure debtors have resources for their post-work years. 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C). This exemption applies only to funds held for the *current* debtor's retirement, not funds held for a predecessor's retirement. See *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d 486, 491 (5th Cir. 2012). The term "retirement funds" inherently connotes a purpose for the debtor's own future use. See *In re Barker*, 768 F.2d 191, 196 (7th Cir. 1985). An inherited IRA fails to meet this requirement because it is not a savings vehicle for the beneficiary's retirement; it is a distribution vehicle for the decedent's estate.

**II. An Inherited IRA Is Not a "Retirement Fund" Because Mandatory Distributions Precede the Beneficiary’s Retirement.**

Under Internal Revenue Code § 402(c)(11)(A), incorporating § 401(a)(9)(B), an inherited IRA must begin distributing assets within one year of the original owner’s death. *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 491. This statutory mandate prevents the beneficiary from holding the account until their own retirement. Instead, the account functions as a time-limited tax deferral vehicle. See *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 491. Because the funds must be distributed before the beneficiary stops working, they do not represent "retirement funds" in the debtor's hands. Consequently, the exemption in § 522(b)(3)(C) does not apply.

**III. The Exemption Does Not Apply to Assets Formerly Held for Another’s Retirement.**

The exemption is inchoate; it depends on the status of the asset at the time of filing. If a debtor withdraws funds from an IRA and gifts them to a relative, those funds lose their exempt status. See *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 492. Similarly, when an IRA passes to a beneficiary upon death, it ceases to be a retirement fund for the original owner and becomes a distribution vehicle for the beneficiary. See *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 492. To hold otherwise would allow debtors to shelter assets from creditors simply by inheriting them, contrary to the statutory purpose. See *In re Nessa*, 426 B.R. 312, 317 (BAP 8th Cir. 2010).

**IV. The Statute’s Purpose Is Not Served by Exempting Inherited IRAs.**

Allowing an inherited IRA to be exempt would frustrate the legislative intent behind the bankruptcy exemption. See Rodriguez v. United States, 480 U.S. 522, 525 (1987). Congress intended to protect savings set aside for a debtor's own retirement, not to create a tax-exempt bequest vehicle. See *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 493. Therefore, the Trustee is entitled to recover the value of the inherited IRA from the estate.
