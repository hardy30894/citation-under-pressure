ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT'S ORDER IS NOT APPEALABLE UNDER 28 U.S.C. § 1291 BECAUSE IT IS NOT A FINAL ORDER.

The State's appeal must be dismissed because the district court's October 27 Order is not a final order. A civil contempt order is ordinarily not appealable until the district court has adjudicated the contempt motion and applied sanctions. SEC v. Hickey, 322 F.3d 1123, 1127 (9th Cir. 2003). The October 27 Order clearly contemplates further proceedings, as it requires the State to show cause why it should not be held in contempt if it fails to transfer $250 million to the Receiver by November 5, 2008. This is not a final adjudication of the contempt motion, and therefore, the Order is not appealable.

The State argues that awaiting sanctions will not enable it to raise its challenges to the underlying order. However, this dilemma is a consequence of the State's failure to follow more appropriate procedures to challenge the receivership and its plans. United States v. Ayres, 166 F.3d 991, 995 (9th Cir. 1999). Moreover, the district court's order may not confine the proposed proceedings to whether or not the State has delivered the $250 million, as the court has called for a witness list and a further scheduling order.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT'S ORDER IS NOT APPEALABLE AS A POST-JUDGMENT ORDER OR UNDER 28 U.S.C. § 1292(a)(1).

The State's argument that the October 27 Order is appealable as a post-judgment order is unpersuasive. Orders that relate to fully adjudicated issues are only appealable if they finally adjudicate the contempt and impose sanctions. Stone v. City & County of San Francisco, 968 F.2d 850, 854-55 (9th Cir. 1992); Hickey, 322 F.3d at 1127. The October 27 Order does not meet this requirement.

Furthermore, the October 27 Order is not an "affirmative injunction" or an order for the immediate payment of funds that is appealable under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(a)(1). FTC v. Overseas Unlimited Agency, Inc., 873 F.2d 1233, 1235 (9th Cir. 1989). The Order was issued pursuant to the underlying consent decree and the Order Appointing Receiver, which the State did not appeal.

III. THE STATE'S PETITION FOR A WRIT OF MANDAMUS SHOULD BE DENIED.

The State's petition for a writ of mandamus is also without merit. The five factors set forth in Bauman v. United States Dist. Court, 557 F.2d 650 (9th Cir. 1977), do not support the issuance of a writ in this case. The State has an adequate means to attain relief through a direct appeal of a final order finding it in contempt. Stone, 968 F.2d at 854-55; Hickey, 322 F.3d at 1127.

Moreover, the district court's October 27 Order is not "clearly erroneous" as required by Bauman. The Order followed from consent orders and a receivership that had not been contested by the State at the time the Order issued. The State's challenges to the Eleventh Amendment and the PLRA are not properly before this court on direct appeal and are not resolvable on this record for purposes of mandamus.

The district court has largely operated with the consent of the parties, and there is nothing in the record to suggest that it has engaged in "oft-repeated error" or "manifest[ed] a persistent disregard of the federal rules." Bauman, 557 F.2d at 655. Finally, the State's challenges to the prison construction program as a whole may be addressed in response to its pending motion to remove the Receiver and terminate his construction program. Issuing a writ of mandamus at this stage would be premature and inappropriate.
