# ARGUMENT

## I. THE PLAIN LANGUAGE OF SECTION 1407(a) REQUIRES REMAND TO THE TRANSFEROR DISTRICT AND DOES NOT PERMIT A TRANSFEREE COURT TO ASSIGN THE CASE TO ITSELF FOR TRIAL

Section 1407(a) provides that any action transferred by the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation "shall be remanded by the panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings to the district from which it was transferred." 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). This Court has repeatedly recognized that the word "shall" is the language of command. *Escoe v. Zerbst*, 295 U.S. 490, 493 (1935). The statute contains no exception for cases in which the transferee court concludes that self-transfer under § 1404(a) would be more efficient. The Panel's remand obligation is mandatory, not discretionary, and it may not be circumvented by the transferee court's invocation of a separate, general venue statute.

The Ninth Circuit majority did not dispute that § 1407(a)'s command is unambiguous. Indeed, the majority acknowledged "what appears to be a clear statutory mandate," Pet. App. 12, yet concluded that Multidistrict Litigation Rule 14(b) and a line of Court of Appeals decisions nonetheless authorized the transferee court to transfer the case to itself under § 1404(a). That reasoning inverts the proper hierarchy of authority: a procedural rule promulgated by the Panel itself cannot override the express textual command of the authorizing statute. As Judge Kozinski correctly observed in dissent, "[i]t's hard to imagine a clearer statutory command, or one more widely ignored." Pet. App. 16.

Section 1404(a) authorizes transfer "[f]or the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice," 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a), but that general grant of authority cannot be read to swallow the specific and mandatory remand requirement of § 1407(a). It is a "commonplace of statutory construction that the specific governs the general," *Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc.*, 504 U.S. 374, 384 (1992), particularly when, as here, Congress has enacted a detailed statutory scheme addressing the precise circumstance—multidistrict transfer for pretrial purposes—at issue.

## II. THE COURTS OF APPEALS THAT HAVE PERMITTED SELF-TRANSFER HAVE DONE SO BY DISREGARDING RATHER THAN INTERPRETING THE STATUTORY TEXT

The genesis of the practice below traces to *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), which rejected as "absurd" a construction of the statute that would delay § 1404(a) motions until after remand. *Id.* at 124–25. But an outcome's perceived inconvenience does not license courts to rewrite unambiguous statutory text. This Court has cautioned that even where the "policy" of a statute might seem to counsel a different result, courts "are not free to rewrite the statute" that Congress enacted. *West Virginia Univ. Hosps., Inc. v. Casey*, 499 U.S. 83, 98–99 (1991). Subsequent decisions, including *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3d Cir. 1982), and the Ninth Circuit's own decision in *Wash. Pub. Util. Group v. U.S. Dist. Court*, 843 F.2d 319, 326 (9th Cir. 1987), simply followed *Pfizer*'s lead without independently confronting the statutory text.

That widespread practice does not transform an atextual interpretation into a correct one. As Judge Kozinski noted, "[m]ost have simply taken their place at the head of an ever-growing string cite." Pet. App. 17. This Court has made clear that "[t]he fact that a statute can be applied in situations not expressly anticipated by Congress does not demonstrate ambiguity. It demonstrates breadth." *Pennsylvania Dep't of Corrections v. Yeskey*, 524 U.S. 206, 212 (1998) (internal quotation marks omitted). Here, however, there is no breadth to exploit: § 1407(a)'s remand command is categorical, and no textual hook permits an exception for self-assigned trials.

## III. PETITIONERS' RIGHT TO HAVE THEIR CASE TRIED IN THE TRANSFEROR FORUM WAS VIOLATED, AND THAT ERROR WAS NOT HARMLESS

Section 1407(a) reflects a considered congressional judgment that consolidation for pretrial purposes must not be allowed to work a permanent alteration of the plaintiff's chosen forum. The mandatory remand provision protects the value that, as Judge Kozinski explained, "plaintiffs retain their ancient right to select the forum" once pretrial coordination has run its course. Pet. App. 19. Multidistrict Litigation Rule 14(b) cannot lawfully override this congressional choice, because "an agency's power to promulgate legislative regulations is limited to the authority delegated by Congress." *Bowen v. Georgetown Univ. Hosp.*, 488 U.S. 204, 208 (1988).

The Ninth Circuit's mandamus decision below acknowledged that petitioners had been "dragged" into an unfamiliar forum but declined relief only because mandamus review was unavailable at that procedural stage, not because the underlying transfer was lawful. Pet. App. 8–10, 19. On direct appeal, with full briefing, the error is squarely presented and warrants correction. Permitting transferee courts to retain cases for trial under the guise of § 1404(a) transforms a statute enacted to facilitate coordinated discovery into a vehicle for permanent forum displacement—a result Congress did not authorize and this Court should not sanction.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be reversed, and the case remanded with instructions that it be returned to the Northern District of Illinois in accordance with 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a).
