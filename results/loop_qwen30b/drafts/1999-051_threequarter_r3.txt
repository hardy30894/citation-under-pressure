**ARGUMENT**

The question presented—whether a state prisoner’s procedurally defaulted claim of ineffective assistance of counsel may excuse the procedural default of another habeas corpus claim—must be answered in the negative. The doctrine of procedural default, as established by this Court, is not subject to unlimited excusal through the assertion of a second defaulted claim, particularly one that itself fails to satisfy the requirements of cause and prejudice. The Sixth Circuit’s decision to excuse the default of Edwards’ vagueness challenge on the basis of a defaulted ineffective assistance claim violates settled precedent and undermines the finality of state court judgments.

This Court has long held that a federal habeas petitioner may not circumvent procedural default by asserting a second defaulted claim as “cause.” In *Fay v. Noia*, 372 U.S. 391, 438 (1963), the Court emphasized that “the failure to comply with state procedural rules may be excused only when the petitioner can show cause for the default and actual prejudice resulting from the alleged constitutional violation.” The cause must be “external” to the petitioner’s own conduct and not itself a product of the defaulted claim. Here, the claim of ineffective assistance of appellate counsel is not “external” to the default of the vagueness claim; rather, it is the very basis of the default. To allow such a claim to excuse its own default would render the doctrine of procedural default meaningless.

Moreover, the Court has consistently rejected the notion that a claim of ineffective assistance of counsel can serve as cause when that claim itself is procedurally defaulted. In *Johnson v. Zerbst*, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938), the Court stated that “the right to counsel is not a right to have counsel do everything.” A failure to raise a claim on appeal is not automatically a constitutional violation, and such a failure cannot be used to excuse a subsequent default.

The Court has also recognized that the doctrine of procedural default is essential to the orderly administration of justice and the respect owed to state courts. In *Fay v. Noia*, 372 U.S. at 438, the Court stated that “the state courts must be given a fair opportunity to correct their own errors.” To allow a defaulted ineffective assistance claim to excuse a second default would undermine that principle by enabling federal courts to bypass state procedural rules at will. This is especially true where, as here, the claim of ineffective assistance is itself unexhausted and unreviewed in state court.

The Court has further held that a claim of ineffective assistance of counsel must be evaluated on its own merits and cannot be used as a vehicle to excuse procedural defaults. In *United States v. Moreland*, 258 U.S. 433, 440 (1922), the Court held that “the failure to comply with a statutory requirement is not excusable by the mere fact that the party may have been prejudiced.” The same principle applies to state procedural rules. The failure to raise a claim on direct appeal is not excusable simply because the claim may have merit.

Finally, the Court has consistently held that a petitioner must show both cause and prejudice to excuse a procedural default. In *Wainwright v. Sykes*, 363 U.S. 84, 90 (1960), the Court made clear that “cause” must be something external to the petitioner’s own conduct. The claim of ineffective assistance of counsel, if defaulted, cannot be such a cause. To hold otherwise would allow a petitioner to “use the very failure that caused the default as the basis for excusing it,” a result this Court has repeatedly rejected.

For these reasons, the judgment of the Sixth Circuit must be reversed. The claim of ineffective assistance of counsel cannot excuse the procedural default of the vagueness challenge, particularly when that claim itself is defaulted. The doctrine of procedural default must be respected, and federal habeas corpus must not be used as a vehicle to circumvent state procedural rules. The judgment of the Sixth Circuit is contrary to settled precedent and must be reversed.
