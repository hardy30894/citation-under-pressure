ARGUMENT

The Bankruptcy Code exempts “retirement funds to the extent that those funds are in a fund or account that is exempt from taxation under sections 401, 403, 408, 408A, 414, 457, or 501(a) of the Internal Revenue Code.” 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). An inherited IRA satisfies the tax-deferral requirement yet fails the distinct requirement that the assets constitute “retirement funds.”

“Retirement funds” are sums “set aside for retirement.” The phrase is not defined in the Code, but its ordinary meaning requires that the assets be held for the owner’s own retirement. See Rodriguez v. United States, 480 U.S. 522, 525–26 (1987) (“no legislation pursues its purposes at all costs”). An inherited IRA cannot serve that purpose. The beneficiary may make no contributions, may not roll the balance into another account, and must begin withdrawals within one year of the decedent’s death, with full distribution often required within five years. 26 U.S.C. § 408(d)(3)(C); § 401(a)(9)(B). The funds therefore represent an opportunity for current consumption, not savings reserved for post-employment years.

Statutory context confirms the distinction. Other exemptions in § 522 expressly refer to “the debtor’s” interests and impose temporal limits measured from the petition date. Section 522(b)(3)(C) omits those limits only to permit continued employer contributions during the case; it does not convert assets that have never been the debtor’s retirement savings into exempt property. Rousey v. Jacoway, 544 U.S. 320, 325 (2005) (exemptions protect interests that qualify under the statutory criteria at the time of bankruptcy).

Treating an inherited IRA as exempt would also produce results the Code does not countenance. If the decedent had withdrawn the funds before death, paid any applicable tax, and transferred the proceeds, the money would lose its exempt character. The same result follows when the transfer occurs by inheritance. Nothing in the text suggests that the mode of transfer alone preserves the exemption once the assets cease to be anyone’s retirement funds.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
