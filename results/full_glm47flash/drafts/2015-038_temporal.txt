**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Plain Text of ANILCA § 103(c) Authorizes the National Park Service to Regulate Navigable Waters within National Park System Units.**

The National Park Service has the authority to regulate hovercrafts on the Nation River because the text of the Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act (ANILCA) authorizes the Secretary to make regulations for the "public lands" within Conservation System Units (CSUs) in Alaska. The district court correctly held that Sturgeon’s interpretation of § 103(c) is foreclosed by the plain language of the statute. *United States v. Utah Construction & Mining Co.*, 384 U.S. 394, 410 (1966). The Supreme Court has held that "words of a statute are to be given their ordinary meaning." Id. Under this principle, the term "public lands" encompasses the navigable waters and submerged lands within the boundaries of a national park, even if the State of Alaska holds title to the underlying land. The NPS’s authority to regulate these waters is essential to the preservation and management of the park unit.

**II. Federal Authority Over Navigable Waters within Parks is Not Limited by State Ownership of Submerged Lands.**

The NPS’s regulatory authority over navigable waters within the National Park System is grounded in the federal government’s inherent power to regulate navigable waters. *United States v. Rio Grande Dam & Irrigation Co.*, 174 U.S. 690, 701 (1899). The Court has long held that "the power to regulate the use of the navigable waters of the United States is vested in the United States." Id. This authority is not diminished by the fact that the State of Alaska owns the submerged lands beneath the river. *United States v. California*, 332 U.S. 19, 23 (1947). The federal government retains the power to regulate activities on navigable waters to ensure the preservation of the national park resources. Therefore, the NPS regulations prohibiting hovercrafts on the Nation River are valid under ANILCA § 103(c).
