# ARGUMENT

The district court granted summary judgment on the ground that Harold Wasek could not show his coworker's conduct occurred "because of" his sex. That conclusion cannot be reconciled with the record or with the Supreme Court's controlling decision in *Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.*, 523 U.S. 75 (1998), which squarely holds that "male-on-male sexual harassment in the workplace was actionable under Title VII." *Id.* at 79. Because a reasonable jury could find that Ottobre's conduct was sexual in nature, severe and pervasive, and that Arrow Energy did nothing to stop it despite repeated notice, summary judgment should be reversed.

## I. A Reasonable Jury Could Find the Harassment Occurred "Because of" Wasek's Sex.

*Oncale* identifies several "evidentiary route[s]" by which a same-sex harassment plaintiff may prove that the conduct constituted discrimination "because of . . . sex." *Id.* at 80–81. Chief among them is proof that the harasser's conduct reflects "explicit or implicit proposals of sexual activity" from which "it is reasonable to assume those proposals would not have been made to someone of the same sex" absent sexual interest. *Id.* at 80. That is precisely this record. Ottobre repeatedly grabbed Wasek's buttocks, poked him in the rear with a hammer handle and a sucker rod, and told him "you've got a pretty mouth," "boy you have pretty lips," and "you know you like it sweetheart." These are not generic taunts; they are sexualized touching accompanied by sexualized commentary directed at Wasek's body. Wasek's own belief that Ottobre was bisexual only reinforces the inference *Oncale* contemplates—that the conduct reflected actual sexual interest rather than ordinary workplace friction. *Id.* ("[H]arassing conduct need not be motivated by sexual desire to support an inference of discrimination on the basis of sex," but such motivation, where present, plainly suffices.).

The district court's contrary view collapses explicit sexual touching into generalized "horseplay" or "bullying." But *Oncale* forecloses that shortcut: the statute does not turn on "the sex of the harasser and the victim," but on whether the conduct is discrimination "because of . . . sex," and repeated genital-adjacent touching paired with comments about Wasek's "pretty lips" is conduct a jury—not a judge on summary judgment—should evaluate. *Id.* at 81. Nothing in *Oncale* requires a plaintiff to produce comparator evidence once the conduct itself is explicitly sexual; comparative evidence is simply one alternative "evidentiary route," not an indispensable element. *Id.* at 80–81.

## II. The Conduct Was Severe or Pervasive Under the Totality of the Circumstances.

Hostile-work-environment liability requires conduct "sufficiently severe or pervasive to 'alter the conditions of [the victim's] employment and create an abusive working environment.'" *Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, 67 (1986). Courts assess "the frequency of the discriminatory conduct; its severity; whether it is physically threatening or humiliating, or a mere offensive utterance; and whether it unreasonably interferes with an employee's work performance." *Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc.*, 510 U.S. 17, 23 (1993). Every one of these factors favors Wasek. The touching was repeated, not isolated; it was physical, not verbal only; it escalated to the point that Wasek shoved Ottobre in frustration; and it culminated in Wasek fleeing the jobsite entirely because supervisors refused to intervene. A jury—not the court—must weigh whether this satisfies *Harris*'s "totality of the circumstances" standard. *Id.*

## III. Arrow Energy Is Liable for Failing to Correct Known Coworker Harassment.

Even coworker (non-supervisor) harassment gives rise to employer liability where the employer "knew or should have known of the charged sexual harassment and failed to implement prompt and appropriate corrective action." *Blankenship v. Parke Care Centers, Inc.*, 123 F.3d 868, 872 (6th Cir. 1997). Wasek told rig boss Tripp directly; Tripp laughed and threatened to put Wasek on a "starvation schedule" if he reported further. Wasek then told Director of Operations Smillie, who responded by asking why Wasek did not simply "kick [Ottobre's] ass." Wasek told Director of Operations Strong, who told Wasek he was "whining" and should "duke it out" with Ottobre. No one investigated, disciplined Ottobre, or separated the men. This is not a case of an employer unaware of a problem; it is a case of an employer told three times, at three levels of management, and choosing to do nothing. That is a textbook failure of "prompt and appropriate corrective action." *Blankenship*, 123 F.3d at 872.

## IV. The EL-CRA Claim Rises and Falls with the Title VII Claim.

Michigan courts apply the same analytical framework to hostile-environment claims under EL-CRA as federal courts apply under Title VII. *Radtke v. Everett*, 442 Mich. 368, 382, 501 N.W.2d 155 (1993); *Chambers v. Trettco, Inc.*, 463 Mich. 297, 313, 614 N.W.2d 910 (2000). Because the Title VII claim survives summary judgment for the reasons above, the EL-CRA claim must be reinstated as well.

For these reasons, the judgment below should be reversed and the case remanded for trial.
