# ARGUMENT

## I. A CLAIM FOR NOMINAL DAMAGES ALONE DOES NOT PRESERVE A LIVE CASE OR CONTROVERSY UNDER ARTICLE III

Article III restricts federal judicial power to actual "Cases" and "Controversies." *Spencer v. Kemna*, 523 U.S. 1, 7 (1998). A case becomes moot when it no longer presents "a live controversy" and the court can no longer provide "any effectual relief whatever." *Church of Scientology of Cal. v. United States*, 506 U.S. 9, 12 (1992) (quoting *Mills v. Green*, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895)). Once GGC rescinded the challenged policies, Petitioners' claims for declaratory and injunctive relief became moot—a point Petitioners did not even contest below. Pet. App. 7 n.1. The sole question is whether a bare demand for nominal damages, untethered to any compensable injury, can keep such a case alive.

It cannot. This Court has consistently required that relief actually redress a *concrete* injury for a case to remain justiciable. *Lewis v. Continental Bank Corp.*, 494 U.S. 472, 477 (1990) ("[A] federal court has neither the power to render advisory opinions nor to decide questions that cannot affect the rights of litigants in the case before them." (quoting *North Carolina v. Rice*, 404 U.S. 244, 246 (1971))). Nominal damages, by definition, do not compensate for any actual injury. As this Court explained in *Carey v. Piphus*, 435 U.S. 247, 266 (1978), nominal damages are appropriate only "to vindicate" the "abstract value of a constitutional right," not to remedy any provable harm. Because Petitioners alleged no compensable injury—no lost wages, no medical expenses, no quantifiable harm of any kind—their remaining request for nominal damages seeks not redress for injury but a judicial pronouncement on the legality of a policy that no longer exists. That is precisely the advisory opinion Article III forbids. *Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc.*, 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013) ("The mootness doctrine ... requir[es] that federal courts must be sure that the plaintiff has a personal stake in the litigation ... at all stages" (internal quotation marks omitted)).

The Eleventh Circuit's decision, and this Court's own precedent, recognize that once the underlying policy is repealed and no compensable injury is pled, the controversy "has been resolved," leaving nothing left for a court to remedy. *Already, LLC*, 568 U.S. at 91. A one-dollar judgment untethered to any actual harm is not "effectual relief" within the meaning of *Church of Scientology*; it is a certificate of correctness that courts do not exist to issue. *Camreta v. Greene*, 563 U.S. 692, 702 (2011) ("Camreta asks us to decide his appeal even though he has no personal stake in its outcome. We generally decline such requests." ).

## II. PETITIONERS' NOMINAL DAMAGES CLAIM CANNOT BE SEVERED FROM THE MOOTED DECLARATORY AND INJUNCTIVE CLAIMS

Petitioners' complaint was, at its core, a facial and as-applied challenge to now-defunct policies. The prayer for nominal damages was appended to claims for prospective relief, not pled as a freestanding request tethered to distinct, cognizable injury. As the Eleventh Circuit correctly found, the First Amended Complaint "mentions 'injury' only twice, and in neither instance does it specify what the injury was." Pet. App. 10. Compensatory damages in a § 1983 action must rest on "actual injury caused by the defendant rather than on the 'abstract value' of the constitutional rights that may have been violated." *Memphis Community School District v. Stachura*, 477 U.S. 299, 308 (1986) (rejecting damages awards based on "the jury's perception of the abstract 'importance' of a constitutional right").

This Court has long cautioned against permitting nominal-damages requests to function as a vehicle for converting moot policy challenges into live controversies. In *Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment*, 523 U.S. 83, 107 (1998), the Court held that a "generalized interest" in vindicating the law, without a redressable injury tied to the requested relief, does not satisfy Article III's case-or-controversy requirement, because "relief that does not remedy the injury suffered cannot bootstrap a plaintiff into federal court." Petitioners' claim is a mirror image: a technical request for a dollar, severed from any live policy or provable harm, cannot resurrect jurisdiction over claims that are otherwise plainly moot.

## III. PERMITTING NOMINAL DAMAGES TO DEFEAT MOOTNESS WOULD UNDERMINE THIS COURT'S VOLUNTARY-CESSATION AND MOOTNESS FRAMEWORK

This Court has developed a careful framework for determining when governmental self-correction moots a challenge to official policy. Under *Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc.*, 528 U.S. 167, 189 (2000), voluntary cessation moots a case only if it is "absolutely clear that the allegedly wrongful behavior could not reasonably be expected to recur." The Eleventh Circuit applied that exacting standard below and found—without objection from Petitioners—that GGC had "unambiguously terminated the Prior Policies" with "no reasonable basis to expect that it will return to them." Pet. App. 7 n.1.

Petitioners do not dispute that determination. Instead, they ask this Court to hold that a nominal-damages request automatically survives that mootness finding, regardless of the rigor with which voluntary cessation was assessed. But that approach would eviscerate the voluntary-cessation doctrine's careful balance: governments could never fully extinguish litigation over rescinded policies merely by fixing the problem, because plaintiffs could always tack on a one-dollar demand. This Court has warned against interpretation that would "have the effect of converting the rule of Article III standing into a magic password" that opens the door to endless litigation over policies no longer in effect. *Cf. Steel Co.*, 523 U.S. at 107.

## IV. AFFIRMING PRESERVES THIS COURT'S AND THE LOWER COURTS' LIMITED ROLE

Finally, sound judicial administration counsels affirmance. Federal courts do not sit to opine on the constitutionality of policies that no longer govern any party's conduct. *Already, LLC*, 568 U.S. at 91. Petitioners' invitation to allow nominal damages, standing alone, to sustain jurisdiction over challenges to superseded policy would effectively write mootness doctrine out of existence in the many cases where defendants voluntarily correct constitutional defects—exactly the responsible, litigation-ending conduct this Court's precedents encourage. *See Friends of the Earth*, 528 U.S. at 194 (recognizing that "it would be counter-productive" to deny defendants the benefit of ceasing challenged conduct).

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
