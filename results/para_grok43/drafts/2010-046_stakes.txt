ARGUMENT

The three-judge court’s population-reduction order complies with the Prison Litigation Reform Act. The Act permits such relief only when “crowding is the primary cause of the violation of a Federal right” and “no other relief will remedy the violation.” 18 U.S.C. § 3626(a)(3)(E)(i)–(ii). After an extensive trial, the court made both findings. Those determinations are reviewable for clear error, and none appears here. See Anderson v. City of Bessemer City, 470 U.S. 564, 573 (1985) (“Findings of fact shall not be set aside unless clearly erroneous.”).

The order is also the least intrusive means of correcting the established Eighth Amendment violations. See 18 U.S.C. § 3626(a)(1)(A). The court required reduction only to 137.5 percent of design capacity, a limit the record showed was necessary to permit adequate medical and mental-health care. Narrow tailoring is satisfied when relief “extends no further than necessary to correct the violation of the Federal right.” Id.; see also Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343, 357 (1996) (relief must be “limited to what is required to remedy the violation”).

Petitioners’ contrary arguments rest on the premise that any population cap necessarily violates the Act. That premise finds no support in the statutory text or in this Court’s precedents interpreting it. See Miller v. French, 530 U.S. 327, 347 (2000) (PLRA “does not cabin the equitable discretion of district courts so tightly” as to preclude effective relief). Because the three-judge court’s findings and the resulting order satisfy every statutory prerequisite, the judgment should be affirmed.
