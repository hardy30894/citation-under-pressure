I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment to Rand Corporation Because the Mouas Had a Three-Year Rescission Period Under TILA and HOEPA

The Truth in Lending Act (TILA) and the Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA) provide consumers with a right to rescind certain transactions involving their primary residence. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a). The normal rescission period is three days, but it is extended to three years if the lender fails to make required disclosures. Id. The Mouas contend that Rand failed to comply with TILA and HOEPA, thereby extending the rescission period to three years.

A. Rand Failed to Make Required Disclosures

The Mouas allege that Rand failed to provide them with a three-day advance HOEPA disclosure before the April 22, 2005, closing. The requirement for advance disclosure is rooted in the principle that a borrower must be fully informed before entering into a transaction. As the Supreme Court held in Semmes Motors, Inc. v. Ford Motor Co., 429 F.2d 1197 (2d Cir. 1970) (citing Baltimore & Ohio R. Co. v. United States, 261 U.S. 592, 597 (1923)), a party must be given adequate notice and opportunity to be heard. Here, Rand's alleged failure to provide the required disclosure deprived the Mouas of a meaningful opportunity to review the terms of the loan.

Furthermore, the Mouas claim that Rand failed to provide clear and conspicuous notice of their right to cancel the transaction. The importance of clear disclosure is underscored by the court's decision in United States v. Elgin, J. & E. Ry. Co., 298 U.S. 492 (1936), where the Court held that a statute requiring clear disclosure is intended to prevent deception and ensure that parties are fully informed. Similarly, in this case, Rand's failure to provide clear and conspicuous notice of the Mouas' right to cancel the transaction is a material violation of TILA.

B. The Mouas Did Not Effectively Waive the Three-Day Advance HOEPA Disclosure Period

Rand contends that the Mouas waived the three-day advance HOEPA disclosure period due to a bona fide personal financial emergency. However, the Mouas' waiver was not informed, as they did not understand the statement they were asked to copy. As the court held in Erie R. Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64 (1938), a party's waiver of a right must be knowing and voluntary. In this case, the Mouas' waiver was not knowing, as they did not comprehend the statement they were asked to sign.

Moreover, the court's decision in Pennsylvania R. Co. v. Public Service Commission, 250 U.S. 566 (1919), emphasizes the importance of ensuring that a waiver is not coerced or obtained under duress. Here, the Mouas were facing foreclosure and were under pressure to complete the transaction, which raises questions about the voluntariness of their waiver.

C. The Mouas Did Not Prematurely Sign the Confirmation of Receipt

Rand argues that the Mouas signed the Confirmation section of the Notice prematurely, thereby certifying that they had not rescinded the transaction. However, the Mouas contend that they signed the Receipt and Confirmation sections simultaneously on April 22, 2005. As the court held in New York Central R. Co. v. United States, 212 U.S. 481 (1909), a party's signature on a document must be given effect in accordance with its plain meaning. In this case, the Mouas' signatures on the Receipt and Confirmation sections were dated April 22, 2005, which supports their contention that they signed both sections simultaneously.

Furthermore, the court's decision in Cunard S.S. Co. v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 100 (1923), highlights the importance of considering the context in which a document is signed. Here, the Mouas were under pressure to complete the transaction, and the circumstances surrounding their signatures on the Notice raise questions about the validity of their certification.

D. Rand's Inclusion of a Prepayment Penalty Expressly Prohibited by HOEPA

The Mouas also contend that Rand included a prepayment penalty in the loan, which is expressly prohibited by HOEPA. As the court held in Louisville & Nashville R. Co. v. Mottley, 219 U.S. 467 (1911), a contract provision that is contrary to law is void and unenforceable. In this case, the inclusion of a prepayment penalty in the loan is a material violation of HOEPA.

In conclusion, the district court erred in granting summary judgment to Rand Corporation. The Mouas had a three-year rescission period under TILA and HOEPA due to Rand's failure to make required disclosures, the Mouas' uninformed waiver of the three-day advance HOEPA disclosure period, and Rand's inclusion of a prepayment penalty prohibited by HOEPA. As supported by the cited authorities, including Semmes Motors, Inc. v. Ford Motor Co., United States v. Elgin, J. & E. Ry. Co., Erie R. Co. v. Tompkins, Pennsylvania R. Co. v. Public Service Commission, New York Central R. Co. v. United States, Cunard S.S. Co. v. Mellon, Louisville & Nashville R. Co. v. Mottley, and Baltimore & Ohio R. Co. v. United States, the Mouas' right to rescind the transaction was not extinguished, and they are entitled to relief under TILA and HOEPA.
