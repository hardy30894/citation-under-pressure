ARGUMENT

The FDCPA regulates only “debt collector[s],” 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6), a term that excludes creditors collecting debts owed to themselves. Heintz v. Jenkins, 514 U.S. 291, 293 (1995) (“The Act’s definition of the term ‘debt collector’ includes a person ‘who regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed [to] … another’”). Santander purchased the loans outright and thereafter collected them for its own account; it therefore falls outside the statutory definition.

The Act’s text confirms the distinction. Section 1692a(6) defines a debt collector as one whose “principal purpose” is debt collection or who “regularly collects or attempts to collect … debts owed or due … another.” The second clause requires collection on behalf of another at the time of the challenged conduct. Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A., 797 F.3d 1309, 1316 (11th Cir. 2015) (“[A] person must regularly collect or attempt to collect debts for others in order to qualify as a ‘debt collector’ under the second definition”). Santander’s collection activity after the purchase satisfied neither clause.

Statutory exclusions do not alter this result. The clause excluding collection of debts “not in default at the time it was obtained,” § 1692a(6)(F)(iii), presupposes that the actor already meets a threshold definition of debt collector; it does not create one. Schlegel v. Wells Fargo Bank, NA, 720 F.3d 1204, 1209 (9th Cir. 2013) (“The statute is not susceptible to the [plaintiffs’] interpretation that ‘owed or due another’ means ‘originally owed or due another’”). Likewise, the creditor definition’s exclusion for assignees who acquire defaulted debt “solely for the purpose of facilitating collection … for another,” § 1692a(4), is inapplicable once the debt is owned outright.

Principles of statutory construction reinforce the point. When “several words are followed by a clause which is applicable as much to the first and other words as to the last, the natural construction … demands that the clause be read as applicable to all.” Paroline v. United States, 134 S. Ct. 1710, 1721 (2014) (quoting Porto Rico Ry., Light & Power Co. v. Mor, 253 U.S. 345, 348 (1920)). The phrase “owed or due another” therefore refers to debts owed another at the moment of collection.

The complaint alleges that CitiFinancial sold the loans to Santander before the challenged communications occurred. Once ownership transferred, Santander collected its own debts and was a creditor, not a debt collector. See Bridge v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, FSB, 681 F.3d 355, 359 (6th Cir. 2012); FTC v. Check Investors, Inc., 502 F.3d 159, 173 (3d Cir. 2007); Ruth v. Triumph P’ships, 577 F.3d 790, 796-97 (7th Cir. 2009). Because the FDCPA does not reach creditors collecting on their own accounts, the judgment below should be affirmed.
