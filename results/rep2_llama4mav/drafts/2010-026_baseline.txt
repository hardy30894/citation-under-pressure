The Ninth Circuit erred in exempting documents relating to the effects of explosions from disclosure under the FOIA because they are "predominantly" for internal use and present a risk of circumventing agency regulation. The court's adoption of the "predominantly internal" standard for Exemption 2 is unfounded and contradicts the statutory text and Supreme Court precedent.

Exemption 2 shields matters "related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency." 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Supreme Court has interpreted this exemption narrowly, holding that it applies to information that is not of "genuine and significant public interest" and to more sensitive information whose disclosure "may risk circumvention of agency regulation." Dep't of the Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S. 352, 363, 369 (1976). The Ninth Circuit's prior caselaw, including Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), limited Exemption 2 to "law enforcement materials, the disclosure of which may risk circumvention of agency regulation." The court's expansion of Exemption 2 to include a broader "predominantly internal" standard is not supported by the statute or Supreme Court precedent.

The Navy's affidavits, including Commander Whitbred's declaration, do not establish that the ESQD arc maps are "related solely" to internal personnel rules and practices. The maps are used to design and construct ammunition storage facilities and to organize ammunition operations for risk mitigation and enhanced safety. This information is not "predominantly internal" in nature, as it relates to the safety of the surrounding community and the potential risks associated with the storage of explosives.

Furthermore, Exemption 7(F) is inapplicable because the ESQD arc maps were not "compiled for law enforcement purposes." Church of Scientology v. Dep't of the Army, 611 F.2d 738, 748 (9th Cir. 1979). The Navy's use of the maps for safety and risk mitigation purposes does not meet this requirement.

The Navy's reluctance to release the ESQD arc maps may be driven by concerns about political fallout rather than genuine national security concerns. The Navy's decision not to classify the maps undermines its claims about the risks of disclosure. The court should remand to allow the Navy to classify the maps if they truly pose a national security risk, rather than expanding Exemption 2 to protect unclassified information.

The Ninth Circuit's decision distorts the balance struck by Congress in FOIA between public access to information and the need to protect certain kinds of information from disclosure. The exemptions under FOIA "must be narrowly construed" and are "explicitly exclusive." Rose, 425 U.S. at 361; U.S. Dep't of Justice v. Tax Analysts, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989). The court should reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision and hold that the ESQD arc maps are not exempt from disclosure under Exemption 2 or Exemption 7(F).
