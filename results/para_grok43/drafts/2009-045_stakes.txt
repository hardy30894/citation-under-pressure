**ARGUMENT**

The lodestar figure for a reasonable attorney’s fee under a federal fee-shifting statute may be enhanced when the fee applicant presents specific evidence that superior performance produced exceptional results. Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984); Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air, 478 U.S. 546, 565 (1986).

In Blum the Court explained that “the quality of representation” and “results obtained” are ordinarily subsumed in the lodestar calculation. 465 U.S. at 899. It nevertheless held that an upward adjustment remains available “in the rare case where the fee applicant offers specific evidence to show that the quality of service rendered was superior to that one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged and that the success was ‘exceptional.’” Id. at 899 (quoting Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424, 435 (1983)). The Court reversed the enhancement in Blum only because the plaintiffs had offered no such evidence. Id.

Delaware Valley I reaffirmed the same principle. The Court stated that “upward adjustments of the lodestar figure are still permissible … in certain ‘rare’ and ‘exceptional’ cases, supported by both ‘specific evidence’ on the record and detailed findings by the lower courts.” 478 U.S. at 565 (quoting Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-901). Although the Court observed that the lodestar “includes most, if not all, of the relevant factors constituting a ‘reasonable’ attorney’s fee,” it expressly left open the possibility of enhancement upon the requisite showing. Id. at 566.

Every circuit that has addressed the question has read Blum and Delaware Valley I to permit enhancements on this ground when the record contains the required evidence. See, e.g., Geier v. Sundquist, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co., 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000); Hyatt v. Apfel, 195 F.3d 188, 192 (4th Cir. 1999); Roe v. Cheyenne Mountain Conference Resort, Inc., 124 F.3d 1221, 1233 n.8 (10th Cir. 1997); Shipes v. Trinity Indus., 987 F.2d 311, 320 (5th Cir. 1993).

Here the district court made detailed, case-specific findings—based on its observation of counsel throughout years of litigation—that the representation far exceeded what the lodestar hourly rates contemplated and produced results of exceptional breadth and durability. Those findings satisfy the Blum standard. The court of appeals therefore correctly held that the enhancement rested within the district court’s discretion.

The judgment should be affirmed.
