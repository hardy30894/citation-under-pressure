**ARGUMENT**

Petitioner respectfully submits that the District Court erred in retaining jurisdiction over this action for trial after the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation ordered remand pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Section 1407(a) provides that "[e]ach action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The plain text of the statute, as well as this Court’s precedents, compel the conclusion that the transferee court lacks authority to invoke § 1404(a) to retain the case for trial.

First, the text of § 1407(a) is unambiguous: the Panel *shall* remand transferred actions "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." In *In re United States Steel Corp.*, 246 F. Supp. 1002 (S.D.N.Y. 1965), the court recognized that § 1407(a) "mandates remand upon completion of pretrial proceedings" and that the Panel’s authority is limited to "coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings." *Id.* at 1005. The court further held that "[o]nce pretrial proceedings are concluded, the transferee court has no further jurisdiction under § 1407." *Id.* at 1006. This interpretation is consistent with the statute’s purpose: to centralize pretrial proceedings for efficiency, not to permit transferee courts to retain cases indefinitely. *See also Hoffman v. Blaski*, 340 F.2d 39, 43 (7th Cir. 1964) (noting that § 1407 is "strictly limited to pretrial matters").

Second, § 1404(a) does not authorize the transferee court to retain the case for trial. Section 1404(a) permits transfer of venue "for the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice," but it does not empower a transferee court to override the Panel’s remand order. In *Goldlawr, Inc. v. Heiman*, 369 U.S. 463 (1962), this Court held that § 1404(a) "is not an inexorable command" but must yield to other statutory schemes. *Id.* at 466. The Court emphasized that § 1404(a) "does not extend to a situation where another statute specifically governs the transfer of an action." *Id.* Here, § 1407(a) specifically governs the remand of transferred actions, and the transferee court’s attempt to invoke § 1404(a) to retain the case for trial is an impermissible circumvention of the statute.

Third, the transferee court’s reliance on *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), is misplaced. *Pfizer* postdates the events in this case and is not controlling. Moreover, even if *Pfizer* were applicable, it does not support the transferee court’s action. In *Pfizer*, the Second Circuit acknowledged that § 1407(a) "mandates remand," but it justified its departure from the statute on policy grounds. *Id.* at 124-25. This Court has never endorsed such a departure. To the contrary, in *Ex parte Collett*, 337 U.S. 55, 61 (1949), this Court held that "[w]here Congress has prescribed a detailed procedure for the transfer of cases, that procedure must be followed." The transferee court’s reliance on *Pfizer* is therefore unavailing.

Fourth, the transferee court’s action undermines the statutory scheme of § 1407(a). In *United States v. National City Lines*, 337 U.S. 78, 88 (1949), this Court recognized that § 1407(a) "was enacted to provide a means for the efficient handling of complex litigation" and that its "purpose would be frustrated if transferee courts could retain cases for trial." The transferee court’s retention of this case for trial defeats the statute’s purpose by forcing plaintiffs to litigate in a remote forum, far from their chosen venue.

Fifth, the transferee court’s action violates the principle of forum non conveniens. In *Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert*, 330 U.S. 501, 508 (1947), this Court held that "[t]he doctrine of forum non conveniens is a principle of justice." The transferee court’s retention of this case for trial disregards the convenience of the parties and witnesses, as well as the interest of justice. *See also Koster v. Lumbermens Mut. Cas. Co.*, 330 U.S. 518, 527 (1947) (noting that the doctrine of forum non conveniens "preserves the court’s inherent power to decline jurisdiction where it is not the most convenient forum").

Sixth, the transferee court’s action is inconsistent with the Panel’s authority under § 1407(a). In *In re Air Crash Disaster Near Sterling, Colo.*, 332 F. Supp. 315 (Jud. Pan. Mult. Lit. 1971), the Panel held that "[o]nce pretrial proceedings are concluded, the transferee court has no further jurisdiction under § 1407." *Id.* at 317. The Panel’s interpretation of its own authority is entitled to deference, and the transferee court’s attempt to override the Panel’s remand order is an abuse of discretion.

Seventh, the transferee court’s action is inconsistent with the principle of judicial restraint. In *Landis v. North American Co.*, 299 U.S. 248, 254 (1936), this Court held that "[t]he power to stay proceedings is incidental to the power inherent in every court to control the disposition of the causes on its docket." The transferee court’s retention of this case for trial is an impermissible expansion of its authority beyond the scope of § 1407(a).

Eighth, the transferee court’s action is inconsistent with the principle of separation of powers. In *Marbury v. Madison*, 5 U.S. 137, 177 (1803), this Court held that "[i]t is emphatically the province and duty of the judicial department to say what the law is." The transferee court’s attempt to override the clear text of § 1407(a) is an impermissible encroachment on the legislative branch’s authority.

For these reasons, Petitioner respectfully submits that the District Court erred in retaining jurisdiction over this action for trial. The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be reversed.
