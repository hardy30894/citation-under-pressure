The District Court's failure to advise Manuel Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2), does not provide a basis for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals correctly held that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review.

As this Court has recognized, not every error in the administration of the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure requires automatic reversal or vacatur of a sentence. See United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725, 731-32 (1993). Rather, the Court has applied a harmless-error analysis to determine whether a defendant's substantial rights were affected by the error. Id. at 734.

In this case, the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was a clear violation of Rule 32(a)(2). However, the court's finding that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing renders the error harmless. As the Court of Appeals noted, the purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that defendants are aware of their appellate rights. See United States v. Deans, 436 F.2d 596, 599 (3d Cir. 1971). Since Peguero was already aware of his right to appeal, the District Court's failure to advise him did not prejudice him.

The harmless-error doctrine, as applied in cases such as Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993), and Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), supports the conclusion that a Rule 32 violation does not automatically entitle a defendant to habeas relief. In Brecht, this Court held that habeas relief is not warranted unless the error "had substantial and injurious effect or influence in determining the jury's verdict." 507 U.S. at 637 (quoting Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750, 776 (1946)). Similarly, in this case, the District Court's error did not have a substantial and injurious effect on Peguero's decision not to appeal, as he was already aware of his right to do so.

Therefore, the Court of Appeals correctly affirmed the District Court's denial of Peguero's habeas claim. The judgment should be affirmed.
