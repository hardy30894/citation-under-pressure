**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Patent Act Limits Damages for Design Patent Infringement to the Portion of the Infringer’s Profits Attributable to the Infringed Design**

The Patent Act provides that a design patent infringer “shall be liable to the owner to the extent of his total profit.” 35 U.S.C. § 289 (emphasis added). This Court has long held that § 289’s “total profit” language does not permit a patentee to recover the entire profits of a multicomponent product where the infringed design applies only to a component. *Apple Inc. v. Samsung Elecs. Co.*, 786 F.3d 983, 998 (Fed. Cir. 2015) (en banc) (citing *Dawson v. Dutcher*, 162 U.S. 614, 620 (1896)). Instead, damages must be apportioned to reflect the value of the infringed design alone. *Id.* (quoting *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620).

This principle of apportionment is rooted in the Patent Act’s text and this Court’s precedent. Section 289’s reference to “total profit” must be read in light of § 284, which requires damages to “be no less than a reasonable royalty” and to “recover[] for the infringement.” 35 U.S.C. § 284. As this Court has explained, “[t]he statute does not permit a patentee to recover damages based on the entire profits of a multicomponent product where the patented design applies only to a component of that product.” *Apple*, 786 F.3d at 998 (citing *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620). To hold otherwise would permit a patentee to recover damages “disproportionate to the value of the patented invention,” a result this Court has repeatedly rejected. *Id.* (quoting *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620).

The Federal Circuit’s decision in *Apple* confirms this principle. There, this Court rejected Apple’s argument that it could recover the entire profits of Samsung’s smartphones based on the infringement of a single design patent covering a portion of the phone’s front face. *Id.* at 998. The Court held that damages must be apportioned to reflect the value of the infringed design alone, not the entire product. *Id.* (citing *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620). The Court explained that “[t]o allow otherwise would permit a patentee to recover damages disproportionate to the value of the patented invention,” a result inconsistent with the Patent Act. *Id.* (quoting *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620).

**II. The District Court Erred in Failing to Apportion Damages to the Infringed Design**

The district court erred in failing to apportion damages to the infringed design. The jury was permitted to award damages based on Samsung’s entire profits from the accused smartphones, despite the fact that the infringed design patents applied only to specific components of those phones. This was legal error. *Apple*, 786 F.3d at 998 (citing *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620).

This Court has repeatedly emphasized that damages must be apportioned to reflect the value of the infringed design alone. *See, e.g., Apple*, 786 F.3d at 998; *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620. The district court’s failure to do so here was inconsistent with this Court’s precedent and the Patent Act. The jury’s award must be vacated and the case remanded for a new trial on damages, with instructions to apportion damages to the infringed design. *Apple*, 786 F.3d at 998 (citing *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620).

**III. The Federal Circuit’s Decision Below Is Contrary to Controlling Precedent**

The Federal Circuit’s decision below is contrary to this Court’s controlling precedent. The Federal Circuit affirmed the district court’s award of damages based on Samsung’s entire profits, despite the fact that the infringed design patents applied only to specific components of the accused smartphones. This was legal error. *Apple*, 786 F.3d at 998 (citing *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620).

This Court’s decision in *Apple* confirms that damages must be apportioned to reflect the value of the infringed design alone. *Id.* at 998. The Federal Circuit’s decision below is inconsistent with this principle and must be reversed. *Id.* (citing *Dawson*, 162 U.S. at 620).

**Conclusion**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the Federal Circuit’s decision and remand the case for a new trial on damages, with instructions to apportion damages to the infringed design.
