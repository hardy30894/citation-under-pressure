ARGUMENT

A district court's technical violation of former Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) supplies no ground for relief under 28 U.S.C. § 2255 when the defendant already knew of his right to appeal and deliberately forwent it. Collateral relief is available only for "a fundamental defect which inherently results in a complete miscarriage of justice." Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424, 428 (1962). The omission here caused no such defect.

The sole purpose of the Rule 32 advisory is to ensure that a defendant is informed of the right to appeal. When that information is already possessed, the Rule's objective is satisfied and the violation is harmless. See United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780, 783-784 (1979) (holding that a Rule 11 violation "which does not affect substantial rights" does not warrant § 2255 relief). The district court's finding that Peguero knew of his appellate rights at sentencing, and elected not to exercise them, establishes the absence of prejudice.

This Court has consistently required a showing of prejudice before vacating a federal sentence on collateral review. See United States v. Addonizio, 442 U.S. 178, 184-185 (1979) (limiting § 2255 to errors that "result[] in a complete miscarriage of justice"); Machibroda v. United States, 368 U.S. 487, 493 (1962) (same). No such miscarriage occurs when the defendant, fully aware of his rights, makes a knowing decision to forgo appeal.

The court of appeals correctly applied harmless-error principles to the Rule 32 violation. Because the record demonstrates that Peguero suffered no actual prejudice, the judgment denying habeas relief should be affirmed.
