**ARGUMENT**

The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act regulates only “debt collector[s],” 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6), a term that excludes creditors collecting debts owed to themselves. The complaint alleges that Santander purchased the defaulted loans outright and thereafter sought to collect them for its own account. That conduct places Santander outside the statutory definition.

Section 1692a(6) defines a “debt collector” as any person “who regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed or due or asserted to be owed or due another.” The phrase “owed or due another” refers to debts owed to a third party at the time of collection. Heintz v. Jenkins, 514 U.S. 291, 293 (1995). A person who has purchased the debt and therefore owns it is not collecting “debts owed or due another.” Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A., 797 F.3d 1309, 1316 (11th Cir. 2015); Schlegel v. Wells Fargo Bank, NA, 720 F.3d 1204, 1209 (9th Cir. 2013).

The statute’s structure confirms the distinction. The Act separates “debt collector” from “creditor,” the latter being “any person who offers or extends credit creating a debt or to whom a debt is owed.” 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(4). Once Santander acquired the loans, the debts were owed to it. Its subsequent collection efforts were therefore those of a creditor, not a debt collector. The default status of the loans at the time of purchase is irrelevant to this threshold inquiry. Davidson, 797 F.3d at 1314.

Petitioners’ contrary reading—that any purchaser of defaulted debt automatically becomes a debt collector—would erase the statutory line between the two categories. It would also subject every finance company that both originates loans and occasionally buys portfolios to FDCPA liability whenever it collects its own debts, a result the text does not support. See 15 U.S.C. § 1692(e) (purpose limited to “abusive debt collection practices by debt collectors”).

Because the complaint fails to allege that Santander was acting as a “debt collector” within the meaning of § 1692a(6) when it communicated with petitioners, the judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
