The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that it could review the entire record, including evidence not presented to the jury, when determining whether the error in Greer's trial affected his substantial rights or the fairness, integrity, or public reputation of the trial. Under plain-error review, a federal appellate court may correct an error not raised at trial if it is plain, affects substantial rights, and seriously affects the fairness, integrity, or public reputation of judicial proceedings. United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725, 732 (1993). 

When assessing whether an error affected a defendant's substantial rights, the court must determine whether it "affected the outcome of the district court proceedings." Id. To make this determination, the Eleventh Circuit properly considered the entire record, including Greer's prior convictions, which were not presented to the jury. This approach is consistent with the Supreme Court's decision in United States v. Vonn, 535 U.S. 55 (2002), where the Court held that a reviewing court may consult the whole record when considering the effect of an error on substantial rights. Id. at 74-75 (quoting United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1, 16 (1985)).

Greer's argument that the Eleventh Circuit erred by looking beyond the trial record is unpersuasive. The plain-error standard does not limit the reviewing court to the trial record when assessing prejudice or the impact on the trial's fairness. See, e.g., United States v. Dominguez Benitez, 542 U.S. 74, 83 (2004) (considering "whatever the trial record shows" when evaluating prejudice). By examining the entire record, the Eleventh Circuit properly applied the plain-error standard and determined that Greer could not demonstrate prejudice or that the error affected the fairness, integrity, or public reputation of his trial.

The judgment of the Eleventh Circuit should be affirmed.
