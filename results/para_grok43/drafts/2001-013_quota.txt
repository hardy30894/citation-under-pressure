ARGUMENT

Section 4(b)(1) of the Occupational Safety and Health Act withholds OSHA jurisdiction only where another federal agency has exercised statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards affecting occupational safety or health. 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1); see also Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. Transport Workers Union, 451 U.S. 77, 94-95 (1981) (preemption requires actual exercise of authority). The Coast Guard has not done so for uninspected vessels such as Rig 52. See, e.g., United States v. Pennsylvania R.R., 323 U.S. 612, 621 (1945) (statutory grants are construed to avoid unnecessary displacement of concurrent regulation).

This Court has repeatedly held that mere statutory authority is insufficient; the agency must have acted. See, e.g., Florida Lime & Avocado Growers, Inc. v. Paul, 373 U.S. 132, 146 (1963); Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp., 331 U.S. 218, 230 (1947). The Coast Guard’s regulations for uninspected vessels address only discrete equipment requirements, 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01 et seq., leaving the working conditions at issue unregulated. See also Bethlehem Steel Corp. v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Comm’n, 607 F.2d 871, 875 (3d Cir. 1979) (OSHA jurisdiction persists absent comprehensive exercise).

Fifth Circuit precedent does not compel a contrary result. Clary v. Ocean Drilling & Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120, 1122 (5th Cir. 1980), and Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825, 827 (5th Cir. 1983), addressed inspected vessels or seamen’s general maritime duties; neither examined the statutory distinction between inspected and uninspected vessels. See also Producers Drilling Co. v. Gray, 361 F.2d 432, 437 (5th Cir. 1966) (distinguishing vessel types). Other circuits have recognized that limited Coast Guard rules on uninspected vessels do not oust OSHA. See, e.g., Southern Ry. v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Comm’n, 539 F.2d 335, 339 (4th Cir. 1976); Columbia Gas of Pa., Inc. v. Marshall, 636 F.2d 913, 918 (3d Cir. 1980).

The Act’s purpose of providing “every working man and woman in the Nation safe and healthful working conditions,” 29 U.S.C. § 651(b), is advanced by concurrent jurisdiction where no conflict exists. See also Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954) (remedial statutes receive practical construction). Because the Coast Guard has not exercised authority over the cited conditions, OSHA retains jurisdiction to issue the citations.
