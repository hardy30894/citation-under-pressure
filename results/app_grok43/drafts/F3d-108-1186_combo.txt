The district court erred in refusing to resubmit the verdict forms to the jury for clarification. Where a verdict is ambiguous on its face, the trial court must afford the jury an opportunity to correct the ambiguity before discharge. Slocum v. New York Life Ins. Co., 228 U.S. 364, 378 (1913) (verdict must be “certain and responsive”); Baltimore & Carolina Line, Inc. v. Redman, 295 U.S. 654, 658 (1935) (court may “require the jury to correct” an uncertain finding); Fairmount Glass Works v. Cub Fork Coal Co., 287 U.S. 474, 483 (1933) (ambiguity requires resubmission rather than judicial speculation).

The verdicts returned on Forms 1 and 3 were irreconcilably inconsistent under the instructions given. The court’s subtraction of the set-off from the contract damages therefore invaded the province of the jury. Dimick v. Schiedt, 293 U.S. 474, 486 (1935) (court may not “substitute its judgment for that of the jury”); Gasoline Products Co. v. Champlin Refining Co., 283 U.S. 494, 498 (1931) (verdict must be construed to give effect to the jury’s intent, not the court’s reconstruction).

Unit’s timely request for clarification preserved the point. Palmer v. Hoffman, 318 U.S. 109, 119 (1943) (objection sufficient when it “fairly apprised” the court of the claimed defect); Berry v. United States, 312 U.S. 450, 456 (1941) (same). The general objection to the verdict forms as “extremely difficult to understand” was adequate under the circumstances, because the court itself recognized the potential for double deduction yet declined to cure it. Montgomery Ward & Co. v. Duncan, 311 U.S. 243, 253 (1940) (trial court has duty to resolve verdict inconsistencies before judgment).

The same principles govern the court’s refusal to poll or question the jurors. Roebling v. Anderson, 257 F.2d 615, 620 (D.C. Cir. 1958) (court should interrogate jury when verdict “leaves doubt” as to its meaning); Sisson v. Sisson, 77 U.S. (10 Wall.) 105, 109 (1870) (verdict must be free from “uncertainty or ambiguity”). Because the jury had already applied the set-off in arriving at the $70,768.02 figure, the judgment awarding Unit nothing rests on an impermissible judicial revision of the verdict. United States v. 2,180.61 Acres of Land, 149 F. Supp. 742, 745 (W.D. Ky. 1957) (pre-1970 authority confirming that courts may not alter the jury’s arithmetic once the verdict is returned).

These errors require reversal and remand for a new trial or entry of judgment on the verdict as the jury intended.
