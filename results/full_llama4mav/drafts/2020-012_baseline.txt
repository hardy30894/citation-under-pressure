## ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act ("RRA") grants federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final decisions" of the United States Railroad Retirement Board ("the Board"). 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review. This Court should hold that it does.

The RRA's judicial review provision, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), is modeled after the Social Security Act's ("SSA") judicial review provision, 42 U.S.C. § 405(g). See Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99, 105-06 (1977) (noting the similarity between the two provisions). In Sanders, this Court held that a decision by the Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare declining to reopen a prior SSA benefits claim is not reviewable under § 405(g) absent a colorable constitutional claim. Id. at 107-09. However, the Court left open the possibility that a different result might obtain under a differently worded judicial review provision.

The D.C. Circuit has persuasively held that the RRA's judicial review provision, § 355(f), is materially different from the SSA's § 405(g) and does grant jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations. Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500, 504-07 (D.C. Cir. 2016). Unlike § 405(g), which limits review to "final decisions" made "after a hearing," § 355(f) permits review of "final decisions" without the "after a hearing" qualification. Id. at 505. This distinction is significant, as it suggests that Congress intended to permit review of a broader range of Board decisions under the RRA.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), which held that a Board decision declining to reopen a prior benefits claim is not reviewable, was wrongly decided. The Roberts court relied heavily on Sanders, which, as explained above, is distinguishable. See Roberts, 346 F.3d at 141. The court also failed to consider the textual differences between § 355(f) and § 405(g), which, as the D.C. Circuit has persuasively held, support a broader reading of the RRA's judicial review provision.

In this case, the Board's decision denying Salinas's request to reopen his 2006 benefits claim is a "final decision" subject to judicial review under § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit erred in dismissing Salinas's petition for review based on its precedent in Roberts. This Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision and hold that the Board's decision is reviewable.

By holding that the Board's decision is reviewable, this Court will ensure that the RRA's judicial review provision is interpreted consistently with its plain language and will provide clarity on the scope of review available to claimants under the RRA.
