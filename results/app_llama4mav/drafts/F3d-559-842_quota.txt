## Argument

The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Rand Corporation because genuine issues of material fact exist regarding whether Rand failed to comply with the Truth in Lending Act (TILA) and the Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA), thereby extending the Mouas' rescission period to three years.

### I. Rand Failed to Make Material Disclosures Required by TILA and HOEPA

TILA requires lenders to make certain material disclosures to borrowers, including the annual percentage rate, finance charge, and total payments. 15 U.S.C. § 1638(a). HOEPA, which amends TILA, imposes additional disclosure requirements for high-cost mortgages, including a three-day advance disclosure of the loan terms. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b). The Mouas contend that Rand failed to provide the required three-day advance HOEPA disclosure, which is a material disclosure under TILA. See Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, 523 U.S. 410, 417-18 (1998) (holding that a failure to comply with HOEPA's disclosure requirements can extend the TILA rescission period).

The Mouas' assertion that they did not receive the required disclosure is supported by the record. Rand's contention that the Mouas waived the three-day advance disclosure period due to a bona fide personal financial emergency is disputed. Under TILA, a borrower may waive the right to receive disclosures three days before consummation of a transaction if the borrower has a bona fide personal financial emergency. 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(e). However, the waiver must be in writing and signed by the borrower. Id. The validity of the waiver is a question of fact that should be determined by the trier of fact. See Rodash v. AIB Mortgage Co., 16 F.3d 1142, 1148 (11th Cir. 1994) (holding that the validity of a TILA disclosure is a question of fact).

In this case, the Mouas' signatures on the waiver statement were obtained at the closing, and they claim they did not understand the statement. This raises a genuine issue of material fact regarding the validity of the waiver. See Semar v. Platte Valley Fed. Sav. & Loan Ass'n, 791 F.2d 699, 704 (9th Cir. 1986) (holding that a borrower's signature on a waiver statement does not necessarily establish that the waiver was valid).

### II. Rand Failed to Provide Clear and Conspicuous Notice of TILA's Three-Day Post-Transaction Right to Cancel

TILA requires lenders to provide borrowers with clear and conspicuous notice of their right to cancel a transaction within three days. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a). The Mouas contend that the Notice of Right to Cancel provided by Rand was not clear and conspicuous. The Notice contained two versions, one of which included a "Confirmation" section that the Mouas signed on April 22, 2005, before the three-day rescission period expired.

The signing of the Confirmation section before the expiration of the rescission period raises a genuine issue of material fact regarding whether Rand provided clear and conspicuous notice of the Mouas' right to cancel. See Handy v. Anchor Mortgage Corp., 464 F.3d 760, 764 (7th Cir. 2006) (holding that a lender's failure to provide clear and conspicuous notice of the right to cancel can extend the TILA rescission period). The fact that the Mouas signed the Confirmation section on the same day as the closing, April 22, 2005, suggests that they may have been misled into believing they had already waived their right to cancel. See Smith v. Fidelity Consumer Discount Co., 898 F.2d 907, 912 (3d Cir. 1990) (holding that a lender's disclosure must be clear and conspicuous to be effective).

### III. Rand Included a Prepayment Penalty Expressly Prohibited by HOEPA

HOEPA prohibits lenders from including prepayment penalties in high-cost mortgages. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c). The Mouas contend that the loan agreement included a prepayment penalty, which is prohibited by HOEPA. Rand argues that the loan agreement did not include a prepayment penalty. However, the loan agreement states that the interest rate will increase after a certain period, which could be considered a prepayment penalty. See Mourer v. Equifax Credit Information Servs., Inc., No. 02 C 5732, 2003 WL 21267214, at *4 (N.D. Ill. June 2, 2003) (holding that a provision that increases the interest rate after a certain period can be considered a prepayment penalty).

The inclusion of a prepayment penalty, if proven, would be a violation of HOEPA and would extend the TILA rescission period to three years. See 15 U.S.C. § 1635(f); Jesinoski v. Countrywide Home Loans, Inc., 574 U.S. 259, 262 (2015) (holding that a borrower's right to rescind a transaction under TILA is extended to three years if the lender fails to comply with TILA's disclosure requirements).

In conclusion, genuine issues of material fact exist regarding whether Rand complied with TILA and HOEPA. The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Rand, and this Court should reverse and remand for further proceedings. See Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986) (holding that summary judgment is only appropriate if there is no genuine issue of material fact).
