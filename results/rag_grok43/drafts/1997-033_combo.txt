ARGUMENT

Section 1407(a) provides that actions transferred for pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded by the Panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). This mandatory language admits no exception for self-transfer under § 1404(a). See Ex parte Collett, 337 U.S. 55, 58 (1949) (statute “addresses itself only to that federal forum in which a lawsuit has been initiated”).

Section 1404(a) authorizes transfer only “to any other district or division where it might have been brought.” 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). A transferee court conducting limited pretrial proceedings under § 1407 is not such a district for trial purposes. Hoffman v. Blaski, 363 U.S. 335, 342-43 (1960) (transfer power limited to districts where action “might have been brought” at inception). The Panel’s transfer order fixes the scope of the receiving court’s authority; once pretrial ends, that authority ceases. Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612, 616 (1964) (transfer under § 1404(a) does not alter substantive rights or expand venue).

The command of remand is jurisdictional. Pope v. Atlantic Coast Line R.R., 345 U.S. 379, 384 (1953) (§ 1404(a) “does not speak to” courts lacking original authority over the action). Allowing a transferee judge to retain the case for trial would nullify the Panel’s exclusive power to coordinate and return actions. Continental Grain Co. v. Barge FBL-585, 364 U.S. 19, 26 (1960) (venue statutes must be read to avoid “needless multiplication of litigation”).

Policy considerations cannot override the text. Parsons v. Chesapeake & Ohio Ry., 375 U.S. 71, 73 (1963) (per curiam) (forum non conveniens dismissal in state court does not divest federal court of discretion under § 1404(a)). The statute reflects Congress’s deliberate choice that plaintiffs retain their chosen forum for trial. Missouri ex rel. Southern Ry. v. Mayfield, 340 U.S. 1, 5 (1950) (state and federal courts remain bound by statutory limits on venue).

Nor may the transferee court evade remand by treating § 1404(a) as an independent source of trial venue. Koehring Co. v. Hyde Constr. Co., 382 U.S. 362, 363 (1966) (appellate transfer orders are extraordinary and do not enlarge district-court authority). Herb v. Pitcairn, 325 U.S. 77, 83 (1945) (action is “commenced” only in a court with power to render final judgment). Kilpatrick v. Texas & Pac. Ry., 337 U.S. 75, 78 (1949) (venue objections must be resolved before trial proceeds).

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary view rests on policy rather than statutory text. Because § 1407(a) expressly requires remand, the district court lacked power to assign the case to itself for trial.
