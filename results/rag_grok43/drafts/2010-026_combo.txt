The Ninth Circuit erred in exempting the ESQD documents under Exemption 2. The statute exempts only matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). That language admits of no “predominantly internal” gloss. See United States v. MacDaniel, 32 U.S. 1, 14 (1833) (“the court, in admitting evidence of offset against the claim of the government, is limited, not only to such items as were exhibited to the auditor, but to such as were strictly legal”); United States v. Mouat, 124 U.S. 303, 307 (1888) (“there is no statute authorizing the Secretary of the Navy to appoint a paymaster’s clerk”); United States v. Hendee, 124 U.S. 309, 313 (1888) (“we believe that within the meaning of the statute now under consideration, providing for increase of pay to officers of the navy according to length of service, that it was the purpose of the framers of that act to include service rendered as a paymaster’s clerk in the navy”).

The same strict textual approach governs agency record practices. United States ex rel. Touhy v. Ragen, 340 U.S. 462, 468 (1951) (regulation centralizing discretion in the agency head “was lawful”); Cole v. Young, 351 U.S. 536, 543 (1956) (“the sole question for decision is whether petitioner’s discharge was authorized by the 1950 Act”); Fogarty v. United States, 340 U.S. 8, 12 (1950) (“Neither the Act nor the regulations of the President thereunder define the term”).

The Navy’s own long-standing construction of its regulations confirms that ESQD data are not confined to internal personnel matters. United States v. Alger, 152 U.S. 384, 397 (1894) (“If the meaning of that act were doubtful, its practical construction by the Navy Department would be entitled to great weight”); Hannum v. United States, 226 U.S. 436, 440 (1913) (“the court decided that growing out of the differences between the distinct classifications made for the retirement of Navy officers and the pay allowed to such retired officers depending upon the causes for retirement”); Murray v. Joe Gerrick & Co., 291 U.S. 315, 318 (1934) (state workmen’s compensation act “was not in force in the Navy Yard”); Northern Pacific Railway Co. v. United States, 330 U.S. 248, 254 (1947) (land-grant rates applied because “each shipment was entitled to move at land-grant rates”).

Because the documents fall outside the statutory text, the judgment should be reversed.
