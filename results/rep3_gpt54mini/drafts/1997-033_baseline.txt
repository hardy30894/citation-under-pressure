**Argument**

Section 1407(a) says what it means and means what it says: a multidistrict transferee court may conduct only “coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings,” and the Panel “shall” remand each transferred action “at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). That command leaves no room for a transferee district court to keep a case for trial by recharacterizing a venue change as a transfer under § 1404(a). The Court should enforce the statute as written. *See* *Connecticut Nat. Bank v. Germain*, 503 U.S. 249, 253–54 (1992) (“courts must presume that a legislature says in a statute what it means and means in a statute what it says there”).

1. **Section 1407(a) expressly requires remand for trial.**  
This Court has repeatedly recognized that statutory text controls when Congress speaks in mandatory terms. *See* *United States v. Monsanto*, 491 U.S. 600, 607 (1989). Here, Congress used the mandatory “shall” and paired it with a specific temporal limit: remand must occur “at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). That language is not aspirational; it is a directive. As Judge Kozinski observed below, “[i]t’s hard to imagine a clearer statutory command.” *Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach*, 61 F.3d 911, 914 (9th Cir. 1995) (Kozinski, J., dissenting).

2. **Section 1404(a) cannot be used to defeat § 1407(a)’s remand requirement.**  
Section 1404(a) permits transfer “[f]or the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice,” but it is a general venue statute. 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). Section 1407(a), by contrast, is a specific statute governing a specific procedural posture: cases transferred for multidistrict pretrial proceedings. Where Congress has enacted a specific statutory scheme, courts must apply it rather than use a more general provision to displace it. *See* *Morton v. Mancari*, 417 U.S. 535, 550–51 (1974). Allowing a transferee judge to invoke § 1404(a) to retain a case for trial would nullify § 1407(a)’s express remand command and collapse the distinction Congress drew between pretrial coordination and trial.

3. **The decisions relied on below cannot overcome the statute’s text.**  
The panel below acknowledged that petitioners’ reading had “merit” and that § 1407 appears to contain “a clear statutory mandate.” *Lexecon*, 61 F.3d at 912–13. It nonetheless relied on lower-court decisions and MDL Rule 14(b). *Id.* at 912. But a procedural rule cannot authorize what Congress forbids. *See* *Sibbach v. Wilson & Co.*, 312 U.S. 1, 10 (1941) (rules of procedure cannot “abridge, enlarge or modify any substantive right”); *Hanna v. Plumer*, 380 U.S. 460, 471 (1965). Likewise, judicial efficiency cannot override unambiguous statutory text. *See* *Pittston Coal Group v. Sebben*, 488 U.S. 105, 121 (1988) (“the fact that the Secretary may not like the result does not give him license to ignore the statute”).

The Second Circuit’s early decision in *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), did not hold otherwise; it simply adopted a policy-based workaround. But policy cannot displace enacted text. *See* *Lamie v. United States Trustee*, 540 U.S. 526, 536 (2004) (“It is well established that when the statute’s language is plain, the sole function of the courts—is to enforce it according to its terms.”) (internal quotation marks omitted). The same is true of *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810 (3d Cir. 1982), and like cases.

4. **The Court should reverse and require remand.**  
The practical consequences underscore why Congress chose a bright line. MDL transfer is an exceptional device justified by pretrial efficiency, not a license to reassign trial forum. *See* *Gelboim v. Bank of America Corp.*, 574 U.S. 405, 413–14 (2015) (MDL actions retain their separate character unless and until the panel orders otherwise). Once pretrial proceedings end, the case returns to the original court. That is what § 1407(a) says, and this Court should give effect to it.

The judgment should be reversed.
