ARGUMENT

The Double Jeopardy Clause bars retrial of the prior conviction allegation. The Clause protects against a second prosecution for the same offense after acquittal. Ex parte Lange, 85 U.S. (18 Wall.) 163, 168 (1873). It applies once a defendant has been placed in jeopardy, which occurs when a jury is empaneled and sworn. Downum v. United States, 372 U.S. 734, 736 (1963). An acquittal on the facts ends the matter. United States v. Ball, 163 U.S. 662, 671 (1896).

The Clause extends to the states. Benton v. Maryland, 395 U.S. 784, 794 (1969). It precludes relitigation of issues necessarily resolved in the defendant's favor. Ashe v. Swenson, 397 U.S. 436, 443 (1970) (pre-1970 decision). A judgment of acquittal on an element bars further proceedings on that element. Green v. United States, 355 U.S. 184, 188 (1957). The protection attaches whether the proceeding is denominated trial or sentencing, for the Clause speaks of jeopardy of "life or limb." Kepner v. United States, 195 U.S. 100, 126 (1904).

The prior-conviction proceeding here required proof beyond a reasonable doubt of facts that increased the prescribed range of punishment. Such a determination constitutes an acquittal when the evidence is found insufficient. In re Nielsen, 131 U.S. 176, 188 (1889). Retrial would subject the defendant to successive jeopardy for the same offense. Grafton v. United States, 206 U.S. 333, 349 (1907). The Clause forbids that result even when the first proceeding is imperfect. United States v. Tateo, 377 U.S. 463, 466 (1964).

The same principle governs successive prosecutions for greater and lesser offenses. Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299, 304 (1932). Once the prosecution fails to prove the enhancement, the defendant stands acquitted of that allegation. North Carolina v. Pearce, 395 U.S. 711, 717 (1969). Allowing a second attempt violates the fundamental rule against multiple trials for the same offense. Trono v. United States, 199 U.S. 521, 529 (1905).

The judgment of the California Supreme Court should be reversed.
