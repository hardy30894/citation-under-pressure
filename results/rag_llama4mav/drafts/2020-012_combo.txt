The Railroad Retirement Act (RRA) and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (RUIA) grant the federal courts of appeals jurisdiction to review certain final decisions of the Railroad Retirement Board (Board). 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The Fifth Circuit held that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas' prior claim, relying on its own precedent in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003). However, the Roberts decision was based on an incomplete understanding of the relevant statutory framework and case law.

United States, 297 U.S. 167 (1936), the Court held that a decision by the Board of Veterans' Appeals was subject to judicial review, despite the agency's contention that its decision was final and not reviewable. Similarly, in Pennsylvania Railroad v. Day, 360 U.S. 548 (1959), the Court held that a decision by the National Railroad Adjustment Board was binding on the parties, but did not preclude judicial review of the underlying claim.

The RRA and RUIA should be interpreted in light of the principles established in these cases. The statute grants the courts of appeals jurisdiction to review "final decisions" of the Board, without defining what constitutes a "final decision." However, the Supreme Court has held that a decision is "final" for purposes of judicial review if it marks the completion of the administrative process and determines the rights of the parties. See Joint Industry Board of Electrical Industry v. United States, 391 U.S. 224 (1968); International Union of Mine, Mill & Smelter Workers, Locals Nos. 15, 17, 107, 108 & 111 v. Eagle-Picher Mining & Smelting Co., 325 U.S. 335 (1945).

In SEC v. Louisiana Pub. Serv. Comm'n, 353 U.S. 368 (1957), the Court held that an agency's denial of a petition to reopen a prior proceeding was subject to judicial review, as it was a "final order" within the meaning of the relevant statute. Similarly, in Robertson v. Chambers, 341 U.S. 37 (1951), the Court held that a decision by an Army Review Board was subject to judicial review, despite the agency's contention that its decision was final and not reviewable.

Furthermore, the Court has recognized that the availability of judicial review is not limited to initial determinations, but also extends to subsequent decisions that affect the rights of the parties. See Wragg v. Federal Land Bank of New Orleans, 317 U.S. 325 (1943); Dodge v. Board of Ed. of Chicago, 302 U.S. 74 (1937).

Alton Railroad, 295 U.S. 330 (1935), the Court held that the RRA was a valid exercise of Congress's power to regulate interstate commerce, and that the Board's decisions were subject to judicial review. This decision establishes that the Board's decisions, including those denying requests to reopen prior claims, are subject to judicial review.
