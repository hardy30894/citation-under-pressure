The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's claim of conspiracy in violation of the Railway Labor Act ("RLA"), 45 U.S.C. § 153. The RLA requires that a union fairly represent its members in grievance proceedings. Steele v. Louisville & Nashville Railroad Co., 323 U.S. 192, 204 (1944). A union breaches its duty of fair representation when it acts arbitrarily or in bad faith towards a member. Ford Motor Co. v. Huffman, 345 U.S. 330, 337-338 (1953). Here, Plaintiff presented evidence that UTU representative Earley argued against Plaintiff's position during the executive session of the Public Law Board No. 3882 ("PLB"), suggesting that Earley may have acted in bad faith.

The district court also erred in awarding Defendants statutory damages, punitive damages, and attorneys' fees under the Federal Wiretap Act (the "Act"), 18 U.S.C. § 2510 et seq. The Act prohibits the interception of oral communications, but provides an exception for recordings made with the consent of one party to the conversation. 18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d) (1968). As established in Rathbun v. United States, 355 U.S. 107, 111 (1957), where one party to a conversation consents to its interception, there is no "interception" within the meaning of the Act. Although the Act was enacted after Rathbun, the reasoning in Rathbun remains relevant as it interprets the underlying principles that were later codified in the Act. Here, Plaintiff was a party to the conversation and had brought the tape recorders to the hearing, suggesting that he may have had the intent to record the proceedings, but it is unclear whether he consented to recording the executive session.

Furthermore, the award of damages and attorneys' fees was excessive and not justified by the circumstances. The Act provides for damages and attorneys' fees, but the award should be reasonable in light of the defendant's conduct. See Benanti v. United States, 355 U.S. 96, 105-106 (1957) (discussing the importance of considering the defendant's conduct when awarding damages under a related statute). In this case, Plaintiff's actions, while potentially wrongful, were taken in the context of a labor dispute and were not intended to cause harm to Defendants.

The district court's grant of summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's RLA claim and its award of damages and attorneys' fees under the Act should be reversed. The case should be remanded for further proceedings to determine whether Defendants conspired to violate the RLA and whether Plaintiff's recording of the executive session was lawful.
