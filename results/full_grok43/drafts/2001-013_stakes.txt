ARGUMENT

Section 4(b)(1) of the Occupational Safety and Health Act provides that the Act does not apply to “working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies * * * exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). The text requires actual exercise of authority over the particular working conditions at issue. See, e.g., Southern Pac. Transp. Co. v. Usery, 539 F.2d 386, 389 (5th Cir. 1976) (preemption turns on whether another agency “has exercised” its authority).

The Coast Guard’s investigation of the explosion on Rig 52 produced no citation and identified no violation of any Coast Guard regulation. The agency’s general statutory authority under 14 U.S.C. § 2 and 46 U.S.C. § 6301 therefore remained unexercised with respect to the cited conditions. Clary v. Ocean Drilling & Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), are not to the contrary. Both cases involved vessels subject to comprehensive Coast Guard inspection and regulation; neither addressed an uninspected vessel on which the Coast Guard had promulgated no applicable standards.

The court of appeals’ extension of those decisions to uninspected vessels erases the statutory requirement of actual exercise. Where, as here, another agency has not acted, OSHA retains jurisdiction under section 4(a) to enforce the Act’s requirements in a workplace located within a State. 29 U.S.C. § 653(a). The judgment vacating the citation should be reversed.
