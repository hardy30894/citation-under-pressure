# ARGUMENT

## I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING SIEBEN'S MOTION FOR JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW ON FELTMANN'S SEX DISCRIMINATION CLAIM BECAUSE FELTMANN FAILED TO PRESENT SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE THAT SIEBEN'S LEGITIMATE, NONDISCRIMINATORY REASONS FOR ITS ACTIONS WERE PRETEXTUAL.

Under the familiar burden-shifting framework of *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792 (1973), once an employer articulates a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason for the challenged employment action, the plaintiff must demonstrate that the proffered reason is pretext for discrimination. *Id.* at 804. This Court has emphasized that a plaintiff's burden at the pretext stage is not satisfied merely by disbelieving the employer's explanation; the plaintiff must show "both that the reason was false, and that discrimination was the real reason." *St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks*, 509 U.S. 502, 515 (1993).

Sieben articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for each employment action at issue. Rich's decision to place Feltmann on probation in March 1993 was based on her documented low sales performance in January and February—a decline shared by her male colleague Rick Beutel, who received identical treatment. The restriction on demo-car use to the St. Louis area was a cost-control measure implemented dealership-wide following the decline in sales after the incentive program ended. And the failure to include Feltmann in the Chicago sales competition resulted from a straightforward business decision to offer the opportunity first to the top two sales consultants, neither of whom were Feltmann; when they declined, Rich extended the opportunity to no one—male or female.

Feltmann's evidence of pretext consists largely of isolated incidents that, even viewed collectively, fail to establish that Sieben's stated reasons were false. The vulgar comment by Anzalone, a co-worker at a different franchise entirely, cannot establish pretext for decisions made by Rich regarding Feltmann's sales performance. Critically, Sieben's owners took immediate remedial action—meeting with Feltmann, reprimanding Anzalone, and warning him of termination for future conduct. Feltmann herself declined the opportunity to have Anzalone fired. This is precisely the type of prompt remedial action that courts have found forecloses employer liability. *See Kotcher v. Rosa & Sullivan Appliance Center, Inc.*, 957 F.2d 59, 63 (2d Cir. 1992) (employer not liable where it took prompt remedial action upon notice of harassment).

Nor does the fact that Beutel received no personal memo about demo-car restrictions establish pretext where the undisputed evidence shows Feltmann was the only consultant persistently and repeatedly violating the restriction with weekly 300-mile round trips to Illinois—conduct continuing for six months after the initial restriction and two months after her personal memo. An employer's decision to address the employee who is actually violating a company policy is not evidence of discriminatory animus. *See Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc.*, 530 U.S. 133, 148 (2000) (plaintiff must show more than a scintilla of evidence that discrimination was the real reason).

Feltmann's own performance data undermines her claim. Rich testified without contradiction that Feltmann's low gross profit per sale—the lowest among all consultants for five consecutive months—resulted from her failure to employ effective sales techniques, specifically her failure to thoroughly explain vehicle features before allowing unaccompanied "guest drives." This is a legitimate business justification untethered to Feltmann's sex. *See Texas Dep't of Community Affairs v. Burdine*, 450 U.S. 248, 259 (1981) (employer need only articulate reason that is not itself discriminatory).

## II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN SUBMITTING THE RETALIATION CLAIM TO THE JURY BECAUSE FELTMANN FAILED TO ESTABLISH A CAUSAL CONNECTION BETWEEN HER PROTECTED ACTIVITY AND ANY ADVERSE EMPLOYMENT ACTION.

To establish a prima facie case of retaliation, a plaintiff must show, among other elements, "a causal connection between the two events." *Clark County School Dist. v. Breeden*, 532 U.S. 268, 273 (2001). Where a substantial period elapses between the protected activity and the alleged retaliatory action, courts have found the temporal gap insufficient, standing alone, to establish causation. *See id.* at 273-74 (finding a three-month gap "far too long" to permit an inference of causation without other evidence).

Here, Feltmann's complaint about Anzalone occurred in November 1992. The personal memo regarding her "work ethic" issued nearly two months later, in January 1993; her probation followed in March 1993—four months after her complaint. This attenuated timeline, standing alone, cannot support an inference of retaliatory motive. Moreover, the intervening conduct—Feltmann's documented sales decline and persistently low gross profit—provides an intervening legitimate business justification that severs any causal link between the protected activity and the adverse actions. *See Kiel v. Select Artificials, Inc.*, 169 F.3d 1131, 1136 (8th Cir. 1999) (en banc) (evidence of a legitimate intervening event defeats an inference of retaliatory causation based on temporal proximity alone).

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the judgment of the district court and direct entry of judgment in favor of Sieben.
