## ARGUMENT

### I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment to Arrow Energy on Wasek's Title VII and Elliot-Larsen Civil Rights Act Claims

The district court incorrectly concluded that Wasek failed to establish a prima facie case of harassment under both Title VII and Michigan's Elliot-Larsen Civil Rights Act ("ELCRA"). To establish a hostile work environment claim, a plaintiff must show that (1) he is a member of a protected class, (2) he was subjected to unwelcome harassment, (3) the harassment was based on his membership in the protected class, (4) the harassment affected a term, condition, or privilege of employment, and (5) the employer knew or should have known of the harassment and failed to take prompt and appropriate corrective action. See Hafford v. Seidner, 183 F.3d 506, 512 (6th Cir. 1999).

Wasek was subjected to unwelcome harassment by Ottobre, who engaged in severe and pervasive conduct, including touching Wasek in a sexual manner, making sexually explicit comments, and telling crude jokes and stories. The harassment was sufficiently severe or pervasive to affect a term, condition, or privilege of Wasek's employment, as it created a hostile work environment that made it difficult for Wasek to perform his job. See Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17, 21 (1993) (holding that a hostile work environment exists when the workplace is "permeated with discriminatory intimidation, ridicule, and insult that is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of the victim's employment and create an abusive working environment").

### II. Wasek's Harassment Was Motivated by His Sex

The district court erred in concluding that Wasek failed to show that the harassment was motivated by his sex. The record demonstrates that Ottobre's conduct was motivated by Wasek's sex, as Ottobre's actions were overtly sexual in nature. See Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc., 523 U.S. 75, 80 (1998) (holding that same-sex harassment is actionable under Title VII when the harasser's conduct is motivated by the victim's sex). The fact that Ottobre made sexually explicit comments and touched Wasek in a sexual manner is sufficient to establish that the harassment was sex-based. See EEOC v. Harbert-Yeargin, Inc., 266 F.3d 498, 522 (6th Cir. 2001) (holding that "sexually explicit or derogatory language directed at a plaintiff" can be evidence of sex-based harassment).

### III. Arrow Energy is Liable for the Harassment

Arrow Energy is liable for the harassment Wasek suffered because it knew or should have known of the harassment and failed to take prompt and appropriate corrective action. See Hafford, 183 F.3d at 513. Wasek reported the harassment to his supervisor, Tripp, and to Smillie and Strong, but they failed to take adequate action to stop the harassment. In fact, Tripp, Smillie, and Strong responded to Wasek's complaints with hostility and indifference, suggesting that Wasek should "duke it out" with Ottobre or "not make waves." This response demonstrates that Arrow Energy failed to take Wasek's complaints seriously and failed to take prompt and appropriate corrective action.

The district court's grant of summary judgment to Arrow Energy should be reversed, and Wasek's claims should be remanded for trial.
