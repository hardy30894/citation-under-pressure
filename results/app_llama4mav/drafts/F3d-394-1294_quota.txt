Argument

The Tax Court erred in denying the Davis estate a marital deduction under Internal Revenue Code section 2056(b)(7) for the trust income passing to Evelyn L. Davis. To qualify for a QTIP deduction, the surviving spouse must have a "qualifying income interest for life" in the trust property, meaning she is entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The Amendment to the Declaration of Trust satisfies this requirement.

The language of the Amendment, specifically paragraph 2, indicates that the trustee shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse "all of the net income from the trust estate" in quarter annual or more frequent installments, as the trustee determines to be proper for her health, education, or support. Although the trustee has some discretion in determining the amount of income to be distributed, this discretion is not unfettered, as it is limited to the surviving spouse's "accustomed manner of living." This construction is consistent with the holding in Estate of Howard v. Commissioner, 91 T.C. 329 (1988), where the court found that a trust provision requiring the trustee to distribute income to the surviving spouse "as she shall from time to time request" satisfied the QTIP requirement.

Furthermore, the fact that the trustee has discretion to consider "any other income or resources of the beneficiary known to the trustee and reasonably available" when making distributions does not negate the surviving spouse's entitlement to all the income. As the court held in Estate of Spencer v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo 1992-579, a trustee's discretion to consider the beneficiary's other resources does not render the beneficiary's interest non-qualifying under section 2056(b)(7).

The Commissioner's reliance on cases such as Estate of Doherty v. Commissioner, 95 T.C. 446 (1990), is misplaced. In Doherty, the court held that a trust did not qualify for a QTIP deduction because the trustee had the power to accumulate income. In contrast, the Amendment to the Declaration of Trust requires the trustee to distribute "all of the net income" to the surviving spouse, albeit with some discretion as to the timing and amount.

Other cases have upheld QTIP deductions where the trustee has some discretion in distributing income to the surviving spouse. See, e.g., Estate of Robertson v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo 2000-212; Estate of Spencer v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo 1992-579; and Estate of Kyle v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo 1991-552. These cases demonstrate that the level of discretion granted to the trustee in the Amendment is consistent with the requirements of section 2056(b)(7).

Additionally, the court in Estate of Clayton v. Commissioner, 976 F.2d 1486 (5th Cir. 1992), held that a trust qualified for a QTIP deduction even though the trustee had some discretion in distributing income. The court emphasized that the surviving spouse was entitled to all the income, and that the trustee's discretion was limited to the timing and manner of distribution.

The Tax Court's decision is also inconsistent with the holding in Commissioner v. Estate of Robertson, 141 F.3d 603 (5th Cir. 1998), where the court reversed a Tax Court decision denying a QTIP deduction. The court held that the trust instrument should be construed to give effect to the testator's intent, and that the QTIP provisions should be applied in a manner consistent with this intent.

In this case, the language of the Amendment and the overall structure of the trust indicate that Ralph H. Davis intended to provide for his surviving spouse, Evelyn L. Davis. The trust satisfies the requirements of section 2056(b)(7), and the Davis estate is entitled to a marital deduction for the trust income passing to Mrs. Davis.

Brown v. United States, 890 F. Supp. 934 (D. Colo. 1995), and Estate of Shelfer v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo 1999-366, also support the conclusion that the trust qualifies for a QTIP deduction. In both cases, the courts upheld QTIP deductions where the trustee had some discretion in distributing income to the surviving spouse.

In conclusion, the Amendment to the Declaration of Trust satisfies the requirements of section 2056(b)(7), and the Davis estate is entitled to a marital deduction for the trust income passing to Evelyn L. Davis. The Tax Court's decision should be reversed.
