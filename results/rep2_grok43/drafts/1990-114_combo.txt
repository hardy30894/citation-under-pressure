The ADEA does not reach appointed state judges. Congress did not manifest an unequivocal intention to intrude upon the States' traditional authority over their own judiciaries. McCulloch v. Maryland, 17 U.S. (4 Wheat.) 316, 421 (1819) (federal power must be exercised with "great respect" for the "original powers" of the States). Absent a clear statement, statutes are construed to preserve the federal balance. Gibbons v. Ogden, 22 U.S. (9 Wheat.) 1, 209 (1824). The definition of "employee" therefore excludes "an appointee on the policymaking level." 29 U.S.C. § 630(f). Judges occupy that level. Their decisions "establish[] the law" for future cases. Erie R.R. Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64, 78 (1938). That function is indistinguishable from the policymaking performed by other high state officers. Ex parte Virginia, 100 U.S. 339, 348 (1880) (judges are "state officers" charged with administering state law).

Even if the ADEA applied, Missouri's age limitation would survive equal-protection scrutiny. The provision rests on the permissible judgment that "faculties" required for judicial service decline with age. Railway Express Agency, Inc. v. New York, 336 U.S. 106, 110 (1949) (classification valid if any state of facts reasonably supports it). A State may act on that basis without "invidious discrimination." Williamson v. Lee Optical of Okla., Inc., 348 U.S. 483, 489 (1955). The line at seventy eliminates case-by-case inquiries into individual capacity, a method long recognized as rational. Ferguson v. Skrupa, 372 U.S. 726, 732 (1963). It also facilitates orderly succession and broadens opportunities for newer members of the bar. United States v. Carolene Prods. Co., 304 U.S. 144, 152 (1938) (regulatory distinctions need only bear a rational relation to a legitimate end).

Because the retirement rule is supported by these considerations, it does not deny equal protection. Marbury v. Madison, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137, 176 (1803) (judicial power does not extend to second-guessing legislative policy choices). The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
