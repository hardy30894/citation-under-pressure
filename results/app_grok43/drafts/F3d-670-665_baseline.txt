**ARGUMENT**

A credible claim of actual innocence equitably tolls AEDPA’s one-year statute of limitations. This Court has held that “a petitioner who can make a credible showing of actual innocence may equitably toll the statute of limitations.” *Souter v. Jones*, 395 F.3d 577, 588 (6th Cir. 2005). The district court’s contrary conclusion rests on a misreading of *Pace v. DiGuglielmo*, 544 U.S. 408 (2005).

I. Actual innocence supplies an independent equitable basis for tolling.

AEDPA’s limitations period is subject to equitable tolling when “extraordinary circumstances” prevent timely filing. *Holland v. Florida*, 560 U.S. 631, 649 (2010). A gateway claim of actual innocence constitutes such a circumstance. In *Schlup v. Delo*, 513 U.S. 298 (1995), the Supreme Court recognized that “a petitioner’s showing of actual innocence” permits review of otherwise barred constitutional claims because “the fundamental miscarriage of justice exception” overrides procedural defaults. *Id.* at 316. This Court applied the same principle to AEDPA’s statute of limitations in *Souter*, holding that “the actual innocence exception” permits tolling when new evidence makes it “more likely than not that no reasonable juror would have convicted him.” 395 F.3d at 590 (quoting *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 327).

The affidavits Perkins proffered—sworn statements from Hudson, Louis, and Fleming—were never presented at trial and directly implicate the State’s sole eyewitness. Under *Souter*, that showing is sufficient to toll the limitations period.

II. *Pace* does not impose a separate diligence requirement on actual-innocence claims.

The district court required Perkins to demonstrate “reasonable diligence” after the factual predicate became available, citing *Pace*. That reliance is misplaced. *Pace* addressed equitable tolling in the ordinary case, where a petitioner seeks to excuse an untimely filing on grounds other than innocence. 544 U.S. at 418. It did not address, let alone overrule, the distinct actual-innocence gateway recognized in *Schlup* and extended to AEDPA limitations in *Souter*.

The Supreme Court has repeatedly distinguished the diligence required for ordinary tolling from the miscarriage-of-justice exception. *See House v. Bell*, 547 U.S. 518, 536–37 (2006) (actual-innocence inquiry focuses on reliability of verdict, not petitioner’s post-conviction diligence). Requiring diligence as a threshold bar would nullify the equitable exception this Court recognized in *Souter*. Because Perkins has made a credible showing that “no reasonable juror would have found him guilty,” the statute of limitations must be tolled regardless of the interval between the affidavits and the petition.

The district court’s judgment should be reversed.
