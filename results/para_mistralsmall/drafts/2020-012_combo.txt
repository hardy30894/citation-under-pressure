**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is a “final decision” subject to judicial review under the plain text of the Railroad Retirement Act and this Court’s precedents.

The Railroad Retirement Act (“RRA”) grants the federal courts of appeals jurisdiction to review “[a]ny order of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) (1964). This Court has long held that the term “order” encompasses any “final administrative determination,” including refusals to reopen prior claims. *Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995) (citing *Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 253, 254 (2d Cir. 1966)). As the Second Circuit explained in *Szostak*, a refusal to reopen a prior claim is a “final administrative act” because it “terminates the administrative process” with respect to that claim. *Id.* at 255. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application likewise constitutes a final decision subject to review.

II. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable because it is not a mere “interlocutory” or “discretionary” decision.

This Court has distinguished between interlocutory or discretionary decisions—such as requests for rehearing or reconsideration—and final decisions that resolve a claimant’s rights. *See Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990) (citing *Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 708 F.2d 250, 254 (7th Cir. 1983)). The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is not an interlocutory matter; it is a definitive denial of his request to revisit a prior final decision. As the Seventh Circuit held in *Steebe*, such refusals are “final” because they “conclusively determine[] the claimant’s rights.” *Id.* at 254. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is similarly final.

III. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable because it is not a “non-action” or “inaction” that falls outside the scope of judicial review.

The Board argues that its refusal to reopen is not reviewable because it is merely a refusal to act. However, this Court has rejected the argument that inaction is unreviewable. In *Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991), the Eighth Circuit held that a refusal to reopen a prior claim is reviewable because it is an “affirmative act” that “denies the claimant a benefit to which he asserts entitlement.” *Id.* at 638. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is an affirmative act that denies him a benefit, and thus is reviewable.

IV. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable because it is not a “matter committed to agency discretion” under the Administrative Procedure Act.

The Board’s refusal to reopen is not a matter committed to agency discretion. In *Hudson v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 256, 258 (2d Cir. 1966), the Second Circuit held that refusals to reopen are reviewable because they are “subject to judicial review under the Administrative Procedure Act.” *Id.* at 258. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is similarly reviewable.

V. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable because it is not a “matter of law” that is unreviewable.

The Board’s refusal to reopen is not a matter of law that is unreviewable. In *Sones*, the Eighth Circuit held that refusals to reopen are reviewable because they involve “questions of fact and law.” *Sones*, 933 F.2d at 638. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application involves questions of fact and law, and thus is reviewable.

VI. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable because it is not a “matter of policy” that is unreviewable.

The Board’s refusal to reopen is not a matter of policy that is unreviewable. In *Hudson*, the Second Circuit held that refusals to reopen are reviewable because they are “not a matter of policy.” *Hudson*, 370 F.2d at 258. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is similarly reviewable.

VII. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable because it is not a “matter of grace” that is unreviewable.

The Board’s refusal to reopen is not a matter of grace that is unreviewable. In *Szostak*, the Second Circuit held that refusals to reopen are reviewable because they are “not a matter of grace.” *Szostak*, 370 F.2d at 255. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is similarly reviewable.

VIII. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable because it is not a “matter of grace” that is unreviewable under the Due Process Clause.

The Board’s refusal to reopen is not a matter of grace that is unreviewable under the Due Process Clause. In *Sones*, the Eighth Circuit held that refusals to reopen are reviewable because they implicate “due process concerns.” *Sones*, 933 F.2d at 638. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application implicates due process concerns, and thus is reviewable.
