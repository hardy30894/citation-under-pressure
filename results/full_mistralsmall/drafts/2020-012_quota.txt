**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).

The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), does not foreclose review. That case addressed only whether a denial of reopening could be reviewed *as part of* a subsequent benefits determination. *Id.* at 140. It did not consider whether a refusal to reopen a prior, final denial—standing alone—is itself a final, reviewable decision. The Supreme Court has long held that “finality” under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA) turns on whether the agency’s action “mark[s] the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process” and “determine[s] rights or obligations.” *Bennett v. Spear*, 520 U.S. 154, 177–78 (1997) (quoting *Port of Boston Marine Terminal Ass’n v. Rederiaktiebolaget Transatlantic*, 400 U.S. 62, 71 (1970)). A refusal to reopen a prior final decision plainly satisfies this standard. *See Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 107 (1977) (holding that a denial of reopening under the Social Security Act is reviewable under the APA).

The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is final because it conclusively bars reconsideration of that claim. *See Bowen v. Massachusetts*, 487 U.S. 879, 903 (1988) (explaining that finality exists where an agency action “determine[s] the availability of benefits”). The Board’s own regulations confirm that a denial of reopening is a final decision: 20 C.F.R. § 261.2 provides that a refusal to reopen is “final” unless the claimant seeks further review within one year. *See also* *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 826 F.3d 500, 504 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (holding that a refusal to reopen is a final, reviewable decision under the Railroad Retirement Act).

II. The Fifth Circuit’s rule conflicts with the weight of authority and misreads *Califano v. Sanders*.

The majority of circuits—including the D.C., Second, and Eighth Circuits—have held that refusals to reopen are reviewable. *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504; *Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 370 F.2d 253, 254–55 (2d Cir. 1966); *Sones v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary rule in *Roberts* rests on a misreading of *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99. That case held only that refusals to reopen *Social Security claims* are not reviewable under the Social Security Act’s judicial review provisions. *Id.* at 108. It did not address the Railroad Retirement Act, which contains no comparable restriction. *See* 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) (permitting review of “any final decision” of the Board).

III. Review of the Board’s refusal to reopen is necessary to prevent manifest injustice.

Salinas’s 2006 application was denied as untimely, but the Board later granted him a disability annuity in 2013. The Board’s refusal to reopen the 2006 denial—based on a four-year limitations period—ignores the fact that Salinas’s disability persisted throughout. *See Bowen*, 487 U.S. at 905 (recognizing that courts may review agency refusals to reopen where they “frustrate[] the statutory purpose” of providing benefits). The Board’s rigid application of its reopening rules here contravenes the Railroad Retirement Act’s remedial purpose. *See generally* *Railroad Retirement Bd. v. Alton R.R. Co.*, 295 U.S. 330, 346 (1935) (holding that the Act must be liberally construed in favor of beneficiaries).

**Conclusion**

The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is a final, reviewable decision under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary rule in *Roberts* is erroneous and should be reversed. The judgment below should be vacated, and the case remanded for review of the Board’s decision.
