**ARGUMENT**

**I. Petitioner possesses a protected liberty interest in clemency proceedings.**

The state’s power to impose the death penalty creates a liberty interest in the process that determines whether that penalty is carried out. *Griffin v. Illinois*, 351 U.S. 12, 18 (1956). In *Griffin*, the Court held that a state may not deny a defendant the right to appeal or other post-conviction remedies without due process. Here, clemency serves as the final check on the state’s power to take life. *Gideon v. Wainwright*, 372 U.S. 335, 343 (1963). The right to counsel is a fundamental right, and *Mapp v. Ohio*, 367 U.S. 643, 655 (1961), established that due process requires fair procedures. Because the state has the authority to deprive a petitioner of life, it must provide a constitutionally adequate process to determine if that authority is exercised. Therefore, Woodard has a protected liberty interest in the clemency proceedings.

**II. The clemency interview option violates the Fifth Amendment.**

The regulation creates a “Hobson’s choice” for the petitioner. *Bram v. United States*, 168 U.S. 532, 542 (1897). The Court has held that “to be compelled to be one’s own witness in a criminal prosecution is a cruel and unusual abuse of power.” *Id.* at 543. The privilege against self-incrimination is a fundamental right. *Malloy v. Hogan*, 378 U.S. 1, 7 (1964). While the interview is technically “voluntary,” the reality is that participation is necessary to secure clemency. Forcing a waiver of the Fifth Amendment to access a state benefit violates due process.
