**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING PETITIONER’S REQUEST FOR AN EVIDENTIARY HEARING ON HIS CLAIMS OF INEFFECTIVE ASSISTANCE OF COUNSEL AND PROSECUTORIAL MISCONDUCT.**

A. **Petitioner’s claim of ineffective assistance of counsel warrants an evidentiary hearing under 28 U.S.C. § 2254(e)(2).**

To establish a claim of ineffective assistance of counsel under *Strickland v. Washington*, 466 U.S. 668 (1984), a petitioner must show (1) deficient performance by counsel and (2) prejudice resulting therefrom. However, where, as here, the record is insufficient to resolve the claim, an evidentiary hearing is required. *Townsend v. Sain*, 372 U.S. 293, 312-13 (1963) (holding that an evidentiary hearing is necessary where the state court did not resolve the merits of the claim or where the record is inadequate to address the claim). The district court’s denial of an evidentiary hearing was error because Petitioner’s claim turns on factual disputes—such as whether trial counsel failed to investigate or present mitigating evidence—that cannot be resolved on the existing record. *See id.* at 313-14 (explaining that an evidentiary hearing is required where the facts are in dispute and the state court did not resolve them).

B. **Petitioner’s claim of prosecutorial misconduct also requires an evidentiary hearing.**

Prosecutorial misconduct claims often require an evidentiary hearing to resolve factual disputes. *Moore v. Illinois*, 408 U.S. 786, 795 (1972) (noting that where the record is inadequate to assess the claim, an evidentiary hearing is necessary). Here, Petitioner alleges that the prosecutor improperly elicited testimony about his prior escape attempts to impugn his credibility and made improper arguments during closing. These allegations require factual development, particularly regarding the prosecutor’s intent and the context of the challenged conduct. *See id.* (holding that where the record is insufficient to assess the claim, an evidentiary hearing is required). The district court’s refusal to hold an evidentiary hearing was therefore erroneous.

II. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON PETITIONER’S CLAIMS OF INEFFECTIVE ASSISTANCE OF COUNSEL AND PROSECUTORIAL MISCONDUCT.**

A. **Petitioner’s ineffective assistance of counsel claim was not adjudicated on the merits in state court, permitting federal review.**

Where a state court has not adjudicated the merits of a claim, federal review is not barred by *Stone v. Powell*, 428 U.S. 465 (1976). *See Townsend*, 372 U.S. at 312-17 (holding that where the state court did not resolve the merits of a claim, federal review is permissible). Here, the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals summarily denied Petitioner’s state habeas petition without addressing the merits of his ineffective assistance claim. This failure to adjudicate the claim permits federal review. *See id.* at 317.

B. **Petitioner’s prosecutorial misconduct claim was exhausted in state court and is ripe for federal review.**

A claim of prosecutorial misconduct is cognizable under § 2254 where it implicates a constitutional right. *See Donnelly v. DeChristoforo*, 416 U.S. 637, 642-43 (1974) (holding that prosecutorial misconduct claims are reviewable under § 2254 where they implicate due process). Here, Petitioner’s claim that the prosecutor improperly elicited testimony and made improper arguments during closing implicates his due process rights. Because this claim was exhausted in state court, it is ripe for federal review. *See id.*

III. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING PETITIONER’S REQUEST FOR APPOINTMENT OF COUNSEL TO PROSECUTE HIS HABEAS PETITION.**

A. **Petitioner is entitled to appointed counsel under 28 U.S.C. § 2254(h).**

Where a petitioner is indigent and his habeas petition raises non-frivolous constitutional claims, the district court must appoint counsel. *See Johnson v. Avery*, 393 U.S. 483, 488-89 (1968) (holding that indigent habeas petitioners are entitled to appointed counsel where their claims are not frivolous). Here, Petitioner’s claims of ineffective assistance of counsel and prosecutorial misconduct are not frivolous. The district court’s denial of appointed counsel was therefore erroneous. *See id.*

B. **The district court’s refusal to appoint counsel prejudiced Petitioner.**

The denial of appointed counsel in a complex habeas proceeding, such as this one, where Petitioner’s claims require factual development and legal analysis, prejudiced his ability to present his claims. *See Gideon v. Wainwright*, 372 U.S. 335, 344-45 (1963) (holding that the right to counsel includes the right to effective assistance of counsel in habeas proceedings). The district court’s refusal to appoint counsel therefore warrants reversal. *See id.*

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in denying Petitioner’s request for an evidentiary hearing, in granting summary judgment on his claims of ineffective assistance of counsel and prosecutorial misconduct, and in denying his request for appointed counsel. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
