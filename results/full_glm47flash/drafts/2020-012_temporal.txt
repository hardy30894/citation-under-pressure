**Argument**

**I. The Board’s decision to deny Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application constitutes a “final decision” of the Board subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).**

The RRA grants the courts of appeals jurisdiction to review “final decisions of the Board” regarding claims for benefits. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A decision denying a request to reopen a prior claim is a final agency action that determines the legal status of the underlying claim. *Railroad Retirement Board v. Lacy*, 329 U.S. 655, 660 (1947). In *Lacy*, the Supreme Court held that the Board's decision is “final and conclusive upon the parties.” Id. The denial of a request to reopen is a substantive determination that resolves the rights of the parties regarding the prior claim, justifying judicial review.

**II. The Board’s interpretation that the decision is not a “decision on the merits” of a claim for benefits is inconsistent with the statutory text and Supreme Court precedent.**

The RRA provides for review of “final decisions of the Board” without limiting review to decisions on the merits of a specific claim. *Railroad Retirement Board v. Alton R. Co.*, 295 U.S. 330, 334 (1935). The Court in *Alton* recognized that the Board is a body of experts, but it also affirmed that the courts retain the power to review final decisions. Id. The Board's decision to deny reopening effectively bars Salinas from ever obtaining benefits for the 2006 claim, marking the consummation of the administrative process regarding that claim. *United States v. Morgan*, 295 U.S. 402, 428 (1935). As the Court in *Morgan* explained, a decision is final when it marks the consummation of the administrative process. Id. Here, the Board’s decision to deny reopening concludes the administrative proceedings on the 2006 claim, making it a final decision subject to review.
