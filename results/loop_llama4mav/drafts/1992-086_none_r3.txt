The Center Moriches Union Free School District's denial of Lamb's Chapel's request to use school facilities for a religious-oriented film series constitutes a content-based exclusion of speech in a limited public forum, violating the First Amendment's freedom of speech. The District's facilities, although not traditional public forums, were opened for use by the general public for certain purposes, thereby creating a limited public forum.

The government's power to regulate expression in a public forum is limited. When the government creates a limited public forum, it is bound by the same standards as those applicable to traditional public forums in terms of content-based exclusions. As the Supreme Court has long held, a state may not exclude speech from a public forum based on its content unless the exclusion is necessary to serve a compelling state interest, as seen in Lovell v. Griffin, 303 U.S. 444, 451 (1938), and Cantwell v. Connecticut, 310 U.S. 296, 304 (1940). The exclusion of speech from a limited public forum based on content must be narrowly drawn to achieve a compelling state interest.

The District's exclusion of Lamb's Chapel's film series, described as "Family oriented movie from a Christian perspective," was based on the religious content of the speech. Local Rule No. 7, which prohibits the use of school premises "by any group for religious purposes," constitutes a content-based exclusion. This rule is not viewpoint-neutral, as it discriminates against religious speech while allowing other groups to use the facilities for various purposes. The District's actions are inconsistent with the principles of equal access to public forums, as emphasized in Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569, 574 (1941).

The District has not demonstrated a compelling state interest in excluding Lamb's Chapel's film series. Allowing the film series would not have violated the Establishment Clause, as the Court noted in Zorach v. Clauson, 343 U.S. 306, 314 (1952). The District's policy and practice of excluding religious groups from using school facilities while allowing other groups to use the facilities for various purposes is not justified. As the Court held in Kunz v. New York, 340 U.S. 290, 294 (1951), and Niemotko v. Maryland, 340 U.S. 268, 272 (1951), a prior restraint on speech is not justified simply because there have been no previous instances of disorder or unrest.

The lower court's reliance on the fact that the District had not previously allowed religious groups to use the facilities is misplaced. The District's exclusion of Lamb's Chapel's film series is not justified by a history of prior religious uses. The District's actions are not viewpoint-neutral and do not serve a compelling state interest. Therefore, the District's denial of Lamb's Chapel's request to use school facilities for a religious-oriented film series constitutes a content-based exclusion of speech in a limited public forum, violating the First Amendment's freedom of speech.
