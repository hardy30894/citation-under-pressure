ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN ITS ADMONITION TO THE JURY TO DISREGARD TESTIMONY CONCERNING SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES

The district court initially granted Wood's motion in limine to exclude evidence of subsequent remedial measures under Rule 407 of the Federal Rules of Evidence, but later allowed Wood to introduce such evidence for impeachment purposes when Morbark's counsel opened the door by implying that the wood chipper's design had not changed since the accident. As this Court has recognized, evidence of subsequent remedial measures is admissible for impeachment purposes. See Werner v. Upjohn Co., Inc., 628 F.2d 848, 853 (4th Cir. 1980) (citing with approval the Advisory Committee's Note to Rule 407, which states that evidence of subsequent remedial measures is admissible "when offered for another purpose, such as... impeachment").

Morbark's counsel created the impression that the seventeen-inch infeed chute was safe and still in use by various government agencies, including the City of DeFuniak Springs and the Army Corps of Engineers. In response, Wood's counsel properly elicited testimony from Infinger that the infeed chute had been lengthened. The district court correctly allowed this rebuttal testimony, stating that Morbark's counsel had "opened the door" to it. (R4-56). However, the court later admonished the jury to disregard this testimony, thereby undermining the impeachment purpose for which it was admitted.

The district court's admonition was erroneous because it effectively nullified the impeachment value of Infinger's testimony. By allowing Morbark's counsel to imply that the wood chipper's design was unchanged, and then permitting Wood to rebut that implication with evidence of subsequent remedial measures, the court created a situation in which the jury was entitled to consider that evidence for impeachment purposes. See Petree v. Victor Fluid Power, Inc., 887 F.2d 34, 38-39 (3d Cir. 1989) (holding that evidence of subsequent remedial measures was admissible for impeachment purposes where defendant's expert testified that the product was designed with safety in mind).

The district court's error in admonishing the jury to disregard Infinger's testimony concerning the lengthening of the infeed chute was not harmless. The testimony was crucial to rebutting Morbark's implication that the wood chipper's design was safe and unchanged. By disregarding this testimony, the jury was left with an incomplete and misleading picture of the facts. Therefore, the district court's denial of Wood's motion for a new trial was erroneous.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT'S ERROR REQUIRES A NEW TRIAL

Given the district court's error in admonishing the jury to disregard Infinger's testimony concerning subsequent remedial measures, a new trial is warranted. As this Court has recognized, a new trial is required when an error affects the substantial rights of a party. See O'Dell v. Hercules, Inc., 904 F.2d 1194, 1200 (8th Cir. 1990). In this case, the district court's error undermined Wood's ability to impeach Morbark's counsel's implication that the wood chipper's design was safe and unchanged. Therefore, a new trial is necessary to ensure that the jury is able to consider all relevant evidence, including the evidence of subsequent remedial measures for impeachment purposes.

The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and this case should be remanded for a new trial.
