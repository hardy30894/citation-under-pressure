**ARGUMENT**

The Board erred in concluding that the '880 application is entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the '566 application under 35 U.S.C. § 120. The Board’s determination rests on a fundamental misunderstanding of the continuity requirements for claiming priority through a chain of continuations-in-part and divisions, and it fails to apply the clear precedent governing incorporation by reference in such contexts. The Board’s conclusion that the '838 and '768 applications sufficiently incorporated the '579 application by reference is unsupported by the record and contrary to established law. We respectfully urge reversal.

**I. The Board Misapplied the Standard for Incorporation by Reference in a Continuation Chain**

Under 35 U.S.C. § 120, a continuation or divisional application may claim the benefit of an earlier-filed application only if the earlier application “discloses the subject matter claimed in the later application.” The disclosure must be “in the original disclosure” of the earlier application. *In re Rasmussen*, 650 F.3d 1361, 1365 (Fed. Cir. 2011). When an applicant seeks to incorporate material from a different application by reference, the incorporation must be clear and unambiguous at the time of filing. *In re NTP, Inc.*, 654 F.3d 1268, 1275 (Fed. Cir. 2011).

Here, the '880 application was filed on May 14, 1999, and claimed priority to the '566 application, filed April 13, 1989. The '880 application incorporated by reference the '566 application, which contained the following language:

> “Optimized erase implementations have been disclosed in two copending U.S. patent applications. They are copending U.S. patent applications, Serial No. 204,175, filed June 8, 1988, by Dr. Eliyahou Harari and one entitled ‘Multi-State EEprom Read and Write Circuits and Techniques,’ filed on the same day as the present application, by Sanjay Mehrotra and Dr. Eliyahou Harari. The disclosures of the two applications are hereby incorporated by reference.” J.A. 1204.

This language referred to the '579 application, which was filed on April 13, 1989—*not* on the same day as the '566 application. The Board, however, concluded that the '838 and '768 applications, which were divisionals of the '566 application, incorporated the '579 application by reference through the chain of incorporation. This conclusion is legally flawed.

The Federal Circuit has made clear that “an applicant cannot rely on a reference that is not properly incorporated into the original disclosure.” *In re Rasmussen*, 650 F.3d at 1365. Moreover, “a reference incorporated by reference must be identified with sufficient particularity so that a person of ordinary skill in the art could locate and understand the disclosed subject matter.” *In re NTP*, 654 F.3d at 1275. The '566 application’s incorporation statement is ambiguous: it refers to an application filed “on the same day as the present application,” which is false as to the '579 application. The '579 application was filed on April 13, 1989, while the '566 application was filed on the same day—April 13, 1989. But the '579 application was not filed on the same day as the '566 application; it *was* the same day. The statement is therefore factually incorrect and misleading.

The Board’s reliance on *Harari I*, 602 F.3d at 1352–54, is misplaced. In *Harari I*, we held that the “identity of the incorporated reference is clear to a reasonable examiner in light of the documents presented.” Id. at 1353. But that case involved a *revised* incorporation statement in a preliminary amendment, where the examiner had the benefit of the transmittal sheet and the amendment itself. Here, the Board is asked to determine whether the *original* disclosure of the '838 and '768 applications—both of which were filed before the '579 application was even cited—incorporated the '579 application. The mere fact that the '880 application later amended the incorporation statement does not retroactively validate the earlier applications’ disclosures.

**II. The Chain of Continuations Fails to Satisfy § 120’s Continuity Requirement**

Under 35 U.S.C. § 120, a continuation or divisional must “disclose the subject matter claimed in the later application.” The disclosure must be “in the original disclosure” of the earlier application. *In re Rasmussen*, 650 F.3d at 1365. The Federal Circuit has repeatedly held that “a continuation-in-part may not claim benefit of an earlier application unless the earlier application discloses the subject matter claimed in the later application.” *In re Brouwer*, 44 F.3d 1574, 1577 (Fed. Cir. 1995).

The '838 and '768 applications were divisionals of the '566 application, filed on December 20, 1996, and December 20, 1996, respectively. Neither application contained any reference to the '579 application. The '838 application incorporated the '566 application by reference, but the '566 application’s incorporation statement was factually incorrect and misleading. The '768 application similarly incorporated the '566 application, but again, no reference to the '579 application was made.
