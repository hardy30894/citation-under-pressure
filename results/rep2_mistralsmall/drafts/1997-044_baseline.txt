**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Bankruptcy Code Does Not Permit Debtors to Discharge Debts Incurred Through Fraud.**

The Bankruptcy Code explicitly excepts from discharge debts obtained by actual fraud. 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A). This provision reflects Congress’s intent to deny the "honest but unfortunate debtor" the benefit of discharge where the debtor’s misconduct undermines the integrity of the bankruptcy system. *Grogan v. Garner*, 498 U.S. 279, 286–87 (1991). The Third Circuit correctly applied this principle in *In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d 1108, 1118–19 (3d Cir. 1995), holding that a debtor’s reckless disregard for the truth may satisfy the scienter requirement under § 523(a)(2)(A). *See also In re Woolley*, 145 B.R. 830, 834 (Bankr. E.D. Va. 1991) (reckless disregard for truth suffices to establish fraud under § 523(a)(2)(A)).

Petitioner’s reliance on *In re Levy*, 951 F.2d 196 (9th Cir. 1991), is misplaced. The Ninth Circuit’s decision rested on a narrow construction of the phrase "to the extent obtained by" fraud, limiting dischargeability to compensatory damages. *Id.* at 198. This Court, however, has rejected such a restrictive reading. *See In re St. Laurent*, 991 F.2d 672, 678–79 (11th Cir. 1993) (holding that punitive damages awarded for fraud are nondischargeable because they arise from the fraudulent conduct itself). The Third Circuit should follow *St. Laurent* and affirm that debts incurred through fraud—including statutorily mandated treble damages—are nondischargeable.

**II. The New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act’s Treble Damages Are Nondischargeable Under § 523(a)(2)(A).**

The Bankruptcy Court correctly held that the treble damages awarded under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act, N.J.S.A. 56:8-19, are nondischargeable. *See In re Cohen*, 185 B.R. 180, 183 (Bankr. D.N.J. 1995). The Act’s treble damages are not punitive in the traditional sense but are instead a statutorily prescribed remedy for fraudulent conduct. *Molzof v. United States*, 502 U.S. 301, 305 (1992) (federal courts must interpret federal statutes without deference to state labels). Because the treble damages here flowed directly from Petitioner’s fraudulent misrepresentations, they are excepted from discharge under § 523(a)(2)(A). *See In re St. Laurent*, 991 F.2d at 678–79.

Petitioner’s argument that treble damages are dischargeable because they are "punitive" misconstrues the statute. The Third Circuit has recognized that debts obtained by fraud—regardless of their label—are nondischargeable. *In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d at 1118–19. The foreseeability of treble damages under New Jersey law only underscores that Petitioner’s fraudulent conduct gave rise to the debt. *Id.*

**III. Petitioner’s Fraudulent Conduct Satisfied the Elements of § 523(a)(2)(A).**

To except a debt from discharge under § 523(a)(2)(A), a creditor must prove: (1) the debtor obtained money or property through a material misrepresentation; (2) the debtor knew the representation was false or acted with reckless disregard for its truth; (3) the debtor intended to deceive; (4) the creditor reasonably relied on the misrepresentation; and (5) the creditor suffered damages as a result. *In re Poskanzer*, 143 B.R. 991, 999 (Bankr. D.N.J. 1992). The Bankruptcy Court’s findings that Petitioner committed actual fraud are not clearly erroneous. *See Universal Minerals, Inc. v. C.A. Hughes & Co.*, 669 F.2d 98, 102 (3d Cir. 1981) (factual findings reviewed for clear error).

Petitioner’s argument that he made no affirmative misrepresentation is unavailing. Silence coupled with charging unlawful rents constituted an implied misrepresentation that the rents were lawful. *See In re Woolley*, 145 B.R. at 834. Moreover, Petitioner’s reckless disregard for the truth—evidenced by his failure to consult the Rent Control Board or review the ordinance—supports the finding of actual fraud. *In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d at 1118–19. The Bankruptcy Court’s conclusion that Petitioner’s conduct satisfied § 523(a)(2)(A) is legally sound and should be affirmed.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment below should be affirmed. The debts arising from Petitioner’s fraud—including the treble damages awarded under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act—are nondischargeable under 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A).
