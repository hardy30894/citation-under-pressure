Louisiana, 379 U.S. 536 (1965), the Court held that the government may regulate speech on its property to prevent disruption or disturbance. Similarly, Milford's exclusion of the Good News Club is justified, as the Club's activities, including prayer and Bible instruction, are inconsistent with the school's limited public forum.

Here, Milford's denial of the Good News Club's request to use its facilities is justified, as the Club's activities are not consistent with the school's community use policy.

The Establishment Clause concerns that motivated Milford's exclusion of the Good News Club are legitimate. The Clause prohibits the government from promoting or endorsing religion, and Milford's exclusion of the Club is a reasonable measure to avoid violating this prohibition.
