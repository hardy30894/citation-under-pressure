The Railroad Retirement Act provides that “[a]ny claimant” may obtain judicial review in the courts of appeals of “any final decision of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Board’s denial of a request to reopen a prior benefits determination satisfies that statutory text. Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (holding that “the Railroad Retirement Act grants the Court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations”).

That conclusion is consistent with the ordinary meaning of finality. Once the Board refuses to reopen, “no further administrative proceedings are available,” and the agency’s action “marks the end of the administrative process.” Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Board, 370 F.2d 253, 254 (2d Cir. 1966). The same result follows when the Board declines to reopen on the ground that the four-year regulatory window has closed. Sones v. Railroad Retirement Board, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991).

Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), does not compel a different result. The Social Security Act there limited review to a “final decision of the Secretary made after a hearing.” Id. at 108. Section 355(f) contains no such limitation. The Board’s reopening regulations themselves treat a denial as the conclusive disposition of the request, thereby satisfying the finality requirement of § 355(f). See Stovic, 826 F.3d at 504.

The contrary view—that reopening decisions are categorically unreviewable—rests on an atextual importation of Social Security precedent into a distinct statutory scheme. Because the Board’s order here is final within the meaning of § 355(f), the court of appeals possessed jurisdiction to review it.
