The United States respectfully submits that the Court should reverse the district court's denial of its motion to dismiss Guam's complaint. The Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA) provides two causes of action for entities seeking recovery from potentially responsible parties (PRPs): a section 107 "cost-recovery" action and a section 113(f) "contribution" action. 42 U.S.C. §§ 9607, 9613(f). Guam's claim against the United States is time-barred under section 113(f) because the 2004 Consent Decree triggered Guam's right to pursue a contribution claim, precluding it from now pursuing a claim under section 107.

The Consent Decree "resolved [Guam's] liability to the United States . . . for some or all of a response action or for some or all of the costs of such action" within the meaning of section 113(f)(3)(B). See Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Aviall Services, Inc., 543 U.S. 157, 167 (2004). The Decree required Guam to take specific remedial actions, including closing the Ordot Dump and designing and installing a "dump cover system." Consent Decree 5-12, J.A. 94-101. This language demonstrates that the parties intended to resolve Guam's liability for the response action. See United States v. Atlantic Research Corp., 551 U.S. 128, 139 (2007) (noting that section 113(f)(3)(B) "permits a PRP to seek contribution from other liable parties" after resolving its liability through a settlement).

Guam's argument that the Consent Decree did not resolve its liability because it did not admit liability is unpersuasive. As the Sixth Circuit has held, "the mere fact that [a party] refused to admit liability is not enough to exempt [a consent] [d]ecree from the reach of section 113(f)(3)(B)." Refined Metals Corp. v. NL Industries, 937 F.3d 928, 931 (6th Cir. 2019); see also Asarco LLC v. Atlantic Richfield Co., 866 F.3d 1108, 1123 (9th Cir. 2017) (noting that "parties often expressly refuse to concede liability under a settlement agreement, even while assuming obligations consistent with a finding of liability").

Furthermore, the Consent Decree's substantive provisions demonstrate that Guam resolved its liability for the response action. See Florida Power Corp. v. FirstEnergy Corp., 810 F.3d 996, 1002 (6th Cir. 2015) (finding that a consent decree resolved the plaintiff's liability in part because it required the plaintiff to take specific remedial actions). The Decree's requirement that Guam complete construction of a cover system to eliminate discharges of untreated leachate is a "remedial action" under CERCLA. See CERCLA § 101(24).

Guam's constitutional argument is also without merit. Guam failed to raise this argument in the district court, and it is therefore forfeited. See Keepseagle v. Vilsack, 815 F.3d 28, 36 (D.C. Cir. 2016). Moreover, Guam's due process claim is based on the alleged deprivation of the property interests of absent third parties, which Guam lacks standing to assert.

The Court's decision in Burlington Northern & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. United States, 556 U.S. 599 (2009), supports the conclusion that Guam's claim is time-barred. In Burlington, the Court held that a PRP's failure to bring a contribution claim within the three-year statute of limitations period bars the claim. Id. at 613. Similarly, Guam's failure to bring a contribution claim within the three-year statute of limitations period bars its claim.

The decisions of other circuits also support the United States' position. See, e.g., Refined Metals Corp., 937 F.3d at 931; Asarco, 866 F.3d at 1123; Florida Power Corp., 810 F.3d at 1002. These cases demonstrate that the Consent Decree triggered Guam's right to pursue a contribution claim under section 113(f)(3)(B), and that Guam's claim is therefore time-barred.

In conclusion, the Court should reverse the district court's denial of the United States' motion to dismiss because Guam's claim is time-barred under section 113(f). The Consent Decree resolved Guam's liability for the response action, triggering the three-year statute of limitations period. Guam's failure to bring a contribution claim within that period bars its claim.
