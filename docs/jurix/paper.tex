\title{Citation under Pressure: What Deployment Constraints Do to Legal Citation Integrity, and What Deterministic Verification Can Repair}

\author{\fnms{Harsh} \snm{Dubey}%
\thanks{Corresponding Author: Harsh Dubey, New York University; hd2225@nyu.edu. Instruments, drafts, records, and ledger: \url{https://github.com/hardy30894/citation-under-pressure}.}}
\runningauthor{H. Dubey}
\address{New York University, New York, USA}

\begin{abstract}
Courts now sanction lawyers for filing citations a language model invented, and most research responds by detecting such errors in finished briefs. This paper measures how they are produced, and what a checker does in the loop instead. Seven models from seven vendors, open-weight 30B to frontier, drafted argument sections for 48 U.S. Supreme Court matters under five conditions: baseline, a citation quota, a restriction to pre-1970 authority, a sanctions warning, and all three combined. Every citation in the \nDrafts{} drafts was adjudicated deterministically, with no language-model judge: existence against an 18-million-row reporter index, quotations verbatim against opinion text with a checker validated on seeded faults and audited by hand, pinpoint pages against reporter pagination. The failure mode moves with capability. Under combined pressure the smallest model's citation existence fell from \existQwenBase{} to \existQwenCombo, quotation accuracy fell for \primQuoteFallModels{} of the seven, \bothTemplatesQuoteFallModels{} of them under both prompt templates, and accurate quotations per draft fell for \countModelsWithFall{} on the single template that outcome was run on, two at the top of the range; the strongest model's rate did not fall, most plausibly because it quoted less. Adjusting for the age of the cited opinion, which the date clause itself sets, removes about \yrShrinkTemporal{} percent of that clause's effect on the log-odds scale and leaves the rest, so it damages integrity partly by degrading quoting and partly by moving the model onto unfamiliar authority. Fed the checker's findings, models satisfy it by deletion: of Sonnet~5's \trFlagRZeroSonnet{} flagged quotations \trTrueFlagRepairedSonnet{} were corrected and \trTrueFlagDeletedSonnet{} deleted, and accurate quotations per draft rose for one model of seven. A flag carrying the cited passage raises repair, which then overtakes deletion in three of seven models. Holding revision rounds fixed, revision with no feedback interpolates to a nominal precision between \crossNomLow{} and \crossNomHigh, or \crossRealLow{} to \crossRealHigh{} realised, the point below which a verifier destroys more correct work than leaving the model to revise alone. Both experiments make one point: a rate computed over what a model chooses to expose to a check can rise while the correct output behind it does not, so an evaluation should report the count beside the rate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Since 2023, courts have repeatedly sanctioned lawyers who filed briefs with citations a language model invented, from \textit{Mata v.~Avianca}~\cite{mata2023} to a docket a public tracker now counts in the thousands~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}. Most published work frames the problem as detection: given a brief that already contains errors, how many can a model find~\cite{liu2026whochecks,chen2026legalcitebench}? That is the right measure for reviewing a filed brief, not for asking which prompting conditions produce the errors, this paper's question.

This paper asks two production-side questions. First, which prompting conditions cause citation failure, and for which models? Deployed prompts carry constraints of the kind Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} studied for scholarly citations, where a publication-year window lowered citation existence even in capable models; we add a sanctions warning and know of no comparable study for law. Second, when a deterministic checker feeds its findings back into revision, does the model correct them, leave them, or change the draft so that the tests pass without the quotations being corrected?

Both questions need an instrument the field has said does not exist in public: the highest-recall published detection agent misses nearly half of incorrect pinpoint citations (52.8 percent recall), which its authors attribute to page-level information living behind commercial databases~\cite{liu2026whochecks}. We built it from public sources: existence against a local index from CourtListener bulk data, verbatim quotation checking validated on seeded faults and audited on real drafts, and page-level pinpoint verification for quotations on U.S. Reports authority against the official pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's archives~\cite{cap2024}.

Our primary setting is the closed-book use of general-purpose models, the setting of most sanctioned filings to date~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}; a grounded arm with ten retrieved authorities in the prompt measures what retrieval changes. Drafts under the four non-baseline conditions are the pressured drafts (\nPressured{} here, three refusals). The paper contributes a constraint-sensitive evaluation, in which reliability at a neutral prompt does not predict reliability under drafting constraints; deterministic verification of existence, quotation, and pinpoint page with no language-model judge, released with the drafts; and the finding that verification is not repair, since flags fed back to the model raise measured accuracy by deletion and an imprecise verifier damages correct content.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Legal citation reliability.} Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} build a detection benchmark from real appellate briefs with injected errors and a taxonomy grounded in real court filings; we adopt their categories and measure the naturally occurring errors their limitations section asks for. LegalCiteBench~\cite{chen2026legalcitebench} shows that models asked closed-book to supply the authorities a proposition rests on almost never produce the right citation, the best under 7 of 100; we ask whether the cases a model chooses exist. Dahl et al.~\cite{dahl2024fictions} and Magesh et al.~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree} measure hallucinated authority in chatbots and research tools.

\textbf{Constraints and fabrication.} Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} is the closest design: five prompting regimes over scholarly claims, verified against two bibliographic services, which left 36 to 61 percent of citations unresolved. We transplant their year-window and breadth regimes to law against a full-text index that leaves \unverifiablePct{} percent unresolved, and add a statistical model.

\textbf{Revision with execution feedback.} The loop follows execution-feedback work in code generation~\cite{gehring2025rlef}, whose random-feedback ablation is the analogue of our false arm; we add a no-feedback arm separating content from extra revision, and mixed arms.

\textbf{AI and law.} Blair-Stanek and Van Durme~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable} found frontier models flipping the winning party across identical zero-temperature runs, which Section~\ref{sec:limits} bounds with repeated generations. Evaluation here pairs automated metrics with the checks lawyers perform~\cite{gultekin2025twodim,sanchi2024hybrid}; citation integrity is one with deterministic ground truth. Garneau et al.~\cite{garneau2024pincites} found retrieving the paragraph a proposition rests on harder than retrieving the case, in EU material. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025overruled} show models miss overruled precedent, which our date clause makes more likely and our checker does not test; ConsRAG~\cite{nguyen2024consrag} constrains retrieval, and Trozze et al.~\cite{trozze2025crypto} and Harasta et al.~\cite{harasta2026cannotberight} study how readers perceive model-written documents. Evaluations of generated legal argument~\cite{gray2025magnitudes,pires2025legalwriting} do not check the authority it cites, and citation-network work~\cite{khatri2023citecaselaw,sadeghian2018edgelabeling,dadgostari2021lawsearch,mumford2025citationnetworks} studies the network our checker queries.

\section{Tasks, Conditions, and Instruments}\label{sec:method}

\textbf{Terms.} In 347 U.S. 483, 495, the second page is the \textit{pincite}, for a quotation the page holding the quoted language. For a draft $d$ let $A_d$ be its \textit{adjudicable} citations, labelled exists or not found, and $S_d$ its \textit{scored} quotations, those it attributes to a case whose text the checker holds. The outcomes are
\begin{equation*}
E_d = \frac{|\{c \in A_d : \text{exists}\}|}{|A_d|},\quad
R_d = \frac{|\{q \in S_d : \text{accurate}\}|}{|S_d|},\quad
K_d = |\{q \in S_d : \text{accurate}\}|,
\end{equation*}
\textit{citation existence}, \textit{strict quotation accuracy}, and the \textit{count} of accurate quotations in the draft; the lenient rate $R^{\ell}_d$ counts near misses as accurate. A quotation the draft does not attribute is outside $S_d$ and so outside $R_d$.

\textbf{Matters.} The matters are forty-eight Supreme Court cases from 1990 to 2020 drawn from the Supreme Court Database~\cite{spaeth2025scdb}; each packet holds the question presented, party names, a factual summary, and a lower-court opinion excerpt, the Court's decision excluded. The model argues an assigned side in under 900 words, and no measurement here predicts a case outcome. A second task uses forty-eight published F.3d decisions from the Caselaw Access Project archive (1995 to 2019, four from each of the First through Eleventh and Federal Circuits, no cross-appeals); the packet is the opinion's statement of the case and facts, cut before the analysis and scrubbed of outcome sentences, argued for the appellant under all five conditions.

\textbf{Conditions.} The five conditions are baseline; quota (``cite at least eight distinct case authorities''); temporal (``every authority you cite MUST have been decided before 1970''), a clause no user writes, a checkable stand-in for the authority limits practitioners impose; unlike a jurisdiction limit, which pushes toward the circuit authority where the second task finds fabrication, it was expected to move citations toward well-memorized landmarks, which Section~\ref{sec:exp1} finds it does not; stakes (``this section will be filed with the court; citing authority that does not exist, or misquoting authority, risks sanctions under Rule~11''); and combined, which concatenates the three clauses, so a combined effect cannot be assigned to one of them. Compliance is partial and uneven, \violSonnetTemporal{} to \violMistralTemporal{} percent of checked citations violating it with no ordering against effect, so contrasts are intention-to-treat across models receiving different doses. Each condition has one fixed template, at temperature 0. The stakes clause names Rule~11, invoked in the sanctioned-filing cases~\cite{mata2023}, which governs district-court civil actions~\cite{frcp2024} and neither task's forum, so it is a stakes signal only. A second template rephrasing every condition was run on all 48 matters. A \textit{grounded} arm reran baseline and combined with the ten U.S. Reports opinions most similar to the question and facts in the prompt (TF-IDF over \retrievalCorpus{} cached opinions decided before argument and, for combined, before 1970, neither party named), each with citation, year, and its best-matching 250-word passage, quotation restricted to the excerpts.

{\sloppy\textbf{Models.} The models are Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mistral Small, Llama-4 Maverick, DeepSeek V4 Flash, Grok~4.3, GPT-5.4-mini, and Claude Sonnet~5, one per vendor, run through OpenRouter in August 2026 with completion limits of 1,400 to 12,000 tokens (identifiers in the repository). Tables are ordered by baseline strict quotation accuracy, and Sonnet~5, GPT-5.4-mini, and Grok~4.3, with the highest baseline existence, are the top of the range. An eighth model, GLM-4.7-flash, exhausted a 30,000-token budget in reasoning on \glmMissing{} of 240 drafts and is excluded.\par}

\textbf{Adjudication.} Full case citations are extracted with eyecite~\cite{cushman2021eyecite} (statutes and rules cite no case and are outside every rate) and labelled exists, not found, or unverifiable, the last for a vendor database number and for a reporter the index carries nothing from, chiefly agency reporters, so coverage is never counted as fabrication (\unverifiablePct{} percent of \nCitationRecords{} records). Citations resolve against an 18-million-row table; quotations are located by balanced quotation marks, which block quotations lack, though that is no route out of the scored set here (\blockLines{} block-quoted lines in \blockTotalDrafts{} drafts, none Sonnet~5's); they are attributed, in order, to the citation whose parenthetical holds them, the case named in their own sentence, the citation following them in that sentence (not one introduced by \textit{see} or \textit{cf.}), the citation an \textit{Id.} points to, or the last citation in the paragraph; one introduced by a statute, rule, the record, or a lower court, or with no citation in its paragraph, is left unpaired, the checker having nothing to test it against: \unpairedPctAll{} percent of the \quotesAll{} quotations in the full run: \unpairedNoncasePct{} percent introduced by non-case material, \unpairedNoCitePct{} percent in a paragraph that cites nothing, \unpairedFarPct{} percent too far from any citation. \textit{Exists} means the volume, reporter, and page resolve to an indexed opinion (\nResolving{} of the \nCitationRecords{} records; \nNotFoundAll{} are not found); for the \nmChecked{} whose draft names the parties the name is compared separately, and \nmMismatch{} (\nmPct{} percent) carry a name the index does not match, abbreviated names included; counting every mismatch as not found keeps the \nmSurvKept{} surviving existence contrasts, adds Mistral Small's temporal fall (corrected $p$=\nmGainMistralTemporalHolm), and removes Sonnet~5's uncorrected combined rise. A quotation $q$ is split into fragments $f_1, \dots, f_n$ at ellipses and bracketed alterations (a lawful substitution is read as an omission and the words around it must match), alteration parentheticals dropped, and compared with the normalized text $T$ of the case it is attributed to: \textit{accurate} when every $f_j$ is a substring of $T$, \textit{near miss} when $\min_j \mathrm{cov}(f_j, T) \ge 0.85$, for $\mathrm{cov}$ the share of a fragment's words of five or more letters occurring in $T$, and \textit{inaccurate} otherwise. Matching is case and punctuation insensitive, and a quotation that drops an internal citation without leaving an ellipsis, which the citation rules permit under a parenthetical, passes when the opinion holds the fragment's halves in order across a citation-shaped gap; the lenient rate is a diagnostic, since 0.85 coverage admits one wrong word in seven. The quotation checker scores \valQtwoPass{} of \valQtwoTotal{} on twenty synthetic drafts planting \valQtwoKinds{} item kinds, six attribution traps and two omissions without an ellipsis among them, and the existence and pinpoint checks \valPinsPass{} of \valPinsTotal{}. Pinpoint pages are verified against the official pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's HTML volumes, which mark every page break; a pincite naming a range or list is credited on any page it names, a quotation is located by its longest fragment, and only those whose fragment is somewhere in the cited case enter the page-level rate.

\textbf{Human reference.} The same instrument scored \humanExcerpts{} pre-ChatGPT appellate brief excerpts from Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} (every brief-derived excerpt carrying no injected error, which is \humanExcerpts{} of their 1,000, their model-written subset excluded; OCR-converted, so a floor); human lawyers reach \humanStrict{} strict and \humanLenient{} lenient quotation accuracy (\humanScored{} scored) and \humanExist{} existence; crediting the \humanFailNearUnaltered{} failures that are near misses without any alteration, the signature of a corrupted character, lifts the floor to \humanFloorCorrected. It is a severity reference only, since human briefs differ in citation mix and the remaining failures are unaudited. The literal matcher the strict standard replaced, which read a bracketed alteration as its contents, puts the human rate at \humanAltStrict{} and moves model cells by at most \altMaxCellDiff{} points, the same \altSurv{} contrasts surviving.

\textbf{Inference.} The primary analysis is a citation-level logistic generalized estimating equation per model, $\text{logit}\,P(y_{imk}=1)=\beta_0+\beta_k$ for the existence or accuracy indicator $y_{imk}$ of item $i$ in matter $m$ under condition $k$, exchangeable within matters~\cite{liang1986gee}, so the 48 matters are the independent units. Each model has \nHolmTests{} contrasts (four conditions, two outcomes); we report Holm-corrected $p$ values within model, the family the paper draws conclusions about, and call an effect significant only when it survives; counts of models are descriptive, and the effective sample is 48 matters. Every other fit is exploratory and follows the same model and the same rule, Holm within model over that fit's own tests: the lenient outcome, distinct authorities, the second template, and pooled templates, tasks, and generations (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), each pooling with its fixed effect; the released matrix gives every contrast under every fit. For the revision experiment, pre-registered descriptively, we add matter-paired comparisons between arms (Wilcoxon, bootstrap interval). Hypotheses and decision rules were frozen before the full run; every later decision, including two withdrawn findings, is in a dated ledger.

\textbf{The strongest model does not degrade; the leading explanation is that it quotes less.} Sonnet~5's strict rate is numerically highest under the combined condition (\strictSonnetBase{} to \strictSonnetCombo; uncorrected $p=\pQuoteSonnetCombo$, corrected $p=\holmQuoteSonnetCombo$), a rise that survives no fit. \section{Experiment 2: The Checker inside the Revision Loop}\label{sec:exp2}

Combined-condition drafts entered a revision loop: each round the checker returned one line per failure, the model revised, and the loop ended on a clean draft or after three revisions (a round is a draft the checker read, so an episode reaching the cap has four). A \textit{false feedback} control gave the same number of identical lines aimed at correct items, \textit{mixed} controls replaced each line with a false one at random so that the feedback's nominal precision $\pi$, the share of its lines drawn from the checker, was 0.25, 0.5, or 0.75 ($\pi = 1$ under true and $\pi = 0$ under false feedback), replacing rather than adding lines, so recall falls with precision and the arms vary both. A \textit{no-feedback} control gave the same rounds with a generic instruction to revise. The loop used the first 24 matters; rates are means over drafts with at least one scored quotation (\lcEmptyTrueTotal{} of the 168 true-arm episodes ended with none, two without passing the checker, \lcEmptyTrueGrok{} of Grok~4.3's 24, so its final rate rests on \lcGrokTrueDrafts{} drafts and Llama-4's on \lcAliveLlamaTrue). The loop was rerun after the attribution rule was corrected; the original run, whose true-arm flags had realized precision \rpTrueAll{} under the corrected rule (\rpTrueSonnet{} for Sonnet~5), is released beside it. The rerun's flags are the corrected rule's verdicts, whose precision on inaccurate verdicts is \auditRulePrecisionPct{} percent by the hand reading of Section~\ref{sec:residual}; Figure~\ref{fig:repair}'s $\pi$ is therefore nominal, its arm at $\pi = 1$ sitting nearer 0.8. \section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Where the checker belongs.} Two recommendations follow, both inferences since no arm had a person in it. The first is the checker at the gate over a loop resolving bare flags: removing an unverifiable quotation answers a flag defensibly, but false flags get the same answer and the tool still reports a clean draft.

\textbf{Verifier precision is a requirement.} Our checker's error is \auditSampleCheckerSidePct{} percent by the hand reading, and published detectors span this axis: on one benchmark the highest-recall agent reaches 84.4 percent recall at 40.8 percent precision, the highest-precision one 76.1 percent at 62.8 percent recall~\cite{liu2026whochecks}, the first below the crossing above and the second above it. A pipeline feeding a detector's flags to a model should report precision beside recall. The second is to report the strict standard as defined here rather than the 0.85-coverage rate, which admits one wrong word in seven.

\textbf{What evaluations should include.} The observed rate is $R_d = K_d / |S_d|$, and a model can raise it by shrinking what it exposes, $|S_d|$, as well as by raising correct output $K_d$: under pressure the strongest model quoted less, under feedback models deleted what was flagged, $|S_d|$ down and $K_d$ flat or lower in both. An evaluation scoring only what a system emits should report $K_d$ beside $R_d$.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\enlargethispage{\baselineskip}%
Both tasks are United States federal case law and the stakes clause names a rule governing neither forum; the checks stop at existence, wording, and page: three model panels for relevance, calibrated against Liu et al.'s content-misrepresentation labels~\cite{liu2026whochecks}, fell short of a \juryBar{} bar we set in advance from reported judge agreement~\cite{zheng2023judging}, so whether a quotation supports its proposition, or the case is good law, is untested. All of it after the primary fit is exploratory. The readings behind the checker's error are a single unblinded pass without a second rater, cover inaccurate verdicts only, and predate two corrections that remove only checker-side verdicts, so \auditRulePrecisionPct{} percent understates it. The between-template spread (\phrMaxStrictDiff{} points) exceeds many single-run effects, the models are one per vendor, and capability is indexed by baseline existence, which orders them differently from other axes.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The failure mode moves with capability: the 30B model loses existence, the middle loses quotation accuracy and correct quotations per draft, and the strongest model's rate holds only because it quotes less. Acting on its flags, a model satisfies the checker by removal.
