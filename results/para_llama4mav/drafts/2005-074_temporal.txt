The reinstatement statute, Section 241(a)(5) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA), does not apply to aliens who re-entered the United States before its effective date. Even if it did, its application to Fernandez-Vargas would be impermissibly retroactive.

First, the statute's language and legislative history do not indicate that it was meant to apply retroactively to pre-enactment re-entries. In United States v. St. Louis, S.F. & T. Ry. Co., 270 U.S. 1 (1926), the Supreme Court held that a statute should not be given retroactive effect unless Congress clearly intended it. The Court stated that "the presumption is very strong that a statute was not meant to act retrospectively, and it ought not to receive such a construction unless the words used are so clear, strong, and imperative, that no other meaning can be annexed to them." Id. at 3. Here, the statute's language does not clearly indicate that it applies to pre-enactment re-entries.

Second, even if the statute were deemed to apply to pre-enactment re-entries, its application to Fernandez-Vargas would be impermissibly retroactive. In Davis v. Mills, 194 U.S. 451 (1904), the Supreme Court held that a statute that alters the legal consequences of past actions is retroactive. The Court stated that "the general rule is that no statute, however positive in its terms, is to be construed as designed to interfere with existing contracts, rights of action, or with vested rights, unless the intention that it shall so operate is expressly declared or is to be necessarily implied from the language used." Id. at 457. Fernandez-Vargas re-entered the United States in 1982 and lived here until 2001, when he applied to adjust his status. Applying the reinstatement statute to him would alter the legal consequences of his past actions and deny him the opportunity to adjust his status, which he had been pursuing in good faith.

The application of the reinstatement statute to Fernandez-Vargas would thus be impermissibly retroactive, as it would attach new legal consequences to his pre-enactment re-entry. The Court should therefore hold that the statute does not apply to aliens who re-entered the United States before its effective date, or that its application to Fernandez-Vargas is impermissibly retroactive.
