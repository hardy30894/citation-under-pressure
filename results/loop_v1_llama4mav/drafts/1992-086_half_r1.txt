The Center Moriches Union Free School District's denial of Lamb's Chapel's request to use school facilities for a religious-oriented film series constitutes a content-based exclusion of speech in a limited public forum, violating the First Amendment's freedom of speech. The District's facilities, although not traditional public forums, were opened for use by the general public for certain purposes, thereby creating a limited public forum.

As established in Hague v. CIO, 307 U.S. 496, 515 (1939), the government's power to regulate expression in a public forum is limited. The exclusion of speech from a public forum based on content is permissible only if it serves a compelling state interest and is narrowly drawn to achieve that interest, as required by Cantwell v. Connecticut, 310 U.S. 296, 304 (1940), and Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569, 574 (1941). The latter case illustrates that content-based exclusions must be scrutinized closely.

The District's exclusion of Lamb's Chapel's film series, which was described as "Family oriented movie from a Christian perspective," was based on the religious content of the speech. The District's policy, as stated in Local Rule No. 7, prohibited the use of school premises "by any group for religious purposes." This rule, as applied to Lamb's Chapel, constitutes a content-based exclusion.

The Supreme Court has held that a state may not exclude speech from a public forum based on its content unless the exclusion is necessary to serve a compelling state interest. In Lovell v. Furthermore, the District's policy and practice of excluding religious groups from using school facilities while allowing other groups to use the facilities for various purposes is not viewpoint-neutral. As the Court held in Kunz v. New York, 340 U.S. 290, 294 (1951), a prior restraint on speech is not justified simply because there have been previous instances of disorder or unrest. The District's exclusion of Lamb's Chapel's film series is not justified by a history of prior religious uses, as there is no evidence that the District had previously allowed such uses.
