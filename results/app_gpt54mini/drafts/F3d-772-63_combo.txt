**ARGUMENT**

The district court erred in dismissing Foley’s complaint. At the pleading stage, Foley needed only to allege facts that, accepted as true, plausibly entitle him to relief. *Conley v. Gibson*, 355 U.S. 41, 45-46 (1957); *Dioguardi v. Durning*, 139 F.2d 774, 775 (2d Cir. 1944). Dismissal is improper unless “it appears beyond doubt” that no set of facts could support recovery. *Conley*, 355 U.S. at 45-46. That standard is especially exacting where, as here, the complaint alleges that Wells Fargo undertook contractual and statutory obligations to consider Foley for modification before foreclosure and then ignored them.

I. **Foley plausibly stated claims based on Wells Fargo’s assumed duties and promises.**

A complaint may state a claim where the defendant has undertaken a duty and then performed it negligently or not at all. *Glanzer v. Shepard*, 233 N.Y. 236, 135 N.E. 275, 276-77 (1922) (Cardozo, J.) (liability arises where one “assumes to act” and must exercise reasonable care); *Cleveland v. City of Bangor*, 87 Me. 259, 32 A. 892, 893 (1895). Foley alleged that Wells Fargo was required by the class settlement to consider him first for HAMP and then for MAP2R, and to provide specific servicing communications. Those allegations describe more than a gratuitous promise; they describe an undertaking that Wells Fargo was bound to perform with due care. *Glanzer*, 135 N.E. at 276-77.

Foley also alleged reliance. He repeatedly sought modification, submitted paperwork when finally sent an application, and delayed other options in the meantime. Reliance supports enforcement of a promise or undertaking where injustice would otherwise result. *Ricketts v. Scothorn*, 57 Neb. 51, 77 N.W. 365, 366-67 (1898); *Allegheny College v. National Chautauqua County Bank of Jamestown*, 246 N.Y. 369, 159 N.E. 173, 175 (1927). Wells Fargo’s conduct—promising an application, then failing to respond while foreclosure advanced—fits squarely within that principle.

II. **Foley’s complaint also alleges conduct that states a claim for misrepresentation and deceit.**

A plaintiff states a claim for deceit by alleging a false statement of material fact, knowledge of falsity, intent that the statement be acted upon, justifiable reliance, and damage. *Pasley v. Freeman*, 3 T.R. 51, 100 Eng. Rep. 450 (K.B. 1789); *Derry v. Peek*, 14 App. Cas. 337, 374-75 (H.L. 1889). Foley alleged that Wells Fargo told him he might qualify for HAMP, promised to send the application, then later claimed not to have received it despite the long delay in providing it, and diverted him to foreclosure alternatives while representing that modification review was ongoing. Those allegations, taken together, plausibly plead a false and misleading course of conduct. *Derry*, 14 App. Cas. at 374-75.

The law does not permit a party to use representations about performance while secretly intending nonperformance. *Stewart v. Emerson*, 52 N.H. 301, 315 (1872); *Paddock v. Stout*, 121 Ill. 338, 13 N.E. 512, 515 (1887). Foley’s allegations support the inference that Wells Fargo’s communications were not merely mistaken but part of a pattern of delay and obstruction incompatible with its obligations under the settlement.

III. **The settlement agreement may be enforced as a contract benefiting Foley.**

Contract rights may be enforced by intended third-party beneficiaries. *Lawrence v. Fox*, 20 N.Y. 268, 272-73 (1859); *Vrooman v. Turner*, 69 N.Y. 280, 284-85 (1877). Foley alleged that the class settlement specifically required Wells Fargo to consider Settlement Class B members for HAMP and MAP2R before foreclosure and to provide notice and explanations to protect that process. Those provisions were plainly intended to benefit class members like Foley, not merely to regulate internal bank procedures. Under *Lawrence* and *Vrooman*, Foley may sue to enforce those obligations.

IV. **Any preemption or exclusivity argument fails on this record.**

Wells Fargo’s contention that federal mortgage law displaces Foley’s state-law claims is unsound. State law is not displaced absent a clear repugnancy or an express congressional command. *Chicago, Milwaukee & St. Paul Ry. Co. v. Solan*, 169 U.S. 133, 137-39 (1898); *People v. Appleby*, 110 N.Y. 200, 18 N.E. 443, 444-45 (1888). Foley’s claims seek enforcement of promises, duties, and settlement obligations; they do not conflict with federal law. At most, they supplement it.

Finally, Wells Fargo’s attempt to uphold dismissal by recasting the complaint under a heightened plausibility screen is inconsistent with the traditional rule that pleadings serve notice, not proof. *Glenn v. Sumner*, 132 U.S. 152, 154-55 (1889); *Conley*, 355 U.S. at 47. Foley alleged a concrete sequence of promises, delay, reliance, and foreclosure. That is enough to require an answer and discovery, not dismissal. The judgment should be reversed.
