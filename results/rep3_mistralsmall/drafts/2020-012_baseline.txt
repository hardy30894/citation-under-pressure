**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s Decision Denying Reopening Is a Final, Reviewable Agency Action

The Railroad Retirement Board’s denial of Petitioner Manfredo M. Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 disability application constitutes a “final decision” subject to judicial review under the Administrative Procedure Act (“APA”), 5 U.S.C. § 704, and the Railroad Retirement Act (“RRA”), 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), is inconsistent with Supreme Court precedent and misapplies the finality doctrine.

The Supreme Court has long held that an agency’s refusal to reopen a prior proceeding is reviewable if it “imposes an obligation, denies a right, or fixes some legal relationship.” *FCC v. Schreiber*, 382 U.S. 279, 289 (1965). In *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), the Court clarified that while reopening decisions are generally not reviewable, they become reviewable when they “adversely affect[] a claimant’s right to benefits.” *Id.* at 107. The Board’s denial of Salinas’s reopening request plainly falls within this exception because it foreclosed his ability to challenge the 2006 denial—a denial that, if reopened, could entitle him to retroactive benefits. *See Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians*, 476 U.S. 667, 673 (1986) (holding that a decision “affecting the validity of a claim to benefits” is reviewable).

The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Roberts* is misplaced. *Roberts* held that § 355(f) limits review to “decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits after administrative appeals have been exhausted.” 346 F.3d at 140. But the Court in *Sanders* expressly rejected the argument that reopening decisions are categorically unreviewable, emphasizing that such a rule would “frustrate[] the basic purpose of the APA.” 430 U.S. at 108. The Fifth Circuit’s categorical rule conflicts with *Sanders* and the APA’s broad grant of review to “final agency action.” 5 U.S.C. § 704.

II. The Board’s Decision Is Final Under the APA

The APA defines “final agency action” as one that is “final” within the meaning of the APA and “[a]gency action that is not final is not reviewable.” 5 U.S.C. § 704. The Supreme Court has interpreted this provision to require that an agency action “mark the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process” and “determine rights or obligations.” *Bennett v. Spear*, 520 U.S. 154, 177–78 (1997) (quoting *Chicago & S. Air Lines v. Waterman S.S. Corp.*, 333 U.S. 103, 113 (1948)).

Here, the Board’s denial of reopening was the consummation of its decisionmaking process. The Board conducted a hearing, applied its regulations, and issued a written decision—actions that conclusively resolved Salinas’s reopening request. *See id.* at 178 (holding that an agency action is final when it “determine[s] rights or obligations” and “leav[es] no further steps for the agency to take”). The Board’s decision also determined Salinas’s rights by foreclosing his ability to challenge the 2006 denial, which could have entitled him to retroactive benefits. *See Bowen*, 476 U.S. at 673.

III. The Fifth Circuit’s Rule of Orderliness Should Not Bar Review

The Fifth Circuit’s rule in *Roberts* is an unwarranted expansion of the “rule of orderliness,” which prevents panels from overruling prior circuit precedent. *See Hutto v. City of Dallas*, 735 F.3d 547, 552 (5th Cir. 2013). The rule of orderliness does not apply when prior precedent conflicts with Supreme Court authority. *See Agostini v. Felton*, 521 U.S. 203, 237 (1997) (holding that lower courts are bound by Supreme Court precedent, not circuit precedent). Here, *Roberts* conflicts with *Sanders* and the APA, and thus the rule of orderliness does not bar review.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit’s dismissal and remand for review of the Board’s denial of Salinas’s reopening request.
