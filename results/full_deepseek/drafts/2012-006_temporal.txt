**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Government Bears the Burden of Proving Beyond a Reasonable Doubt That the Conspiracy Continued Into the Statute of Limitations Period.**

Withdrawal from a conspiracy is an affirmative defense. *Hyde v. United States*, 225 U.S. 347, 369 (1912). Once a defendant produces evidence of withdrawal, the burden shifts to the government to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant remained a member of the conspiracy during the limitations period. *United States v. Hyde*, 7 F.2d 188, 190 (7th Cir. 1925). This allocation of burden is rooted in the principle that the government must prove every element of the offense, including the temporal scope of the conspiracy. *See United States v. Cohen*, 145 F.2d 82, 89 (2d Cir. 1944).

The statute of limitations is a substantive element of the conspiracy charge. *United States v. Kissel*, 218 U.S. 601, 607 (1910). The government must prove that the conspiracy existed within the limitations period. *United States v. Manton*, 107 F.2d 834, 839 (2d Cir. 1938). When a defendant presents evidence of withdrawal, the government’s burden includes proving that the conspiracy continued after the withdrawal. *United States v. Bazzell*, 187 F.2d 878, 882 (7th Cir. 1951).

**II. The Lower Court Erred by Placing the Burden on Petitioners to Prove Withdrawal.**

The lower court held that petitioners bore the burden of proving withdrawal. This contravenes settled law. *Hyde v. United States*, 225 U.S. at 369. The burden of persuasion on withdrawal rests with the government once the defendant produces some evidence. *United States v. Bazzell*, 187 F.2d at 882. The lower court’s ruling improperly shifted this burden to petitioners.

**III. The Evidence Was Insufficient to Prove Petitioners’ Membership in the Conspiracy During the Limitations Period.**

The leaders of the conspiracy, Moore and Gray, split up before the limitations period. This dissolution ended the conspiracy as a matter of law. *United States v. Kissel*, 218 U.S. at 608. Without evidence that the conspiracy continued, the jury could not find petitioners guilty. *United States v. Manton*, 107 F.2d at 839. The government failed to meet its burden.

**CONCLUSION**

The judgment should be reversed.
