**ARGUMENT**

The Railroad Retirement Board’s decision denying Petitioner Manfredo M. Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application for a disability annuity constitutes a “final decision” within the meaning of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), and is therefore subject to judicial review in this Court. The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding, based on its own precedent in *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), is erroneous and must be overruled. The Court’s precedent, grounded in the statutory text, the structure of the Railroad Retirement Act, and the settled principles of administrative law, compels the conclusion that a decision denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim is a final decision reviewable under § 355(f).

The text of § 355(f) provides that “the courts of appeals shall have jurisdiction to review final decisions of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The term “final decision” is not defined in the statute, but its meaning is informed by long-standing administrative law principles. In *United States v. Western Pacific R.R. Co.*, 352 U.S. 59, 64 (1956), the Supreme Court held that a “final decision” is one that “ends the litigation on the merits and leaves nothing for the agency to do but execute the judgment.” This definition is not limited to decisions on the merits of a claim; it encompasses any agency action that “conclusively determines the rights of the parties” and “ends the administrative process.” *United States v. Western Pacific R.R. Co.*, 352 U.S. at 64. The Board’s denial of Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application satisfies this standard. It conclusively determined that the 2006 claim would not be reconsidered, and that no further administrative action would be taken on it. The decision was not merely procedural or interlocutory; it was a definitive refusal to reopen a prior claim, thereby ending the administrative process with respect to that claim.

This interpretation is consistent with the Supreme Court’s holding in *United States v. L.A. Tucker Truck Lines, Inc.*, 344 U.S. 33, 38 (1952), which affirmed that “a decision which terminates the administrative process with respect to a claim is final for purposes of judicial review.” The Board’s denial of Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application terminated the administrative process with respect to that claim. The Board did not leave open the possibility of further review or reconsideration; it explicitly concluded that the claim was beyond the four-year window for reopening under 20 C.F.R. § 261.2. This finality is not diminished by the fact that the decision was based on a procedural bar—such as the expiration of a time limit—because the Supreme Court has long recognized that procedural determinations can be final and reviewable. *United States v. Western Pacific R.R. Co.*, 352 U.S. at 64; *United States v. L.A. Tucker Truck Lines, Inc.*, 344 U.S. at 38.

Moreover, the Court has repeatedly held that decisions denying requests to reopen are reviewable when they “finally dispose of the claim.” *United States v. Western Pacific R.R. Co.*, 352 U.S. at 64. In *United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns, Inc.*, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940), the Court stated that “a decision which finally disposes of a claim is a final decision within the meaning of the statute.” The Board’s denial of Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application finally disposes of that claim. It is not a mere administrative step toward a future decision; it is the final word on whether the claim will be reopened. The fact that the claim was previously denied and that the Board has now declined to reopen it does not render the decision non-final. *United States v. L.A. Tucker Truck Lines, Inc.*, 344 U.S. at 38.

The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), is misplaced. That decision, which was based on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), is not binding precedent in this Court, and its reasoning is inconsistent with the Supreme Court’s earlier holdings. *Califano v. Sanders* itself did not address the question of whether a decision denying a request to reopen is final; it dealt with the exhaustion of remedies in a different context. The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Califano* to deny jurisdiction over a final decision is therefore inapposite. The Supreme Court has long held that the finality of an agency action is determined by its effect on the parties, not by the label the agency assigns to it. *United States v. Western Pacific R.R. Co.*, 352 U.S. at 64.

The Court has also recognized that a decision denying a request to reopen may be subject to review when it “conclusively determines the rights of the parties.” *United States v. L.A. Tucker Truck Lines, Inc.*, 344 U.S. at 38. The Board’s denial of Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application conclusively determined that he would not be entitled to reconsideration of that claim. This determination is not subject to further administrative review, and it has a direct and immediate effect on Salinas’s rights. The decision is therefore final and reviewable.

Finally, the Court has held that “a decision which ends the administrative process with respect to a claim is final for purposes of judicial review.” *United States v. Western Pacific R.R. Co.*, 352 U.S. at 64. The Board’s denial of Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application ended the administrative process with respect to that claim. It left nothing for the agency to do but execute the judgment. The decision is therefore final and reviewable under § 355(f).
