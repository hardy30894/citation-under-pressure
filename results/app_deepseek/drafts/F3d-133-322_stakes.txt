**ARGUMENT**

**I. The indictment was deficient because Mrs. Parker lacked authority to perform the acts alleged, and the jury instructions failed to require a finding that she received a bribe for an “official act” within the meaning of 18 U.S.C. § 201.**

Mrs. Parker’s conviction for public bribery under 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C) must be reversed because the indictment and the government’s theory of the case impermissibly extended the statute to conduct that did not constitute an “official act” as defined by law. Section 201(b)(2)(C) prohibits a public official from being induced to do or omit any act “in violation of that official’s official duty.” An “official act” is defined as “any decision or action on any question, matter, cause, suit, proceeding or controversy, which may at any time be pending, or which may by law be brought before any public official, in such official’s official capacity.” 18 U.S.C. § 201(a)(3).

Mrs. Parker was a clerk. Her duties were limited to assisting Administrative Law Judge Aronson, taking notes at hearings, typing and mailing decisions, and accessing the office computer system. She had no authority to approve or deny SSI benefits. She was not authorized to sign ALJ Aronson’s name. She could not grant or deny benefits as a matter of law, practice, or “established usage.” *United States v. Birdsall*, 233 U.S. 223, 231 (1914) (holding that duties not defined by written rules must be “established by settled practice”). The government’s theory—that Mrs. Parker violated her official duty by doing what she lacked authority to do—is circular. An act cannot violate a duty that does not exist. *See United States v. Gjieli*, 717 F.2d 968, 973 (6th Cir. 1983) (noting that an “official act” requires “some nexus” to the official’s authority).

The government urged the jury to convict Mrs. Parker based on her unauthorized forgery and false benefit letters. But the statute requires that the act be one the official is *duty-bound* to perform or refrain from. *Cf. United States v. Sun-Diamond Growers of Cal.*, 526 U.S. 398, 406 (1999) (emphasizing that bribery statutes target “a specific quid pro quo” tied to official action). Mrs. Parker had no duty to approve benefits; she had a duty *not* to forge signatures. The government effectively prosecuted her for an act she was *prohibited* from doing, not an act she was *required* to perform. That is not a violation of section 201(b)(2)(C). *Accord United States v. Harlan*, 35 F.3d 176, 179 (5th Cir. 1994) (holding that a defendant cannot be convicted of bribery for acts outside the scope of official authority). The district court erred in denying the motion to dismiss the indictment.

**II. The evidence was insufficient to convict Ralph Parker for conspiracy or for the substantive bribery counts.**

Mr. Parker’s convictions must be reversed because the government failed to prove that he shared the specific intent to corruptly influence an official act or that he acted willfully. *See United States v. Hooten*, 942 F.2d 878, 882 (5th Cir. 1991) (requiring proof that the defendant “knowingly and intentionally joined the conspiracy”). The record shows only that Mr. Parker accompanied his wife to Simmons’ home and accepted $500 from her. No evidence establishes that Mr. Parker knew his wife was a public official, that the payment was for a past or future official act, or that he intended to facilitate bribery. *See United States v. Montague*, 958 F.2d 109, 112 (5th Cir. 1992) (holding that mere presence at the scene of a crime is insufficient for conspiracy). The district court should have granted Mr. Parker’s motion for judgment of acquittal.

**III. The district court erred in instructing the jury on the definition of “official act.”**

The jury instructions failed to define “official act” with the precision required by statute and precedent. By charging that any action related to Mrs. Parker’s employment could constitute an official act, the court allowed conviction for conduct—like typing or mailing letters—that had no connection to a “decision or action on any question, matter, cause, suit, proceeding or controversy.” 18 U.S.C. § 201(a)(3). *See United States v. Bhagat*, 436 F.3d 620, 627 (5th Cir. 2006) (requiring that the alleged act “be part of the official’s duties”). This error was not harmless.

**CONCLUSION**

For these reasons, Mrs. Parker’s convictions and sentence should be reversed and her indictment dismissed; Mr. Parker’s convictions should be reversed and his indictment dismissed.
