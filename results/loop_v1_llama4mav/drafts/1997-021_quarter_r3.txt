The National Labor Relations Board's (NLRB) "good-faith reasonable doubt" standard for polling employees on union support is not rational and consistent with the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). Gissel Packing Co., 395 F.2d 389 (4th Cir. 1968)).

In Celanese Corp. of America, 95 N.L.R.B. 664 (1951), the NLRB itself recognized that an employer may have a good-faith doubt about a union's majority status, even if the doubt is not objectively reasonable. The NLRB's standard is overly restrictive and fails to account for the employer's legitimate interest in ascertaining employee sentiment.

The NLRB's determination that Allentown Mack lacked a good-faith reasonable doubt about the union's majority status is not supported by substantial evidence in the record.

Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp., 301 U.S. 1 (1937), the Court emphasized the importance of the NLRB's findings being supported by substantial evidence.

Furthermore, the NLRB's standard is inconsistent with the NLRA's goal of promoting stable collective-bargaining relationships (Labor Board v. Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp., 301 U.S. 1 (1937)). By allowing employers to conduct polls only when they have objective evidence of a loss of majority support, the NLRB's standard may actually destabilize collective-bargaining relationships.

In conclusion, the NLRB's "good-faith reasonable doubt" standard is not rational and consistent with the NLRA, and the NLRB's factual determination that Allentown Mack lacked such doubt is not supported by substantial evidence in the record.
