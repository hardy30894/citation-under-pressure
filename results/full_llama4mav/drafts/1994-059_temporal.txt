ARGUMENT

I. THE ARKANSAS SUPREME COURT CORRECTLY HELD THAT SECTION 3 OF AMENDMENT 73 IS UNCONSTITUTIONAL BECAUSE IT IMPOSES ADDITIONAL QUALIFICATIONS FOR MEMBERS OF CONGRESS BEYOND THOSE ENUMERATED IN THE U.S. CONSTITUTION.

The U.S. Constitution sets forth specific qualifications for members of Congress in Article I, Sections 2 and 3. The Constitution does not grant states the power to add to or alter these qualifications. In Powell v. McCormack, 395 U.S. 486 (1969), the Supreme Court held that Congress may not impose additional qualifications for membership beyond those specified in the Constitution. The Court stated that the Constitution is "the sole source of the qualifications for membership in Congress." Id. at 550. Similarly, in U.S. v. Ballin, 144 U.S. 1 (1892), the Court held that the Constitution "deals with the members of Congress as representatives of the people, and not as representatives of particular states or districts." Id. at 7.

Section 3 of Amendment 73 imposes term limits on members of Congress from Arkansas, effectively adding a qualification not enumerated in the Constitution. This provision is therefore unconstitutional.

II. THE ARKANSAS SUPREME COURT CORRECTLY HELD THAT THE UNCONSTITUTIONALITY OF SECTION 3 RENDERS THE ENTIRE AMENDMENT INVALID.

The severability clause in Amendment 73 is insufficient to save the remaining provisions because the unconstitutional provision is inextricably linked to the rest of the amendment. In Hoban v. Hall, 229 Ark. 416, 316 S.W.2d 185 (1958), this Court held that a severability clause is not effective when the provisions are so intertwined that it is impossible to determine whether the voters would have approved the remaining provisions without the unconstitutional portion. The ballot title and the Preamble to Amendment 73 lumped state and federal officials together, making it clear that the voters were presented with an "all or nothing" package.

The practice of coupling a legitimate objective with one of doubtful legality, as was done in Amendment 73, is not fair to voters and should be discouraged. As this Court stated in Hoban, "the voter is not given a fair opportunity to approve or reject one proposal without being required to approve or reject the other." Id. at 418.

Because Section 3 is unconstitutional and cannot be severed from the rest of the amendment, the entire Amendment 73 is invalid.

III. THE CIRCUIT COURT HAD JURISDICTION TO HEAR THE CHALLENGE TO AMENDMENT 73.

The circuit court correctly determined that it had jurisdiction to hear the challenge to Amendment 73. The appellees' complaint raised a justiciable controversy regarding the constitutionality of the amendment, and the court had the authority to declare the rights of the parties. Andres v. First Ark. Development Finance Corp., 230 Ark. 594, 324 S.W.2d 97 (1959).

The judgment of the Arkansas Supreme Court should be affirmed.
