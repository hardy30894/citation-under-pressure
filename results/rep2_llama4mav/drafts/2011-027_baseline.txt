The Montana Supreme Court correctly held that the trial court properly applied the constitutional test for determining whether a section of a river is navigable for title purposes. The equal footing doctrine, which grants states title to the beds of navigable rivers upon admission to the Union, is a matter of federal law. Under this doctrine, a river is considered navigable if it was navigable at the time of statehood. United States v. Utah, 283 U.S. 64, 75 (1931). The trial court's determination that the Missouri, Madison, and Clark Fork rivers were navigable at the time of Montana's statehood in 1889 was therefore proper.

PPL argues that the trial court erred in relying on historical evidence to determine navigability, claiming that such evidence is hearsay and lacks credibility. However, as the Montana Supreme Court noted, courts applying the navigability for title test routinely rely on historical evidence. See, e.g., Utah, 283 U.S. at 77-82 (relying on historical accounts and reports to determine navigability). Moreover, the State's expert, Foley, provided authenticated documents from the Montana Historical Society, which are self-authenticating under M. R. Evid. 902. The trial court properly considered this evidence, as it is admissible under various exceptions to the hearsay rule, including the public records and reports exception, M. R. Evid. 803(8). See Mont. Petroleum Tank Release Compensation Bd. v. Crumleys Inc., 2008 MT 2, ¶¶ 79-81, 341 Mont. 33, 174 P.3d 948.

The Montana Supreme Court correctly concluded that the trial court's findings regarding navigability were supported by sufficient evidence. The court's determination that the relevant stretches of the Missouri, Madison, and Clark Fork rivers were navigable at the time of statehood is consistent with the federal test for navigability, which considers whether a river was "used, or was susceptible of being used, in its ordinary condition, as a highway for commerce." The Daniel Ball, 77 U.S. 557, 563 (1870). The trial court's conclusion that the State obtained title to the riverbeds at issue is therefore affirmed.

In sum, the trial court properly applied the constitutional test for determining navigability, and its findings are supported by sufficient evidence. The Montana Supreme Court's affirmance of the trial court's decision was correct, and this Court should affirm the judgment below.
