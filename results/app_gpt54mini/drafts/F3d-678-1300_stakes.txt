**ARGUMENT**

## I. The jury’s § 102(b) verdict cannot stand because Facebook failed to prove that Leader’s pre-critical-date product embodied every limitation of the asserted claims.

An anticipation verdict survives only if substantial evidence shows that a single prior art reference or prior use disclosed each and every element of the claimed invention, arranged as in the claim. *Verdegaal Bros., Inc. v. Union Oil Co. of Cal.*, 814 F.2d 628, 631 (Fed. Cir. 1987); *Net MoneyIN, Inc. v. VeriSign, Inc.*, 545 F.3d 1359, 1369 (Fed. Cir. 2008). Anticipation must be proved by clear and convincing evidence. *Microsoft Corp. v. i4i Ltd. P’ship*, 564 U.S. 91, 95 (2011); *SmithKline Beecham Corp. v. Apotex Corp.*, 403 F.3d 1331, 1342 (Fed. Cir. 2005).

Facebook did not carry that burden. The asserted claims require, among other things, “dynamically associating metadata with the data,” “tracking movement of the user” between environments, and “dynamically updating” the metadata to reflect the new environment. J.A. 21:38-58. Leader’s evidence showed only that Leader2Leader was a collaborative software product, not that it practiced the specific claimed architecture of automatically tagging data with claim-required metadata and updating that metadata as a user moved between environments. The district court’s judgment therefore cannot rest on generalized testimony that the product was functionally similar. *Finisar Corp. v. DirectTV Grp., Inc.*, 523 F.3d 1323, 1334 (Fed. Cir. 2008) (anticipation requires every claim limitation to be found in the prior art); *Schering Corp. v. Geneva Pharm., Inc.*, 339 F.3d 1373, 1380 (Fed. Cir. 2003).

Leader’s founder, Michael McKibben, testified that the patent claims cover the “underlying engine,” referred to as Digital Leaderboard. J.A. 25585-86. But McKibben’s testimony does not establish that Leader2Leader, as used, sold, or demonstrated before December 10, 2002, included every limitation of the asserted claims. Indeed, the record reflects only a white paper, a “Big Board,” and promotional statements that the platform was “operational now with low user volumes” and “already commercializing.” J.A. 27203, 27207, 27210. Promotional materials and high-level descriptions do not, without more, prove actual prior use of the claimed invention. *In re Chudik*, 851 F.3d 1365, 1373 (Fed. Cir. 2017); *SmithKline Beecham Corp. v. Apotex Corp.*, 403 F.3d at 1342.

Nor did Facebook establish public use or on-sale bar invalidity under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b). To trigger the bar, the invention must have been both ready for patenting and the subject of commercial exploitation before the critical date. *Pfaff v. Wells Elecs., Inc.*, 525 U.S. 55, 67-68 (1998); *Allen Eng’g Corp. v. Bartell Indus., Inc.*, 299 F.3d 1336, 1352 (Fed. Cir. 2002). Ready for patenting requires either reduction to practice or enabling documentation. *Pfaff*, 525 U.S. at 67-68. Facebook’s proof fell short on both prongs. The record showed development was ongoing in the 1999-2002 period and that the product was still being refined, with completion occurring only in the “2002ish time frame.” J.A. 24829. That is not the kind of definitive proof required to establish that the claimed invention was complete and enabled before the critical date. *Netscape Commc’ns Corp. v. Konrad*, 295 F.3d 1315, 1321-22 (Fed. Cir. 2002).

The district court likewise erred in allowing the jury to infer anticipation from Leader’s commercial discussions. A commercial offer for sale is not enough unless it is tied to a product that embodied the full claimed invention and was ready for patenting. *Pfaff*, 525 U.S. at 67; *Minton v. Nat’l Ass’n of Sec. Dealers, Inc.*, 336 F.3d 1373, 1379 (Fed. Cir. 2003). The January 2002 white paper shows, at most, an invitation to discuss licenses for a product Leader said was being commercialized. J.A. 27203-04. It does not prove that the particular claim limitations—especially dynamic metadata association and updating across environments—were present in the product as sold or offered. *Allen Eng’g*, 299 F.3d at 1352-53.

At bottom, the verdict rests on an impermissible leap from evidence of a collaborative software platform to a finding that the exact claimed invention was publicly used or on sale more than one year before the filing date. That is not substantial evidence of invalidity. *Verdegaal Bros.*, 814 F.2d at 631; *Finisar*, 523 F.3d at 1334. The Court should reverse the judgment of invalidity and direct entry of judgment for Leader on the § 102(b) defense, or at minimum remand for a new trial.
