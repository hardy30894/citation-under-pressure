**Argument**

The Board’s dismissal of Perry’s mixed case for lack of jurisdiction is judicially reviewable in **district court**, not the Federal Circuit. The Civil Service Reform Act channels mixed cases to district court, and the Supreme Court’s decision in **Kloeckner v. Solis, 568 U.S. 41 (2012),** supplies the controlling rule: when a federal employee “claims that an agency action appealable to the MSPB violated an antidiscrimination statute,” review lies in district court whether the MSPB resolved the case on the merits or dismissed it before reaching the merits. **Id. at 56, 60; see also 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1), § 7703(b)(2).**

1. **This is a mixed case.** Perry challenged an adverse employment action that, if involuntary, was appealable to the MSPB, and he alleged race, age, and disability discrimination. That fits the statutory definition of a mixed case. **5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1); Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 44–45, 56.** The relevant inquiry is not whether the Board ultimately accepted jurisdiction, but whether the employee asserted discrimination in connection with an action that is ordinarily within the Board’s appellate authority. **Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 56; Perry v. Merit Sys. Prot. Bd., 829 F.3d 760, 767–69 (D.C. Cir. 2016).**

2. **Kloeckner forecloses Federal Circuit review.** The Supreme Court rejected the argument that mixed cases belong in the Federal Circuit whenever the MSPB dismisses on a threshold ground rather than reaching the merits. The Court held that “mixed cases” go to district court “even if the MSPB dismissed the case on procedural grounds.” **Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 56, 60.** The Court’s reasoning was textual and categorical: § 7703(b)(2) routes “cases of discrimination” to district court, while § 7703(b)(1) is the default for non-discrimination appeals. **Id. at 46–49, 54–56.** Nothing in the statute carves out a different route for jurisdictional dismissals.

3. **The text does not support a jurisdictional/procedural split.** Section 7703(b)(2) applies to a “case of discrimination” covered by § 7702(a)(1), and § 7702(a)(1) describes a case in which an employee “has been affected by an action which is appealable to the Merit Systems Protection Board” and alleges discrimination. **5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1); Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 54–56.** The Supreme Court explained that the statute asks whether the employee was affected by an action appealable to the Board, not whether the Board later adjudicated jurisdiction differently. **Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 55–56.** That construction leaves no room for a jurisdictional-dismissal exception. **See id.; Williams v. Department of the Army, 715 F.2d 1485, 1490–91 (Fed. Cir. 1983) (en banc).**

4. **The Board’s contrary forum rule cannot stand.** Before **Kloeckner**, some courts distinguished between procedural and jurisdictional dismissals. **Powell v. Department of Defense, 158 F.3d 597, 599–600 (D.C. Cir. 1998); Harms v. Internal Revenue Service, 321 F.3d 1001, 1007–08 (10th Cir. 2003).** But **Kloeckner** rejected the underlying premise that forum depends on the nature of the MSPB’s threshold ruling rather than on the employee’s mixed-case allegations. **568 U.S. at 56, 60.** Where the Supreme Court has resolved the meaning of a jurisdictional statute, lower-court precedents to the contrary cannot survive. **Agostini v. Felton, 521 U.S. 203, 237 (1997); Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc., 490 U.S. 477, 484 (1989).**

5. **This Court should follow the Supreme Court’s broader mixed-case precedents.** In **Perry v. Merit Systems Protection Board, 137 S. Ct. 1975 (2017),** the Court reaffirmed that the proper judicial forum for MSPB decisions in mixed cases is determined by the case’s discrimination character, not by the MSPB’s label for its dismissal. **Id. at 1978–80.** That rule accords with **Kloeckner** and with the statutory structure. **Id.; Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 56.**

6. **Federal Circuit review would defeat the statute’s design.** Congress created one path for ordinary adverse-action appeals and a different path for discrimination claims. **5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(1), (2); Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 55–56.** District court review allows the discrimination claims to be heard in the forum Congress selected for Title VII, ADEA, and Rehabilitation Act issues. **See 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1); Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 52–53.** Routing jurisdictional dismissals to the Federal Circuit would force the employee to litigate the very forum question Congress resolved in favor of district court. **Id. at 54–56.**

Because Perry’s petition seeks review of an MSPB decision in a mixed case, district court review is proper. The judgment below should be reversed, and the case remanded with instructions to transfer or file the petition in district court.
