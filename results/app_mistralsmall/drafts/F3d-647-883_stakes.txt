**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Concluding That Buckles’ § 2255 Motion Was Untimely.**

Buckles’ § 2255 motion was timely because the 90-day period to file a petition for certiorari did not begin to run until this Court recalled the mandate on June 12, 2007. *Supreme Court Rule 13(3)* provides that “[i]f the lower court appropriately entertains an untimely petition for rehearing ... the time to file the petition for a writ of certiorari ... runs from the date of the denial of rehearing.” *Supreme Court Rule 13(1)* imposes a 90-day deadline for filing a petition for certiorari. *See also* *Clay v. United States*, 537 U.S. 522, 527 (2003) (holding that the 90-day period begins to run from the date of the denial of rehearing). However, this Court’s order recalling the mandate on June 12, 2007, effectively reset the 90-day clock. *See* *United States v. Rodriguez*, 313 F.3d 1204, 1207 (9th Cir. 2002) (explaining that a mandate recall resets the time for filing a petition for certiorari).

The district court’s contrary conclusion ignored this Court’s mandate recall and the misinformation provided by the Clerk’s office. *See* *Irwin v. Dep’t of Veterans Affairs*, 498 U.S. 89, 96 (1990) (holding that equitable tolling may apply where a party has been misled by court officials). Buckles’ § 2255 motion, filed within one year of the Supreme Court’s denial of certiorari, was therefore timely.

**II. Buckles Is Entitled to Equitable Tolling Based on the Clerk’s Misleading Advice.**

Even if the 90-day period began to run on April 3, 2007, Buckles is entitled to equitable tolling because the Clerk’s office misled his sister regarding the deadline. *See* *Pace v. DiGuglielmo*, 544 U.S. 408, 418 (2005) (holding that equitable tolling may apply where a party has received inaccurate information from a court official). Buckles’ declaration establishes that the Clerk’s office told his sister that the deadline would be “stayed until a ruling was made on the Petition for Appointment of Counsel and a new mandate date would be issued.” This misinformation directly contributed to Buckles’ untimely filing.

Equitable tolling is appropriate where a party has been misled by a court official and has acted diligently in pursuing his rights. *See* *Baldwin County Welcome Ctr. v. Brown*, 466 U.S. 147, 151 (1984) (explaining that equitable tolling may apply where a party has been prevented from timely filing by “extraordinary circumstances”). Buckles acted diligently by pursuing his Rule 4-1(e) motion and seeking assistance from retained counsel. The Clerk’s misinformation frustrated his efforts, warranting equitable tolling.

**III. Buckles’ § 2255 Motion Should Be Considered on the Merits.**

Because Buckles’ § 2255 motion was timely, the district court erred in dismissing it without considering the merits. *See* *Hill v. United States*, 368 U.S. 424, 428 (1962) (holding that § 2255 motions should not be dismissed for technical procedural defects where the movant has shown cause for the defect). Buckles’ motion raises substantial claims that warrant review, including ineffective assistance of counsel and misconduct by government officials. The district court should have addressed these claims rather than dismissing the motion as untimely.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in dismissing Buckles’ § 2255 motion as untimely. Buckles is entitled to equitable tolling based on the Clerk’s misleading advice, and his motion should be considered on the merits. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
