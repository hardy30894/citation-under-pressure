ARGUMENT

Section 1366(d)(1) limits a shareholder’s deduction of S corporation losses to the sum of his basis in stock and “any indebtedness of the S corporation to the shareholder.” 26 U.S.C. § 1366(d)(1). The regulation requires that any such indebtedness be “bona fide” and “run[] directly” to the shareholder. Treas. Reg. § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i). Subchapter S attributes corporate losses to shareholders “in a manner akin to the tax treatment of partnerships.” Bufferd v. Comm’r, 506 U.S. 523, 525, 113 S.Ct. 927, 122 L.Ed.2d 306 (1993).

Meruelo satisfied both statutory and regulatory requirements. He contributed $4,985,035 of personally borrowed funds that the Commissioner concedes created basis. The remaining net transfers, recorded on Merco’s books as accounts payable to affiliates, were reclassified annually by Merco’s accountant as amounts due under the $10 million promissory note Meruelo executed in favor of Merco. That note created an unconditional, direct obligation running from Merco to Meruelo, not to the affiliates. The Commissioner’s contrary position—that the debt ran only to the affiliates—ignores the reclassification and the note’s plain terms.

The Tax Court’s additional requirement of an “actual economic outlay” finds no support in the statute or regulation. Section 1366(d)(1) demands only indebtedness, not a second layer of economic sacrifice beyond the shareholder’s personal liability. Because Meruelo’s personal loan supplied the funds that ultimately reached Merco, and because the note formalized Merco’s direct repayment obligation to him, the indebtedness ran directly to Meruelo within the meaning of Treas. Reg. § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i). The $9 million net transfers therefore increased his basis, permitting deduction of his allocable share of Merco’s $26.6 million loss.

The judgment should be reversed.
