**ARGUMENT**

Section 523(a)(2)(A) excepts from discharge only “any debt . . . for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by . . . actual fraud.” 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A). The statutory text limits nondischargeability to the sum actually obtained by the fraud; it does not reach additional statutory multiples imposed as a penalty. *In re Levy*, 951 F.2d 198, 202 (9th Cir. 1991). Because the $31,382.50 in overcharges alone was obtained by the implied representation, the treble damages awarded under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act fall outside the exception.

This Court has repeatedly emphasized that the Bankruptcy Code embodies a “fresh start” policy available to the “honest but unfortunate debtor.” *Grogan v. Garner*, 498 U.S. 279, 286-87 (1991); *Local Loan Co. v. Hunt*, 292 U.S. 234, 244 (1934). Allowing discharge of statutory penalties preserves that policy while denying relief only for the fruits of fraud. *Williams v. United States Fidelity & Guaranty Co.*, 236 U.S. 549, 554-55 (1915). The contrary view adopted below would convert every state-law multiplier into a nondischargeable debt, expanding § 523(a)(2)(A) beyond its plain terms. *Perez v. Campbell*, 402 U.S. 637, 649 (1971).

The Third Circuit’s reliance on *In re St. Laurent*, 991 F.2d 672 (11th Cir. 1993), is misplaced. That decision treated the entire judgment as a single “debt” without examining whether the punitive component was “obtained by” fraud. *Id.* at 679. The Ninth Circuit correctly rejected that approach, holding that only the compensatory sum satisfies the statutory phrase. *In re Levy*, 951 F.2d at 202. Federal law, not state law, determines the meaning of “punitive damages” in a federal statute. *Molzof v. United States*, 502 U.S. 301, 305 (1992).

Even assuming reckless indifference can satisfy the scienter element, *In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d 1108, 1118-19 (3d Cir. 1995); *In re Van Horne*, 823 F.2d 1285, 1287 (8th Cir. 1987), that showing reaches only the money actually received. The additional $62,765 in treble damages was not received; it was later imposed by statute. *Brown v. Felsen*, 442 U.S. 127, 138 (1979) (dischargeability turns on the nature of the debt, not the label attached by a subsequent judgment).

The judgment below should be reversed to the extent it declares the treble damages, attorney’s fees, and costs nondischargeable.
