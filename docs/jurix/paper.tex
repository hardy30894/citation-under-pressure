\title{Citation under Pressure: What Deployment Constraints Do to Legal Citation Integrity, and What Deterministic Verification Can Repair}

\author{\fnms{Harsh} \snm{Dubey}%
\thanks{Corresponding Author: Harsh Dubey, New York University; hd2225@nyu.edu.}}
\runningauthor{H. Dubey}
\address{New York University, New York, USA}

\begin{abstract}
Courts now sanction lawyers for filing citations a language model invented, and most research responds by detecting such errors in finished briefs. This paper measures how they are produced, and what a checker does when it is put in the loop instead. Seven models from seven vendors, open-weight 30B to frontier, drafted argument sections for 48 U.S. Supreme Court matters under five conditions: baseline, a citation quota, a restriction to pre-1970 authority, a sanctions warning, and all three combined. Every citation in the \nDrafts{} drafts was adjudicated deterministically, with no language-model judge: existence against an 18-million-row reporter index, quotations verbatim against opinion text with a checker validated on seeded faults and audited by hand, and pinpoint pages against reporter pagination. The failure mode moves with capability. Under combined pressure the smallest model's citation existence fell from \existQwenBase{} to \existQwenCombo, verbatim quotation accuracy fell for \primQuoteFallModels{} of the seven, and the number of accurate quotations a draft contains fell for \countModelsWithFall, two at the top of the range; the strongest model's rate did not fall, most plausibly because it quoted less. Stratifying by the age of the cited opinion shows the date restriction damaging integrity chiefly by pushing a model onto authority it would not otherwise cite. Fed the checker's findings, models satisfy it by deletion: of Sonnet~5's \trFlagRZeroSonnet{} flagged quotations \trTrueFlagRepairedSonnet{} were corrected and \trTrueFlagDeletedSonnet{} deleted, and accurate quotations per draft rose for one model of seven. A flag carrying the cited passage raises repair without overtaking removal, and a verifier whose flags are true half the time removed \trHalfAccRemovedSonnet{} of Sonnet~5's \trAccRZeroSonnet{} correct quotations, as many as revision with no feedback. Both experiments make one point: a rate computed over what a model chooses to expose to a check can rise while the correct output behind it does not, so an evaluation should report the count beside the rate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Since 2023, courts have repeatedly sanctioned lawyers who filed briefs with citations a language model invented, from \textit{Mata v.~Avianca}~\cite{mata2023} to a docket a public tracker now counts in the thousands~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}. Most published work frames the problem as detection: given a brief that already contains errors, how many can a model find~\cite{liu2026whochecks,chen2026legalcitebench}? That is the right measure for reviewing a filed brief, not for asking which prompting conditions produce the errors, this paper's question.

This paper asks two production-side questions. First, which prompting conditions cause citation failure, and for which models? Deployed prompts carry constraints of the kind Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} studied for scholarly citations, where a publication-year window lowered citation existence even in capable models; we add a sanctions warning and know of no comparable study for law. Second, when a deterministic checker feeds its findings back into revision, does the model correct them, leave them, or change the draft so that the tests pass without the quotations being corrected?

Both questions need an instrument the field has said does not exist in public: the highest-recall published detection agent misses nearly half of incorrect pinpoint citations (52.8 percent recall), which its authors attribute to page-level information living behind commercial databases~\cite{liu2026whochecks}. We built it from public sources: existence against a local index from CourtListener bulk data, verbatim quotation checking validated on seeded faults and audited on real drafts, and page-level pinpoint verification for quotations on U.S. Reports authority against the official pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's archives~\cite{cap2024}.

Our primary setting is the closed-book use of general-purpose models, the setting of most sanctioned filings to date~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}; a grounded arm with ten retrieved authorities in the prompt measures what retrieval changes. Drafts under the four non-baseline conditions are the pressured drafts (\nPressured{} here, three refusals). The paper contributes a constraint-sensitive evaluation, in which reliability at a neutral prompt does not predict reliability under drafting constraints; deterministic verification of existence, quotation, and pinpoint page with no language-model judge, released with the drafts; and the finding that verification is not repair, since flags fed back to the model raise measured accuracy by deletion and an imprecise verifier damages correct content.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Legal citation reliability.} Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} build a detection benchmark from real appellate briefs with injected errors and a taxonomy grounded in sanctioned filings; we adopt their categories and measure the naturally occurring errors their limitations section asks for. LegalCiteBench~\cite{chen2026legalcitebench} shows that models asked closed-book for a named case's citation almost never produce it; we ask whether the cases a model chooses to cite exist. Dahl et al.~\cite{dahl2024fictions} and Magesh et al.~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree} measure hallucinated authority in chatbots and research tools.

\textbf{Constraints and fabrication.} Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} is the closest design: five prompting regimes over scholarly claims, verified by fuzzy bibliographic matching that left 36 to 61 percent of citations unresolved. We transplant their factorial to law with deterministic verification (\unverifiablePct{} percent unresolved) and a statistical model.

\textbf{Revision with execution feedback.} The loop follows execution-feedback work in code generation~\cite{gehring2025rlef}, with its no-feedback control separating feedback content from extra revision; we add controls in which a quarter to all of the feedback is false.

\textbf{AI and law.} Blair-Stanek and Van Durme~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable} found frontier models flipping the winning party across identical zero-temperature runs, which Section~\ref{sec:limits} bounds with repeated generations. Evaluation here pairs automated metrics with the checks lawyers perform~\cite{gultekin2025twodim,sanchi2024hybrid}; citation integrity is one with deterministic ground truth. Garneau et al.~\cite{garneau2024pincites} found retrieving the paragraph a proposition rests on harder than retrieving the case; our verifier checks a pincite against the page text. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025overruled} show models miss overruled precedent, ConsRAG~\cite{nguyen2024consrag} constrains retrieval, and Trozze et al.~\cite{trozze2025crypto} and Harasta et al.~\cite{harasta2026cannotberight} study how readers perceive model-written documents. Evaluations of generated legal argument~\cite{gray2025magnitudes,pires2025legalwriting} do not check the authority it cites; citation-network work~\cite{khatri2023citecaselaw,sadeghian2018edgelabeling,dadgostari2021lawsearch,mumford2025citationnetworks} studies the network our checker queries.

\section{Tasks, Conditions, and Instruments}\label{sec:method}

\textbf{Terms.} In 347 U.S. 483, 495, the second page is the \textit{pincite}, for a quotation the page holding the quoted language. For a draft $d$ let $A_d$ be its \textit{adjudicable} citations, labelled exists or not found, and $S_d$ its \textit{scored} quotations, those it attributes to a case whose text the checker holds. The outcomes are
\begin{equation*}
E_d = \frac{|\{c \in A_d : \text{exists}\}|}{|A_d|},\quad
R_d = \frac{|\{q \in S_d : \text{accurate}\}|}{|S_d|},\quad
K_d = |\{q \in S_d : \text{accurate}\}|,
\end{equation*}
\textit{citation existence}, \textit{strict quotation accuracy}, and the \textit{count} of accurate quotations in the draft; the lenient rate $R^{\ell}_d$ counts near misses as accurate. A quotation the draft does not attribute is outside $S_d$ and so outside $R_d$.

\textbf{Matters.} The matters are forty-eight Supreme Court cases from 1990 to 2020 drawn from the Supreme Court Database~\cite{spaeth2025scdb}; each packet holds the question presented, party names, a factual summary, and a lower-court opinion excerpt, the Court's decision excluded. The model argues an assigned side in under 900 words; no measurement here predicts an outcome, so the memorisation confound documented for classification of Supreme Court cases does not arise~\cite{ortega2025scotusmem}. A second task uses forty-eight published F.3d decisions from the Caselaw Access Project archive (1995 to 2019, four from each of the First through Eleventh and Federal Circuits, no cross-appeals); the packet is the opinion's statement of the case and facts, cut before the analysis and scrubbed of outcome sentences, argued for the appellant under all five conditions.

\textbf{Conditions.} The five conditions are baseline; quota (``cite at least eight distinct case authorities''); temporal (``every authority you cite MUST have been decided before 1970''), a clause no user writes, a checkable stand-in for the authority limits practitioners impose; unlike a jurisdiction limit, which pushes toward the circuit authority where the second task finds fabrication, it moves citations toward well-memorized opinions; stakes (``this section will be filed with the court; citing authority that does not exist, or misquoting authority, risks sanctions under Rule~11''); and combined, which concatenates the three clauses, so a combined effect cannot be assigned to one of them. Each condition has one fixed template, at temperature 0. The stakes clause names Rule~11, invoked in the sanctioned-filing cases~\cite{mata2023}, which governs district-court civil actions~\cite{frcp2024} and neither task's forum, so it is a stakes signal only. A second template rephrasing every condition was run on all 48 matters. A \textit{grounded} arm reran baseline and combined with the ten U.S. Reports opinions most similar to the question and facts in the prompt (TF-IDF over \retrievalCorpus{} cached opinions decided before argument and, for combined, before 1970, neither party named), each with citation, year, and its best-matching 250-word passage, quotation restricted to the excerpts.

{\sloppy\textbf{Models.} The models are Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mistral Small, Llama-4 Maverick, DeepSeek V4 Flash, Grok~4.3, GPT-5.4-mini, and Claude Sonnet~5, one per vendor, run through OpenRouter in August 2026 with completion limits of 1,400 to 12,000 tokens (identifiers in the repository). Tables are ordered by baseline strict quotation accuracy, and Sonnet~5, GPT-5.4-mini, and Grok~4.3, with the highest baseline existence, are the top of the range. An eighth model, GLM-4.7-flash, exhausted a 30,000-token budget in reasoning on \glmMissing{} of 240 drafts and is excluded.\par}

\textbf{Adjudication.} Full case citations are extracted with eyecite~\cite{cushman2021eyecite} (statutes and rules cite no case and are outside every rate) and labelled exists, not found, or unverifiable, the last for a reporter the index does not carry, so coverage is never counted as fabrication; \unverifiablePct{} percent of the \nCitationRecords{} records are unverifiable. Citations resolve against an 18-million-row table; quotations are located by balanced quotation marks and attributed, in order, to the citation whose parenthetical holds them, the case named in their own sentence, the citation following them in that sentence (not one introduced by \textit{see} or \textit{cf.}), the citation an \textit{Id.} points to, or the last citation in the paragraph; one introduced by a statute, rule, the record, or a lower court, or with no citation in its paragraph, is left unpaired, the checker having nothing to test it against: \unpairedPctAll{} percent of the \quotesAll{} quotations in the full run, \unpairedNoncasePct{} introduced by non-case material, \unpairedNoCitePct{} standing in a paragraph that cites nothing, \unpairedFarPct{} too far from any citation. \textit{Exists} means the volume, reporter, and page resolve to an indexed opinion (\nResolving{} of the \nCitationRecords{} records; \nNotFoundAll{} are not found); for the \nmChecked{} whose draft names the parties the name is compared separately, and \nmMismatch{} (\nmPct{} percent; \nmPctBase{} at baseline against \nmPctTemporal{} under the temporal clause) carry a name the index does not match, abbreviated names included; counting every mismatch as not found keeps the \nmSurvKept{} surviving existence contrasts, adds Mistral Small's temporal fall (OR \nmGainMistralTemporalOr, corrected $p$=\nmGainMistralTemporalHolm), and removes Sonnet~5's uncorrected combined rise (OR \nmSonnetComboOr). A quotation $q$ is split into fragments $f_1, \dots, f_n$ at ellipses and bracketed alterations (so that a lawful substitution is read as an omission and the words around it must match), alteration parentheticals dropped, and compared with the normalized opinion text $T$ of the case it is attributed to: \textit{accurate} when every $f_j$ is a substring of $T$, \textit{near miss} when $\min_j \mathrm{cov}(f_j, T) \ge 0.85$, where $\mathrm{cov}$ is the share of a fragment's words of five or more letters that occur in $T$, and \textit{inaccurate} otherwise. Matching is case and punctuation insensitive, and a quotation that drops an internal citation without leaving an ellipsis, which the citation rules permit under a parenthetical, passes when the opinion holds the fragment's halves in order across a citation-shaped gap; the lenient rate is a diagnostic, since 0.85 coverage admits one wrong word in seven. The quotation checker scores 280 of 280 on twenty synthetic drafts planting fourteen item kinds, six attribution traps and two omissions without an ellipsis among them, and the existence and pinpoint checks \valPinsPass{} of \valPinsTotal{} on \valPinsCases{} seeded opinions. Pinpoint pages are verified against the official reporter pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's HTML volumes, which mark every page break; a pincite that names a range or a list supports the citation on any page it names, a quotation is located by its longest fragment, so one straddling a page break is placed where that fragment sits, and only quotations whose fragment is somewhere in the cited case enter the page-level rate.

\textbf{Human reference.} The same instrument scored \humanExcerpts{} pre-ChatGPT appellate brief excerpts from Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} (every brief-derived excerpt of theirs carrying no injected error, their model-written subset excluded; OCR-converted, so a floor); human lawyers reach \humanStrict{} strict and \humanLenient{} lenient quotation accuracy (\humanScored{} scored) and \humanExist{} existence, a severity reference only, since human briefs differ in citation mix. The literal matcher the strict standard replaced, which read a bracketed alteration as its contents, puts the human rate at \humanAltStrict{} and moves model cells by at most \altMaxCellDiff{} points, the same \altSurv{} contrasts surviving; \humanFailNearUnaltered{} of the \humanFailTotal{} human strict failures are near misses without any alteration, the signature of a corrupted character.

\textbf{Inference.} The primary analysis is a citation-level logistic generalized estimating equation per model, $\text{logit}\,P(y_{imk}=1)=\beta_0+\beta_k$ for the existence or accuracy indicator $y_{imk}$ of item $i$ in matter $m$ under condition $k$, exchangeable within matters~\cite{liang1986gee}, so the 48 matters are the independent units. Each model has \nHolmTests{} contrasts (four conditions, two outcomes); we report Holm-corrected $p$ values within model, the family the paper draws conclusions about, and call an effect significant only when it survives; counts of models are descriptive, and the effective sample is 48 matters. Every other fit is exploratory and follows the same model and the same rule, Holm within model over that fit's own tests: the lenient outcome, distinct authorities, the second template, and pooled templates, tasks, and generations (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), each pooling with its fixed effect; the released matrix gives every contrast under every fit. For the revision experiment, pre-registered descriptively, we add matter-paired comparisons between arms (Wilcoxon, bootstrap interval). Hypotheses and decision rules were frozen before the full run; every later decision, including two withdrawn findings, is in a dated ledger.

\section{Experiment 2: The Checker inside the Revision Loop}\label{sec:exp2}

Combined-condition drafts entered a revision loop: each round the checker returned one line per failure, the model revised, and the loop ended on a clean draft or after three revisions. A \textit{false feedback} control gave the same number of identical-looking lines aimed at correct items, \textit{mixed} controls replaced each line with a false one at random so that the feedback's nominal precision $\pi$, the share of its lines drawn from the checker, was 0.25, 0.5, or 0.75 ($\pi = 1$ under true and $\pi = 0$ under false feedback), replacing rather than adding lines, so recall falls with precision and the arms vary both. A \textit{no-feedback} control gave the same rounds with a generic instruction to revise. The loop used the first 24 matters; rates are means over drafts with at least one scored quotation (\lcEmptyTrueTotal{} of the 168 true-arm episodes ended with none, two without passing the checker, \lcEmptyTrueGrok{} of Grok~4.3's 24, so its final rate rests on \lcGrokTrueDrafts{} drafts and Llama-4's on \lcAliveLlamaTrue). The loop was rerun after the attribution rule was corrected; the original run, whose true-arm flags had realized precision \rpTrueAll{} under the corrected rule (\rpTrueSonnet{} for Sonnet~5), is released beside it. The rerun's flags are the corrected rule's verdicts, whose precision is \auditRulePrecisionPct{} percent by the hand reading of Section~\ref{sec:residual} (\auditRulePrecisionCiLow{} to \auditRulePrecisionCiHigh), so Figure~\ref{fig:repair}'s $\pi$ is nominal and its arm at $\pi = 1$ sits nearer 0.8. Every scored round-0 quotation was traced to the final draft (Table~\ref{tab:true}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Where the checker belongs.} Two recommendations follow, both inferences, since no arm had a person in it. The first is the checker at the gate over a loop resolving bare flags, and the passage in the flag if a loop is built: removing an unverifiable quotation answers a flag defensibly, but false flags get the same answer and the tool then reports a clean draft.

\textbf{Verifier precision is a deployment requirement.} Our checker's error is \auditSampleCheckerSidePct{} percent by the hand reading, and published detectors span this axis: on one benchmark the highest-recall agent reaches 84.4 percent recall at 40.8 percent precision, the highest-precision one, best on F1 there, 76.1 percent precision at 62.8 percent recall~\cite{liu2026whochecks}. A loop fed by the first would run near the arm where removal already matches revision alone. At nominal precision one half Sonnet~5 lost \trHalfAccRemovedSonnet{} of \trAccRZeroSonnet{} correct quotations, no more than revision with no feedback removed (\trNoneAccRemovedSonnet), the damage beyond that appearing at 0.25 and 0 (Figure~\ref{fig:repair}). A pipeline feeding a detector's flags to a model should report its precision beside its recall. The second recommendation is to report the strict standard as defined here, which the human reference passes at \humanStrict.

\textbf{What evaluations should include.} The two experiments make one point. The observed rate is $R_d = K_d / |S_d|$, and a model can raise it by shrinking what it exposes to the check, $|S_d|$, as well as by raising the correct output $K_d$: under pressure the strongest model quoted less, and under feedback every model deleted what was flagged, $|S_d|$ down and $K_d$ flat or lower in both. A rising rate therefore does not imply more correct output; an evaluation scoring only what a system chooses to emit should report $K_d$ beside $R_d$.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

Both tasks are United States federal case law, the stakes clause names a rule governing neither forum, and the checks stop at existence, wording, and page: three model panels for relevance fell short of a \juryBar{} bar against expert labels~\cite{liu2026whochecks,zheng2023judging}. The controls, grounded arm and loop, second task, trace, count outcome, stratification, and corrected rule postdate the pre-registration and are reported apart. The readings behind the checker's error are a single unblinded pass over released samples, without a second rater, and predate two corrections that only remove checker-side verdicts. Three generations bound run-to-run variation~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable} (cell standard deviations at most \repMaxExistSd{} for existence, \repMaxStrictSd{} for strict), but the between-template spread (\phrMaxStrictDiff{} points) exceeds many single-run effects, so we headline only contrasts surviving both. The models are one per vendor, confounding capability and family.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Under deployment constraints the failure mode moves with capability: the 30B model loses existence, the middle loses verbatim quotation and correct quotations per draft, and the strongest model's rate holds only because it quotes less. Returned to the model, the checker's flags are removed far more often than corrected. Instruments and data: \url{https://github.com/hardy30894/citation-under-pressure}.
