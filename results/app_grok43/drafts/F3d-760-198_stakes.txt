ARGUMENT

The district court erred in dismissing Moll’s hostile work environment claim by limiting its review to allegations of sexually offensive conduct and excluding facially sex-neutral incidents from the totality of circumstances.

A plaintiff may establish a sex-based hostile work environment through evidence that includes facially sex-neutral incidents. Alfano v. Costello, 294 F.3d 365, 375 (2d Cir. 2002). The district court therefore applied an incorrect legal standard when it refused to consider all allegations in the complaint, including those that were not sexually offensive in nature.

Whether an environment is hostile or abusive can be determined only by looking at all the circumstances. Harris v. Forklift Sys., Inc., 510 U.S. 17, 23 (1993). These circumstances include the frequency of the discriminatory conduct, its severity, whether it is physically threatening or humiliating, and whether it unreasonably interferes with an employee’s work performance. Id. Conduct that is not overtly sexual may nevertheless contribute to a hostile environment when it is directed at an employee because of sex. Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc., 523 U.S. 75, 80 (1998).

Moll’s complaint alleged a continuous pattern of adverse treatment beginning in 1998, encompassing Irving’s refusal to permit email or telephone communication, his denial of promotion assessment while male colleagues advanced, the counseling plan imposed in 2002, the prohibition on working from home while male counterparts continued to do so, the denial of vacation requests granted to less-tenured males, and exclusion from work-related social events. These incidents, taken together with the three notes and hotel-room calls, must be evaluated as a whole to determine whether the workplace was permeated with discriminatory intimidation. Harris, 510 U.S. at 21.

Because the district court failed to apply the totality-of-circumstances standard required by Alfano and Harris, its dismissal of the hostile work environment claim cannot stand. The judgment should be vacated and the claim remanded for consideration of all evidence under the proper legal framework.
