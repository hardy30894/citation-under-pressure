**ARGUMENT**

I. **A Rule 32 Violation Is Subject to Harmless-Error Review**

The Third Circuit correctly held that a district court’s failure to advise a defendant of his right to appeal under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) is subject to harmless-error review. *See United States v. Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. 74, 80 (2004) (applying harmless-error review to Rule 32 violations); *see also United States v. Vonn*, 535 U.S. 55, 62-63 (2002) (holding that Rule 32 violations are not structural errors and thus subject to harmless-error analysis). Harmless-error review applies because the error does not undermine the fundamental fairness of the proceeding or the reliability of the conviction. *See Arizona v. Fulminante*, 499 U.S. 279, 306-07 (1991) (explaining that harmless-error review applies to non-structural errors).

The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that defendants are informed of their right to appeal. *See Fed. R. Crim. P. 32 advisory committee’s note (1966)* ("The purpose of the rule is to insure that the defendant is advised of his right to appeal."). When a defendant is already aware of that right, the error is harmless because the defendant’s decision not to appeal is unaffected by the court’s omission. *See Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. at 83 ("If the defendant already knows of his right to appeal, the error is harmless.").

II. **The District Court Correctly Found the Error Harmless**

The district court found that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing. *See* J.A. 25. This factual finding is entitled to deference under *Anderson v. Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 573-74 (1985) (holding that findings of fact after an evidentiary hearing are reviewed for clear error). Given this finding, the Third Circuit properly concluded that the Rule 32 violation was harmless because Peguero’s decision not to appeal was not influenced by the court’s failure to advise him. *See Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. at 83.

Peguero’s awareness of his right to appeal is further evidenced by his subsequent filing of a habeas petition challenging his sentence. *See* J.A. 12. If he lacked knowledge of his right to appeal, he would not have been able to challenge his sentence in this manner. *See United States v. Calderon*, 428 F.3d 928, 932 (7th Cir. 2005) (holding that a defendant’s post-conviction challenge demonstrates awareness of appellate rights).

III. **The Rule 32 Violation Does Not Warrant Habeas Relief**

Habeas relief is available only for constitutional errors or errors that seriously affect the fairness, integrity, or public reputation of judicial proceedings. *See United States v. Cotton*, 535 U.S. 625, 631-32 (2002) (holding that non-constitutional errors do not warrant habeas relief unless they seriously affect fairness). A Rule 32 violation, standing alone, does not meet this standard. *See Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. at 80 (holding that a Rule 32 violation does not warrant habeas relief when harmless).

The Third Circuit’s decision is consistent with this Court’s precedent. *See Vonn*, 535 U.S. at 62-63 (applying harmless-error review to Rule 32 violations); *see also Calderon*, 428 F.3d at 932 (holding that a Rule 32 violation is harmless when the defendant is aware of his right to appeal). The district court’s failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal did not prejudice him, as he elected not to appeal despite his awareness of the right. *See Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. at 83.

**CONCLUSION**

The judgment of the Third Circuit should be affirmed because the district court’s failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was harmless given his awareness of that right. The error did not seriously affect the fairness of the proceedings and thus does not warrant habeas relief.
