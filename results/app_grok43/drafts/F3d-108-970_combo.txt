ARGUMENT

The district court erred in entering judgment for Feltmann. Sieben’s actions rested on legitimate, performance-based grounds unrelated to sex. See Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356, 373-74 (1886) (equal protection forbids only “unjust and illegal discrimination” arising from “hostility” to a class). Feltmann’s low gross profits and repeated violation of the demo-car restriction supplied nondiscriminatory reasons for probation and discipline. See The Civil Rights Cases, 109 U.S. 3, 25 (1883) (Fourteenth Amendment reaches only state action that “denies” equal protection, not private business judgments).

No hostile environment existed. Anzalone’s single vulgar remark was promptly investigated and sanctioned; Feltmann heard nothing further. See Strauder v. West Virginia, 100 U.S. 303, 310 (1880) (Fourteenth Amendment protects against “discrimination” that is “systematic” and “persistent”). Isolated comments do not constitute the “purposeful” denial of equal terms required by the statute. Steele v. Louisville & N.R. Co., 323 U.S. 192, 203 (1944) (union’s duty of fair representation violated only by “hostile discrimination” that is “intentional”).

The retaliation claim likewise fails. Feltmann’s November 1992 letter addressed a personal slur, not sex discrimination. See Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1, 13 (1948) (constitutional protection triggered only when state action enforces “discrimination” on racial or analogous grounds). Subsequent performance memos and the March probation predated any protected activity and tracked identical treatment of male consultant Beutel. Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629, 635 (1950) (equal protection violated only when similarly situated individuals receive disparate treatment “solely” on the forbidden ground).

Feltmann’s exclusion from the Chicago contest and continued demo use outside the St. Louis area reflected documented sales decline and cost-control needs, not sex. Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954) (equal protection forbids classifications that “generate a feeling of inferiority” when imposed “solely because of” race or sex). No evidence shows any male consultant received more favorable treatment after comparable performance failures.

Because the record contains no proof of intentional, class-based animus, judgment for Feltmann cannot stand.
