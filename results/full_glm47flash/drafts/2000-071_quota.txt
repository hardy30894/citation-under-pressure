**ARGUMENT**

**I. Milford’s Community Use Policy is a valid, neutral, generally applicable restriction on a limited public forum.**

Milford Central School’s Community Use Policy authorizes the use of school facilities for "social, civic and recreational meetings" and other uses "pertaining to the welfare of the community." *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 267 (1981). This policy creates a limited public forum, which the school may open to certain groups while excluding others, provided the exclusion is based on the nature of the speech rather than the speaker’s viewpoint. *Id.* at 271. The Supreme Court has held that a public school may exclude religious speech if it is "religious instruction" or "worship," even if it permits secular speech on the same topic. *Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School Dist.*, 508 U.S. 384, 392 (1993). Milford’s policy is a valid, content-based restriction that excludes religious use while permitting secular community use.

**II. The Good News Club’s activities constitute religious instruction, not permissible moral development.**

The Second Circuit correctly determined that the Good News Club’s activities are "religious instruction and prayer" rather than the teaching of morals from a religious perspective. *Id.* at 391. The Club’s materials explicitly teach that salvation is available only through belief in Jesus Christ and include a "Challenge and Invitation" segment urging children to "believe on the Lord Jesus as your Savior." *Id.* at 392. The Supreme Court has consistently held that a school may exclude speech that is "religious instruction." *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 271. In *Board of Educ. of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226, 249 (1990), the Court noted that a school may exclude religious speech if it is "religious instruction" or "worship." Because the Club’s primary purpose is to teach the "good news" of Christ’s gospel and to lead children to salvation, its activities fall squarely within the category of religious instruction that a school may exclude. *Id.* at 250.

**III. The Establishment Clause justifies the exclusion of the Good News Club.**

Even if the First Amendment applied, Milford’s exclusion of the Club is justified by the compelling state interest in avoiding the appearance of state endorsement of religion. *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 269. The Supreme Court has recognized that a school may not "endorse" religion. *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 392. Allowing a private religious group to use public school facilities for worship or religious instruction would send the message that the State endorses the Club’s religious message. *Id.* at 393. Furthermore, the Court has held that a school may not discriminate against religion, but it may exclude religious speech if it is "religious instruction" or "worship." *Mergens*, 496 U.S. at 250. The state interest in maintaining the neutrality of its facilities is compelling and justifies the exclusion of the Good News Club’s religious activities. *Id.* at 251. This principle is consistent with the Court’s decision in *Edwards v. Aguillard*, 482 U.S. 578, 584 (1987), which struck down a law that favored religious instruction over secular instruction, thereby upholding the state’s interest in avoiding religious establishment.
