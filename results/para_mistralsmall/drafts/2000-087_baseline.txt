**ARGUMENT**

I. THE STATUTE DOES NOT AUTHORIZE INDEFINITE DETENTION BEYOND THE REASONABLE TIME NECESSARY TO EFFECT REMOVAL.

The post-removal-period statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(6), does not permit the Attorney General to detain a removable alien indefinitely once removal is no longer reasonably foreseeable. *Zadvydas v. Davis*, 533 U.S. 678, 682 (2001). The Supreme Court construed the statute to contain an implicit “reasonable time” limitation to avoid serious constitutional concerns. *Id.* at 690. The Court held that “once removal is no longer reasonably foreseeable, continued detention is no longer authorized by the statute.” *Id.* at 701.

The Fifth Circuit erred in requiring Zadvydas to show that deportation is “impossible” before release is warranted. *Zadvydas v. Underdown*, 185 F.3d 279, 291 (5th Cir. 1999). The Supreme Court rejected that standard as imposing too high a burden on the alien. *Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 704. Instead, the habeas court must ask whether the detention exceeds a period reasonably necessary to secure removal, measured primarily by the statute’s basic purpose: assuring the alien’s presence at the moment of removal. *Id.* at 699–700. If removal is not reasonably foreseeable, continued detention is unreasonable and unauthorized. *Id.* at 701.

II. THE SIX-MONTH PRESUMPTION ESTABLISHED BY *ZADVYDAS* HAS LONG SINCE EXPIRED.

The Supreme Court recognized a presumptively reasonable period of six months, measured from the beginning of the removal period, after which the Government must rebut a showing that removal is not likely in the reasonably foreseeable future. *Id.* at 705. Here, Zadvydas was detained well beyond that six-month window. The Fifth Circuit acknowledged that when the district court granted habeas relief, Zadvydas had been in post-removal custody for “several months in excess of three years.” *Zadvydas v. Davis*, 185 F.3d at 284. The Court’s six-month presumption has long since expired, and the Government has failed to rebut Zadvydas’s showing that removal is not reasonably foreseeable. *Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 705.

III. THE STATUTE’S DETENTION AUTHORITY IS NOT LIMITLESS AND DOES NOT PERMIT PUNITIVE OR INDEFINITE CONFINEMENT.

The Government’s reliance on *Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei*, 345 U.S. 206 (1953), is misplaced. *Mezei* involved an excludable alien held at the border, not a lawful resident alien subject to a final removal order. *Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 700. The Court distinguished *Mezei* on that basis, noting that the alien in *Mezei* had never effected entry into the United States. *Id.* at 700–01. Moreover, *Mezei* did not address the constitutional concerns raised by prolonged detention of a lawful resident alien. *Id.*

The statute’s detention authority is not a license for punitive or indefinite confinement. *Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 701. The Court emphasized that the choice is not between imprisonment and the alien “living at large,” but between imprisonment and supervised release under appropriate conditions. *Id.* at 702 (citing 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(3)). The Government has not demonstrated that Zadvydas poses a risk to the community or is unlikely to comply with release conditions. *Id.* at 705.

IV. THE DISTRICT COURT PROPERLY ORDERED ZADVYDAS’S RELEASE SUBJECT TO CONDITIONS.

The district court’s order releasing Zadvydas on conditions was consistent with the Supreme Court’s guidance. *Id.* at 704–05. The Court expressly contemplated that aliens in Zadvydas’s position could be released subject to supervision, with the possibility of return to custody for violations of release conditions. *Id.* The conditions imposed by the district court—including regular reporting, employment, residence restrictions, and a bond—are tailored to ensure Zadvydas’s presence and compliance with the law. *Id.*

**CONCLUSION**

The Fifth Circuit erred in upholding Zadvydas’s continued detention beyond the reasonable time necessary to effect removal. The statute does not authorize indefinite detention, and the six-month presumption established by *Zadvydas* has long since expired. The Government has failed to rebut Zadvydas’s showing that removal is not reasonably foreseeable. The district court’s order releasing Zadvydas on conditions is consistent with the Supreme Court’s decision and should be affirmed.
