The government bears the burden of proving beyond a reasonable doubt that a defendant was a member of a conspiracy during the relevant statute of limitations period. When a defendant presents evidence of withdrawal from a conspiracy prior to the statute of limitations period, the government must prove that the defendant was still a member of the conspiracy during the relevant period. See United States v. Lothian, 976 F.2d 1257, 1261-62 (9th Cir. 1992); United States v. Steele, 685 F.2d 793, 803-04 (3d Cir. 1982). The D.C. Circuit correctly held that the government met its burden in this case, as the evidence presented at trial established that the conspiracy continued into the valid statute of limitations period and that the defendants were still members. See United States v. Moore, 651 F.3d 30, 63-64 (D.C. Cir. 2011).

The petitioners argue that the D.C. Circuit erred in affirming the district court's denial of their motions for a new trial, claiming that the jury did not have sufficient evidence to prove that all defendants were part of a single conspiracy. However, the D.C. Circuit correctly applied the standard set forth in United States v. Tarantino, 846 F.2d 1384, 1393 (D.C. Cir. 1988), which holds that the government must prove that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy during the relevant statute of limitations period.

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has held that the government bears the burden of proving the defendant's participation in a conspiracy beyond a reasonable doubt. See United States v. Gaudin, 515 U.S. 506, 522-23 (1995); In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358, 364 (1970). When a defendant presents evidence of withdrawal, the government must prove that the defendant was still a member of the conspiracy during the relevant period. See Lothian, 976 F.2d at 1261-62.

In this case, the government presented sufficient evidence to prove that the defendants were members of a single conspiracy that continued into the valid statute of limitations period. The D.C. Circuit correctly affirmed the district court's denial of the petitioners' motions for a new trial. Therefore, the judgment of the D.C. Circuit should be affirmed, as the government met its burden of proving the petitioners' membership in the conspiracy during the relevant statute of limitations period.

The petitioners' argument that the government failed to prove their membership in the conspiracy during the relevant period is unpersuasive. The evidence presented at trial established that the conspiracy continued into the valid statute of limitations period, and the D.C. Circuit correctly applied the relevant case law. See Moore, 651 F.3d at 63-64; Tarantino, 846 F.2d at 1393. The government met its burden, and the judgment of the D.C. Circuit should be affirmed.
