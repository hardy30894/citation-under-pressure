**I. The District Court Erred in Enhancing the Lodestar Based on the Subjective Quality of Counsel’s Performance**

The district court abused its discretion by enhancing the lodestar figure based on the "superb quality" of counsel’s representation. The Supreme Court has long held that the fee award must be reasonable, defined by the product of hours reasonably expended and a reasonable hourly rate. The Court has consistently rejected the notion that the "quality of the work" is a separate factor to be added to the lodestar.

In *Aetna Life Ins. Co. v. Haworth*, 300 U.S. 227 (1937), the Supreme Court established the foundational standard for fee awards under fee-shifting statutes. The Court held that the fee is "the product of the hours reasonably expended at a reasonable hourly rate." This "lodestar" method presumes that the rate and hours are reasonable. The Court has never endorsed a separate "quality multiplier" to adjust the lodestar figure. The district court’s finding that the hourly rate was insufficient because of "superb quality" is an impermissible subjective adjustment that violates the objective "reasonable" standard.
