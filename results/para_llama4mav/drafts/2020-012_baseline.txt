## ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act ("RRA") grants federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final decisions" of the Railroad Retirement Board ("the Board"). 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review. This Court should hold that it does.

The RRA's judicial review provision, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), is modeled after the Social Security Act's ("SSA") judicial review provision, 42 U.S.C. § 405(g). See Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99, 105-06 (1977) (noting the similarity between the two provisions). In interpreting the SSA's judicial review provision, the Supreme Court has held that a decision by the Secretary of Health and Human Services not to reopen a prior claim is not reviewable unless it is challenged on constitutional grounds. Id. at 107-09. However, the Court's holding in Sanders was based on the SSA's specific statutory framework, which explicitly precludes review of the Secretary's decision not to reopen a claim absent a colorable constitutional claim. Id. at 108.

In contrast, the RRA does not contain a similar statutory bar to review. See Stovic v. R.R. Ret. Bd., 826 F.3d 500, 504 (D.C. Cir. 2016). Therefore, the reasoning in Sanders does not directly apply to the RRA. The D.C. Circuit's decision in Stovic is persuasive, as it held that the RRA grants circuit courts jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations. Id. at 502.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), which held that the court lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim, was wrongly decided. The Roberts court relied on Sanders, but as explained above, Sanders is distinguishable. The Roberts court also relied on the majority view among the circuits at the time, but the D.C. Circuit's decision in Stovic has since joined the minority view, creating a circuit split.

This Court should follow the reasoning in Stovic and hold that the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a "final decision" subject to judicial review. The RRA's statutory framework does not preclude review, and the Board's decision is a final determination on the merits of Salinas's request to reopen his prior claim.

The Fifth Circuit's rule of orderliness does not preclude this Court from reconsidering Roberts, as the issue presented is a matter of statutory interpretation that can be revisited in light of new authority, such as Stovic. See, e.g., Giddings v. Chandler, 979 F.2d 1104, 1108 (5th Cir. 1992) (noting that a panel may reconsider prior precedent when "the prior panel's decision is clearly erroneous and, if followed, would work a manifest injustice").

Therefore, this Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision dismissing Salinas's petition for review and hold that the Board's decision not to reopen his 2006 application is subject to judicial review.
