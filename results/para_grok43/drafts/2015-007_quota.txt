The Sixth Amendment does not require a jury to find the facts necessary to impose a death sentence under Florida’s scheme. In Proffitt v. Florida, 428 U.S. 242, 252 (1976), the Court upheld Florida’s capital sentencing statute because the trial judge’s findings on aggravating and mitigating circumstances satisfied the need for “guided discretion.” Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447, 459-60 (1984), confirmed that the Sixth Amendment permits a judge to make the ultimate sentencing determination even when a jury has rendered an advisory verdict. Hildwin v. Florida, 490 U.S. 638, 640-41 (1989) (per curiam), likewise held that the Constitution does not mandate a jury finding on the existence of aggravating circumstances.

Ring v. Arizona, 536 U.S. 584, 609 (2002), overruled Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990), only with respect to schemes in which the judge alone finds aggravating facts that increase the maximum penalty. Florida’s statute, by contrast, channels the jury’s advisory recommendation through the same statutory factors reviewed by the judge, preserving the division of labor approved in Proffitt and Spaziano. The requirement of jury unanimity on aggravating circumstances is not compelled by the Sixth Amendment. McKoy v. North Carolina, 494 U.S. 433, 443 (1990), recognized that states may structure capital sentencing procedures without mandating unanimity on every factual predicate, provided the overall scheme channels discretion.

The Eighth Amendment likewise permits Florida to assign the determination of intellectual disability to the court. Atkins v. Virginia, 536 U.S. 304, 317 (2002), expressly left “to the States the task of developing appropriate ways to enforce the constitutional restriction” on executing persons with intellectual disability. Florida’s three-prong statutory test, applied by the trial court after an evidentiary hearing, satisfies that delegation. Buchanan v. Angelone, 522 U.S. 269, 275-76 (1998), confirms that the Eighth Amendment does not require every mitigating fact to be found by a jury when the sentencing authority has been given adequate guidance.

Because Florida’s procedures rest on the holdings of Proffitt, Spaziano, Hildwin, McKoy, and Atkins, they do not violate the Sixth or Eighth Amendments.
