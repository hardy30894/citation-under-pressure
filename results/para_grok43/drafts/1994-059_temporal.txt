ARGUMENT

The Qualifications Clauses of the Constitution fix the exclusive criteria for service in Congress. U.S. Const. art. I, § 2, cl. 2; art. I, § 3, cl. 3. Those clauses were understood at the founding to be both minimum and exclusive. Powell v. McCormack, 395 U.S. 486, 522 (1969) (“the Convention also defeated a proposal to add a property qualification”).

Because the clauses speak to a national legislature whose members “partake of the same national character,” no State may engraft additional eligibility requirements. The power to regulate the “Times, Places and Manner of holding Elections,” U.S. Const. art. I, § 4, cl. 1, does not extend to redefining who may hold the office itself. See Ex parte Siebold, 100 U.S. 371, 388 (1880) (federal authority over congressional elections is “paramount”); In re Green, 134 U.S. 377, 379 (1890) (States may not “interfere with” the federal qualifications fixed by the Constitution).

Any contrary rule would produce the very “hodge-podge of qualifications” the Framers rejected. Powell, 395 U.S. at 533. The Arkansas amendment therefore exceeds the power reserved to the States and is void.
