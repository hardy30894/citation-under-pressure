**Argument**

The Railroad Retirement Act authorizes judicial review of “any final decision” of the Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A Board refusal to reopen a prior benefits claim is a “final decision” because it is the Board’s last word on whether the claimant may obtain further administrative relief, and because such a decision determines substantial rights with practical finality. See *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 826 F.3d 500, 502, 504 (D.C. Cir. 2016). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary rule rests on an unduly cramped reading of § 355(f) and should not be followed.

This Court should begin with the text Congress chose. Section 355(f) authorizes review of “any final decision” of the Board. Congress did not limit review to merits determinations on an initial benefits application, nor did it exclude reopening decisions. *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504. As the D.C. Circuit explained, a reopening denial is itself a final agency action because it conclusively resolves whether the earlier claim will be reconsidered. Id. at 502, 504. That conclusion is consistent with the ordinary understanding of finality: a decision is final when it marks the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking and determines rights or obligations. See *Bennett v. Spear*, 520 U.S. 154, 177–78 (1997).

The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), is misplaced. *Sanders* interpreted the Social Security Act’s review provision, not the Railroad Retirement Act, and it did so in the context of a statute that did not authorize review of reopening denials. Id. at 107–09. The Court there emphasized that reopening decisions are ordinarily unreviewable under the APA because they do not constitute separate “final agency action” absent a jurisdictional basis in the statute. Id. at 107–08. Here, by contrast, § 355(f) supplies that basis by authorizing review of “any final decision” of the Board. *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504. The Railroad Retirement Act thus contains broader language than the statute construed in *Sanders*. See *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139, 141 (5th Cir. 2003) (acknowledging the issue and the split of authority).

The majority view adopted by the Fifth Circuit in *Roberts* is also unpersuasive on first principles. The Seventh Circuit in *Steebe v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 708 F.2d 250, 254–55 (7th Cir. 1983), the Sixth Circuit in *Gutierrez v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990), the Tenth Circuit in *Abbruzzese v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995), and the Fourth Circuit in *Harris v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999), each held that reopening denials are not reviewable. But those decisions treated reopening as an internal housekeeping mechanism rather than a judicially cognizable final determination. That approach reads “any final decision” too narrowly and creates a gap where the Board may conclusively deny reconsideration without any judicial oversight. *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504.

This Court should also consider the consequences of the jurisdictional bar. Here, the Board’s refusal to reopen rested on a finding that Salinas’s 2006 claim fell outside the reopening period. That decision was not tentative or interlocutory; it ended the matter. A refusal to reopen is therefore a final decision in the same sense that the Supreme Court has recognized finality where the agency has “rendered its last word” on the issue. *Bennett*, 520 U.S. at 178. Treating such decisions as unreviewable would insulate the Board’s application of its reopening regulations from judicial scrutiny even when it may have misapplied its own rules. See *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504.

Nor does *Roberts* compel dismissal today. *Roberts* acknowledged the circuit split, 346 F.3d at 141, but a prior panel decision should not be extended beyond what its reasoning requires. The better reading of § 355(f), consistent with *Stovic* and with the Supreme Court’s finality jurisprudence, is that a denial of reopening is a reviewable final decision. See *Bennett*, 520 U.S. at 177–78; *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 502, 504.

Because the Board’s denial of reopening was its final disposition of Salinas’s request for further administrative relief, the court of appeals had jurisdiction under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The dismissal should be reversed, and the case remanded for review on the merits.
