**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE OCSLA’S WORKERS’ COMPENSATION PROVISION REQUIRES THAT AN INJURY OCCUR ON THE OUTER CONTINENTAL SHELF.**

The Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (“OCSLA”) extends Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act (“LHWCA”) coverage to employees injured “as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that this provision does not require the injury itself to occur on the outer Continental Shelf. The Supreme Court’s decision in *Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire*, 477 U.S. 207 (1986), confirms that the OCSLA’s coverage is “determined principally by locale.” *Id.* at 219. The Court specifically noted that § 1333(b) “has no bearing” on a case where the injury did not occur on the outer Continental Shelf. *Id.* at 219 n.2. This dictum, while not dispositive, is entitled to “considerable weight” as a “prophecy of what [the Court] might hold.” *District of Columbia v. Heller*, 128 S. Ct. 2783, 2816 n.25 (2008) (quoting *United States v. Montero-Camargo*, 208 F.3d 1122, 1132 n.17 (9th Cir. 2000)).

The Fifth Circuit’s interpretation of § 1333(b) in *Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs*, 877 F.2d 356 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc), is consistent with the Supreme Court’s guidance. The Fifth Circuit held that an injury must occur on the outer Continental Shelf to trigger OCSLA coverage, reasoning that the statute’s “situs requirement” is “explicit.” *Id.* at 362. This interpretation aligns with the OCSLA’s purpose of extending federal jurisdiction over submerged lands seaward of state waters. *See Shell Oil Co. v. Iowa Dep’t of Revenue*, 488 U.S. 19, 25 (1988) (“Congress intended to provide ‘for the orderly development of offshore resources’”).

The Ninth Circuit’s “substantial nexus” test—under which an injury occurring on land may trigger OCSLA coverage—is unsupported by the statutory text. Section 1333(b) requires that the injury occur “as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” The statute does not extend coverage to injuries that merely bear a “substantial nexus” to such operations. *See Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray*, 470 U.S. 414, 424 (1985) (holding that an injury occurring in state waters is not eligible for OCSLA benefits because the OCSLA’s coverage is tied to the geographic area of the outer shelf).

**II. THE LHWCA’S MARITIME SITUS REQUIREMENT WAS PROPERLY APPLIED TO DENY BENEFITS.**

The LHWCA provides compensation only for injuries occurring on “navigable waters of the United States (including any adjoining pier, wharf, dry dock, terminal, building way, marine railway, or other adjoining area customarily used by an employer in loading, unloading, repairing, or building a vessel).” 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). The Ninth Circuit correctly held that La Conchita, the onshore facility where Mr. Valladolid was killed, is not an “adjoining area” under § 903(a).

The court applied the “functional relationship” test from *Brady-Hamilton Stevedore Co. v. Herron*, 568 F.2d 137, 141 (9th Cir. 1978), which considers factors such as the facility’s suitability for maritime uses, proximity to navigable waters, and whether the site is used as an integral part of longshoring operations. The court found that La Conchita, located 250-300 feet from the ocean and separated from the water by a highway and railroad tracks, is not an adjoining area. This conclusion is consistent with *Motoviloff v. Dir., Office of Workers’ Comp. Programs*, 692 F.2d 87, 89 (9th Cir. 1982), which held that a facility must be physically and functionally integrated with maritime activities to qualify as an adjoining area.

The Ninth Circuit also correctly rejected the argument that La Conchita was a “transshipment” facility under *P.C. Pfeiffer Co. v. Ford*, 444 U.S. 69, 82-83 (1979). Unlike the warehousemen in *Pfeiffer*, who handled cargo directly between ship and land transportation, the scrap metal at La Conchita was transported by third parties and stored for up to two years before being removed. The maritime activities occurred at the Casitas Pass Pier, not at La Conchita. *See Director, Office of Workers’ Comp. Programs v. Perini N. River Assocs.*, 459 U.S. 297, 301 (1983) (holding that the LHWCA’s situs requirement is strictly construed).

**III. THE BRB’S DECISION SHOULD BE AFFIRMED.**

The Benefits Review Board (“BRB”) properly applied the OCSLA’s situs requirement and the LHWCA’s maritime situs requirement to deny benefits. The Ninth Circuit’s decision conflicts with the Supreme Court’s guidance in *Tallentire* and the Fifth Circuit’s interpretation in *Mills*. The BRB’s decision is consistent with the OCSLA’s purpose of extending federal jurisdiction over submerged lands and the LHWCA’s requirement that injuries occur on navigable waters or adjoining areas customarily used for maritime activities.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit’s decision and affirm the BRB’s denial of benefits.
