## ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act ("RRA") grants federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final decisions" of the Railroad Retirement Board ("the Board"). 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review. This Court should hold that it does.

The RRA's judicial review provision, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), is modeled after the Social Security Act's ("SSA") judicial review provision, 42 U.S.C. § 405(g). See Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 708 F.2d 250, 254 (7th Cir. 1983). In Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), this Court held that the SSA's judicial review provision does not permit review of the Secretary's decision not to reopen a prior claim, absent a constitutional challenge. Id. at 107-09. However, as the D.C. Circuit correctly observed in Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), the RRA's judicial review provision contains language not found in the SSA's provision, which "plausibly can be read to expand the scope of judicial review under the RRA beyond that available under the SSA." Id. at 507.

Specifically, the RRA permits review of "any final decision" of the Board, whereas the SSA permits review of "any final decision of the Commissioner of Social Security made after a hearing." 45 U.S.C. § 355(f); 42 U.S.C. § 405(g) (emphasis added). The omission of the "after a hearing" language in the RRA suggests that Congress intended to permit review of a broader range of Board decisions, including those declining to reopen prior claims.

The majority of circuits to have considered this issue, including the Fifth Circuit in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), have held that the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim is not reviewable. However, as the D.C. Circuit noted in Stovic, this interpretation "fails to give effect to the difference between the two statutes' judicial-review provisions." 826 F.3d at 507.

This Court should adopt the reasoning of the D.C. Circuit in Stovic and hold that the Board's decision not to reopen Mr. Salinas's 2006 application is a "final decision" subject to judicial review. The RRA's plain language and the distinction between the RRA and SSA's judicial review provisions support this conclusion.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts, which bound the court below, was wrongly decided. The rule of orderliness, which prevents a panel from reconsidering a prior circuit decision, does not bind this Court. See Vaughan v. Anderson Reg’l Med. Ctr., 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir.), cert. denied, 138 S. Ct. 101 (2017). This Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's dismissal of Mr. Salinas's petition and hold that the Board's decision not to reopen his 2006 application is reviewable.
