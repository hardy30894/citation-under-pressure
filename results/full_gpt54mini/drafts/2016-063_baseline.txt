**Argument**

The jurisdictional question should be answered by the statute’s text and the Supreme Court’s instruction in *Kloeckner v. Solis*, 568 U.S. 41, 133 S. Ct. 596 (2012). Under the Civil Service Reform Act, ordinary review of MSPB decisions lies in the Federal Circuit, 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(1)(A), but “cases of discrimination” are reviewed in district court, 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2). The statute defines those cases as ones in which an employee “has been affected by an action which [he] may appeal to the [MSPB]” and also alleges discrimination prohibited by federal law. 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1); *Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 50-51, 133 S. Ct. at 603-04. Perry’s case fits that definition. He was affected by a removal/suspension/retirement action appealable to the MSPB, and he alleged disability, race, age, and retaliation claims. The Board’s later decision to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction cannot strip district court of review.

In *Kloeckner*, the Court held that a mixed case belongs in district court whether the MSPB resolves it on the merits or dismisses it on procedural grounds. 568 U.S. at 56, 133 S. Ct. at 607. The Court rejected the Government’s attempt to send dismissed mixed cases to the Federal Circuit, explaining that “mixed cases” are to be filed in district court. Id. at 50-52, 56, 133 S. Ct. at 603-04, 607. Nothing in the statutory text authorizes a forum switch merely because the Board labels its ruling “jurisdictional” rather than “procedural.” The reviewing court’s task is to decide whether the employee brought a mixed case, not whether the Board ultimately agreed with the employee’s jurisdictional theory. See *Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 50-52, 133 S. Ct. at 603-04.

That is especially true here because the Board’s jurisdictional ruling was intertwined with Perry’s discrimination allegations. The Board concluded that his suspension and retirement were voluntary because he entered a settlement agreement; Perry argued that the agreement was coerced by discrimination and misinformation about his rights. A federal district court is the proper forum to adjudicate that kind of mixed record, because district courts are the forum Congress selected for discrimination claims under the CSRA. 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2); *Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 50-52, 133 S. Ct. at 603-04. The Federal Circuit, by contrast, lacks the district court’s general authority to hear federal employment-discrimination claims de novo. See *Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 52-53, 133 S. Ct. at 604-05.

The D.C. Circuit’s contrary rule rests on *Powell v. Department of Defense*, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), but *Powell* cannot survive *Kloeckner*. *Powell* predates the Supreme Court’s definitive interpretation of the mixed-case provisions and would force employees to split review based on the Board’s own characterization of its decision. That approach is inconsistent with *Kloeckner*’s holding that “when the MSPB dismisses a mixed case on procedural grounds,” review lies in district court. 568 U.S. at 56, 133 S. Ct. at 607. A jurisdictional dismissal should not receive a different forum from the same mixed case dismissed on timeliness or other threshold grounds. The relevant statutory trigger is the employee’s allegations and the nature of the adverse action, not the Board’s label.

The Federal Circuit itself has recognized the force of that reading in cases following *Kloeckner*. See *Conforto v. Merit Systems Protection Board*, 713 F.3d 1111, 1116-18 (Fed. Cir. 2013). And this Court has repeatedly held that where a lower-court rule cannot be reconciled with an intervening Supreme Court decision, the Supreme Court controls. *Agostini v. Felton*, 521 U.S. 203, 237 (1997); *Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc.*, 490 U.S. 477, 484 (1989). Under those principles, *Powell* cannot govern here.

The better rule is the one the statute and *Kloeckner* require: when a federal employee brings a mixed case and the MSPB disposes of it on threshold grounds, judicial review belongs in district court. Perry therefore should not have been transferred to the Federal Circuit. The judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded with instructions to proceed in district court.
