I. The Evidence Was Insufficient to Prove That Nieves-Castaño Knew the Firearm Was a Machinegun.

A conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 922(o) requires proof that the defendant “knew of the features of his gun that made it a machinegun.” Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600, 619 (1994). The government therefore must establish the defendant’s awareness of the weapon’s automatic-fire capability. Id. at 602.

The applicable standard of review asks “whether, after viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to the prosecution, any rational trier of fact could have found the essential elements of the crime beyond a reasonable doubt.” Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307, 319 (1979).

The sole evidence of knowledge came from the interviewing agent’s testimony that Nieves-Castaño “knew it was an AK-47.” That testimony establishes awareness of the weapon’s make and model, but not of its automatic-fire mechanism. An AK-47 rifle may be configured for either semi-automatic or automatic operation; nothing in the record indicates that Nieves-Castaño was told, observed, or otherwise learned which configuration applied here. The government introduced no testimony that she had fired the weapon, received instructions on its selector switch, or been informed of its classification as a machinegun.

Because the evidence fails to show that Nieves-Castaño possessed the requisite knowledge of the firearm’s automatic features, the conviction under § 922(o) cannot stand.
