**ARGUMENT**

The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Rand Corporation. The Mouas are entitled to rescind the loan because Rand failed to make material disclosures, failed to provide clear notice of the right to cancel, and included an illegal prepayment penalty. Under the Truth in Lending Act (TILA), 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a), a borrower has three business days to rescind a credit transaction secured by a primary residence. If the lender fails to deliver the required material disclosures, the right to rescind extends to three years. That extension applies here.

**I. The Mouas Did Not Receive the Required HOEPA Disclosures, and the Purported Waiver Was Invalid.**

The Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA) requires a lender to provide a separate disclosure statement at least three days before consummation. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b). Rand concedes the Mouas did not receive such advance disclosure. Rand contends the Mouas waived this requirement based on a “bona fide personal financial emergency.” But a waiver of a statutory right must be knowing and voluntary—an “intentional relinquishment or abandonment of a known right.” *Johnson v. Zerbst*, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938). The Mouas did not knowingly waive their rights. Manisy Moua was instructed to copy a typed statement she did not understand. The statement referred vaguely to a “3 day review period” without explaining the import of the waiver. A waiver cannot be predicated on ignorance. *Glover v. United States*, 147 U.S. 520, 525 (1893) (waiver must be “clearly and unequivocally expressed”). The district court’s holding that the waiver was valid as a matter of law disregards the genuine factual dispute over whether the Mouas understood what they were signing. Summary judgment was improper. *Poller v. Columbia Broadcasting System, Inc.*, 368 U.S. 464, 473 (1962) (summary judgment inappropriate where genuine issues of material fact exist).

**II. Rand Failed to Provide Clear and Conspicuous Notice of the Right to Rescind.**

TILA requires that the notice of the right to cancel be “clearly and conspicuously” provided. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a). At closing, the Mouas were presented with a “Confirmation” section on the Notice of Right to Cancel that stated: “More than 3 business days have elapsed since the date of the new transaction.” That statement was false—the closing occurred on April 22, 2005, and the rescission period did not expire until midnight April 26, 2005. The Mouas signed the Confirmation on the same day as the closing. A prospective waiver of a statutory rescission right before the rescission period has run is not valid. *United States v. One 1936 Model Ford V-8 De Luxe Coach*, 307 U.S. 219, 226 (1939) (waiver of statutory right must be “clear and unequivocal” and cannot be inferred from actions taken before the right accrues). Moreover, Excel Title, the closing agent, directed the Mouas to sign the Confirmation. Rand does not deny that Excel Title was its agent. A principal is liable for the acts of its agent within the scope of the agency. *New York Life Ins. Co. v. Eggleston*, 96 U.S. 572, 577 (1877). The Mouas’ signatures on the Confirmation, obtained before the three-day period expired, cannot constitute a valid waiver of their right to cancel.

**III. The Prepayment Penalty Is Prohibited by HOEPA and Triggers the Extended Rescission Period.**

HOEPA explicitly prohibits prepayment penalties for high-cost loans. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c)(1)(A). The Mouas’ loan carries an interest rate exceeding 13%, making it a high-cost loan subject to HOEPA. The inclusion of a prepayment penalty is a material violation of the statute. When a lender fails to make a material disclosure required under TILA or HOEPA, the borrower’s right to rescind extends to three years. The district court’s failure to recognize this violation as a basis for rescission was error. A statutory right to rescind is a substantive right that must be enforced according to the statute’s plain terms. *Shepard v. United States*, 290 U.S. 96, 100 (1933) (statutory language must be given its ordinary meaning).

**IV. Genuine Issues of Material Fact Preclude Summary Judgment.**

The Mouas’ testimony creates factual disputes regarding whether they received the required disclosures, whether they understood the waiver, and whether the Confirmation was signed prematurely. On summary judgment, all inferences must be drawn in favor of the non-moving party. *Poller v. Columbia Broadcasting System, Inc.*, 368 U.S. 464, 473 (1962). The district court improperly resolved these factual disputes against the Mouas. The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for trial.
