The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Rand Corporation because genuine issues of material fact exist regarding whether Rand failed to comply with the Truth in Lending Act (TILA) and the Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA), thereby extending the Mouas' rescission period to three years.

First, the Mouas contend that Rand failed to provide the required three-day advance HOEPA disclosure, which is a material disclosure under TILA. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b); see also Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, 523 U.S. 410, 417-18 (1998) (noting that HOEPA disclosures are considered "material disclosures" under TILA). Rand's claim that the Mouas waived this disclosure due to a bona fide personal financial emergency is disputed. The validity of the waiver is questionable, as the Mouas allege they did not understand the statement they were asked to copy. See 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(e) (requiring that a consumer's waiver of the right to rescind be "in writing, using language designated by the [Federal Reserve] Board"). The Mouas' testimony raises a genuine issue of fact regarding the voluntariness of their waiver.

Second, the Mouas argue that Rand failed to provide clear and conspicuous notice of their right to cancel the transaction within three days, as required by TILA. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a); see also In re Porter, 961 F.2d 1066, 1075-76 (3d Cir. 1992) (holding that a lender's failure to provide clear and conspicuous notice of the right to rescind extends the rescission period). The Mouas signed the Confirmation section of the Notice on April 22, 2005, which is before the three-day rescission period expired. This raises a question about whether the Notice was effective, as it may have been premature. See Aquino v. Public Fin. Consumer Disc. Co., 606 F. Supp. 504, 507 (E.D. Pa. 1985) (noting that a lender's premature confirmation of receipt of notice can render the notice ineffective).

Lastly, the Mouas assert that the loan contains a prepayment penalty prohibited by HOEPA. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c)(1)(A); see also In re Community Bank of N. Va., 418 F.3d 277, 304 (3d Cir. 2005) (holding that a lender's inclusion of a prohibited prepayment penalty can extend the rescission period). The loan documents reveal a significant increase in the interest rate after the initial period, which may be considered a prepayment penalty. See 12 C.F.R. § 226.32(d)(6) (defining a prepayment penalty).

In light of these disputed issues, the district court's grant of summary judgment was improper. The Mouas are entitled to a trial to resolve the factual disputes surrounding Rand's compliance with TILA and HOEPA. See Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986) (noting that summary judgment is only appropriate when there is no genuine issue of material fact). Therefore, the judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
