ARGUMENT

I. The OCSLA Workers’ Compensation Provision Requires a Situs-of-Injury Test

The Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (“OCSLA”) extends workers’ compensation coverage under the Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act (“LHWCA”) to injuries occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf. 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The Fifth Circuit has adopted a situs-of-injury test for OCSLA claims, Mills v. Dir., Office of Workers’ Comp. Programs, 877 F.2d 356, 362 (5th Cir. 1947) (en banc), requiring that the injury occur on an outer continental shelf platform or on the waters above the outer continental shelf. This interpretation is consistent with the LHWCA, which requires that the injury occur on navigable waters, as interpreted in Swanson v. Marra Bros., 328 U.S. 1, 4-5 (1946); see also O’Leary v. Brown-Pacific-Maxon, Inc., 340 U.S. 504 (1951); Parker v. Motor Boat Sales, 314 U.S. 244 (1941). The LHWCA's situs requirement has been consistently applied by the courts, see e.g., State Industrial Comm’n v. Nordenholt Corp., 259 U.S. 263, 272-73 (1922); London Guarantee & Accident Co. v. Indus. Accident Comm’n, 279 U.S. 109 (1929); Nogueira v. New York, N.H. & H.R. Co., 281 U.S. 128 (1930).

II. La Conchita is Not a Maritime Situs

The LHWCA requires that the injury occur on a maritime situs, defined in 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). La Conchita does not meet this definition, as it is not adjacent to navigable waters and is not used for maritime purposes. See e.g., T. Smith & Son, Inc. v. Taylor, 276 U.S. 179 (1928); Minnie v. Port Huron Terminal Co., 295 U.S. 647 (1935). The Ninth Circuit has applied a functional relationship test to determine whether a facility is a maritime situs, considering factors such as the site's suitability for maritime uses and proximity to the waterway, as seen in Brady-Hamilton Stevedore Co. v. Herron, 568 F.2d 137, 141 (9th Cir. 1978). Under this test, La Conchita is not a maritime situs, as it is not used for maritime purposes and is not adjacent to navigable waters.

The primary purpose of La Conchita is to process crude oil slurry, a non-maritime activity, similar to the platform in Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray, 470 U.S. 414 (1985). 

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should affirm the BRB's denial of benefits under the OCSLA and LHWCA.
