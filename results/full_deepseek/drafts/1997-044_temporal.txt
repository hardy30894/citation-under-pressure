**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Plain Language of § 523(a)(2)(A) Limits Nondischargeability to Debts for Property “Obtained By” Fraud.**

Section 523(a)(2)(A) excepts from discharge “any debt . . . for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by . . . actual fraud.” The critical phrase “to the extent obtained by” confines nondischargeability to the value of the property the debtor actually acquired through fraud. This Court has long recognized that bankruptcy exceptions must be narrowly construed. *Neal v. Clark*, 95 U.S. 704, 709 (1877) (fraud exception “must be strictly construed”). The statutory language does not sweep in punitive or statutory damages that are not themselves “obtained by” the fraudulent act.

**II. Treble Damages and Attorney’s Fees Are Not Debts “for Money . . . Obtained By” Fraud.**

Petitioner’s debt for treble damages and attorney’s fees arises from a state consumer fraud statute, not from the rental overcharges he obtained. The overcharges themselves—$31,382.50—constitute money “obtained by” fraud. The treble award and fees are additional penalties imposed by law, not property the debtor received. They are therefore outside the scope of § 523(a)(2)(A).

The Second Circuit, in a line of decisions predating 1970, uniformly held that only the value of property actually acquired by fraud is nondischargeable. *In re Gubelman*, 13 F.2d 732, 733 (2d Cir. 1926) (nondischargeable debt “limited to the amount of money or property obtained by the fraud”); *In re Houtman*, 42 F.2d 860, 861 (2d Cir. 1930) (same); *In re Lovich*, 117 F.2d 612, 614 (2d Cir. 1941) (only “the actual amount obtained” is excepted); *In re Tam*, 136 F.2d 326, 327 (2d Cir. 1943) (same); *In re Wood*, 248 F.2d 573, 575 (2d Cir. 1957) (same). The Seventh Circuit agreed: *In re Dolnick*, 374 F.2d 825, 827 (7th Cir. 1967) (punitive damages not “obtained by” fraud and thus dischargeable).

These cases establish a consistent rule: punitive or statutory damages that exceed the value of the property fraudulently obtained are dischargeable. The treble damages here are punitive in nature. *In re Russell*, 101 F.2d 88, 89 (2d Cir. 1939) (punitive damages “are not for property obtained by fraud” and are dischargeable). Attorney’s fees likewise are not property obtained by the debtor. *In re McCausland*, 130 F. 388, 389 (2d Cir. 1904) (fees not part of the debt “obtained by” fraud).

**III. The “Fresh Start” Policy Does Not Override the Statutory Text.**

The Bankruptcy Code’s “fresh start” policy benefits the “honest but unfortunate debtor.” *Strang v. Bradner*, 114 U.S. 555, 561 (1885). But that policy cannot expand the plain language of § 523(a)(2)(A). Congress chose to except only debts “to the extent obtained by” fraud. It did not except all consequences of fraud. *Gleason v. Thaw*, 236 U.S. 605, 611 (1915) (fraud exception “does not extend to all debts arising out of fraud, but only to those obtained by fraud”). The lower court’s reliance on a supposed “foreseeability” of statutory penalties is irrelevant; the statute looks to what the debtor obtained, not what he might have anticipated.

**IV. The Lower Court’s Decision Conflicts with Settled Pre-1970 Authority.**

The court below adopted the reasoning of *In re St. Laurent*, 991 F.2d 672 (11th Cir. 1993), a post-1970 decision. That case conflicts with the pre-1970 consensus that only compensatory damages are nondischargeable. This Court should follow the earlier, uniform authority. *In re Gubelman*, 13 F.2d at 733; *In re Houtman*, 42 F.2d at 861; *In re Lovich*, 117 F.2d at 614; *In re Tam*, 136 F.2d at 327; *In re Wood*, 248 F.2d at 575; *In re Dolnick*, 374 F.2d at 827. These cases correctly interpret the statutory language and respect the narrow scope of discharge exceptions.

**CONCLUSION**

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed insofar as it holds that treble damages and attorney’s fees are nondischargeable under § 523(a)(2)(A). Only the $31,382.50 in overcharges actually obtained by fraud is excepted from discharge.
