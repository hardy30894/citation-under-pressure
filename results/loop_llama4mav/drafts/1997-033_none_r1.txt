The District of Arizona's decision to invoke 28 USC section 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial directly contravenes the clear and unambiguous language of 28 USC section 1407(a). The statute explicitly mandates that the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation "shall" remand any action transferred for pretrial proceedings to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." This statutory directive is not merely a suggestion, but a binding requirement that must be followed.

The plain meaning of section 1407(a) is controlling, as the Supreme Court has consistently held that statutory provisions should be interpreted in a manner that gives effect to their plain meaning. See, e.g., Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917) ("It is elementary that the meaning of a statute must, in the first instance, be sought in the language in which the act is framed, and if that is plain...the sole function of the courts is to enforce it according to its terms."); United States v. Wiltberger, 18 U.S. 76, 95-96 (1820) (Marshall, C.J.) ("The intention of the legislature is to be collected from the words they employ."). The language of section 1407(a) is plain, and its meaning is clear: cases transferred for pretrial proceedings must be remanded to the original district at or before the conclusion of those proceedings.

Allowing a transferee court to invoke section 1404(a) to retain a case for trial would undermine the statutory scheme established by section 1407(a) and contravene the policy choice reflected in the statute. Section 1407(a) is designed to facilitate the efficient management of pretrial proceedings in multidistrict litigation, while preserving the plaintiff's traditional right to select the forum for trial. See, e.g., Baltimore & Ohio R. Co. v. Kepner, 314 U.S. 44, 52-53 (1941) (emphasizing the importance of the plaintiff's choice of forum). By permitting transferee courts to retain cases for trial, the Ninth Circuit's decision would effectively eviscerate this policy choice and grant transferee courts a power not contemplated by the statute.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that specific statutory provisions take precedence over more general provisions. See, e.g., Ginsberg & Sons v. Popkin, 285 U.S. 204, 208 (1932) ("General language of a statutory provision, although broad enough to include it, will not be held to apply to a matter specifically dealt with in another part of the same enactment."). In this case, the specific mandate of section 1407(a) governs the transfer of cases for pretrial proceedings, and its provisions should be given effect over the more general transfer authority granted by section 1404(a). As the Court noted in Ex parte Collett, 337 U.S. 55, 58 (1949), section 1404(a) is a general transfer provision that must be read in conjunction with other statutory provisions governing the transfer of cases.

Furthermore, the practice of allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial under section 1404(a) is inconsistent with the principles of statutory construction. See, e.g., FTC v. Bunte Bros., 312 U.S. 349, 352-53 (1941) (noting that "the plain words of the statute" should be given effect); United States v. Dickerson, 310 U.S. 554, 562 (1940) ("The meaning to be ascribed...is to be drawn from the statute as a whole."). The Ninth Circuit's decision, as well as other cases that have permitted transferee courts to invoke section 1404(a) to retain cases for trial, fails to give effect to the plain meaning of section 1407(a) and undermines the statutory scheme established by Congress.

In light of these authorities, it is clear that the District of Arizona erred in invoking section 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial. The plain language of section 1407(a) and the principles of statutory construction require that the case be remanded to the original district for trial. The Ninth Circuit's affirmance of the District of Arizona's decision should be reversed, and the case should be remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
