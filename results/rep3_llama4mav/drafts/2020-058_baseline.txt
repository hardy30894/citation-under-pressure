ARGUMENT

I. The Conferral of Temporary Protected Status Under 8 U.S.C. § 1254a Constitutes an "Admission" into the United States Under 8 U.S.C. § 1255

The plain text of 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4) provides that an alien granted Temporary Protected Status (TPS) "shall be considered as being in, and maintaining, lawful status as a nonimmigrant" for purposes of adjustment of status under 8 U.S.C. § 1255. This language indicates that Congress intended for TPS recipients to be treated as nonimmigrants with lawful status, which is a status that is typically associated with admission. See 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15) (defining "nonimmigrant" as an alien who is "admitted to the United States for a specific temporary period").

The Ninth Circuit's decision in Ramirez v. Brown, 852 F.3d 954 (9th Cir. 2017), supports this interpretation. The court held that a grant of TPS constitutes an admission for purposes of § 1255, reasoning that the statutory text and structure indicate that Congress intended for TPS recipients to be eligible for adjustment of status. Id. at 960-61. The Sixth Circuit has also adopted this view, holding that a grant of TPS satisfies the admission requirement under § 1255. Flores v. USCIS, 718 F.3d 548 (6th Cir. 2013).

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has emphasized the importance of considering the statutory context and purpose when interpreting immigration statutes. See I.N.S. v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421, 432 (1987). In this case, the purpose of TPS is to provide temporary relief to aliens who are unable to return to their home countries due to extraordinary conditions. 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(b)(1). Allowing TPS recipients to adjust their status under § 1255 is consistent with this purpose, as it provides a pathway to permanent residence for individuals who have been living in the United States for an extended period.

II. The Government's Interpretation is Inconsistent with the Statutory Text and Purpose

The Government argues that a grant of TPS does not constitute an admission under § 1255, relying on a narrow reading of the statutory text. However, this interpretation is inconsistent with the plain meaning of § 1254a(f)(4) and the statutory context. The Government's view would also undermine the purpose of TPS by creating a barrier to adjustment of status for individuals who have been granted temporary relief.

The Third Circuit's decision in this case, which held that a grant of TPS does not constitute an admission, is based on a flawed analysis that fails to consider the statutory context and purpose. The court's reliance on Hanif v. Att'y Gen., 694 F.3d 479 (3d Cir. 2012), and Taveras v. Att'y Gen., 731 F.3d 281 (3d Cir. 2013), is misplaced, as those cases involved different statutory provisions and did not address the specific issue presented here.

In conclusion, the conferral of Temporary Protected Status under 8 U.S.C. § 1254a constitutes an "admission" into the United States under 8 U.S.C. § 1255. The statutory text, context, and purpose all support this interpretation, which is consistent with the decisions of the Ninth and Sixth Circuits. This Court should reverse the Third Circuit's decision and hold that TPS recipients are eligible for adjustment of status under § 1255.
