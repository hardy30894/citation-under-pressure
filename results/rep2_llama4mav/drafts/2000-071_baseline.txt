The exclusion of the Good News Club from meeting after hours at Milford Central School did not violate the Club's First Amendment free speech rights. The school's community use policy, which prohibits use for "religious purposes," is a permissible restriction on speech in a limited public forum. The policy is viewpoint-neutral small it excludes all religious instruction and worship, regardless of the viewpoint expressed.

The Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit correctly held that the Club's activities were "quintessentially religious" and not merely the teaching of morals from a religious perspective. The Club's meetings involved prayer, Bible lessons, and scripture memorization, which are characteristic of religious worship. See Good News Club v. Milford Central School, 202 F.3d 502 (2d Cir. 2000). The Club's own materials and lesson plans demonstrate that its purpose is to instruct children in Christian values and to encourage them to accept Jesus Christ as their savior. 

The Supreme Court has recognized that the government may restrict speech in a limited public forum as long as the restriction is viewpoint-neutral and reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum. See Rosenberger v. Rector & Visitors of Univ. of Va., 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995). Milford's community use policy meets this standard, as it excludes all religious instruction and worship, regardless of the viewpoint expressed. The policy is not intended to suppress a particular viewpoint, but rather to maintain the school's facilities as a neutral, non-sectarian space for community activities.

Furthermore, permitting the Good News Club to use the school's facilities would have raised Establishment Clause concerns. The Club's activities, which involve prayer and religious instruction, would have created a risk of perceived endorsement by the school. See Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School Dist., 508 U.S. 384, 394-95 (1993). The school's exclusion of the Club was therefore justified by its concern for avoiding an Establishment Clause violation.

In sum, Milford Central School did not violate the Good News Club's First Amendment free speech rights by excluding it from meeting after hours at the school. The school's community use policy is a permissible restriction on speech in a limited public forum, and the exclusion of the Club was justified by the school's concern for avoiding an Establishment Clause violation.
