I. ARGUMENT

The Tax Court erred in denying Meruelo basis for the net transfers. Under the predecessor provisions to 26 U.S.C. § 1366, a shareholder obtains basis in corporate indebtedness when advances are made for his account and he bears the economic burden. See Edwards v. Commissioner, 19 T.C. 275, 279 (1952) (holding that shareholder loans increase basis where funds are supplied directly or through controlled entities and repayment obligation runs to the shareholder).

The netting of intercompany accounts payable and the annual charges against the promissory note executed by Meruelo created direct indebtedness. Substance governs over form in determining whether an obligation runs to the shareholder. See Gregory v. Helvering, 293 U.S. 465, 469 (1935) (recognizing that the incidence of taxation depends upon the substance of the transaction). The undisputed record shows Meruelo supplied the funds through entities he controlled, and the affiliates recorded the net amounts as owing to him. This arrangement satisfies the requirement of an actual economic outlay by the shareholder. See Commissioner v. Court Holding Co., 324 U.S. 331, 334 (1945) (looking to the true nature of the transaction rather than its form).

The Commissioner’s position that the debt ran only to the affiliates ignores the undisputed fact that Meruelo alone bore the repayment risk through his personal loan and ownership interests. See John Kelley Co. v. Commissioner, 326 U.S. 521, 526 (1946) (examining the parties’ intent and economic realities to classify advances as indebtedness). Because the transfers established bona fide indebtedness running directly to Meruelo, he is entitled to the claimed basis.
