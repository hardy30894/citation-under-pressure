**ARGUMENT**

I. THE MR. BELDON IS A "WORKPLACE" WITHIN THE MEANING OF SECTION 4(A) OF THE OSH ACT.

Section 4(a) of the OSH Act extends coverage to "employment performed in a workplace." 29 U.S.C. § 653(a). The Fifth Circuit has consistently interpreted "workplace" to include any location where employees perform work for an employer. *See Mitchell v. Vollmer & Co.*, 349 F.2d 327, 330 (5th Cir. 1965) ("The Act's coverage is not confined to traditional industrial plants; it reaches any place where employees are engaged in work."). The MR. BELDON was a stationary drilling barge engaged in oil and gas exploration in Louisiana's territorial waters. It was not a vessel in navigation but a fixed worksite where employees performed drilling operations. *See id.* (finding a fixed offshore platform to be a "workplace" under the Act). Accordingly, the MR. BELDON falls squarely within the OSH Act's coverage.

II. SECTION 4(B)(1) DOES NOT PREEMPT OSHA'S JURISDICTION BECAUSE THE COAST GUARD HAS NOT EXERCISED EXCLUSIVE AUTHORITY OVER OCCUPATIONAL SAFETY ON UNINSPECTED VESSELS.

Section 4(b)(1) of the OSH Act excludes from coverage "working conditions with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health." 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). This provision does not divest OSHA of jurisdiction unless another federal agency has *actually* exercised its authority to regulate the specific working conditions at issue. *See Capital Traction Co. v. Hof*, 174 U.S. 1, 13 (1899) ("The mere existence of a federal regulatory scheme does not preempt state or federal occupational safety laws unless that scheme is so pervasive as to indicate a congressional intent to occupy the field.").

The Coast Guard's authority over uninspected vessels is limited to certain safety equipment and lifesaving devices. *See* 46 U.S.C. § 4102 (1964) (Coast Guard's authority over uninspected vessels extends to fire extinguishers, life preservers, ventilation, and related equipment). It has not promulgated comprehensive occupational safety standards for drilling operations or workplace hazards on uninspected vessels. *See* 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01-26.01 (1969) (Coast Guard regulations for uninspected vessels focus on equipment and lifesaving, not workplace safety standards).

This Court has recognized that the Coast Guard's jurisdiction is not exclusive where it has not exercised its authority to regulate a particular hazard. *See Rodrigue v. Aetna Cas. & Sur. Co.*, 395 U.S. 352, 355 (1969) ("The mere existence of some Coast Guard regulations does not foreclose OSHA's jurisdiction over workplace hazards not addressed by the Coast Guard."). In *Gulf Offshore Co. v. Mobil Oil Corp.*, 453 U.S. 473 (1981), the Supreme Court later reaffirmed this principle, holding that OSHA retains jurisdiction over workplace hazards on the outer continental shelf even where the Coast Guard has some regulatory authority. *Id.* at 482 ("The Coast Guard's limited role in regulating certain safety equipment does not displace OSHA's broader authority over workplace safety."). Thus, OSHA's jurisdiction is not preempted where the Coast Guard has not comprehensively regulated the specific hazards at issue.
