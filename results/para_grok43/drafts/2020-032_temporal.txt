ARGUMENT

The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that this action became moot upon revision of the challenged policies. As this Court has long recognized, “[t]he duty of this court, as of every other judicial tribunal, is to decide actual controversies by a judgment which can be carried into effect, and not to give opinions upon moot questions or abstract propositions.” Mills v. Green, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895). Once the college eliminated the speech zones and the relevant code provisions, no live controversy remained between the parties concerning prospective relief. See California v. San Pablo & Tulare R.R. Co., 149 U.S. 308, 314 (1893) (“[T]he court is not empowered to decide moot questions or abstract propositions, or to declare, for the government of future cases, principles or rules of law which cannot affect the result as to the thing in issue in the case before it.”).

Petitioners’ request for nominal damages does not revive the extinguished controversy. This Court has required an actual, ongoing dispute capable of judicial resolution through an enforceable judgment. See Muskrat v. United States, 219 U.S. 346, 361 (1911) (jurisdiction exists only where the judgment “affects the rights of the parties”). A bare claim for nominal relief, untethered to any allegation of compensable injury, presents no such controversy. See Aetna Life Ins. Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227, 240–41 (1937) (requiring “a real and substantial controversy admitting of specific relief through a decree of a conclusive character”).

The district court found, and the record confirms, that petitioners alleged no actual damages. Their complaint rested solely on the abstract violation of constitutional rights. Under settled principles, such an abstract grievance supplies no basis for continuing adjudication once the underlying conduct has ceased. See Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126, 129 (1922) (“[The plaintiff] has only the right, possessed by every citizen, to require that the government be administered according to law and that the public moneys be not wasted. Obviously this general right does not entitle him to institute [suit].”).

Because the policies have been superseded and no compensable injury was pleaded, the action is moot. The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
