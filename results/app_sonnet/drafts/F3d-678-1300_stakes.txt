# ARGUMENT

## I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING JMOL BECAUSE FACEBOOK FAILED TO PROVE BY CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE THAT THE PRE-DECEMBER 2002 LEADER2LEADER® PRODUCT EMBODIED EVERY LIMITATION OF THE ASSERTED CLAIMS

### A. Standard of Review

This Court reviews the denial of JMOL under the standard applied by the regional circuit, here the Third Circuit, asking whether the record contains "the minimum quantum of evidence from which a jury might reasonably afford relief." *Lightning Lube, Inc. v. Witco Corp.*, 4 F.3d 1153, 1166 (3d Cir. 1993). Because invalidity must be proven by clear and convincing evidence, *Microsoft Corp. v. i4i Ltd. Partnership*, 564 U.S. 91, 95 (2011), a reasonable jury could not have found invalidity unless the evidence, viewed in that heightened light, compelled that conclusion. Whether a claimed invention was in public use or on sale is a question of law based on underlying facts, and the ultimate legal conclusion is reviewed without deference to the extent the district court's application of law to the facts is at issue. *Delano Farms Co. v. California Table Grape Comm'n*, 655 F.3d 1337, 1344 (Fed. Cir. 2011).

### B. Facebook Did Not Prove That the Leader2Leader® Product Practiced Every Element of the Asserted Claims

Invalidity under the on-sale and public-use bars of 35 U.S.C. § 102(b) requires proof that the product embodies every limitation of the claim. *Zacharin v. United States*, 213 F.3d 1366, 1370 (Fed. Cir. 2000) ("Invalidity of a patent claim based on a prior art reference or activity requires that each element of the claim in issue be found, either expressly or under principles of inherency, in a single prior art reference[.]"). The party asserting invalidity bears the burden of identifying, with particularity, where each claim limitation is met by the allegedly invalidating device. *TypeRight Keyboard Corp. v. Microsoft Corp.*, 374 F.3d 1151, 1157 (Fed. Cir. 2004).

Here, the trial record contained no competent evidence—let alone clear and convincing evidence—that the pre-2002 Leader2Leader® product performed the specific claimed functions of "dynamically associating metadata," "tracking movement of the user" between environments, and "dynamically updating the stored metadata" to reflect that movement, as required by claim 9 and its dependents. '761 patent, col.21 ll.38-58. Facebook's expert relied almost exclusively on marketing materials—principally the January 2002 white paper presented at Wright Patterson Air Force Base—rather than on the source code, system architecture documents, or technical testimony establishing that the underlying Digital Leaderboard® engine actually performed these operations before the critical date.

Marketing puffery describing a product as "already commercializing," J.A. 27203, or "[f]ully developed," J.A. 27204, is not substantive evidence of the internal technical operation of software. This Court has cautioned that promotional statements, without corroborating technical proof, are insufficient to establish that a device practices specific claim limitations. *Juicy Whip, Inc. v. Orange Bang, Inc.*, 292 F.3d 728, 737-38 (Fed. Cir. 2002) (rejecting reliance on general product descriptions untethered to claim-by-claim analysis). The white paper's reference to a "Browser" as an "Input & Display Collaboration Device," J.A. 27210, and its rhetoric about breaking through communications "silos," J.A. 27202, describe a user-facing concept, not the specific metadata-tracking architecture the claims require.

Mr. McKibben's testimony that the claims cover the "underlying engine," J.A. 25585-86, does not supply the missing technical proof. A patentee's general characterization of what his invention covers is not an admission that a specific pre-critical-date embodiment practiced every limitation. *SRI Int'l, Inc. v. Internet Sec. Sys., Inc.*, 511 F.3d 1186, 1192 (Fed. Cir. 2008) (limitation-by-limitation comparison required; conclusory testimony is inadequate). Likewise, Mr. Lamb's estimate that the project was completed in the "2002ish time frame," J.A. 24829, is precisely the kind of imprecise, uncorroborated recollection this Court has held insufficient to satisfy the clear-and-convincing standard for prior invalidating activity. *Woodland Trust v. Flowertree Nursery, Inc.*, 148 F.3d 1368, 1373 (Fed. Cir. 1998) ("Oral testimony ... is regarded with skepticism, and clear and convincing evidence is required to corroborate it.").

### C. The District Court Improperly Allowed the Jury to Infer Claim Limitations from General Product Descriptions

The district court's post-trial opinion, *Leader Techs., Inc. v. Facebook, Inc.*, 770 F.Supp.2d 686 (D. Del. 2011), affirmed the verdict based on the same undifferentiated marketing evidence, without requiring Facebook to map specific claim terms—such as "dynamically updating the stored metadata with an association of the data, the application, and the second user environment"—onto specific, dated technical evidence of the Leader2Leader® system's actual code or architecture. '761 patent, col.21 ll.53-56. This Court has repeatedly held that "[i]t is not enough to show that a claimed invention was obvious or even that all elements were known; the elements must be present, arranged as in the claim." *Net MoneyIN, Inc. v. VeriSign, Inc.*, 545 F.3d 1359, 1371 (Fed. Cir. 2008). By permitting the jury to infer the "tracking movement of the user" and "dynamically updating" limitations from a single sample "Big Board" diagram, J.A. 27210, and generalized commercialization statements, the district court effectively relieved Facebook of its burden to prove invalidity limitation-by-limitation under the clear and convincing standard.

### D. Conclusion

Because no reasonable jury could have found, on this record, that Facebook proved by clear and convincing evidence that the pre-critical-date Leader2Leader® product embodied every limitation of the asserted claims, the district court erred in denying Leader's motion for JMOL. This Court should reverse the judgment of invalidity and remand for entry of judgment in Leader's favor, or, in the alternative, order a new trial. *Unisplay, S.A. v. American Elec. Sign Co.*, 69 F.3d 512, 517 (Fed. Cir. 1995) (reversing denial of JMOL where verdict was not supported by substantial evidence under the clear and convincing standard).
