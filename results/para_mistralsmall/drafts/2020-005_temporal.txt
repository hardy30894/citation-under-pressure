**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Due Process Clause Permits Specific Jurisdiction Where a Defendant’s Forum Activities Are Substantially Related to the Claims, Even If the Claims Did Not Arise from the Defendant’s Contacts with the Forum.**

The Due Process Clause does not require that a defendant’s forum contacts be the *proximate cause* of the plaintiff’s injury; it requires only that the defendant have sufficient contacts with the forum such that the exercise of jurisdiction is consistent with “traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.” *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 316 (1945). This Court has long held that a state may exercise specific jurisdiction over a nonresident defendant when the defendant’s forum activities are “so continuous and systematic as to render [it] essentially at home in the forum state” and when the claims “arise out of or relate to” those activities. *Perkins v. Benguet Consol. Mining Co.*, 342 U.S. 437, 447 (1952) (emphasis added). The phrase “arise out of or relate to” does not demand a causal nexus between the defendant’s forum conduct and the plaintiff’s injury; it merely requires that the claims be connected to the defendant’s forum activities in some meaningful way. *See McGee v. International Life Ins. Co.*, 355 U.S. 220, 223 (1957) (upholding jurisdiction where the defendant’s forum activity—issuing a policy to a resident—was “a substantial connection” with the forum, even though the policy was issued in another state).

Here, Ford’s systematic and continuous contacts with Montana—including advertising, selling parts, and providing service and support—establish that it purposefully availed itself of the benefits of conducting business in the state. *See* *Hanson v. Denckla*, 357 U.S. 235, 253 (1958) (noting that jurisdiction is proper where a defendant “purposefully avails itself of the privilege of conducting activities within the forum”). The claims against Ford—design defect, failure to warn, and negligence—relate directly to its Montana activities, as Ford’s design and warning decisions affect all vehicles it sells, including those in Montana. *See* *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980) (noting that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant’s forum activities are “purposefully directed” at the forum).

**II. Ford’s Nationwide Business Model Subjects It to Jurisdiction in Any State Where It Conducts Business, Including Montana.**

This Court has recognized that a defendant engaged in interstate commerce may be subject to jurisdiction in any state where it conducts business, provided the claims relate to those activities. *See* *McGee*, 355 U.S. at 223 (upholding jurisdiction where the defendant’s insurance business was conducted in the forum state, even though the policy was issued elsewhere). Ford’s nationwide distribution network—including sales, service, and parts distribution—demonstrates that it has purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *See* *Gray v. American Radiator & Standard Sanitary Corp.*, 22 Ill. 2d 432, 440 (1961) (holding that a manufacturer’s nationwide distribution system subjected it to jurisdiction in any state where its products were sold).

The fact that the vehicle in question was first sold outside Montana does not defeat jurisdiction, as Ford’s ongoing business activities in Montana—including advertising, parts sales, and service—create a sufficient connection to the forum. *See* *Kulko v. Superior Court*, 436 U.S. 84, 96 (1978) (noting that jurisdiction is proper where a defendant’s forum activities are “purposefully directed” at the forum, even if the injury occurred elsewhere).

**III. The Due Process Clause Does Not Require a Direct Causal Link Between the Defendant’s Forum Contacts and the Plaintiff’s Claims.**

The Due Process Clause does not demand that a defendant’s forum contacts be the *direct cause* of the plaintiff’s injury; it requires only that the defendant have sufficient contacts with the forum such that jurisdiction is fair and reasonable. *See* *International Shoe*, 326 U.S. at 316. This Court has upheld jurisdiction where the defendant’s forum activities were merely “related to” the claims, even if the injury occurred elsewhere. *See* *McGee*, 355 U.S. at 223 (upholding jurisdiction where the defendant’s forum activity—issuing a policy to a resident—was “a substantial connection” with the forum, even though the policy was issued in another state).

Here, Ford’s Montana activities—advertising, selling parts, and providing service—are sufficiently related to the claims to justify jurisdiction, as the design and warning decisions at issue affect all vehicles Ford sells, including those in Montana. *See* *Perkins*, 342 U.S. at 447 (holding that jurisdiction is proper where the claims “arise out of or relate to” the defendant’s forum activities).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Due Process Clause permits Montana to exercise specific jurisdiction over Ford, as Ford’s systematic and continuous contacts with the state are sufficiently related to the claims to satisfy traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice. The judgment of the Montana Supreme Court should be affirmed.
