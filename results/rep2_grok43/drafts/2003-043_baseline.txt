**ARGUMENT**

The IRS’s timely assessment against the Partnership extended the ten-year collection period under 26 U.S.C. § 6502(a) only with respect to the Partnership. Because no assessment was made against Debtors within the three-year period prescribed by 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a), the statute of limitations bars collection from them.

Section 6203 requires that an assessment “be made by recording the liability of the taxpayer.” 26 U.S.C. § 6203. The Code defines “taxpayer” to include both a “partnership” and an “individual.” 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14), (a)(1). The implementing regulation mandates that a valid assessment “shall provide identification of the taxpayer.” 26 C.F.R. § 301.6203-1. Debtors, each possessing a distinct taxpayer identification number, are therefore separate taxpayers from the Partnership. An assessment naming only the Partnership cannot extend the limitations period applicable to them. See United States v. Coson, 286 F.2d 453, 458 (9th Cir. 1961) (lien invalid where assessment failed to identify the individual taxpayer).

This Court’s decision in Young v. Riddell, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir. 1960), does not compel a different result. There the partner had already paid the tax; the opinion addressed only his entitlement to a refund and did not hold that an assessment against the partnership alone permits involuntary collection from an unassessed partner. Id. at 910. The district court in Young expressly recognized that, absent payment, the IRS would have to “resort[] to court action” or make an individual assessment. Young v. Riddell, 60-1 U.S. Tax Cas. (CCH) ¶ 9831, at 76,050 (S.D. Cal. 1959).

California partnership law reinforces the same conclusion. A judgment against the partnership “is not by itself a judgment against a partner,” and a partner’s personal assets may not be reached unless “there is also a judgment against the partner.” Cal. Corp. Code § 16307(c). The IRS obtained no such judgment, and the three-year period for doing so has expired. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a). The Bankruptcy Code therefore permits disallowance of the claims as “unenforceable against the debtor.” 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1).

The Seventh Circuit’s decision in United States v. Wright, 57 F.3d 561 (7th Cir. 1995), is inapposite; there the IRS had assessed both the partnership and the individual partners. Id. at 562. No published Ninth Circuit authority supports the proposition that an assessment against a partnership alone extends the ten-year collection period to unassessed general partners. The judgments below should be affirmed.
