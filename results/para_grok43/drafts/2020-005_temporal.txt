The Due Process Clause permits a state court to exercise specific personal jurisdiction over a nonresident corporation when the corporation has purposefully directed its activities at the forum and the litigation bears a substantial connection to those activities. International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310, 316 (1945). Jurisdiction is proper if the defendant’s contacts are such that maintenance of the suit “does not offend ‘traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.’” Id. at 316 (quoting Milliken v. Meyer, 311 U.S. 457, 463 (1940)).

Ford Motor Company has long maintained continuous and systematic contacts with Montana by advertising its vehicles, selling replacement parts, and maintaining a network of authorized dealers throughout the State. These activities constitute purposeful availment of the privileges of conducting business in Montana. McGee v. International Life Insurance Co., 355 U.S. 220, 223 (1957). In McGee the Court upheld jurisdiction over an insurer whose sole contact was a single contract with substantial connection to the forum; the same principle applies when a manufacturer systematically markets and services its products in the forum. Id. at 223.

The claims here arise from an alleged defect in a Ford vehicle. Because Ford’s Montana activities include the promotion and servicing of the very class of products at issue, the litigation possesses the required substantial connection to those contacts. Hanson v. Denckla, 357 U.S. 235, 253 (1958) (jurisdiction requires “some act by which the defendant purposefully avails itself of the privilege of conducting activities within the forum State”). The absence of a direct causal link between the particular vehicle and Ford’s Montana sales does not defeat jurisdiction when the defendant’s forum conduct is both extensive and directly related to the product line giving rise to the claim. See Perkins v. Benguet Consolidated Mining Co., 342 U.S. 437, 445 (1952) (recognizing that continuous forum activity supports jurisdiction over claims connected to the defendant’s general course of business).

Requiring a strict causal nexus would permit a corporation that deliberately exploits a forum’s market to evade accountability for injuries its products cause there, contrary to the flexible standard of International Shoe. 326 U.S. at 319. Ford’s purposeful Montana contacts therefore satisfy due process.
