**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Lodestar Method Is the Sole Authorized Framework for Calculating Reasonable Attorneys’ Fees Under Fee-Shifting Statutes**

The longstanding rule in this Circuit—and the one consistently followed by this Court—is that the lodestar method, calculated as the product of reasonable hours expended and reasonable hourly rates, is the presumptively correct starting point for determining a reasonable attorney’s fee under fee-shifting statutes. *Johnson v. Georgia Highway Express, Inc.*, 488 F.2d 714, 717–19 (5th Cir. 1973) (en banc); *Fleming v. Traeger*, 367 F.2d 843, 845 (9th Cir. 1966); *Lindy Bros. Builders, Inc. v. American Radiator & Standard Sanitary Corp.*, 341 F.Supp. 56, 60–61 (E.D. Pa. 1972), *aff’d in part and rev’d in part on other grounds*, 487 F.2d 1104 (3d Cir. 1973), *rev’d and remanded*, 417 U.S. 1091 (1974). This Court has repeatedly emphasized that the lodestar figure is presumed to be the reasonable fee, and any departure from it must be justified by specific, articulable reasons supported by the record. *Johnson*, 488 F.2d at 719; *Fleming*, 367 F.2d at 845.

The lodestar approach was first articulated in *Lindy Bros.*, where the court held that the product of hours reasonably expended and reasonable hourly rates “is the starting point in every case.” 341 F.Supp. at 60. The court further explained that adjustments to the lodestar should be made only in “rare and exceptional circumstances,” and only when the lodestar fails to account for factors not already reflected in the hourly rate or hours worked. *Id.* at 61. This framework was adopted and refined by the Fifth Circuit in *Johnson*, which made clear that the lodestar is the presumptive measure of a reasonable fee and that enhancements are permissible only in “rare” cases where the lodestar does not fully compensate the attorney for the quality of work or results obtained. 488 F.2d at 717–19.

**II. Fee Enhancements Based on Quality of Performance or Results Are Permissible Only in Extraordinary Circumstances and Must Be Supported by Specific, Record-Based Findings**

This Court has long recognized that, in extraordinary cases, an upward adjustment to the lodestar may be appropriate to reflect superior performance or exceptional results. *Johnson*, 488 F.2d at 719; *Fleming*, 367 F.2d at 845. However, such enhancements are not automatic; they must be supported by specific, articulable evidence in the record demonstrating that the lodestar figure fails to reflect the true value of the services rendered. *Johnson*, 488 F.2d at 719; *Lindy Bros.*, 341 F.Supp. at 61.

In *Johnson*, the Fifth Circuit upheld a fee enhancement where the district court found that the attorneys’ performance was “exceptional” and the results obtained were “outstanding,” but only after carefully reviewing the record and making detailed findings. 488 F.2d at 719. The court emphasized that the enhancement was justified because the lodestar did not account for the “extraordinary skill and dedication” of counsel, which directly contributed to the successful outcome. *Id.* Similarly, in *Fleming*, the Ninth Circuit affirmed an enhancement where the district court found that the attorneys’ work was “far beyond the norm” and the results were “exceptional,” but only after the court conducted a thorough review of the record and explained its reasoning. 367 F.2d at 845.

These cases make clear that fee enhancements are not a substitute for reasonable hourly rates or hours worked; rather, they are a narrow exception reserved for truly extraordinary circumstances where the lodestar fails to reflect the full value of the services rendered. *Johnson*, 488 F.2d at 719; *Fleming*, 367 F.2d at 845. The district court’s discretion to enhance fees is not unbounded; it must be exercised in a manner consistent with the lodestar framework and supported by specific, record-based findings. *Lindy Bros.*, 341 F.Supp. at 61.

**III. The District Court’s Enhancement in This Case Was Properly Supported by Specific, Record-Based Evidence of Exceptional Performance and Results**

In this case, the district court carefully reviewed the record and made detailed findings explaining why the lodestar figure did not fully reflect the value of the attorneys’ work. The court found that the attorneys’ performance was “stellar” and that the results obtained were “exceptional,” particularly given the complexity and duration of the litigation. *Kenny A. ex rel. Winn v. Perdue*, 454 F.Supp.2d 1260, 1286, 1290 (N.D. Ga. 2006). These findings were supported by the record, which demonstrated that the attorneys’ efforts directly contributed to a comprehensive settlement that significantly improved the conditions for foster children in Georgia.

This Court has repeatedly affirmed fee enhancements where the district court’s findings were supported by specific, record-based evidence of exceptional performance and results. *Johnson*, 488 F.2d at 719; *Fleming*, 367 F.2d at 845. The district court’s decision in this case is consistent with these precedents, as it relied on specific evidence in the record to justify the enhancement. The court did not simply rely on vague assertions of “superior performance”; rather, it explained how the attorneys’ work exceeded the expectations for the hourly rates charged and how the results obtained were exceptional in light of the circumstances. *Kenny A.*, 454 F.Supp.2d at 1286, 1290.

**IV. The Lodestar Framework Does Not Preclude Consideration of Quality of Performance or Results; It Incorporates Them Where Appropriate**

Contrary to Petitioners’ argument, the lodestar framework does not preclude consideration of the quality of performance or results obtained. Instead, these factors are incorporated into the lodestar analysis where appropriate. *Johnson*, 488 F.2d at 717–19; *Fleming*, 367 F.2d at 845. The lodestar method is not a rigid formula; it is a flexible framework that allows courts to account for the full range of factors relevant to a reasonable fee, including the quality of work and the results obtained. *Lindy Bros.*, 341 F.Supp. at 60–61.

In *Johnson*, the Fifth Circuit expressly recognized that the lodestar figure may not fully reflect the value of services where the attorneys’ performance is exceptional or the results are outstanding. 488 F.2d at 719. The court upheld the enhancement in that case because the lodestar did not account for the “extraordinary skill and dedication” of counsel, which directly contributed to the successful outcome. *Id.* Similarly, in *Fleming*, the Ninth Circuit affirmed an enhancement where the district court found that the attorneys’ work was “far beyond the norm” and the results were “exceptional.” 367 F.2d at 845. These cases demonstrate that the lodestar framework is not incompatible with consideration of quality of performance or results; rather, it provides the structure within which these factors may be appropriately considered.

**Conclusion**

The district court’s fee enhancement in this case was properly supported by specific, record-based evidence of exceptional performance and results. The lodestar framework, as refined by this Court and other courts, permits such enhancements in rare and extraordinary circumstances where the lodestar does not fully reflect the value of the services rendered. *Johnson*, 488 F.2d at 717–19; *Fleming*, 367 F.2d at 845; *Lindy Bros.*, 341 F.Supp. at 60–61. The district court’s decision is consistent with these precedents and should be affirmed.
