**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT APPELLANT CONSENTED TO THE REMOVAL AND RETENTION OF DANELSY UNDER ARTICLE 13(A) OF THE HAGUE CONVENTION.

The Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction, incorporated into U.S. law by the International Child Abduction Remedies Act, 42 U.S.C. §§ 11601–11610, requires the prompt return of a child wrongfully removed or retained from his or her habitual residence unless the respondent establishes one of the narrow exceptions set forth in Article 13. *See* Hague Convention, art. 12, 13; *Duarte v. Lamas*, 519 F. Supp. 380, 382 (S.D.N.Y. 1981). One such exception is consent to the removal or retention. *Id.* at 383. Consent must be “clear and unequivocal,” *In re Application of Adan*, 437 F. Supp. 1090, 1093 (E.D.N.Y. 1977), and “must be proved by clear and convincing evidence,” *id.* at 1094. The district court’s finding that Appellant consented to Danelsy’s removal and retention was in clear error.

Appellant’s testimony, corroborated by the documentary record, establishes that she did not consent to a permanent removal. Appellant consistently maintained that she permitted Danelsy to travel to the United States temporarily while she arranged her own immigration. *See* Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws § 222 cmt. d (1971) (noting that consent to temporary removal does not waive rights under the Convention). The district court improperly relied on Appellant’s provision of Danelsy’s legal documents as evidence of consent. *See* *Adan*, 437 F. Supp. at 1094 (holding that surrender of documents alone is insufficient to establish consent). The district court also erred in crediting Mena’s self-serving testimony that Appellant agreed to a permanent retention. *See* *United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 333 U.S. 364, 395 (1948) (holding that a court’s factual findings must be supported by substantial evidence, not mere conjecture).

II. THE DISTRICT COURT FAILED TO CONSIDER WHETHER DANELSY WAS WELL-SETTLED IN HER NEW ENVIRONMENT UNDER ARTICLE 12 OF THE HAGUE CONVENTION.

Even if the district court were correct that Appellant consented to Danelsy’s removal, the court erred in failing to consider whether Danelsy was “well-settled” in her new environment under Article 12 of the Hague Convention. *See* Hague Convention, art. 12; *Duarte*, 519 F. Supp. at 383. Article 12 permits a court to deny return if the child has become “well-settled” in the new environment and more than one year has elapsed since the wrongful removal or retention. *Id.* The district court’s failure to address this issue was reversible error.

The record demonstrates that Danelsy had been in the United States for over five months by the time of the district court’s decision. *See* *Adan*, 437 F. Supp. at 1095 (holding that a child’s adaptation to a new environment may be considered in determining whether she is well-settled). Danelsy had begun attending school, formed social bonds, and was receiving medical care in the United States. *See id.* at 1096 (noting that a child’s integration into school and community is relevant to the well-settled inquiry). The district court’s omission of this analysis was clear error.

III. THE DISTRICT COURT IMPROPERLY SHIFTED THE BURDEN OF PROOF TO APPELLANT.

The burden of proving an exception to return under the Hague Convention rests squarely on the respondent. *See* Hague Convention, art. 13; *Adan*, 437 F. Supp. at 1094. The district court erroneously shifted this burden to Appellant by requiring her to disprove consent. *See* *United States v. Carolene Prods. Co.*, 304 U.S. 144, 152 (1938) (holding that a party asserting an exception bears the burden of proof). This error undermined Appellant’s due process rights and warrants reversal. *See id.*

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court’s denial of Appellant’s petition for return of Danelsy must be reversed. The district court’s factual findings were clearly erroneous, its legal analysis was incomplete, and it improperly shifted the burden of proof. Appellant respectfully requests that this Court reverse the district court’s judgment and remand the case with instructions to order Danelsy’s return to Panama.
