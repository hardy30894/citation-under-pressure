**ARGUMENT**

I. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE “INTERNAL PERSONNEL RULES AND PRACTICES” UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 2.

FOIA Exemption 2 permits withholding only documents “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Ninth Circuit’s expansive interpretation of this exemption conflicts with the Supreme Court’s instruction that FOIA exemptions must be “narrowly construed.” *Dep’t of Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 361 (1976) (quoting *S.Rep. No. 813, 89th Cong., 1st Sess. 3* (1965)). The Court has emphasized that Exemption 2 applies only to “trivial administrative matters,” *id.* at 369 (quoting *S.Rep. No. 813 at 8*), and not to documents that, like the ESQD arc maps, are used for external safety planning and risk mitigation.

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), is misplaced. *Hardy* involved a law enforcement manual governing search warrants—materials that were “internal” in the sense of being operational protocols for agency personnel. *Id.* at 656. The ESQD arc maps, by contrast, are not “personnel rules” at all; they are technical safety calculations used to protect the public and surrounding communities. *See* *Milner v. Dep’t of Navy*, 563 F.3d 1111, 1120 (9th Cir. 2009) (noting that ESQD data “aids the Navy in storing ordnance in such a way that the risk of chain reactions . . . is minimized”). The Supreme Court has cautioned that Exemption 2 does not shield documents merely because they are “used by the agency in its internal operations.” *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 369. The Ninth Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test thus distorts the statutory text.

This Court should reject the D.C. Circuit’s *Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 670 F.2d 1051 (D.C. Cir. 1981), approach, which the Ninth Circuit adopted. *Crooker* expanded Exemption 2 beyond its statutory bounds by allowing withholding of any document that is “predominantly internal” and poses a circumvention risk. *Id.* at 1073-74. But the Supreme Court has repeatedly warned against such expansive readings of FOIA exemptions. *See, e.g.,* *EPA v. Mink*, 410 U.S. 73, 87 (1973) (Exemption 1 must be “narrowly construed”); *FTC v. Grolier Inc.*, 462 U.S. 19, 27 (1983) (Exemption 4 applies only to “confidential” commercial information). The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of *Crooker* thus contravenes the Supreme Court’s admonition that FOIA exemptions are “explicitly exclusive.” *Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989) (quoting *Adm’r, FAA v. Robertson*, 422 U.S. 255, 262 (1975)).

II. THE ESQD ARC MAPS DO NOT POSE A RISK OF CIRCUMVENTING AGENCY REGULATION.

The Ninth Circuit further erred in holding that disclosure of the ESQD arc maps could “circumvent agency regulation.” *Milner*, 563 F.3d at 1120. The Supreme Court has made clear that Exemption 2’s circumvention risk applies only to documents that, if disclosed, would “frustrate the effective operation of a statute or regulation.” *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 379 (quoting *S.Rep. No. 813 at 8*). The ESQD arc maps do not implicate this concern. They are not rules or procedures governing agency conduct; they are technical data used to ensure compliance with safety regulations. *See* *Milner*, 563 F.3d at 1120 (describing ESQD data as a “long-term planning tool”).

The D.C. Circuit’s decision in *Crooker* is again inapposite. There, the court upheld withholding of a law enforcement manual because disclosure could enable targets to evade raids. *Crooker*, 670 F.2d at 1074. The ESQD arc maps, by contrast, are not operational protocols but safety calculations. The Supreme Court has distinguished between documents that “facilitate the operation of government” and those that merely “inform the public.” *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 372. The ESQD arc maps fall into the latter category.

III. THE NINTH CIRCUIT’S HOLDING CONFLICTS WITH THIS COURT’S PRECEDENT.

This Court has long held that Exemption 2 applies only to “trivial” administrative matters. *See, e.g.,* *Soucie v. David*, 448 F.2d 1067, 1078 (D.C. Cir. 1971) (Exemption 2 covers only “routine matters”); *Getman v. NLRB*, 450 F.2d 38, 43 (D.C. Cir. 1971) (Exemption 2 does not shield “policy statements or manuals”). The ESQD arc maps are neither. They are critical safety documents that inform the public about risks posed by naval operations. *See* *Milner*, 563 F.3d at 1120. The Ninth Circuit’s contrary holding thus conflicts with this Court’s precedent.

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that the ESQD arc maps are exempt under FOIA Exemption 2. The maps are not “internal personnel rules and practices,” nor do they pose a risk of circumventing agency regulation. The judgment below should be reversed.
