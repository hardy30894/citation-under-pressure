ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Correctly Concluded That Appellees Were Not Joint Employers Under the AWPA.

The AWPA defines “employ” to include “suffer or permit to work,” 29 U.S.C. § 1802(5), a standard “not intended to expand the definition of ‘employee’ under the FLSA.” Aimable v. Long & Scott Farms, 20 F.3d 434, 439 (11th Cir. 1994). Joint employment exists only when “two or more employers . . . are not completely disassociated with respect to the employment of a particular employee.” 29 C.F.R. § 500.20(h)(5); see also Antenor v. D & S Farms, 88 F.3d 925, 929 (11th Cir. 1996). Courts weigh six factors derived from Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb, 331 U.S. 722 (1947), none of which is dispositive. Aimable, 20 F.3d at 439-44.

The Burtons exercised no control over the manner or means of harvesting. Luxama alone “directed and supervised the harvest,” set piece rates, and paid the workers. The Burtons’ twice-daily observations constituted mere “sporadic monitoring” insufficient to establish control. Id. at 441. Little Rock and Hall supplied boxes and a trailer but “did not supervise or direct the workers in any way.” Id. at 442. The workers’ brief, one-half-day engagement in 1990 and seasonal returns thereafter demonstrate that the relationship was not “permanent or exclusive.” Id. at 443. Because the undisputed facts satisfy none of the Rutherford factors in appellants’ favor, appellees were not joint employers.

II. The District Court Properly Limited Recovery for the Registration Violation to Statutory Damages.

Section 1842 requires a person who “utilizes” the services of a farm labor contractor to verify registration. The Burtons concededly failed to verify Luxama’s registration. The AWPA, however, authorizes “actual damages . . . or statutory damages of up to $500 per plaintiff per violation.” 29 U.S.C. § 1854(c)(1). Where plaintiffs prove no actual loss causally linked to the registration violation itself, only statutory damages are available. See Washington v. Miller, 721 F.2d 797, 803 (11th Cir. 1983) (construing parallel MSPA provision). Appellants’ injuries arose from the uninsured vehicle, not from the failure to confirm registration papers. The district court therefore correctly denied actual damages.

III. Little Rock and Hall Did Not “Utilize” Luxama’s Services.

Section 1842 liability attaches only to one who “utilizes” a contractor’s services. Little Rock advanced funds and received produce but never “engaged” Luxama or directed his crew. The district court’s finding that Little Rock “had not utilized the services of Luxama” is consistent with the statutory text and Aimable’s emphasis on direct engagement. 20 F.3d at 440. Summary judgment was therefore proper.

Conclusion

The judgment should be affirmed.
