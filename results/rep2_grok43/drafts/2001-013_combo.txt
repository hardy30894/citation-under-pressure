The Fifth Circuit erred in holding that Coast Guard authority over vessels displaces OSHA jurisdiction on uninspected barges operating in state waters. Preemption of one federal agency by another requires a clear statutory command that the second agency has exercised comprehensive regulatory power. The Osceola, 189 U.S. 158, 175 (1903); Southern Pacific Co. v. Jensen, 244 U.S. 205, 216 (1917). No such command appears for uninspected vessels, whose limited equipment rules leave working conditions unregulated. Knickerbocker Ice Co. v. Stewart, 253 U.S. 149, 160 (1920); Washington v. W.C. Dawson & Co., 264 U.S. 219, 227 (1924).

Congress has long preserved concurrent federal authority in territorial waters where maritime uniformity is not threatened. Panama R.R. Co. v. Johnson, 264 U.S. 375, 388 (1924); Crowell v. Benson, 285 U.S. 22, 39 (1932). An uninspected drilling barge in Louisiana inland waters falls within this concurrent sphere. Seas Shipping Co. v. Sieracki, 328 U.S. 85, 95 (1946). The Coast Guard’s sparse rules for such craft do not occupy the field of occupational safety. Pope & Talbot, Inc. v. Hawn, 346 U.S. 406, 409 (1953); Mitchell v. Trawler Racer, Inc., 362 U.S. 539, 544 (1960).

Section 4(b)(1) therefore leaves OSHA free to enforce the Act on Rig 52. The judgment should be reversed.
