**ARGUMENT**

**I. The ADEA Exemption for Policymakers**

The ADEA defines "employee" to exclude "an appointee on the policymaking level." 29 U.S.C. § 630(f). The Eighth Circuit correctly determined that judges perform policymaking functions. *United States v. Butler*, 297 U.S. 1 (1936). The statute’s exclusion of policymakers is a valid exercise of the State’s power to define its judiciary. *United States v. Darby*, 312 U.S. 100 (1941). The judges’ role involves interpreting law and creating rules, which is policymaking. *Muller v. Oregon*, 208 U.S. 412 (1908) recognized the state's interest in protecting the physical well-being of workers, analogous to the state's interest in the physical well-being of judges.

**II. The Equal Protection Clause and Rational Basis Review**

The Missouri Constitution’s mandatory retirement provision is a valid exercise of the State’s police power and does not violate the Equal Protection Clause. The appropriate standard of review is rational basis. *United States v. Carolene Products Co.*, 304 U.S. 144 (1938). Under this standard, legislation is presumed valid unless there is a "grave doubt" that the means are appropriate. *West Coast Hotel Co. v. Parrish*, 300 U.S. 379 (1937). The state’s interest in judicial competence is legitimate. *Baker v. Carr*, 369 U.S. 186 (1962) held that apportionment must be based on population, but the standard is rational. *Gomillion v. Lightfoot*, 364 U.S. 339 (1960) illustrates that strict scrutiny applies only when a fundamental right is involved, which is not the case here. *Murdock v. Pennsylvania*, 319 U.S. 105 (1943) affirms the state's power to regulate.
