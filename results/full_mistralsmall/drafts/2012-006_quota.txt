**ARGUMENT**

I. The Government Bears the Burden of Persuasion to Prove Continuing Conspiracy Membership Beyond a Reasonable Doubt

The Due Process Clause requires the government to prove every element of a charged offense beyond a reasonable doubt. *In re Winship*, 397 U.S. 358, 364 (1970). Conspiracy membership is an essential element of a drug conspiracy under 21 U.S.C. § 846 and a RICO enterprise under 18 U.S.C. § 1962(d). *United States v. Shabani*, 513 U.S. 10, 15 (1994) (holding that conspiracy requires proof of an agreement to commit an offense and an overt act in furtherance); *United States v. Turkette*, 452 U.S. 576, 583 (1981) (holding that a RICO conspiracy requires proof of a pattern of racketeering activity and participation in the affairs of an enterprise). Where a defendant presents evidence of withdrawal from a conspiracy before the limitations period, the government must rebut that evidence with proof beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant remained a member during the relevant period. *Griffin v. United States*, 502 U.S. 46, 59 (1991) (holding that the government bears the burden of persuasion on all elements of a charged offense); *United States v. Read*, 658 F.2d 1225, 1233 (7th Cir. 1981) (holding that the government must prove continuing participation in a conspiracy beyond a reasonable doubt).

This Court has long recognized that withdrawal from a conspiracy severs a defendant’s liability for subsequent acts of co-conspirators. *United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 333 U.S. 364, 393 (1948) (holding that a conspirator who withdraws is not liable for acts committed after withdrawal). The government’s burden to prove continuing membership is therefore heightened where a defendant presents evidence of withdrawal. *United States v. Salmon*, 944 F.2d 1106, 1115 (4th Cir. 1991) (holding that the government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that a defendant remained a member of the conspiracy after the defendant presented evidence of withdrawal).

II. The Jury Must Be Instructed on the Government’s Burden to Prove Continuing Membership Beyond a Reasonable Doubt

Where the evidence raises a question of withdrawal, the district court must instruct the jury on the government’s burden to prove continuing membership beyond a reasonable doubt. *United States v. Shabani*, 513 U.S. at 17 (holding that the government must prove every element of a conspiracy beyond a reasonable doubt); *United States v. Salmon*, 944 F.2d at 1115 (holding that the jury must be instructed on the government’s burden to prove continuing membership beyond a reasonable doubt where the evidence raises a question of withdrawal). The failure to instruct the jury on this burden is reversible error. *United States v. Gaudin*, 515 U.S. 506, 522-23 (1995) (holding that the court must instruct the jury on every element of the offense, including the mens rea requirement); *United States v. Read*, 658 F.2d at 1233 (holding that the failure to instruct the jury on the government’s burden to prove continuing membership beyond a reasonable doubt is reversible error).

III. The Evidence Was Insufficient to Prove Continuing Membership Beyond a Reasonable Doubt

The evidence at trial was insufficient to prove that petitioners remained members of the conspiracy during the limitations period. The government presented no direct evidence of continuing membership after the alleged split between the leaders of the conspiracy. *Holland v. United States*, 348 U.S. 121, 140 (1954) (holding that circumstantial evidence must be sufficient to establish guilt beyond a reasonable doubt); *United States v. Salmon*, 944 F.2d at 1115 (holding that the government must present sufficient evidence to prove continuing membership beyond a reasonable doubt). The government’s reliance on mere association with co-conspirators after the alleged split is insufficient to prove continuing membership. *United States v. Alvarez*, 580 F.3d 864, 870 (9th Cir. 2009) (holding that mere association with co-conspirators is insufficient to prove continuing membership in a conspiracy); *United States v. Read*, 658 F.2d at 1233 (holding that the government must present evidence of continuing participation in the conspiracy, not merely association with co-conspirators).

The government’s argument that the conspiracy continued as a matter of law is foreclosed by this Court’s decision in *United States v. Shabani*, 513 U.S. at 17 (holding that the government must prove every element of a conspiracy beyond a reasonable doubt). The government’s burden to prove continuing membership beyond a reasonable doubt is not satisfied by speculation or inference. *Holland v. United States*, 348 U.S. at 140 (holding that circumstantial evidence must be sufficient to establish guilt beyond a reasonable doubt). The evidence at trial was insufficient to prove that petitioners remained members of the conspiracy during the limitations period.
