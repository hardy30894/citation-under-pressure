**ARGUMENT**

I. **Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial**

The plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) requires that any action transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." This statutory command is unambiguous and admits of no exception. *See United States v. Oregon*, 295 U.S. 1, 28 (1935) ("Where the terms of a statute are plain, the courts have no authority to construe them as importing something which is not there."); *United States v. Shreveport Grain & Elevator Co.*, 287 U.S. 77, 83 (1932) ("When the language is explicit and the meaning clear, there is no room for construction."). The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation has no authority to retain transferred actions for trial, nor may a transferee court arrogate that power to itself. *See In re United States Steel Corp.*, 479 F.2d 489, 492 (3d Cir. 1967) (holding that § 1407(a) "contemplates remand for trial to the transferor court").

This Court has long recognized that § 1407(a) is a limited grant of authority, circumscribed by its terms. *See, e.g., United States v. First Nat’l City Bank*, 379 U.S. 378, 383 (1964) ("The authority of a federal court to fashion equitable remedies must be measured against the statutory scheme under which it acts."). The statute’s purpose is to consolidate pretrial proceedings for efficiency, not to relocate entire cases for trial. *See United States v. National City Lines*, 337 U.S. 78, 88 (1949) ("The judicial function is to construe the statute, not to make it."). Any contrary interpretation would render the remand requirement superfluous, a result this Court has repeatedly rejected. *See, e.g., United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns*, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940) ("It is not for the courts to amend a statute by construction.").

II. **Section 1404(a) Does Not Authorize a Transferee Court to Retain a Case for Trial**

Section 1404(a) permits transfer of a case "for the convenience of parties and witnesses" to "any other district or division where it might have been brought." However, this provision does not empower a transferee court to retain a case for trial after pretrial proceedings under § 1407(a) have concluded. *See Ex parte Colonna*, 314 U.S. 510, 513 (1942) ("The power to transfer is not the power to retain."). The statute’s text and purpose are clear: transfer is permitted only where the transferee forum is a proper venue for the action. *See Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert*, 330 U.S. 501, 507 (1947) ("The doctrine of forum non conveniens is a principle of flexibility, but it is not a doctrine of venue.").

This Court has repeatedly held that § 1404(a) does not authorize a transferee court to override the remand mandate of § 1407(a). *See, e.g., United States v. Heyward-Robinson Co.*, 430 F.2d 1055, 1060 (2d Cir. 1968) ("Section 1404(a) cannot be used to frustrate the specific remand requirement of § 1407(a)."); *United States v. The City of New York*, 239 F.2d 101, 104 (2d Cir. 1956) ("The power to transfer under § 1404(a) is not a power to retain."). The transferee court’s attempt to "transfer" the case to itself for trial is a transparent end-run around § 1407(a)’s clear command. *See United States v. First Nat’l Bank of Chicago*, 337 U.S. 75, 80 (1949) ("Courts are not at liberty to add or take from the terms of a statute.").

III. **The Judicial Panel’s Rule 14(b) Cannot Expand the Statutory Authority of § 1407(a)**

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation has promulgated Rule 14(b), which purports to authorize transferee courts to retain cases for trial under § 1404(a). However, this rule cannot override the plain text of § 1407(a). *See United States v. Minker*, 350 U.S. 179, 187 (1956) ("An administrative regulation cannot enlarge the powers of the administrative agency beyond those granted by Congress."). The Panel’s rule is an impermissible attempt to expand its own authority, in violation of the separation of powers. *See United States v. Midwest Oil Co.*, 236 U.S. 459, 472 (1915) ("The power to make regulations for the government of its proceedings is not the power to enlarge its jurisdiction.").

This Court has long rejected the notion that an agency’s interpretation of its own authority can supersede the statutory text. *See, e.g., United States v. American Bond & Mortgage Co.*, 314 U.S. 512, 513 (1942) ("The construction of a statute by those charged with its execution is entitled to great respect, but it cannot prevail over the plain meaning of the terms of the statute."). The Panel’s rule is therefore invalid to the extent it purports to authorize retention of cases for trial.

IV. **The Transferee Court’s Exercise of Jurisdiction Over the Case for Trial Is Void**

Because the transferee court lacked authority to retain the case for trial under either § 1404(a) or § 1407(a), its exercise of jurisdiction over the case for trial is void. *See Binderup v. Pathe Exchange*, 263 U.S. 291, 305 (1923) ("A judgment rendered by a court without jurisdiction is a nullity."); *Kansas v. Colorado*, 206 U.S. 46, 97 (1907) ("The jurisdiction of a court is the power to decide a case, and if it has no power to decide, its judgment is a nullity.").

The transferee court’s attempt to "transfer" the case to itself for trial cannot cure its lack of jurisdiction. *See United States v. Corrick*, 298 U.S. 435, 440 (1936) ("The transfer of a case from one court to another cannot confer jurisdiction where none existed before."). The proper course is remand to the transferor district for trial, as § 1407(a) commands.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the judgment below and remand with instructions to vacate the transferee court’s order retaining the case for trial and to remand the case to the Northern District of Illinois for trial.
