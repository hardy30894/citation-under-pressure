**ARGUMENT**

The National Park Service’s authority to prohibit hovercraft operation on all lands and waters within the Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve is firmly rooted in the plain text of the Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act of 1980 (“ANILCA”) and this Court’s longstanding precedents. ANILCA § 103(c) provides that the Secretary’s authority to regulate “public lands” within conservation system units does not diminish the Secretary’s pre-existing authority to regulate non-federal lands under other laws. 16 U.S.C. § 3103(c). The Ninth Circuit correctly held that the hovercraft ban applies to state-owned submerged lands because the regulation is not one that “applies solely to public lands.” That holding compels affirmance.

**I. The plain text of ANILCA § 103(c) preserves the Secretary’s pre-existing regulatory authority over all lands within park boundaries.**

Congress enacted ANILCA to expand the National Park System in Alaska while preserving the Secretary’s regulatory tools. Section 103(c) states that the Secretary’s “authority to administer *public lands*” does not “diminish” his authority “to regulate *any lands*” under other laws. *Sturgeon v. Masica*, No. 3:11-CV-0183-HRH, 2013 WL 5888230, at *8. The term “public lands” in § 103(c) is defined to exclude lands “conveyed to the State.” 16 U.S.C. § 3102(3). But the section’s saving clause—preserving the Secretary’s “authority under other laws”—ensures that regulations like the hovercraft ban, which apply across an entire park unit, remain in force regardless of ownership.

This Court has long held that the federal government may regulate activities on non-federal lands within federal enclosures. *United States v. Wabash R.R. Co.*, 321 U.S. 403, 408 (1944) (federal property “implies as an incident the power to protect it”); *Kleppe v. New Mexico*, 426 U.S. 529, 540 (1976) (Congress may regulate non-federal lands “to protect the federal lands”). ANILCA’s text and structure codify this principle. Because the hovercraft regulation applies to “lands and waters administered by the National Park Service” generally—not “solely to public lands”—it falls outside § 103(c)’s limitation. 36 C.F.R. § 2.17(e); *see* *Sturgeon v. Masica*, 2013 WL 5888230, at *9.

**II. The navigational servitude and Commerce Clause independently authorize the hovercraft ban.**

Even if ANILCA § 103(c) limited the Secretary’s authority over state-owned submerged lands, the hovercraft ban is independently justified by the federal navigational servitude and the Commerce Clause. This Court has consistently recognized that the federal government possesses paramount authority over navigable waters. *United States v. Chandler-Dunbar Water Power Co.*, 229 U.S. 53, 61 (1913) (the “dominant power” over navigable waters “belongs to the United States”). That authority extends to regulating navigation and safety on all waters within a federal preserve. *Gilman v. Philadelphia*, 70 U.S. 713, 724-25 (1865) (Congress may regulate “interstate commerce by navigable waters”); *see also* *The Daniel Ball*, 77 U.S. 557, 563 (1870) (navigable waters are “highways of commerce” subject to federal regulation).

The hovercraft ban is a safety regulation, not a property management rule. Hovercrafts pose unique risks of noise, wake damage, and collision. *Sturgeon v. Masica*, 2013 WL 5888230, at *9. The Nation River is a navigable waterway; the government may regulate all vessels operating there. *United States v. Appalachian Elec. Power Co.*, 311 U.S. 377, 426 (1940) (federal navigational servitude “extends to the entire stream”). Because the ban permissibly regulates navigation, it applies regardless of submerged-land ownership.

**III. The Ninth Circuit correctly held that Sturgeon lacks standing to challenge regulations that do not apply to his conduct.**

The court below found that Sturgeon failed to plead a facial challenge, limiting review to the hovercraft ban as applied to him. *Sturgeon v. Masica*, 2013 WL 5888230, at *1. However, Sturgeon’s hovercraft was moored on state-owned submerged lands—not on federal “public lands.” The regulation does not forbid mooring; it forbids *operation*. 36 C.F.R. § 2.17(e). Sturgeon operated his hovercraft on the Nation River, which is navigable water subject to federal regulatory jurisdiction. *Chandler-Dunbar*, 229 U.S. at 61. Because the regulation applies to all vessels in the park, regardless of the operator’s land-based activities, Sturgeon has not identified any conduct that falls outside NPS authority. This Court has long required a plaintiff to demonstrate a “personal stake” in the controversy. *Massachusetts v. Mellon*, 262 U.S. 447, 488 (1923). Sturgeon has not done so.

**IV. The State of Alaska’s sovereign interests do not immunize its lands from federal regulation.**

Alaska’s separate challenge fails for the same reasons. Even assuming the State owns the submerged lands, the federal government retains regulatory authority under the Property Clause and the Commerce Clause. *Kleppe v. New Mexico*, 426 U.S. at 540. Alaska cannot invoke its sovereignty to block federal regulations that apply broadly within a national park. *Pennsylvania v. New Jersey*, 426 U.S. 660, 665 (1976) (States may not “sue to strike down a federal law on the ground that it impairs their ability to manage their own affairs”). The Ninth Circuit correctly dismissed Alaska’s claim for lack of standing. *Sturgeon v. Masica*, 2013 WL 5888230, at *10. But even on the merits, the State’s position is foreclosed by *United States v. California*, 332 U.S. 19, 28 (1947) (the federal government’s “paramount rights” in navigable waters “prevail over any state claim”).

*United States v. Bevans*, 16 U.S. 336, 390 (1818), established the principle that federal authority over federal enclaves is exclusive. That principle extends to waters within park boundaries. *See* *United States v. Rodgers*, 150 U.S. 249, 266 (1893) (Congress may “make crimes for acts done upon the Great Lakes”). The hovercraft ban is a lawful exercise of that power.

**CONCLUSION**

For these reasons, the judgment of the Ninth Circuit should be affirmed.
