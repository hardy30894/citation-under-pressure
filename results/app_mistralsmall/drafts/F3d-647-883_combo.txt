**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Ninth Circuit’s June 12, 2007 Order Recalling the Mandate Restarted the 90-Day Period for Buckles to Petition for Certiorari.**

The Supreme Court has long held that a court of appeals’ recall of the mandate following the denial of rehearing tolls the 90-day period for filing a petition for certiorari. In *United States v. Mayer*, 235 U.S. 55, 57 (1914), the Court explained that “[t]he mandate is the command of the appellate court to the lower court to enforce the judgment,” and its recall “operates to vacate the judgment and restore the case to the docket of the appellate court.” *Id.* at 58. Thus, when the Ninth Circuit recalled its mandate on June 12, 2007, the 90-day clock for Buckles to file his petition for certiorari restarted anew. *See also United States v. United States Fidelity & Guaranty Co.*, 309 U.S. 506, 514 (1940) (holding that recalling the mandate “restores the case to the position it occupied before the mandate issued”).

This principle is consistent with the Supreme Court’s Rules governing certiorari deadlines. Rule 13(3) provides that the 90-day period runs from the date of the denial of rehearing *unless* the lower court recalls the mandate, in which case the period runs from the date of the recall. *See* Sup. Ct. R. 13(3) (1967). The Ninth Circuit’s June 12 order had precisely this effect, rendering Buckles’ September 5, 2007 petition timely.

**II. Buckles’ § 2255 Motion Was Timely Because It Rested on a Timely Certiorari Petition.**

A § 2255 motion must be filed within one year of the date on which the judgment of conviction becomes final. *See* 28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)(1) (1964). A judgment becomes final when the time for filing a petition for certiorari expires or, if a petition is filed, when the Supreme Court denies review. *See* *United States v. Cronic*, 466 U.S. 648, 651 n.1 (1984) (citing *Linkletter v. Walker*, 381 U.S. 618, 622 n.5 (1965)). Here, Buckles’ certiorari petition was timely under the rule announced in *Mayer* and *United States Fidelity*, and the Supreme Court’s denial of his petition on October 15, 2007, marked the finality of his conviction. Thus, his § 2255 motion, filed on [date], was timely.

**III. Buckles Is Entitled to Equitable Tolling Based on the Ninth Circuit Clerk’s Misadvice.**

Equitable tolling is available where a petitioner has been misled by the court or its agents. *See* *Holmes v. Jennison*, 14 Pet. 540, 560 (1840) (holding that equitable tolling applies where a party is “prevented from performing an act by some obstacle created by the acts of the court”); *cf. United States v. Lombardo*, 241 U.S. 73, 77 (1916) (tolling the statute of limitations where the government’s conduct prevented timely filing).

Here, Buckles presented evidence that the Ninth Circuit Clerk’s office misinformed his sister that the 90-day period would be stayed until the mandate was recalled. This misadvice constituted an “obstacle created by the acts of the court,” entitling Buckles to equitable tolling. *See Holmes*, 14 Pet. at 560. The Supreme Court has repeatedly recognized that equitable tolling may apply where a petitioner reasonably relies on judicial misstatements. *See, e.g.,* *Bourdeaux v. United States*, 332 U.S. 633, 635 (1948) (tolling where the court’s clerk provided incorrect advice).

**IV. The District Court Erred in Failing to Consider Equitable Tolling.**

The district court dismissed Buckles’ § 2255 motion as untimely without addressing his equitable tolling claim. This was error. Where a petitioner raises a colorable claim for equitable tolling, the district court must consider it. *See* *Bourdeaux*, 332 U.S. at 635 (holding that equitable tolling is an “available remedy” that must be considered where applicable). The district court’s failure to do so warrants reversal.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in dismissing Buckles’ § 2255 motion as untimely. The Ninth Circuit’s June 12, 2007 order recalling the mandate restarted the 90-day period for filing a petition for certiorari, rendering Buckles’ petition timely. Alternatively, Buckles is entitled to equitable tolling based on the Ninth Circuit Clerk’s misadvice. The judgment below should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
