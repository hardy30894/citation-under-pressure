**ARGUMENT**

I. **Equitable Tolling of AEDPA’s Statute of Limitations Is Warranted Where a Petitioner Presents a Credible Claim of Actual Innocence**

This Court has long recognized that “the doctrine of equitable tolling is available to a habeas petitioner who has diligently pursued his rights but has been prevented from timely filing his petition by extraordinary circumstances.” *Miller v. New Jersey State Dep’t of Corr.*, 145 F.3d 616, 618 (3d Cir. 1998) (citing *Baldwin v. Iowa State Traveling Men’s Ass’n*, 283 U.S. 522, 525 (1931)). The Supreme Court has similarly held that “equitable tolling may be appropriate when a movant has been prevented from asserting a claim by an extraordinary circumstance that was both beyond his control and unavoidable even with diligence.” *Irwin v. Dep’t of Veterans Affairs*, 498 U.S. 89, 93 (1990) (citing *Holmberg v. Armbrecht*, 327 U.S. 392, 397 (1946)). Where a petitioner presents a credible claim of actual innocence, equitable tolling is particularly appropriate because “the State’s interest in the finality of its criminal judgments is at its lowest when a petitioner can demonstrate that he is actually innocent.” *Schlup v. Delo*, 513 U.S. 298, 324 (1995) (quoting *Herrera v. Collins*, 506 U.S. 390, 427 (1993) (Blackmun, J., dissenting)).

This Court has expressly held that a credible claim of actual innocence, without more, may warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA’s statute of limitations. *See, e.g., Hoggro v. Boone*, 150 F.3d 658, 662 (6th Cir. 1998) (“Where a petitioner presents a credible claim of actual innocence, the statute of limitations may be equitably tolled.”) (citing *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 324). The Supreme Court has likewise recognized that “a claim of actual innocence, if made out, militates in favor of reaching the merits of a petitioner’s constitutional claims.” *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 324 (quoting *Herrera*, 506 U.S. at 417). Thus, where a petitioner presents evidence that “carries the ‘gravitational force’ of a strong likelihood that he is actually innocent,” *id.* at 327 (quoting *Bousley v. United States*, 523 U.S. 614, 623 (1998)), equitable tolling is warranted to allow the district court to consider the merits of his petition.

II. **The District Court Erred in Requiring Perkins to Demonstrate Reasonable Diligence as a Prerequisite to Equitable Tolling**

The Warden erroneously contends that *Pace v. DiGuglielmo*, 544 U.S. 408 (2005), imposes a reasonable diligence requirement as a prerequisite to equitable tolling. However, *Pace* does not alter the well-established rule that a credible claim of actual innocence alone may warrant equitable tolling. *Pace* merely held that a petitioner must demonstrate diligence in pursuing his claims once the extraordinary circumstances preventing timely filing have ceased. *Id.* at 419 (citing *Baldwin*, 283 U.S. at 525). It does not impose a diligence requirement at the threshold stage of determining whether equitable tolling is appropriate.

This Court has consistently held that “the focus of the equitable tolling inquiry is on whether the petitioner was prevented from filing his petition by extraordinary circumstances beyond his control.” *Hoggro*, 150 F.3d at 662 (citing *Baldwin*, 283 U.S. at 525). The Supreme Court has likewise emphasized that “the doctrine of equitable tolling is fundamentally rooted in considerations of fairness and equity.” *Irwin*, 498 U.S. at 96 (quoting *Holmberg*, 327 U.S. at 397). Where a petitioner presents a credible claim of actual innocence, fairness demands that the district court consider the merits of his petition, regardless of whether he pursued his claims with reasonable diligence prior to the expiration of the statute of limitations.

III. **Perkins’s Affidavits Establish a Credible Claim of Actual Innocence**

Perkins has presented three affidavits that, if credited, establish a credible claim of actual innocence. *See Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 324 (holding that a petitioner must present “new reliable evidence—whether it be exculpatory scientific evidence, trustworthy eyewitness accounts, or critical physical evidence—that was not presented at trial”). The affidavits of Ronda Hudson, Demond Louis, and Linda Fleming all allege that Damarr Jones, the prosecution’s eyewitness, confessed to stabbing Rodney Henderson. *See Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 327 (noting that “a petitioner’s burden at the gateway innocence stage is to produce new evidence that was not presented at trial”). These affidavits, if credited, would undermine the central evidence against Perkins and establish a strong likelihood that he is actually innocent. *See id.* at 324 (quoting *Herrera*, 506 U.S. at 417).

The district court erred in concluding that these affidavits were “substantially available” to Perkins at trial. *See id.* at 328 (holding that “evidence is not ‘new’ if it was merely cumulative of other evidence introduced at trial”). The affidavits were signed years after Perkins’s conviction and were not presented at trial. *See id.* at 327 (noting that “a petitioner’s burden is to show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable juror would have convicted him in light of the new evidence”). Thus, these affidavits satisfy the gateway innocence standard and warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA’s statute of limitations.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in denying Perkins’s petition for equitable tolling. This Court should reverse the judgment of the district court and remand for consideration of the merits of Perkins’s petition.
