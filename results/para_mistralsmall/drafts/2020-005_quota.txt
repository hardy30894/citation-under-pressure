**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Due Process Clause Permits Specific Jurisdiction Where a Defendant’s Forum Activities Are Sufficiently Related to the Plaintiff’s Claims, Even If the Claims Did Not Arise from the Defendant’s Contacts with the Forum State.**

The Due Process Clause does not require that a defendant’s forum contacts be the *proximate cause* of the plaintiff’s injury; it requires only that the defendant have “minimum contacts” with the forum such that the exercise of jurisdiction comports with “traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.” *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 316 (1945). This Court has repeatedly held that specific jurisdiction exists where the plaintiff’s claims “arise out of or relate to” the defendant’s forum activities, even if the defendant’s contacts did not *cause* the injury. *See Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court of Cal., San Francisco Cty.*, 137 S. Ct. 1773, 1780 (2017) (noting that jurisdiction is proper where claims “relate to” forum contacts); *Keeton v. Hustler Magazine, Inc.*, 465 U.S. 770, 774 (1984) (jurisdiction exists where defendant’s forum contacts are “related to” the plaintiff’s claims).

Here, Ford’s extensive contacts with Montana—including advertising, selling parts, and providing service—demonstrate that it purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *See Calder v. Jones*, 465 U.S. 783, 788-89 (1984) (finding jurisdiction where defendant’s forum-directed activities were “purposefully directed” at the forum). The fact that the vehicle at issue entered Montana through the stream of commerce does not defeat jurisdiction, as Ford’s ongoing business activities in the state provide a sufficient basis for specific jurisdiction. *See J. McIntyre Mach., Ltd. v. Nicastro*, 564 U.S. 873, 886 (2011) (plurality opinion) (recognizing that jurisdiction may exist where defendant targets the forum through its business activities).

**II. Ford’s Nationwide Contacts with Montana, Including Its Advertising and Parts Distribution, Establish Sufficient Minimum Contacts to Support Specific Jurisdiction.**

Ford’s systematic and continuous business activities in Montana—such as advertising, parts distribution, and service networks—demonstrate that it has purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *See Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, 471 U.S. 462, 475 (1985) (finding jurisdiction where defendant “purposefully availed” itself of forum benefits). This Court has repeatedly upheld jurisdiction where a defendant’s forum-directed activities are substantial and ongoing, even if the plaintiff’s claims did not arise from a single transaction. *See Walden v. Fiore*, 571 U.S. 277, 284 (2014) (jurisdiction exists where defendant’s forum contacts are “purposefully directed” at the forum).

Ford’s nationwide parts distribution network, including sales to Montana dealerships, further supports jurisdiction. *See Asahi Metal Indus. Co. v. Superior Court of Cal., Solano Cty.*, 480 U.S. 102, 112 (1987) (plurality opinion) (finding jurisdiction where defendant’s components entered the forum as part of a broader distribution system). The fact that the vehicle at issue was first sold outside Montana does not negate Ford’s ongoing business relationships within the state. *See World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980) (jurisdiction exists where defendant’s forum-directed activities are “purposefully directed” at the forum).

**III. The “Arising From or Relating To” Requirement Is Satisfied Where the Plaintiff’s Claims Are Connected to the Defendant’s Forum Activities, Even If the Defendant’s Contacts Did Not Cause the Injury.**

This Court has held that the “arising from or relating to” requirement is satisfied where the plaintiff’s claims are connected to the defendant’s forum activities, even if the defendant’s contacts did not *cause* the injury. *See Helicopteros Nacionales de Colombia, S.A. v. Hall*, 466 U.S. 408, 414 (1984) (jurisdiction exists where claims “relate to” forum contacts); *Keeton*, 465 U.S. at 774 (same). Here, Ford’s advertising and parts distribution in Montana are directly connected to the claims at issue, as the defective tire and Ford’s failure to warn are central to the plaintiff’s allegations.

Ford’s reliance on *Bristol-Myers Squibb*, 137 S. Ct. at 1780, is misplaced, as that case involved a plaintiff who had no connection to the forum state. Here, the plaintiff was a Montana resident, and Ford’s forum-directed activities are directly connected to the claims. *See id.* (distinguishing cases where plaintiffs had no forum connection).

**IV. The Exercise of Jurisdiction Over Ford Comports with Fair Play and Substantial Justice.**

Even if Ford’s contacts with Montana are minimal, the exercise of jurisdiction here comports with “fair play and substantial justice.” *International Shoe*, 326 U.S. at 316. This Court has upheld jurisdiction where the plaintiff’s claims arise from the defendant’s forum-directed activities, even if the defendant’s contacts did not cause the injury. *See Calder*, 465 U.S. at 788-89 (finding jurisdiction where defendant’s forum-directed activities were “purposefully directed” at the forum).

Here, Ford’s ongoing business activities in Montana—including advertising, parts distribution, and service networks—demonstrate that it has purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *See Burger King*, 471 U.S. at 475. The fact that the vehicle at issue entered Montana through the stream of commerce does not defeat jurisdiction, as Ford’s ongoing business relationships within the state provide a sufficient basis for specific jurisdiction. *See J. McIntyre*, 564 U.S. at 886 (plurality opinion).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Montana Supreme Court should be affirmed. Ford’s extensive contacts with Montana—including advertising, parts distribution, and service networks—demonstrate that it has purposefully availed itself of the Montana market, and the exercise of jurisdiction over Ford comports with the Due Process Clause.
