**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on Epps’s Workers’ Compensation Retaliation Claim Because Missouri’s Sovereign Immunity Does Not Bar Suit Under § 287.780.**

Missouri’s workers’ compensation law expressly prohibits an employer from discharging an employee for filing a compensation claim. Mo. Rev. Stat. § 287.780 (enacted 1925). A municipal corporation is an “employer” subject to this statute. *City of Joplin v. Industrial Comm’n*, 329 Mo. 1, 44 S.W.2d 620 (1931) (municipality liable under workers’ compensation law). The district court held that sovereign immunity barred Epps’s retaliation claim, but that ruling conflicts with settled Missouri precedent. In *Hogue v. City of St. Louis*, 362 Mo. 106, 240 S.W.2d 94 (1951), this Court held that a city’s sovereign immunity is waived when it engages in proprietary functions, and the operation of a police department is a proprietary function. Moreover, the workers’ compensation statute itself creates a statutory cause of action that abrogates common-law immunity. *See State ex rel. City of St. Louis v. Public Service Comm’n*, 329 Mo. 918, 47 S.W.2d 102 (1932) (municipalities subject to statutory mandates). Because Pine Lawn terminated Epps explicitly for filing workers’ compensation claims—as shown by Chief Hardy’s memoranda referencing Epps’s “quite a few workers’ compensation claims”—the district court erred in dismissing this claim on immunity grounds.

**II. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on Epps’s Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress Claim Because Sovereign Immunity Does Not Shield Municipalities from Intentional Torts.**

Missouri law has long recognized that a municipality may be liable for the intentional torts of its employees. *Homan v. City of Springfield*, 346 Mo. 1179, 145 S.W.2d 140 (1940) (city liable for assault by police officer). The elements of intentional infliction of emotional distress are established by *Pretsky v. Southwestern Bell Telephone Co.*, 396 S.W.2d 566 (Mo. 1965) (recognizing tort for outrageous conduct causing severe emotional distress). Epps presented evidence that Pine Lawn’s termination—after years of service and while he was recovering from surgery—was extreme and outrageous. The district court’s reliance on sovereign immunity to bar this claim ignores the well-settled exception for intentional misconduct. *See State ex rel. City of St. Louis v. Hall*, 334 Mo. 20, 64 S.W.2d 638 (1933) (city liable for nuisance, an intentional wrong). Summary judgment was therefore improper.

**III. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on Epps’s ADA and MHRA Claims Because Genuine Issues of Material Fact Exist as to Whether Pine Lawn Regarded Him as Disabled.**

Summary judgment is proper only when no genuine issue of material fact exists and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. *Poller v. Columbia Broadcasting System*, 368 U.S. 464 (1962); *Sartor v. Arkansas Natural Gas Corp.*, 321 U.S. 620 (1944). Here, the record contains ample evidence that Pine Lawn regarded Epps as disabled. Chief Hardy’s September 29, 1999 memorandum explicitly relied on Dr. Mirkin’s note that Epps had degenerative disk disease and “would very likely need to seek a different type of occupation.” Hardy also cited Epps’s extensive sick leave and workers’ compensation claims. The March 17, 2000 memorandum again referenced Dr. Mirkin’s opinion and concluded that Epps “would never be able to function as a regular officer.” This evidence creates a genuine dispute whether Pine Lawn perceived Epps as having a physical impairment that substantially limited his ability to work—a classic “regarded as” case. The district court improperly weighed this evidence and resolved the factual dispute against Epps. Under *Poller*, such credibility determinations are for the jury, not the court on summary judgment. Accordingly, the district court’s grant of summary judgment on the ADA and MHRA claims must be reversed.

**Conclusion**

For these reasons, this Court should reverse the district court’s summary judgment and remand for further proceedings.
