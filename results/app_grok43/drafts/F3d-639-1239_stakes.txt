The district court erred in holding that BCR’s Rule 68 offer of judgment mooted the action. In a properly pleaded class action, “the class of unnamed persons described in the complaint acquired a legal status separate from the interest asserted by [the named plaintiff].” Sosna v. Iowa, 419 U.S. 393, 399 (1975). That separate legal status persists even when the named plaintiff’s individual claim is satisfied, provided the plaintiff has not unduly delayed seeking certification. U.S. Parole Comm’n v. Geraghty, 445 U.S. 388, 404 (1980) (“A plaintiff who brings a class action presents two separate issues for adjudication: the claim of the plaintiff on the merits and the claim that he is entitled to represent a class.”).

The Rule 68 offer addressed only Lucero’s individual damages. It left unresolved the distinct question whether Lucero could represent the class alleged in the complaint. Because that question remained live when the motion for certification was filed without undue delay, the case retained the “concrete adverseness” required by Article III. Geraghty, 445 U.S. at 403. The district court’s contrary conclusion—that satisfaction of the individual claim automatically ends the controversy—overlooks the class representative’s ongoing interest in obtaining a ruling on certification.

This Court has recognized that the general presumption of mootness yields when a class plaintiff acts promptly. Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir. 2009), itself notes that dismissal is appropriate only “in the absence of undue delay in filing a motion for class certification.” Here the parties’ joint discovery plan, adopted by the court, deferred class discovery and set a certification deadline of February 23, 2010. Lucero filed his motion on February 22, 2010. That timing precludes any finding of undue delay.

Accordingly, the Rule 68 offer did not divest the district court of jurisdiction over the class claims. The judgment of dismissal should be reversed.
