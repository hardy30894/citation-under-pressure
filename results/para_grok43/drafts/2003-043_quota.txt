I. The Assessment Against the Partnership Did Not Extend the Limitations Period to the Individual Partners.

Section 6501(a) requires the IRS to assess tax against “the taxpayer” within three years of the return or commence collection proceedings. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a). An assessment records “the liability of the taxpayer.” 26 U.S.C. § 6203. Because each partner is a distinct “taxpayer,” 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14), (a)(1), an assessment naming only the partnership leaves the partners’ three-year period unaltered. United States v. Coson, 286 F.2d 453, 458 (9th Cir. 1961) (lien invalid where assessment failed to identify the individual); Young v. Riddell, 283 F.2d 909, 910 (9th Cir. 1960) (partner’s liability arises under state law, yet collection without assessment or judgment requires court action).

The timely partnership assessment therefore extended only the partnership’s ten-year collection period under § 6502(a). United States v. Wright, 57 F.3d 561, 562-63 (7th Cir. 1995) (limitations tolled only as to assessed parties); Goldston v. United States (In re Goldston), 104 F.3d 1198, 1200-01 (10th Cir. 1997) (assessment or suit against the taxpayer is prerequisite). No separate assessment having been made against respondents, the ordinary three-year bar applies. Neilson v. Chang (In re First T.D. & Inv., Inc.), 253 F.3d 520, 526 (9th Cir. 2001).

II. California Partnership Law Does Not Supply an Independent Right to Payment.

Under California law a judgment against the partnership “is not by itself a judgment against a partner.” Cal. Corp. Code § 16307(c). Collection from a partner’s personal assets therefore requires a separate judgment. The IRS obtained none. Because the limitations period for obtaining such a judgment expired, 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a), the claim is unenforceable. 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1).

Gruntz v. County of Los Angeles (In re Gruntz), 202 F.3d 1074, 1084 n.9 (9th Cir. 2000) (en banc) (bankruptcy court determines enforceability under applicable law); Beaupied v. Chang (In re Chang), 163 F.3d 1138, 1140 (9th Cir. 1998) (same). The bankruptcy court therefore properly disallowed the claims.

III. The IRS’s Contrary Authorities Are Inapposite.

Young v. Riddell permitted no collection without assessment or judgment; it merely denied a refund after voluntary payment. 283 F.2d at 910. Wright addressed tolling during partnership bankruptcy after assessments against both the partnership and the partners. 57 F.3d at 562. Neither decision supports collection here.

The district court’s decision should be affirmed.
