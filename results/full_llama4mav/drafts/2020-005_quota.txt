The Montana Supreme Court correctly held that a plaintiff may present evidence of the full amount of their medical expenses, rather than just the amount paid by insurance, to establish the reasonable value of their medical care. This Court has long recognized that the reasonable value of medical services is a question for the jury to determine. Burley v. Burlington Northern, 2012 MT 28, ¶ 91, 364 Mont. 77, 273 P.3d 825. In making this determination, the jury should be allowed to consider all relevant evidence, including the amount billed by the medical providers. Chapman v. Mazda Motor of Am., 7 F. Supp. 2d 1123, 1125 (D. Mont. 1998).

The Montana Supreme Court's decision is consistent with the collateral source rule, which prohibits the reduction of a plaintiff's damages by the amount of collateral source payments. Section 27-1-308, MCA; Alexander v. Bozeman Motors, Inc., 2012 MT 301, ¶¶ 41-42, 367 Mont. 401, 291 P.3d 1120. While the rule prevents the jury from considering collateral source payments, it does not preclude the plaintiff from presenting evidence of the full amount of their medical expenses.

Ford Motor Company's reliance on Newbury v. State Farm Fire & Casualty Insurance, 2008 MT 156, 343 Mont. 279, 184 P.3d 1021, and Conway v. Benefis Health System, 2013 MT 73, 369 Mont. 309, 297 P.3d 1200, is misplaced. Those cases involved contract disputes between insureds and insurers, and did not address the admissibility of evidence in a personal injury action against a tortfeasor. Harris v. St. Vincent Healthcare, 2013 MT 207, 371 Mont. 133, 305 P.3d 852, is similarly distinguishable.

The decision below is also supported by the principle that damages must be reasonable. Section 27-1-302, MCA; Tidyman's Management Services v. Davis, 2014 MT 205, ¶ 40, 376 Mont. 80, 330 P.3d 1139. The jury's determination of reasonable damages is entitled to deference, and the plaintiff should be allowed to present all relevant evidence to support their claim. Redies v. Attorney Liability Prot. Soc., 2007 MT 9, ¶ 26, 335 Mont. 233, 150 P.3d 930.

Furthermore, the amount billed by medical providers is relevant to the nature and severity of the plaintiff's injuries, and the medical procedures and treatments they received. Willirik v. Boyne USA, Inc., No. CV 12-74-BU-DLC, 2013 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 152566, at *6-7 (D. Mont. Oct. 23, 2013). This evidence is admissible even if it exceeds the amount paid by insurance, as it is relevant to the plaintiff's damages claim. Hulse v. Department of Justice, 1998 MT 108, ¶ 15, 289 Mont. 1, 961 P.2d 75.

The Montana Supreme Court's decision is also consistent with the majority rule, which allows plaintiffs to present evidence of the full amount of their medical expenses. Bynum v. Magno, 101 P.3d 1149 (Haw. 2004). This approach ensures that plaintiffs are not unfairly limited in their ability to prove their damages, and that juries have access to all relevant evidence when determining reasonable damages.

In conclusion, the Montana Supreme Court correctly held that a plaintiff may present evidence of the full amount of their medical expenses to establish the reasonable value of their medical care. This decision is supported by Montana law, including the collateral source rule and the principle that damages must be reasonable. The court should affirm the decision below and allow plaintiffs to present all relevant evidence to support their damages claims.
