**ARGUMENT**

I. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE “INTERNAL PERSONNEL RULES AND PRACTICES” UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 2.

The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test for Exemption 2 is inconsistent with the plain text of the statute and this Court’s precedent. Exemption 2 shields only “matters that are related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). As the Supreme Court has emphasized, FOIA exemptions are to be “narrowly construed” and are “explicitly exclusive.” *Dep’t of Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 361 (1976) (quoting *Adm’r, FAA v. Robertson*, 422 U.S. 255, 262 (1975)); *Tax Analysts v. Dep’t of Justice*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989).

The Ninth Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test expands Exemption 2 far beyond its statutory bounds. The statute does not exempt all materials that are *primarily* internal; it exempts only those that are *solely* related to internal personnel rules and practices. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Supreme Court has cautioned against such an expansive reading, noting that Exemption 2 was intended to cover only “trivial matters” like parking facilities and lunch hours. *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 363 (quoting S. Rep. No. 813, 89th Cong., 1st Sess. 8 (1965)). The ESQD arc maps at issue here are not internal personnel rules or practices; they are operational safety data used to design and secure ammunition storage facilities. *See* Joint Appendix at 45-46 (Commander Whitbred’s affidavit describing ESQD arcs as “long term planning tool[s] for the Navy”).

The Ninth Circuit’s test also conflicts with this Court’s decision in *Rose*, which limited Exemption 2 to “low 2” materials—mundane employment matters—and “high 2” materials that risk circumvention of agency regulation. *Id.* at 369. The ESQD arc maps do not fall into either category. They are not personnel rules, nor do they risk circumvention of agency regulation. Instead, they are operational safety data that the Navy itself has not classified as sensitive under Exemption 1. *See Weinberger v. Catholic Action of Hawaii/Peace Educ. Project*, 454 U.S. 139, 144 (1981) (noting that properly classified information is exempt under Exemption 1).

II. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT DISCLOSURE OF ESQD ARC MAPS WOULD RISK CIRCUMVENTING AGENCY REGULATION.

The Ninth Circuit’s conclusion that disclosure of ESQD arc maps would risk circumvention of agency regulation is unsupported by the record and contrary to law. Exemption 2’s “risk of circumvention” test applies only to materials that are themselves *rules or practices* governing agency personnel. *See Rose*, 425 U.S. at 369. The ESQD arc maps are not rules or practices; they are factual data derived from the OP-5 manual, which the Navy itself has made publicly available in part. *See Joint Appendix at 44-45.*

Moreover, the Supreme Court has made clear that Exemption 2 does not shield operational safety data merely because disclosure *might* aid those seeking to circumvent regulations. *See id.* at 361-62 (exemptions must be narrowly construed). The Ninth Circuit’s holding would allow agencies to withhold any document that could theoretically be misused, a standard that would eviscerate FOIA’s mandate of full disclosure. *See id.* at 360-61 (quoting S. Rep. No. 813, at 3).

III. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER WHETHER THE DOCUMENTS ARE EXEMPT UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 7(F).

The Ninth Circuit did not address whether the ESQD arc maps are exempt under Exemption 7(F), which shields records compiled for law enforcement purposes if disclosure “could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of any individual.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F). The dissent correctly noted that the ESQD arc maps are not compiled for law enforcement purposes; they are operational safety data used for long-term planning. *See Joint Appendix at 45-46.* The Navy’s claim that disclosure could endanger lives is undermined by its own failure to classify the documents under Exemption 1. *See Weinberger*, 454 U.S. at 144.

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit’s holding that ESQD arc maps are exempt under Exemption 2 is contrary to the plain text of the statute and this Court’s precedent. The judgment below should be reversed.
