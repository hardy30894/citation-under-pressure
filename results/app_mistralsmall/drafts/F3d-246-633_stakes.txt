**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON PLAINTIFF’S CONSPIRACY CLAIM UNDER THE RAILWAY LABOR ACT (RLA), 45 U.S.C. § 153.**

The district court improperly dismissed Plaintiff’s conspiracy claim under the RLA, which prohibits "[c]onspirac[ies] to interfere with or influence, by intimidation or threats, the exercise of any right under the Railway Labor Act." *See* 45 U.S.C. § 153 First (i). To establish a conspiracy under the RLA, a plaintiff must show "(1) a conspiracy between two or more persons; (2) an object of the conspiracy was to interfere with or influence the exercise of a right under the RLA; and (3) the conspiracy was effectuated by intimidation or threats." *See United Steelworkers of Am. v. Phelps Dodge Corp.*, 865 F.2d 1539, 1544 (11th Cir. 1989) (citing *Railway Labor Executives’ Ass’n v. Norfolk & W. Ry. Co.*, 808 F.2d 1035, 1040 (D.C. Cir. 1987)).

Here, Plaintiff presented sufficient evidence to create a genuine dispute of material fact regarding whether Defendants conspired to interfere with his RLA rights. Specifically, Plaintiff demonstrated that UTU and CSX engaged in coordinated efforts to prevent him from obtaining the CSX stock shares to which he was entitled under the labor agreement. *See* Pl.’s Resp. to Defs.’ Mot. for Summ. J. Ex. A (deposition testimony of V.V. Elswick acknowledging that UTU and CSX coordinated on the interpretation of the labor agreement). This evidence, if believed, would support a finding that Defendants acted in concert to deprive Plaintiff of his RLA-protected right to grieve and arbitrate his claim. *See Norfolk & W. Ry. Co.*, 808 F.2d at 1040 (finding a conspiracy where defendants coordinated to undermine employees’ RLA rights).

The district court’s reliance on *Anderson v. Consolidated Rail Corp.*, 297 F.3d 160 (3d Cir. 2002), is misplaced. Unlike in *Anderson*, where the plaintiff failed to present evidence of a conspiracy, Plaintiff here has proffered evidence of coordinated action between UTU and CSX. *See id.* at 168. Accordingly, summary judgment was inappropriate.

II. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN AWARDING DEFENDANTS STATUTORY AND PUNITIVE DAMAGES ON THEIR COUNTERCLAIMS UNDER THE FEDERAL WIRETAP ACT, 18 U.S.C. § 2510 ET SEQ.**

A. **The District Court Misapplied the Wiretap Act’s "Consent" Exception.**

The Wiretap Act prohibits the intentional interception of oral communications unless one of the statute’s exceptions applies. *See* 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(a). One such exception is where "one of the parties to the communication has given prior consent to the interception." *Id.* § 2511(2)(d). The Sixth Circuit has held that this exception applies where "a party to the conversation has consented to the recording, regardless of whether the other parties knew of the recording." *See United States v. Jones*, 542 F.3d 661, 667 (6th Cir. 2008) (quoting *United States v. Amen*, 831 F.2d 373, 378 (2d Cir. 1987)).

Here, Plaintiff did not consent to the recording of the executive session. The record shows that Plaintiff left his tape recorders in the room while the executive session was held, but there is no evidence that he affirmatively consented to the recording. *See* Pl.’s Resp. to Defs.’ Mot. for Summ. J. Ex. B (deposition testimony of Plaintiff denying consent). Thus, the district court erred in finding that the "consent" exception applied.

B. **The District Court Improperly Awarded Punitive Damages.**

Punitive damages under the Wiretap Act are available only where the defendant’s conduct was "willful and malicious." *See* 18 U.S.C. § 2520(c)(2)(B). The Sixth Circuit has held that punitive damages are appropriate where the defendant "acted with reckless disregard for the rights of others." *See Doe v. XYZ Corp.*, 887 F.3d 191, 205 (6th Cir. 2018) (quoting *Doe v. Durtschi*, 686 F.3d 513, 522 (6th Cir. 2012)).

In this case, Defendants’ conduct did not rise to the level of "willful and malicious" or "reckless disregard." The record shows that Defendants acted in good faith to enforce the labor agreement and the RLA’s arbitration procedures. *See* Defs.’ Mot. for Summ. J. Ex. C (affidavit of H. Raymond Cluster stating that the executive session was held to discuss procedural matters). Thus, the district court’s award of punitive damages was erroneous.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in granting summary judgment on Plaintiff’s conspiracy claim under the RLA and in awarding Defendants statutory and punitive damages on their counterclaims under the Wiretap Act. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and this matter should be remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
