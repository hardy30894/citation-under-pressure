**ARGUMENT**

I. **A Rule 32(a)(2) Violation Is Subject to Harmless-Error Review**

The Third Circuit correctly held that a district court’s failure to advise a defendant of his right to appeal under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) is amenable to harmless-error analysis. *United States v. Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. 74, 80 (2004) (applying harmless-error review to Rule 32 violations); *see also United States v. Vonn*, 535 U.S. 55, 62–63 (2002) (holding that Rule 32 errors are subject to harmless-error review unless they implicate structural constitutional rights). The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized that not all procedural errors warrant automatic reversal. *Neder v. United States*, 527 U.S. 1, 8 (1999) ("Most constitutional errors can be harmless."). A Rule 32(a)(2) violation, which is purely procedural, does not implicate the fundamental fairness concerns that trigger structural error review. *Compare Johnson v. United States*, 520 U.S. 461, 468–69 (1997) (structural error where counsel was entirely absent), *with United States v. Timmreck*, 441 U.S. 780, 784 (1979) (procedural error harmless where defendant was aware of his rights).

II. **The District Court Correctly Found the Error Harmless**

The district court’s finding that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal is a factual determination entitled to deference under 28 U.S.C. § 2255(h). *See Marshall v. Lonberger*, 459 U.S. 422, 431 (1983) (factual findings in habeas proceedings reviewed for clear error). The court’s conclusion that Peguero’s knowledge rendered the Rule 32 error harmless aligns with the Supreme Court’s guidance in *Timmreck*, where the Court declined to vacate a sentence where the defendant was aware of his rights despite a technical violation. 441 U.S. at 784. Here, as in *Timmreck*, the purpose of Rule 32(a)(2)—ensuring defendants are informed of their appellate rights—was satisfied because Peguero knew of his right to appeal and chose not to pursue it. *See also United States v. Smith*, 995 F.2d 662, 666 (7th Cir. 1993) (holding that a Rule 32 violation is harmless where the defendant was aware of his appellate rights).

Peguero’s reliance on *United States v. Rosales*, 49 F.3d 145 (7th Cir. 1995), is misplaced. In *Rosales*, the Seventh Circuit vacated a sentence where the defendant was *not* aware of his right to appeal. Id. at 147. Unlike *Rosales*, Peguero was fully informed of his rights and made a deliberate choice not to appeal. The district court’s harmless-error analysis thus comports with both the text of Rule 32 and the Supreme Court’s precedent.

III. **Habeas Relief Is Unwarranted Where the Error Did Not Affect the Outcome**

Under 28 U.S.C. § 2255, a petitioner is entitled to relief only if the error "had substantial and injurious effect or influence in determining the jury’s verdict." *Brecht v. Abrahamson*, 507 U.S. 619, 637 (1993) (quoting *Kotteakos v. United States*, 328 U.S. 750, 776 (1946)). Here, the district court’s failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal had no effect on his decision not to appeal. The record confirms that Peguero was aware of his rights and elected not to pursue an appeal. Thus, the error was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, 24 (1967) (harmless-error standard in habeas cases).

**CONCLUSION**

The district court properly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32(a)(2) violation and correctly concluded that Peguero was not entitled to habeas relief. The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
