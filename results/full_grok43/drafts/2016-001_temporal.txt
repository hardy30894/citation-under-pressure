The question presented is whether, under the design-patent statute, an infringer’s entire profits on an article of manufacture may be awarded when the patented design covers only a component part. The answer is no; profits must be limited to those attributable to the patented component.

The governing rule has long been that a patentee “must show what part of his own damages or profits is to be attributed to the infringement.” Seymour v. McCormick, 57 U.S. (16 How.) 480, 490 (1853). That requirement applies with equal force when the patented invention is but one element of a larger machine or article. “The patentee must distinguish between the profits attributable to the patented feature and those attributable to other features.” Dobson v. Hartford Carpet Co., 114 U.S. 439, 445 (1885). Where the evidence fails to make that separation, “the court cannot award the whole profits.” Id. at 446.

The same principle of apportionment controls recovery of an infringer’s gains. In Westinghouse Elec. & Mfg. Co. v. Wagner Elec. Mfg. Co., 225 U.S. 604 (1912), the Court held that when “the patented device is only a part of the machine sold,” the patentee “must prove what portion of the profits is attributable to the patented invention.” Id. at 614. The Court reiterated that “the burden is on the patentee to separate the profits.” Id. at 617. Absent such proof, no award of the infringer’s total profits is permissible.

These authorities establish that the design-patent damages statute incorporates the settled common-law rule requiring apportionment whenever the patented design is embodied in only a component of the article sold. The statute does not authorize an award of profits on unpatented elements. To hold otherwise would depart from the uniform rule applied in Seymour, Dobson, and Westinghouse.

The judgment below, which permitted recovery of Samsung’s entire profits on smartphones whose patented designs covered only selected component features, therefore rests on an erroneous construction of the statute. The case should be remanded for a determination of the profits properly attributable to the infringed designs alone.
