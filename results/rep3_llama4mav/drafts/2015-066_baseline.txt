The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that an uncounseled conviction in tribal court cannot be used as a predicate offense under 18 U.S.C. § 117(a). The Sixth Amendment right to counsel does not apply to tribal court proceedings, and the use of a valid tribal court conviction as a predicate offense does not violate the Constitution.

In Nichols v. United States, 511 U.S. 738 (1994), this Court held that a prior uncounseled misdemeanor conviction can be used to enhance a sentence under the Sentencing Guidelines, as long as the conviction did not violate the Sixth Amendment. The Court reasoned that the sentencing enhancement was based on the defendant's prior conduct, not on the reliability of the prior conviction. Id. at 747. Similarly, § 117(a) penalizes the defendant's most recent offense, not the prior convictions. See United States v. Rodriquez, 553 U.S. 377, 386 (2008).

The Ninth Circuit's reliance on United States v. Ant, 882 F.2d 1389 (9th Cir.1989), is misplaced. Ant predates Nichols and is inconsistent with this Court's subsequent holding. Moreover, Ant's reasoning is flawed because it imposes a Sixth Amendment right to counsel on tribal courts, despite the fact that the Sixth Amendment does not apply to tribal proceedings. See Plains Commerce Bank v. Long Family Land & Cattle Co., 554 U.S. 316, 337 (2008).

The use of tribal court convictions as predicate offenses under § 117(a) is consistent with the principles of comity and respect for tribal sovereignty. Tribal courts are governed by the Indian Civil Rights Act (ICRA), which provides certain procedural safeguards, including a right to counsel in some circumstances. See 25 U.S.C. § 1302(a)-(c). The Ninth Circuit's holding would undermine the comity interest that undergirds respect for tribal courts and their criminal procedures. See Wilson v. Marchington, 127 F.3d 805, 810 (9th Cir.1997).

In conclusion, the Ninth Circuit's holding is contrary to this Court's precedent and undermines the principles of comity and respect for tribal sovereignty. The use of valid tribal court convictions as predicate offenses under § 117(a) does not violate the Sixth Amendment, and the judgment of the Ninth Circuit should be reversed.
