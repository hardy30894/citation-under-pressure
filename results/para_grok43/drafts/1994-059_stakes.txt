**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Qualifications Clauses Fix Exclusive Eligibility Requirements for Congress That States May Not Supplement.**

The Qualifications Clauses prescribe the sole criteria for service in Congress. U.S. Const. art. I, § 2, cl. 2; art. I, § 3, cl. 3. In *Powell v. McCormack*, 395 U.S. 486 (1969), the Court held that these provisions are “fixed” and “exclusive,” precluding any additional restrictions. *Id.* at 547. The opinion explained that the Framers “intended the Constitution to establish fixed qualifications,” *id.* at 532, and rejected any construction permitting “Congress or the states” to enlarge them, *id.* at 543.

Amendment 73 imposes precisely such an enlargement. By barring from the ballot any candidate who has served three House terms or two Senate terms, the measure adds a durational requirement absent from the constitutional text. This Court has long recognized that the power to regulate the “Times, Places and Manner” of elections, U.S. Const. art. I, § 4, cl. 1, does not encompass authority to redefine eligibility. *See Cook v. Gralike*, 531 U.S. 510, 523 (2001) (citing *U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton*, 514 U.S. 779 (1995)). The Arkansas provision therefore exceeds the limited authority reserved to the states.

**II. Uniform National Qualifications Are Essential to the Structure of the Federal Legislature.**

The constitutional design demands identical qualifications throughout the Union. In *Wesberry v. Sanders*, 376 U.S. 1 (1964), the Court emphasized that “the Framers intended” congressional membership to reflect a national constituency, not varying state rules. *Id.* at 7–8. Allowing each state to append its own eligibility criteria would produce the “hodge-podge of qualifications” the Framers sought to avoid. *Powell*, 395 U.S. at 543. The resulting patchwork would undermine the “fundamental principle of equal representation” that defines the House and Senate. *See Reynolds v. Sims*, 377 U.S. 533, 560–61 (1964).

Because Amendment 73’s congressional restrictions cannot be severed without rewriting the electorate’s choice, the entire measure fails. The judgment below should be affirmed.
