**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE OCSLA’S WORKERS’ COMPENSATION PROVISION REQUIRES THAT AN INJURY OCCUR ON THE OUTER CONTINENTAL SHELF.**

Section 1333(b) of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (“OCSLA”) extends Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act (“LHWCA”) coverage to “the disability or death of an employee resulting from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf for the purpose of exploring for, developing, removing, or transporting by pipeline the natural resources of the outer Continental Shelf.” 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) (emphasis added). The plain text of the statute requires that the injury occur “as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” *See generally* *United States v. Ron Pair Enters., Inc.*, 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989) (“Where the statutory language provides a clear answer, our inquiry must cease.”). The statute does not extend coverage to injuries occurring on land, no matter how closely connected to operations on the outer Continental Shelf.

The Fifth Circuit has correctly interpreted this provision to require that the injury occur on the outer Continental Shelf. *Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs*, 877 F.2d 356, 362 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc) (“[A]n OCSLA claimant must show that the injury occurred on an outer continental shelf platform or on the waters above the outer continental shelf, in addition to satisfying the ‘but for’ test.”). *See also Becker v. Tidewater, Inc.*, 586 F.3d 358, 366-67 (5th Cir. 2009); *Pickett v. Petroleum Helicopters, Inc.*, 266 F.3d 366, 368 (5th Cir. 2001); *Sisson v. Davis & Sons, Inc.*, 131 F.3d 555, 558 (5th Cir. 1998). The Third Circuit, by contrast, has adopted a more expansive interpretation, holding that an injury need only be “the result of” operations on the outer Continental Shelf, regardless of where the injury occurs. *Curtis v. Schlumberger Offshore Serv., Inc.*, 849 F.2d 805, 809-11 (3d Cir. 1988). This Court should reject the Third Circuit’s approach and follow the Fifth Circuit’s well-reasoned interpretation.

The Fifth Circuit’s interpretation is consistent with the Supreme Court’s guidance in *Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire*, 477 U.S. 207 (1986). In *Tallentire*, the Court noted that “Congress determined that the general scope of OCSLA’s coverage . . . would be determined principally by locale, not by the status of the individual injured or killed.” *Id.* at 219. The Court further observed that “[o]nly one provision of OCSLA superimposes a status requirement on the otherwise determinative OCSLA situs requirement; § 1333(b).” *Id.* at 219 n.2. Although this statement was dictum, it reflects the Court’s understanding that § 1333(b) incorporates the LHWCA’s situs requirement, which limits coverage to injuries occurring on navigable waters or adjoining areas. *See* 33 U.S.C. § 903(a); *Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray (Herb’s Welding II)*, 766 F.2d 898, 900 (5th Cir. 1985) (“[T]he injury must occur on the outer Continental Shelf or the waters above it.”).

The Ninth Circuit’s decision below conflicts with *Tallentire* and the Fifth Circuit’s interpretation of § 1333(b). The court below held that § 1333(b) does not require a situs-of-injury requirement, instead adopting a “substantial nexus” test. *Valladolid v. Pacific Operations Offshore, LLP*, 6933, 6940 (9th Cir. 2010). This interpretation stretches the statute beyond its plain meaning and ignores the Supreme Court’s guidance. The statute’s requirement that the injury occur “as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf” does not eliminate the situs requirement; it merely clarifies that the operations must be conducted on the outer Continental Shelf. *See* 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The injury itself must still occur on the outer Continental Shelf.

**II. THE LHWCA’S MARITIME SITUS REQUIREMENT IS NOT SATISFIED HERE.**

Even if § 1333(b) were interpreted to extend coverage to injuries occurring on land, the LHWCA’s maritime situs requirement is not satisfied in this case. The LHWCA provides compensation for injuries occurring “upon the navigable waters of the United States” or “[a]ny adjoining area customarily used by an employer in loading, unloading, repairing, or building a vessel.” 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). The Ninth Circuit has held that a facility is an “adjoining area” only if it is “used as an integral part of longshoring operations.” *Brady-Hamilton Stevedore Co. v. Herron*, 568 F.2d 137, 141 (9th Cir. 1978).

Here, the decedent was killed at Pacific Operations’s onshore oil-processing facility, La Conchita, which is located 250-300 feet from the ocean but separated from the water by a highway and railroad tracks. The facility has no direct access to any pier, dock, or loading facility. The scrap metal that the decedent was handling was transported to La Conchita by third-party drivers after being unloaded at the Casitas Pass Pier, three miles away. This does not satisfy the maritime situs requirement. *See Motoviloff v. Dir., Office of Workers’ Comp. Programs*, 692 F.2d 87, 89 (9th Cir. 1982) (facility not a maritime situs where separated from water by a highway and railroad tracks).

The petitioner’s reliance on *P.C. Pfeiffer Co. v. Ford*, 444 U.S. 69 (1979), is misplaced. In *Pfeiffer*, the Supreme Court held that warehouse employees handling cargo within a port were engaged in maritime employment, but the Court did not address the situs requirement because the injuries occurred on a dock and a pier, which are indisputably maritime situs. *Id.* at 71, 83. Here, by contrast, the injury occurred at a facility that is not an “adjoining area” within the meaning of § 903(a). The scrap metal handling at La Conchita was not an “intermediate step[] of moving cargo between ship and land transportation.” *Id.* at 82-83. The maritime activities began and ended at the Casitas Pass Pier, three miles away.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit’s decision and hold that the OCSLA’s workers’ compensation provision requires that an injury occur on the outer Continental Shelf and that the LHWCA’s maritime situs requirement is not satisfied in this case.
