**Argument**

**I. The First Circuit Correctly Applied the Time-Place-Manner Test Established in *Hill v. Colorado*.**

The First Circuit properly upheld the Massachusetts buffer zone as a valid time-place-manner regulation. Under *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*, 491 U.S. 781, 791 (1989), such regulations must be justified by a significant government interest and leave open ample alternative channels of communication. The Massachusetts statute is content-neutral, as it regulates conduct rather than the message of speech. *McCullen v. Coakley*, 571 F.3d 167, 176 (1st Cir. 2009).

The state has a significant interest in protecting the safety and privacy of patients and clinic workers. *Hill v. Colorado*, 530 U.S. 703, 715 (2000). The First Circuit affirmed this interest. *McCullen*, 571 F.3d at 176.

The regulation is narrowly tailored to serve that interest. *Hill*, 530 U.S. at 721. The First Circuit found the 35-foot zone was the least restrictive means to ensure safe passage. *McCullen*, 571 F.3d at 176-81.

Finally, the law leaves open ample alternative channels. The First Circuit noted that, notwithstanding the buffer zones, "adequate communicative channels remain available to the plaintiffs, including oral speech of varying degrees of volume and amplification, distribution of literature, displays of signage and symbols, and prayer alone and in groups." *McCullen*, 571 F.3d at 176-81. Therefore, the First Circuit did not err in affirming the judgment below.
