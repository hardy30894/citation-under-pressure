ARGUMENT

I. Stelwagon Failed to Prove Actual Antitrust Damages Recoverable Under Section 4 of the Clayton Act.

Section 4 of the Clayton Act authorizes treble damages only upon proof that the plaintiff “has been injured in his business or property by reason of anything forbidden in the antitrust laws.” 15 U.S.C. § 15(a). The Supreme Court has held that this language requires a plaintiff to establish “actual injury” attributable to the violation; injury is not presumed from the fact of a Robinson-Patman violation itself. J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp., 451 U.S. 557, 562 (1981) (“[T]he plaintiff must show that the defendant’s unlawful conduct caused him injury.”). See also Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat, Inc., 429 U.S. 477, 489 (1977) (“Plaintiffs must prove antitrust injury, which is to say injury of the type the antitrust laws were intended to prevent.”).

Stelwagon’s evidence fell short of this standard. Although the jury found a prima facie violation, Stelwagon introduced no competent proof that the price differentials caused it to lose sales or profits that it otherwise would have made. The district court’s post-trial reduction of the antitrust award to $450,383.60 rested on the same evidentiary gap the Supreme Court identified in J. Truett Payne: an absence of “some evidence of actual injury.” 451 U.S. at 565. Without such evidence, the trebled judgment cannot stand.

II. The District Court Erred in Denying Judgment as a Matter of Law on the Antitrust Claim.

A motion for judgment as a matter of law tests whether “there is no legally sufficient evidentiary basis for a reasonable jury to find for” the nonmoving party. Fed. R. Civ. P. 50(a). Where, as here, the record contains no proof of actual damages, the motion must be granted. See Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986) (summary judgment appropriate when nonmovant fails to make a sufficient showing on an essential element). The same standard governs Rule 50 motions after trial. See Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 250-52 (1986).

Because Stelwagon presented insufficient evidence of actual antitrust injury, the district court should have entered judgment for Tarmac on the Robinson-Patman claim. The judgment awarding treble damages must therefore be vacated.
