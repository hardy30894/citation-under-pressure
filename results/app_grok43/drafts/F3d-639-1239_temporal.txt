Argument

The district court erred in holding that BCR’s Rule 68 offer extinguished the controversy. A class suit, once filed, implicates the rights of absent members whose joinder is impracticable. Hansberry v. Lee, 311 U.S. 32, 41 (1940) (“class suits”). The controversy therefore does not become hypothetical or abstract merely because the named plaintiff’s individual demand has been tendered. Aetna Life Ins. Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227, 240 (1937) (“A justiciable controversy is thus distinguished from a difference or dispute of a hypothetical or abstract character”).

Equity long recognized that a representative action proceeds to judgment binding the class even when the named party’s personal interest later changes, provided the suit was properly brought on behalf of the class. Supreme Tribe of Ben Hur v. Cauble, 255 U.S. 356, 366 (1921) (“the decree binds all of the class properly represented”). An unaccepted offer of judgment does not erase the class allegations or the live dispute over whether the defendant’s practices violate the rights asserted for the class. See Smith v. Swormstedt, 57 U.S. 288, 302 (1853) (representative suits “prevent a failure of justice” where numerous parties share a common interest).

Because the complaint placed the class claims in controversy from the outset, satisfaction of the named plaintiff’s individual claim leaves the action justiciable. The district court’s dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction therefore cannot stand.
