ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Lucero's Class-Action Complaint for Lack of Subject Matter Jurisdiction Based on Mootness.

The district court's dismissal of Lucero's complaint was based on its conclusion that, because BCR offered to satisfy Lucero's entire individual claim, there was no longer a justiciable dispute. However, this conclusion ignores the Supreme Court's holdings in Sosna v. Iowa, 419 U.S. 393 (1975), and U.S. Parole Commission v. Geraghty, 445 U.S. 388 (1980), which establish that a class action is not rendered moot when the named plaintiff's individual claims are satisfied, if the plaintiff has a continuing personal stake in the class claims.

In Sosna, the Supreme Court held that a class action is not moot when the named plaintiff's individual claim becomes moot after the class has been certified. 419 U.S. at 399-401. Although the Court noted that the mootness of the named plaintiff's individual claim does not necessarily moot the class action, it also recognized that the class action "acquires a legal status independent of the named plaintiffs' claims." Id. at 399. The Court later extended this principle in Geraghty, holding that a plaintiff who has lost standing to pursue their individual claim may still have standing to pursue a class claim, if the plaintiff had a "personal stake" in the class claim at the time the complaint was filed. 445 U.S. at 404.

The key issue here is whether Lucero had a "personal stake" in the class claims at the time BCR made the Rule 68 offer of judgment. The Supreme Court has recognized that a plaintiff who files a class action complaint has a personal stake in the class claims, even if their individual claims are later satisfied. See Deposit Guar. Nat'l Bank v. Roper, 445 U.S. 326, 336-37 (1980). This personal stake is not eliminated by an offer of judgment that satisfies the plaintiff's individual claims.

The Tenth Circuit has not directly addressed this issue, but our decision in Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 590 F.3d 1134 (10th Cir. 2009), is distinguishable. In Clark, we stated that "a suit brought as a class action must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified." 590 F.3d at 1138 (internal quotation marks and citation omitted). However, this statement was made in the context of a case where the plaintiffs had not taken any steps to pursue class certification. In contrast, Lucero and BCR had agreed to a joint status report and provisional discovery plan that prioritized class certification discovery, and Lucero had filed a motion for class certification before the district court dismissed his complaint.

Furthermore, allowing a defendant to "buy off" a class action plaintiff by tendering an early Rule 68 judgment for the full amount of individual claims undermines the purpose of the class action device. See Roper, 445 U.S. at 339. The Supreme Court has recognized that defendants should not be able to "pick off" named plaintiffs in this manner, as it would allow them to avoid liability for class-wide claims. Id.

In this case, Lucero had a personal stake in the class claims, and BCR's offer of judgment did not eliminate that stake. The district court erred in dismissing Lucero's complaint for lack of subject matter jurisdiction based on mootness.

II. The District Court Should Have Related Lucero's Motion for Class Certification Back to the Filing of the Complaint.

Alternatively, even if Lucero's individual claims were rendered moot by BCR's offer of judgment, the district court should have related Lucero's motion for class certification back to the filing of the complaint. This approach is consistent with the Supreme Court's holdings in Sosna and Geraghty, which recognize that a class action is not moot when the named plaintiff's individual claims are satisfied, if the plaintiff has taken timely steps to pursue class certification.

The Seventh Circuit has adopted this approach in similar cases. See Susman v. Lincoln Am. Corp., 587 F.2d 866, 870 (7th Cir. 1978). Relating the motion for class certification back to the filing of the complaint ensures that the class action is not rendered moot by a defendant's attempt to "buy off" the named plaintiff.

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the district court's dismissal of Lucero's complaint and remand for further proceedings.
