ARGUMENT

The Due Process Clause permits a state court to exercise specific personal jurisdiction over a nonresident corporation whose in-state activities create a substantial connection to the litigation. *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 316 (1945). Jurisdiction is proper when the defendant has “certain minimum contacts with [the forum] such that the maintenance of the suit does not offend ‘traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.’” *Id.* at 316 (quoting *Milliken v. Meyer*, 311 U.S. 457, 463 (1940)).

Ford’s continuous and systematic course of conduct in Montana satisfies this standard. For decades Ford has advertised, sold, and serviced vehicles and replacement parts throughout the State. Such purposeful availment of Montana’s market renders it reasonable to require Ford to answer claims arising from the use of its products there. *See Travelers Health Ass’n v. Virginia*, 339 U.S. 643, 647-48 (1950); *Perkins v. Benguet Consol. Mining Co.*, 342 U.S. 437, 445 (1952); *McGee v. International Life Ins. Co.*, 355 U.S. 220, 223 (1957).

The requisite relationship between Ford’s Montana contacts and the present claims exists even though the particular Explorer was not first sold in Montana. When a defendant regularly places goods into the stream of commerce that foreseeably reach the forum, jurisdiction follows. *Hess v. Pawloski*, 274 U.S. 352, 356-57 (1927); *Kane v. New Jersey*, 242 U.S. 160, 167 (1917). Ford’s extensive Montana dealership network, parts distribution, and advertising directly facilitated the presence of its vehicles on Montana roads, including the one involved in the accident.

Traditional principles confirm that the suit “arises out of or relates to” those contacts. *See Henry L. Doherty & Co. v. Goodman*, 294 U.S. 623, 628 (1935); *Pennoyer v. Neff*, 95 U.S. 714, 733 (1877). Requiring Ford to litigate in Montana imposes no undue burden, for it has long maintained a substantial presence there and the evidence concerning the vehicle’s maintenance and the accident is located in the State. *See Milliken*, 311 U.S. at 463-64.

Because Ford’s Montana activities created the substantial connection that Due Process demands, the judgments below should be affirmed.
