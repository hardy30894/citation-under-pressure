ARGUMENT

The Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act does not divest the National Park Service of authority to enforce its hovercraft regulation within the boundaries of the Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve. Federal power to protect lands and waters placed under its administration remains intact.

The Property Clause confers upon Congress plenary authority over federal lands. U.S. Const. art. IV, § 3, cl. 2; Camfield v. United States, 167 U.S. 518, 525 (1897) (“The government has, with respect to its own lands, the rights of an ordinary proprietor”). That power extends to the adoption of rules “necessary to the protection of the public lands.” United States v. Grimaud, 220 U.S. 506, 516 (1911); Light v. United States, 220 U.S. 523, 537 (1911). Regulations promulgated under this authority apply throughout areas Congress has designated for federal management, even when nonfederal parcels lie within the exterior boundaries. Gibson v. Chouteau, 80 U.S. 92, 99 (1872); Irvine v. Marshall, 61 U.S. 558, 564 (1858).

Navigable waters within those boundaries are likewise subject to federal control. The Daniel Ball, 77 U.S. (10 Wall.) 557, 563 (1870). The United States may regulate activities upon such waters to safeguard the dominant federal interest in navigation and commerce. United States v. Chandler-Dunbar Water Power Co., 229 U.S. 53, 62 (1913); United States v. Appalachian Elec. Power Co., 311 U.S. 377, 404 (1940). This authority is not lost merely because the submerged land is owned by a State. Ashwander v. Tennessee Valley Authority, 297 U.S. 288, 338 (1936).

Congress may, of course, limit the reach of federal regulation. Yet nothing in the 1980 Act repeals the preexisting power to enforce generally applicable rules inside conservation system units. United States v. Midwest Oil Co., 236 U.S. 459, 474 (1915) (repeals by implication are disfavored). The hovercraft prohibition, 36 C.F.R. § 2.17(e), is not a measure directed solely at nonfederal land; it governs all lands and waters administered by the Service. Therefore the Act leaves the regulation undisturbed.

The Service’s enforcement action rests on settled constitutional and statutory foundations. The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
