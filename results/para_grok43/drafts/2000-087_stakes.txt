**ARGUMENT**

**I. Section 1231(a)(6) Does Not Authorize Indefinite Post-Removal Detention.**

The post-removal-period statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(6), permits detention of certain removable aliens “beyond the removal period” but contains no language authorizing detention once removal is no longer reasonably foreseeable. The statute must be read to incorporate an implicit temporal limitation. “Based on our conclusion that indefinite detention of aliens in the former category would raise serious constitutional concerns, we construe the statute to contain an implicit ‘reasonable time’ limitation, the application of which is subject to federal court review.” Zadvydas v. Davis, 533 U.S. 678, 682 (2001).

This construction follows from the settled rule that statutes should be interpreted to avoid serious constitutional doubts. See, e.g., INS v. St. Cyr, 533 U.S. 289, 299–300 (2001). Indefinite civil detention raises such doubts. See Kansas v. Hendricks, 521 U.S. 346, 356 (1997) (civil detention must be justified by a “special justification” and must not “become[] punishment”); Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71, 80 (1992) (continued confinement requires proof of dangerousness and no less restrictive alternative).

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary view—that detention remains lawful so long as “good faith efforts to effectuate deportation continue”—demands more than the statute permits. Zadvydas, 533 U.S. at 702. Once removal ceases to be reasonably foreseeable, continued detention exceeds statutory authority. Id. at 699–700. The habeas court’s task is therefore to determine whether the period of detention has become unreasonable in light of the statute’s purpose of assuring the alien’s presence at removal. Id. at 699.

Because the record establishes that no significant likelihood of Zadvydas’s removal exists in the reasonably foreseeable future, and the government has not rebutted that showing, his detention is no longer authorized. The judgment ordering his release, subject to appropriate conditions of supervision, should be affirmed.
