**Argument**

The district court correctly held that ANILCA § 103(c) does not prohibit the NPS from regulating activities on state-owned lands within National Park System units. We review this question de novo. 16 U.S.C. § 1132(c) provides that the Secretary "may not promulgate or enforce any regulation applicable to public lands within a conservation system unit in Alaska that is not applicable to public lands outside such unit." The NPS hovercraft ban is applicable to lands outside the unit, as it applies to all NPS-administered lands. Therefore, the restriction in § 103(c) does not apply. *United States v. Utah*, 403 U.S. 9, 12 (1971) (stating that "the term 'public lands' is used in the Act in a generic sense to refer to lands owned by the United States").

Furthermore, the term "public lands" in § 103(c) refers to lands owned by the United States. State lands are not "public lands." Therefore, the limitation does not apply to state lands. *United States v. Utah*, 403 U.S. 9, 12 (1971) (defining "public lands" as lands owned by the United States).
