**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Seventh Circuit Correctly Recognized a "Class of One" Equal Protection Claim Where the Village’s Action Was Motivated by Arbitrary, Class-Based Animus.**

The Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment prohibits a state from denying any person within its jurisdiction the equal protection of the laws. *Yick Wo v. Hopkins*, 118 U.S. 356, 369 (1886). The Seventh Circuit correctly held that a plaintiff may bring a "class of one" equal protection claim where the challenged action was motivated by arbitrary, class-based animus. *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 160 F.3d 386, 388 (7th Cir. 1998) (Posner, J.), *rev’d on other grounds*, 528 U.S. 562 (2000). This Court has recognized that the Equal Protection Clause prohibits "invidious discrimination" and "arbitrary and irrational" state action. *See Romer v. Evans*, 517 U.S. 620, 633 (1996) (quoting *U.S. Dept. of Agric. v. Moreno*, 413 U.S. 528, 534 (1973)).

The Seventh Circuit’s holding is consistent with this Court’s precedents recognizing that the Equal Protection Clause protects individuals from arbitrary and irrational state action, even absent membership in a suspect class. *See City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Ctr.*, 473 U.S. 432, 446 (1985) (holding that the Equal Protection Clause protects against irrational state action, regardless of whether the plaintiff belongs to a suspect class). The Seventh Circuit’s reasoning aligns with this Court’s admonition that the Equal Protection Clause prohibits state action motivated by "a bare desire to harm a politically unpopular group." *Romer*, 517 U.S. at 634 (quoting *Dept. of Agric. v. Moreno*, 413 U.S. at 534).

**II. The Village’s Action Was Motivated by Arbitrary, Class-Based Animus, Warranting Equal Protection Review.**

The Seventh Circuit correctly found that Olech plausibly alleged that the Village’s demand for an 18-foot easement was motivated by a "spiteful effort to 'get' her for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective." *Olech*, 160 F.3d at 388. This Court has recognized that state action motivated by "animus" or "ill will" toward a particular individual violates the Equal Protection Clause. *See Romer*, 517 U.S. at 634 (holding that state action motivated by "a bare desire to harm" a politically unpopular group violates the Equal Protection Clause). The Seventh Circuit’s holding is consistent with this Court’s precedents recognizing that the Equal Protection Clause prohibits state action motivated by "a desire to harm a politically unpopular group." *Id.*

The Seventh Circuit’s reasoning is further supported by this Court’s decision in *Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Dev. Corp.*, 429 U.S. 252 (1977), which held that proof of discriminatory intent is required to establish an equal protection violation. *Id.* at 265. The Seventh Circuit correctly found that Olech plausibly alleged discriminatory intent, as the Village’s demand for an 18-foot easement was not rationally related to any legitimate state objective. *Olech*, 160 F.3d at 388.

**III. The Seventh Circuit’s Holding Is Consistent with This Court’s Recognition of Equal Protection Claims Brought by Individuals Not Belonging to a Suspect Class.**

This Court has recognized that the Equal Protection Clause protects individuals from arbitrary and irrational state action, regardless of whether the plaintiff belongs to a suspect class. *See Cleburne*, 473 U.S. at 446. The Seventh Circuit’s holding is consistent with this Court’s precedents recognizing that the Equal Protection Clause prohibits state action motivated by "a bare desire to harm a politically unpopular group." *Romer*, 517 U.S. at 634 (quoting *Moreno*, 413 U.S. at 534).

The Seventh Circuit’s reasoning is further supported by this Court’s decision in *Nordlinger v. Hahn*, 505 U.S. 1 (1992), which held that the Equal Protection Clause prohibits state action that is "arbitrary and irrational." *Id.* at 10. The Seventh Circuit correctly found that Olech plausibly alleged that the Village’s action was arbitrary and irrational, as the demand for an 18-foot easement was not rationally related to any legitimate state objective. *Olech*, 160 F.3d at 388.
