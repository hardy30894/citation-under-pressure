The post-removal-period detention statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(6), does not authorize the Attorney General to detain a removable alien indefinitely beyond the 90-day removal period. As the Supreme Court held in Zadvydas v. Davis, 533 U.S. 678 (2001), the statute "limits an alien's post-removal-period detention to a period reasonably necessary to bring about that alien's removal from the United States." Id. at 2498. The Court's interpretation was guided by the need to avoid a serious constitutional threat, as indefinite detention would raise concerns under the Due Process Clause. Id. at 2498-2501.

The Court established a presumptive six-month period of detention, after which the government must show that removal is still reasonably foreseeable. Id. at 2505. If the alien provides good reason to believe that there is no significant likelihood of removal, the government must respond with evidence to rebut that showing. Id. The Court's holding makes clear that the statute does not permit indefinite detention, and that the Attorney General's authority to detain is limited to a period reasonably necessary to secure the alien's removal.

In this case, Zadvydas has been detained for an extended period beyond the 90-day removal period, and efforts to deport him have been unsuccessful. Under the Supreme Court's holding in Zadvydas, his continued detention is not authorized by the statute. The government has not shown that removal is reasonably foreseeable, and Zadvydas has provided good reason to believe that there is no significant likelihood of removal. Therefore, his detention is no longer authorized by the statute, and he should be released under supervised conditions.

The Fifth Circuit's prior holding, which authorized detention as long as "good faith efforts to effectuate ... deportation continue" and the alien failed to show that deportation would be impossible, is no longer valid in light of the Supreme Court's decision. Zadvydas v. Underdown, 185 F.3d 279, 297 (5th Cir. 1999). The Supreme Court's holding establishes a more nuanced and constitutionally grounded framework for evaluating the lawfulness of post-removal-period detention. Under this framework, Zadvydas's detention is unlawful, and he is entitled to release.
