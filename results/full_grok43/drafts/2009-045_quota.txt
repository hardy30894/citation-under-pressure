**Argument**

The lodestar figure may be enhanced under a federal fee-shifting statute when the fee applicant presents specific evidence that superior representation produced exceptional results. Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984); Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens' Council for Clean Air, 478 U.S. 546, 565 (1986).

In Blum the Court held that an enhancement is permissible in the “rare case where the fee applicant offers specific evidence to show that the quality of service rendered was superior to that one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged” and produced “exceptional” success. 465 U.S. at 899. The same principle was reaffirmed in Delaware Valley I, which stated that “upward adjustments of the lodestar figure are still permissible … in certain ‘rare’ and ‘exceptional’ cases, supported by both ‘specific evidence’ on the record and detailed findings by the lower courts.” 478 U.S. at 565 (quoting Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-901). Hensley v. Eckerhart likewise recognized that “in some cases of exceptional success an enhancement award may be justified.” 461 U.S. 424, 435 (1983).

The Eleventh Circuit has consistently applied these standards. Kenny A. v. Perdue, 532 F.3d 1209, 1242 (11th Cir. 2008); Norman v. Housing Auth. of the City of Montgomery, 836 F.2d 1292, 1302 (11th Cir. 1988); NAACP v. City of Evergreen, 812 F.2d 1332, 1336-37 (11th Cir. 1987) (per curiam). Every other circuit to address the question has reached the same conclusion. Geier v. Sundquist, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co., 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000); Quaratino v. Tiffany & Co., 166 F.3d 422, 425 (2d Cir. 1999); Forshee v. Waterloo Indus., Inc., 178 F.3d 527, 532 (8th Cir. 1999); Hyatt v. Apfel, 195 F.3d 188, 192 (4th Cir. 1999); Roe v. Cheyenne Mountain Conference Resort, Inc., 124 F.3d 1221, 1233 n.8 (10th Cir. 1997); Shipes v. Trinity Indus., 987 F.2d 311, 320 (5th Cir. 1993).

The district court made detailed findings, based on its observation of counsel’s performance throughout the litigation, that the lodestar did not capture the superior quality of representation or the exceptional results obtained. Those findings satisfy the “specific evidence” requirement of Blum and Delaware Valley I. Because the enhancement rests on record-supported determinations that fall squarely within the discretion recognized by the governing precedents, the judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
