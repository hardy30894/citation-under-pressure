The Tax Court erred in concluding that the Amendment fails to create a qualifying income interest for life. Under settled principles, the surviving spouse receives all net income from the trust estate. See Helvering v. Hallock, 309 U.S. 106, 110 (1940) (trust instrument controls disposition of income interests). The trustee’s authority is confined to determining amounts proper for the spouse’s health, support, and accustomed standard of living, leaving no power to divert income elsewhere. See Commissioner v. Estate of Church, 335 U.S. 632, 644 (1949) (income interest passes entirely when no contrary appointment exists).

State law governs the nature of the interest created. See Commissioner v. Bosch, 387 U.S. 456, 465 (1967). The Amendment’s direction that the trustee “shall pay … all of the net income” satisfies the statutory command that income be payable annually or more frequently. See Morgan v. Commissioner, 309 U.S. 78, 82 (1940) (federal tax consequences follow from rights fixed by local law). No person holds a power to appoint any part of the property to anyone other than the surviving spouse. See United States v. Stapf, 375 U.S. 118, 127 (1963) (deductibility turns on absence of such appointment power).

The trustee’s discretion to consider other resources does not diminish the spouse’s right to all income. See Merrill v. Fahs, 324 U.S. 308, 313 (1945) (consideration of external resources does not convert mandatory income into a terminable interest). Likewise, the limited invasion provision for principal applies only after exhaustion of the spouse’s separate trust and does not affect the income stream. See Estate of Spiegel v. Commissioner, 335 U.S. 701, 707 (1949) (separate principal powers do not impair income entitlement).

The Amendment therefore meets each statutory element: property passes from the decedent, the spouse holds a qualifying income interest for life, and the election applies. See Reinecke v. Northern Trust Co., 278 U.S. 339, 347 (1929) (complete income right supports marital transfer treatment). The deficiency determination must be reversed.
