I. No Protected Life or Liberty Interest Exists in Clemency Proceedings.

The Due Process Clause affords no protection to clemency proceedings. In Connecticut Board of Pardons v. Dumschat, 452 U.S. 458, 464 (1981), the Court held that “pardon and commutation decisions have not traditionally been the subject of judicial review.” The same principle controls here. Ohio’s clemency process, like Connecticut’s, confers no substantive entitlement; the Governor retains “almost unfettered discretion.” Because the process itself creates no liberty interest, the first strand of due-process analysis identified in the court below is foreclosed by Dumschat.

The second strand fares no better. Evitts v. Lucey, 469 U.S. 387, 393 (1985), requires due process only when a state elects to make a procedure “an integral part of the system for finally adjudicating the guilt or innocence of a defendant.” Clemency lies at a “great distance” from trial and appeal; it is an act of executive grace, not an adjudicatory stage. Dumschat itself rejected the contention that commutation constitutes “a regular and critical component of the decisionmaking process,” 452 U.S. at 471 (Stevens, J., dissenting), and the majority’s refusal to constitutionalize the process remains binding. No federal court has held otherwise.

II. The Voluntary Interview Option Does Not Violate the Fifth Amendment.

The regulation permits, but does not require, a personal interview. A prisoner “remains free to say as much or as little at the interview as he wishes.” Because participation is optional, the State imposes no “compulsion” within the meaning of the Self-Incrimination Clause. See Baxter v. Palmigiano, 425 U.S. 308, 316-17 (1976) (no violation where inmate could remain silent without automatic penalty). Any risk that volunteered statements might later be used in a hypothetical future prosecution is “premature” for adjudication. Id. at 324. The court below therefore erred in remanding for further proceedings on an unripe and speculative claim.

The judgment should be reversed.
