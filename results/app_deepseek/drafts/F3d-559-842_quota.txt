**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment Because Genuine Issues of Material Fact Exist as to Whether Rand’s Violations of TILA and HOEPA Extended the Rescission Period to Three Years.**

Under the Truth in Lending Act (TILA), 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a), a borrower’s right to rescind a consumer credit transaction secured by a principal dwelling ends three business days after consummation, unless the lender fails to deliver material disclosures or a proper notice of the right to cancel. *Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank*, 523 U.S. 410, 416 (1998). When such a failure occurs, the rescission period extends to three years. *Id.* at 417. The Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA), 15 U.S.C. § 1639, imposes additional requirements for high-cost loans, including a three-day advance disclosure period before closing. *In re Steinbrecher*, 110 B.R. 155, 160 (Bankr. E.D. Pa. 1990). The Mouas presented evidence that Rand failed to make material disclosures, failed to provide clear and conspicuous notice of the cancellation right, and included an unlawful prepayment penalty—each of which independently triggers the three-year rescission period.

**A. Rand’s Failure to Provide Material Disclosures Under TILA and HOEPA Extends the Rescission Period.**

TILA requires that lenders clearly and conspicuously disclose the “finance charge,” “annual percentage rate,” “total of payments,” and other terms. *Koos v. First Nat’l Bank of Peoria*, 496 F.2d 1162, 1164 (8th Cir. 1974). HOEPA requires a separate three-day advance disclosure for high-cost loans. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b). The Mouas assert that Rand provided no HOEPA-specific disclosure before the April 22, 2005, closing. Rand’s sole defense is that the Mouas waived the three-day period due to a “bona fide personal financial emergency.” However, any waiver of TILA rights must be knowing, voluntary, and strictly construed. *Smith v. Chapman*, 614 F.2d 968, 972 (5th Cir. 1980). Manisy Moua testified that she did not understand the handwritten waiver statement and was instructed to copy it without explanation. This creates a genuine dispute over whether the waiver was valid. *Williams v. Homestake Mortg. Co.*, 968 F.2d 1137, 1142 (11th Cir. 1992) (holding that waiver of TILA rights is not presumed). Because material disclosures were not made, the three-year rescission period applies.

**B. Rand’s Failure to Provide Clear and Conspicuous Notice of the Right to Cancel Extends the Rescission Period.**

TILA mandates that the notice of the right to cancel “clearly and conspicuously” disclose the borrower’s three-day rescission right. 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(a)(3). When the notice is confusing or misleading, the rescission period is extended. *Rodash v. AIB Mortg. Co.*, 16 F.3d 1142, 1144 (11th Cir. 1994). Here, Rand provided two versions of the Notice of Right to Cancel. One version included a “Confirmation” section that prematurely stated “more than 3 business days have elapsed” and certified that the transaction had not been rescinded—yet the Mouas signed this section on the same day as closing, April 22, 2005. This contradictory language undermines the clarity of the notice. *Murphy v. Empire of Am., FSA*, 746 F.2d 931, 937 (2d Cir. 1984) (holding that a notice that is “not clear and conspicuous” fails to trigger the rescission period). Moreover, Rand’s closing agent instructed the Mouas to sign the Confirmation before the three-day period expired, effectively inducing them to waive a right they had not yet been able to exercise. *Semar v. Platte Valley Fed. Sav. & Loan Ass’n*, 791 F.2d 699, 704 (9th Cir. 1986) (rescission period cannot be waived before it runs). These facts raise a triable issue as to whether the notice was clear and conspicuous.

**C. Rand’s Inclusion of an Unlawful Prepayment Penalty Under HOEPA Extends the Rescission Period.**

HOEPA prohibits prepayment penalties for high-cost loans. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c)(1). The loan at issue here is a high-cost loan because it has an APR exceeding 8% above the comparable Treasury yield and points exceed 8% of the loan amount. *In re Steinbrecher*, 110 B.R. at 160. Rand’s note includes a prepayment penalty, which alone constitutes a violation of HOEPA. *Williams*, 968 F.2d at 1142. Under TILA, any violation of HOEPA that involves material disclosure requirements extends the rescission period to three years. *Beach*, 523 U.S. at 416. Because the prepayment penalty is a material term that was not properly disclosed, the Mouas remain entitled to rescind.

**II. Genuine Issues of Material Fact Preclude Summary Judgment.**

The district court improperly resolved factual disputes in Rand’s favor. The Mouas’ sworn testimony that they did not receive required disclosures, did not understand the waiver, and were misled by the Confirmation form directly contradicts Rand’s evidence. *Koos*, 496 F.2d at 1164 (summary judgment improper where material facts are disputed). At a minimum, a reasonable jury could find that Rand’s failures violated TILA and HOEPA, entitling the Mouas to a three-year rescission period.

**Conclusion**

For these reasons, the district court’s judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for trial.
