The judgment must be reversed because the district court admitted extensive, highly prejudicial testimony from two assistant district attorneys and a police lieutenant. These witnesses opined on the credibility of Officers Ramos and Rivera, the existence of probable cause, and the weight of evidence that the jury was equally capable of evaluating. Such testimony contravenes settled rules of evidence.

Witnesses may not vouch for the credibility of other witnesses. United States v. Forrester, 60 F.3d 52, 63 (2d Cir. 1995). Nor may they testify in the form of legal conclusions. Hygh v. Jacobs, 961 F.2d 359, 363 (2d Cir. 1992) (expert witnesses may not “offer opinions as to the legal implications of a party’s conduct”). Finally, witnesses may not interpret evidence that jurors can analyze without assistance. United States v. Scop, 846 F.2d 135, 142 (2d Cir. 1988).

The admitted testimony violated each prohibition. The ADAs and lieutenant directly addressed Ramos’s and Rivera’s truthfulness, stated that probable cause existed for the arrests and prosecution, and explained how particular photographs and statements strengthened or weakened appellants’ case. These opinions spoke to the two issues the parties contested most vigorously.

The errors were not harmless. An evidentiary error requires reversal when it “had substantial and injurious effect or influence in determining the jury’s verdict.” Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750, 764-65 (1946). The testimony came from ostensibly neutral government officials and addressed the precise questions the jury had to decide. Its admission therefore requires a new trial.

Appellants are also entitled to a punitive-damages instruction on retrial. A jury may award punitive damages in a § 1983 case upon a showing of malice or reckless disregard of constitutional rights. Smith v. Wade, 461 U.S. 30, 56 (1983). Appellants’ account, if credited, permits such a finding. The district court should likewise instruct that, under New York law, a person cannot be guilty of obstructing governmental administration unless the officer was performing a lawful function. People v. Greene, 5 N.Y.3d 171, 175 (2005).

For these reasons, the judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for a new trial.
