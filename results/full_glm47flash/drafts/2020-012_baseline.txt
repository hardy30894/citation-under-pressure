**Argument**

I. The decision by the United States Railroad Retirement Board (“Board”) refusing to reopen Salinas’s prior claim is a “final decision” on the merits of a claim for benefits and therefore subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding in *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.* is erroneous and conflicts with the plain language of the statute and the reasoning of the D.C. Circuit. A decision denying a request to reopen a prior claim is a substantive determination of the claimant’s rights, not merely a procedural refusal. The Fifth Circuit in *Roberts* held that a decision not to reopen is not a “final decision” because it does not determine entitlement to benefits. *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003). This interpretation is too narrow. A decision that a claim is barred by the statute of limitations is a final determination of the claimant’s rights regarding that claim. The D.C. Circuit has rejected the majority view that decisions not to reopen are non-reviewable. In *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, the D.C. Circuit held that the Railroad Retirement Act grants the circuit court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations. *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016). The court reasoned that a decision not to reopen is a final decision on the merits of the underlying claim. *Id.* at 504. The Supreme Court has emphasized that the statute allows review of “final decisions.” *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 106 (1977). A decision that a claim is time-barred is a final decision. The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Roberts* to create a jurisdictional bar where none exists in the text of the statute is misplaced. Therefore, the Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim is a final decision subject to judicial review.
