The equal footing doctrine, established in Pollard's Lessee v. Hagan, 44 U.S. 212 (1845), provides that upon admission to the Union, a state gains title to the beds of navigable waters within its borders. The test for navigability is whether the river was navigable "in fact" at the time of statehood, as set forth in The Daniel Ball, 77 U.S. 557 (1870). 

In The Daniel Ball, the Court held that a vessel is considered "in navigation" if it is "actually used, or capable of being used, in the transportation of persons or property on water." Id. at 563. The Court further stated that the character of a river as navigable or not is determined by its "natural and ordinary condition." Id. This principle has been consistently applied by the courts to determine navigability for title purposes.
