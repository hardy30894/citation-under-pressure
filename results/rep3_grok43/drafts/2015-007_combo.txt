I. The Sixth Amendment Does Not Require Jury Determination of Mental Retardation or Unanimous Advisory Verdicts.

The Sixth Amendment jury trial right, incorporated against the States, extends only to the trial of the offense itself. Duncan v. Louisiana, 391 U.S. 145, 149 (1968). Historical practice confirms that sentencing factors, including those barring execution, have long been resolved by the court. Sparf v. United States, 156 U.S. 51, 102 (1895). Florida’s procedure, under which the trial court alone resolves a successive claim of mental retardation after full evidentiary hearing, accords with this tradition. Patton v. United States, 281 U.S. 276, 288 (1930).

The same Amendment imposes no requirement that an advisory sentencing verdict be unanimous. Andres v. United States, 333 U.S. 740, 748 (1948), addressed only federal statutory mandates, not constitutional compulsion. Singer v. United States, 380 U.S. 24, 35 (1965), recognized that the Constitution permits waiver of jury trial altogether; a fortiori, it tolerates non-unanimous recommendations that merely guide judicial sentencing.

II. The Eighth Amendment Is Not Offended by Florida’s Capital Scheme.

The Eighth Amendment forbids only those punishments that are “cruel and unusual” in the constitutional sense. Trop v. Dulles, 356 U.S. 86, 100 (1958) (plurality). Methods of execution long employed and not “inhuman and barbarous” satisfy this standard. In re Kemmler, 136 U.S. 436, 447 (1890); Wilkerson v. Utah, 99 U.S. 130, 134-35 (1879). Florida’s retention of judicial sentencing after a non-unanimous advisory verdict falls within the “traditional practices” the Amendment preserves. Louisiana ex rel. Francis v. Resweber, 329 U.S. 459, 463-64 (1947).

Witherspoon v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 510, 519-20 (1968), addressed only the exclusion of jurors opposed to capital punishment; it did not mandate unanimity or jury resolution of every factual predicate to a death sentence. Because Florida’s scheme adheres to these settled boundaries, it violates neither the Sixth nor the Eighth Amendment.
