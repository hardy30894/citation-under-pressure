Ford is subject to specific personal jurisdiction in Montana because this suit arises out of and relates to Ford’s systematic contacts with the forum. The Due Process Clause permits specific jurisdiction when “the litigation results from alleged injuries that arise out of or relate to” the defendant’s forum conduct. Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz, 471 U.S. 462, 472 (1985) (internal quotation marks omitted). The Court has repeatedly rejected the notion that a plaintiff must prove a strict causal link between the defendant’s forum contacts and the injury. In Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court, the Court explained that specific jurisdiction requires “an affiliation between the forum and the underlying controversy,” but it did not require that the forum contact be the immediate cause of the plaintiff’s injury. 582 U.S. 255, 262 (2017). And in Walden v. Fiore, the Court emphasized that jurisdiction turns on the defendant’s “suit-related conduct” that creates a substantial connection with the forum. 571 U.S. 277, 284 (2014).

That standard is satisfied here. Ford extensively cultivated the Montana market for the very vehicles and parts at issue, including the Explorer model involved in the accident. It advertised, sold, and serviced Ford Explorers in Montana, maintained a network of dealers there, and profited from a continuous flow of replacement parts, maintenance, and consumer reliance in the State. Under World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson, a manufacturer that “purposefully avails itself of the privilege of conducting activities within the forum State” may be sued there when its forum activities make litigation foreseeable. 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980). Ford did exactly that. It did not merely place a product in the stream of commerce and leave the forum entirely to chance; it deliberately served the Montana market for the same product line and the same replacement and repair ecosystem implicated by this case.

Ford’s contrary argument—that jurisdiction fails because the particular Explorer at issue was first sold in Washington and later resold in Montana—would impose a causation requirement this Court has never adopted. Specific jurisdiction does not turn on whether the defendant’s Montana contacts “caused” the precise vehicle to arrive in Montana. In Keeton v. Hustler Magazine, Inc., the Court held that a defendant may be sued in a forum even when the plaintiff had no forum-based connection beyond the defendant’s in-forum conduct and the claim’s relation to that conduct. 465 U.S. 770, 779–80 (1984). Likewise, in Calder v. Jones, the Court sustained jurisdiction because the defendant’s intentional conduct was “expressly aimed” at the forum and the effects were felt there. 465 U.S. 783, 789–90 (1984). Here, Ford’s forum conduct was not incidental; it was directed at Montana consumers and the same product family that injured Montana residents on Montana roads.

Nor is this a case where Montana’s exercise of jurisdiction offends “traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.” International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310, 316 (1945). Ford is a global manufacturer that purposefully exploits the Montana market. Litigating in Montana is foreseeable and reasonable given Ford’s sustained commercial presence there. Burger King, 471 U.S. at 476–77. Montana, for its part, has a strong sovereign interest in providing a forum for its residents injured by allegedly defective products on its highways. McGee v. International Life Insurance Co., 355 U.S. 220, 223 (1957).

This Court’s recent decision in Ford Motor Co. v. Eighth Judicial District Court, 592 U.S. 351 (2021), resolves the issue. The Court held that Ford was subject to specific jurisdiction in Montana and Minnesota because the plaintiffs’ claims “related to” Ford’s extensive forum contacts even though the particular vehicles were not designed, manufactured, or first sold there. Id. at 351. The Court explained that “arise out of or relate to” is disjunctive and that the “relate to” prong “contemplates that some relationships will support jurisdiction without a strict causal showing.” Id. at 356–57. That is this case. Ford systematically served the Montana market for the very models and parts implicated by the suit, and due process permits Montana to hear the claims.

Accordingly, the Montana courts properly exercised specific personal jurisdiction over Ford.
