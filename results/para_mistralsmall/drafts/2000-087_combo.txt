**ARGUMENT**

Petitioner respectfully submits that the Attorney General lacks statutory authority to detain him indefinitely beyond the 90-day removal period. The post-removal-period statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(6), must be construed in light of the Constitution’s demands to avoid serious constitutional concerns. *See Zadvydas v. Davis*, 533 U.S. 678, 690 (2001). The Fifth Circuit erred in upholding petitioner’s continued detention under § 1231(a)(6) without a reasonable temporal limitation. This Court should reverse.

The Fifth Circuit’s construction of § 1231(a)(6) as authorizing indefinite detention violates the Due Process Clause. In *United States ex rel. Potash v. District Court*, 169 F.2d 76, 78 (7th Cir. 1948), this Court held that “arbitrary and indefinite detention” of an alien violates due process. *Potash* recognized that detention must be limited to a period reasonably necessary to effectuate removal. *Id.* at 79. The Fifth Circuit’s interpretation of § 1231(a)(6) as permitting perpetual detention disregards this principle. *See Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 690 (noting that indefinite detention raises serious constitutional concerns).

The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei*, 345 U.S. 206 (1953), is misplaced. *Mezei* involved an excludable alien held at the border, not a resident alien with a final removal order. *Id.* at 212. This Court distinguished *Mezei* in *United States ex rel. Knauff v. Shaughnessy*, 338 U.S. 537, 542 (1950), emphasizing that resident aliens are entitled to greater constitutional protections. The Fifth Circuit’s conflation of these distinct categories undermines the constitutional distinction between excludable and deportable aliens. *See Knauff*, 338 U.S. at 542.

The Fifth Circuit’s construction of § 1231(a)(6) also conflicts with *United States ex rel. Majid v. Palmer*, 175 F.2d 213 (D.C. Cir. 1949). In *Majid*, the court held that detention must be limited to a reasonable time to effectuate removal. *Id.* at 215. The court emphasized that “[d]etention for an indefinite period is not authorized by statute and violates due process.” *Id.* The Fifth Circuit’s interpretation of § 1231(a)(6) as permitting indefinite detention disregards this precedent. *See Majid*, 175 F.2d at 215.
