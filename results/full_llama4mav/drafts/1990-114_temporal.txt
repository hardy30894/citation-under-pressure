The Missouri Constitution's mandatory retirement requirement for state court judges at the age of seventy does not violate the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA) or the Fourteenth Amendment's Equal Protection Clause.

First, the ADEA does not apply to Missouri's appointed state court judges because they are "appointee[s] on the policymaking level." 29 U.S.C. Sec. 630(f). As the court below recognized, judges exercise significant policymaking authority through their decisions, which create law and establish general standards or specific rules of decision. See, e.g., Marbury v. Madison, 5 U.S. 137, 177 (1803) (judicial power includes the authority to interpret the law and declare what the law is). The policymaking exception is not limited to the executive and legislative branches, and it is reasonable to interpret it to include judges who make important policy decisions.

Moreover, the legislative history of the ADEA exemptions suggests that Congress intended to exclude high-level appointees, including those in the judiciary, from the Act's coverage. See, e.g., United States v. Harriss, 347 U.S. 612, 620 (1954) (legislative history can provide insight into congressional intent). The fact that Missouri's judges are appointed and not elected does not exempt them from the policymaking level exception.

The mandatory retirement requirement also does not violate the Equal Protection Clause. The Clause requires only that the state's action be rationally related to a legitimate government interest. See, e.g., Williamson v. Lee Optical Co., 348 U.S. 483, 488 (1955) (state action is constitutional if it is rationally related to a legitimate government interest).
