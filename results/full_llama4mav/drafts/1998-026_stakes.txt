The District Court's failure to advise Manuel Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32, does not provide a basis for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation and determined that the error was harmless because Peguero knew of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing.

The harmless-error doctrine, as established in Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), requires that a court disregard errors that do not affect the substantial rights of the parties. In Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993), the Court held that the harmless-error standard applies to habeas proceedings. The Court of Appeals properly applied this standard to Peguero's claim, finding that the District Court's failure to advise him of his right to appeal was harmless because he was aware of that right.

The purpose of Rule 32 is to ensure that defendants are informed of their right to appeal. See United States v. Deans, 436 F.2d 596, 599 (3d Cir. 1971). When a defendant is already aware of this right, as Peguero was, the rule's purpose is served, and a technical violation of the rule does not warrant relief. The Court of Appeals' decision is consistent with this principle, as it held that the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was harmless because he knew of his right.

Peguero's argument that any violation of Rule 32 requires vacatur of his sentence is unpersuasive. The rule itself does not mandate such a result, and the case law supports a more nuanced approach. In United States v. Benthien, 434 F.2d 1031 (1st Cir. 1970), the court held that a defendant's knowledge of his right to appeal rendered the District Court's failure to advise him of that right harmless. Similarly, in this case, Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal renders the District Court's error harmless.

In sum, the Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation and determined that the error was harmless. Peguero's claim for habeas relief should be denied because he was aware of his right to appeal and elected not to exercise it. The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
