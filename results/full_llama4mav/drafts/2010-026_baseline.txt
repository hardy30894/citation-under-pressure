The Ninth Circuit erred in exempting documents relating to the effects of explosions from disclosure under the FOIA because they are "predominantly" for internal use and present a risk of circumventing agency regulation. The court's adoption of the "predominantly internal" standard for Exemption 2 is unfounded and contravenes the statutory text and Supreme Court precedent.

Exemption 2 shields matters "related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency." 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Supreme Court has interpreted this exemption narrowly, emphasizing that it applies to materials that are not of "genuine and significant public interest" and pose a risk of circumvention of agency regulation. Dep't of the Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S. 352, 369 (1976). The Ninth Circuit's prior caselaw, including Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), limited Exemption 2 to "law enforcement materials" that risk circumvention of agency regulation. 

The Ninth Circuit's adoption of the D.C. Circuit's "predominantly internal" standard in Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 670 F.2d 1051 (D.C. Cir. 1981), is not supported by the statutory text or Supreme Court precedent. The phrase "law enforcement" is not found in Exemption 2, and the Supreme Court has not endorsed a broader interpretation. Rose, 425 U.S. at 369. The Ninth Circuit's existing caselaw is consistent with a narrower interpretation of Exemption 2, and the "predominantly internal" standard is not necessary to achieve the purposes of the exemption.

Moreover, the ESQD arc maps at issue are not "related solely" to internal personnel rules and practices. They are used for planning and designing ammunition storage facilities, which has significant implications for public safety. The Navy's own manual, the OP-5 manual, indicates that the ESQD calculations are used to minimize the risk of chain reactions or "sympathetic detonations." The public has a significant interest in understanding the potential risks associated with the storage of explosives at NMII.

The Navy has not demonstrated that the disclosure of the ESQD arc maps would pose a significant risk of circumvention of agency regulation. The agency's affidavits, including Commander Whitbred's declaration, are conclusory and lack specific evidence to support the claim that disclosure would risk circumvention. Minier v. CIA, 88 F.3d 796, 800 (9th Cir. 1996). Furthermore, the Navy's decision not to classify the ESQD arc maps under Exemption 1 suggests that the agency does not consider the information to be sensitive or threatening to national security.

In conclusion, the Ninth Circuit erred in exempting the ESQD arc maps from disclosure under Exemption 2. The court's adoption of the "predominantly internal" standard is unfounded, and the agency has not demonstrated a significant risk of circumvention. The documents should be disclosed to the public, as they are of significant public interest and relate to the safety of the surrounding community.
