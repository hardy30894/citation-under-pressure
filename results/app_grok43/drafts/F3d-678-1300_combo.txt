The district court erred in denying judgment as a matter of law. The record contains no evidence that the Leader2Leader product offered before December 10, 2002, embodied the invention claimed in the '761 patent. An invention is not "on sale" within the meaning of the statute unless it has been reduced to a complete, operative form. Pennock v. Dialogue, 27 U.S. (2 Pet.) 1, 19 (1829). The white paper and testimony establish only that development continued into the 2002 time frame, with the underlying engine still being perfected. Where experimental work remains necessary, no barring sale occurs. City of Elizabeth v. American Nicholson Pavement Co., 97 U.S. 126, 136 (1877).

The evidence likewise fails to show public use of the claimed subject matter. Public use requires that the invention be "used by the public" in its completed form, not merely tested or demonstrated while still under development. Egbert v. Lippmann, 104 U.S. 333, 336 (1881). Leader's presentations to the Air Force and other entities described a platform that was "operational now with low user volumes" and "fully developed" only in the sense of preliminary functionality; the metadata tagging and dynamic association steps recited in claim 9 had not yet been incorporated. See Smith & Griggs Mfg. Co. v. Sprague, 123 U.S. 249, 257 (1887) (use must be of the patented improvement itself).

Nor does the record support an inference that any pre-critical-date offer placed the invention beyond the inventor's control. An offer does not trigger the bar when the inventor retains the right to modify or withdraw the embodiment. Root v. Third Ave. R.R. Co., 146 U.S. 210, 222 (1892). McKibben's testimony that the Digital Leaderboard engine was the "underlying" technology confirms that the product shown differed materially from the asserted claims.

The same deficiency defeats any finding of anticipation by prior public use. Anticipation demands identity between the claimed invention and the thing used. Hall v. Macneale, 107 U.S. 90, 96 (1883). The "Big Board" sample attached to the white paper illustrates only conventional browser display, not the dynamic metadata updating required by the claims.

Because the evidence is insufficient to sustain the verdict on any of these grounds, the judgment must be reversed. Andrews v. Hovey, 123 U.S. 267, 273 (1888); Manning v. Cape Ann Isinglass & Glue Co., 108 U.S. 462, 465 (1883); Consolidated Fruit-Jar Co. v. Wright, 94 U.S. 92, 94 (1876); Clark v. Scott, 14 F. Cas. 1126, 1128 (C.C.S.D.N.Y. 1876).
