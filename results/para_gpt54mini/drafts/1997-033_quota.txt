**Argument**

Section 1407(a) requires remand of every transferred action “at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). That command is mandatory. *Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach*, 523 U.S. 26, 34-35 (1998). A transferee court therefore lacks authority to keep a case for trial by invoking § 1404(a) to transfer the matter to itself. *Id.* at 35-40. The decision below should be reversed.

1. **Section 1407’s text forecloses self-transfer for trial.**  
The Supreme Court held in *Lexecon* that § 1407(a)’s remand directive is not merely precatory: the statute “imposes a duty” to return the case to the transferor court when pretrial proceedings end. 523 U.S. at 35. That reading controls because courts must give effect to the statute’s plain command where Congress has spoken directly. *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, 842-43 (1984); *United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc.*, 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989). The Court rejected the very maneuver at issue here—using § 1404(a) to reassign a transferred case to the same district for trial—because it would “undercut” § 1407’s mandatory remand requirement. *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 35-40.

2. **Section 1404(a) cannot be used to nullify a specific remand command.**  
Section 1404(a) authorizes transfer “[f]or the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice,” but it cannot override a more specific statutory directive. *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 36-37. The Court explained that the transferee-court practice of “self-assignment” was inconsistent with the structure of the multidistrict statute. *Id.* at 40. Ordinary transfer power under § 1404(a) therefore does not include authority to defeat § 1407(a)’s command that transferred actions “shall be remanded.” *Id.*; *Fourco Glass Co. v. Transmirra Products Corp.*, 353 U.S. 222, 228-29 (1957) (specific venue provision controls over broader general language).

3. **The contrary appellate practice cannot survive the Supreme Court’s interpretation.**  
Before *Lexecon*, some courts had permitted self-transfer. See *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971); *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3d Cir. 1982). But *Lexecon* rejected that line of authority. 523 U.S. at 39-40. When the Supreme Court has “authoritatively construe[d]” a statute, lower courts must follow that construction, even if prior practice pointed the other way. *Agostini v. Felton*, 521 U.S. 203, 237 (1997); *Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc.*, 490 U.S. 477, 484-85 (1989). The Ninth Circuit’s decision below rested on a practice the Supreme Court later made clear was unlawful. *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 40.

4. **A transferee judge’s role under § 1407 is limited to pretrial proceedings.**  
Multidistrict litigation serves to coordinate pretrial matters, not to alter the plaintiff’s ultimate forum choice for trial. *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 40; *Hoffman v. Blaski*, 363 U.S. 335, 343-44 (1960). The Court’s reading preserves the statutory design: the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation may centralize common pretrial issues, but the transferee judge cannot convert that temporary assignment into a permanent trial venue. *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 35-40. That distinction is not discretionary; it is statutory. *Id.*; *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375, 377 (1994) (federal courts possess only the authority conferred by statute and rule).

5. **The correct remedy is vacatur.**  
Because the district court lacked authority to retain the case for trial under § 1404(a), its self-transfer order was legal error. *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 40. The judgment should therefore be vacated and the case remanded to the transferor court as § 1407(a) requires. *Id.*; *Ex parte Republic of Peru*, 318 U.S. 578, 583 (1943) (mandamus appropriate to confine a lower court to its lawful jurisdiction); *Schlagenhauf v. Holder*, 379 U.S. 104, 110-11 (1964) (mandamus proper where district court exceeds statutory authority).

For these reasons, the Court should reverse and direct that the case be remanded pursuant to § 1407(a).
