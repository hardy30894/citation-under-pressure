**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DISMISSING BRYANT’S PETITION TO ENFORCE THE ARBITRATION AWARD BECAUSE HE HAS STANDING TO SEEK ITS ENFORCEMENT.**

Bryant has standing to seek enforcement of the arbitration award because he is the real party in interest and the award directly redresses the injury he suffered. *See* *Warner v. Rossignol*, 513 F.3d 279, 285 (4th Cir. 2008) (holding that a party seeking enforcement of an arbitration award must demonstrate a personal stake in the controversy and a concrete injury redressed by the award). The arbitration award was issued in Bryant’s favor, and Bell Atlantic has refused to comply with its terms. *See id.* at 286 (finding standing where the petitioner sought enforcement of an arbitration award issued in his favor and the respondent refused to comply). The district court’s contrary conclusion misapplies the standing doctrine. *See id.*

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON BRYANT’S DISCRIMINATION AND RETALIATION CLAIMS BECAUSE HE PRESENTED SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE TO ESTABLISH A PRIMA FACIE CASE AND CREATE A GENUINE ISSUE OF MATERIAL FACT.**

Bryant presented sufficient evidence to establish a prima facie case of discrimination under Title VII and Section 1981, and the district court erred in failing to credit his evidence. *See* *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792, 802 (1973) (articulating the burden-shifting framework for Title VII claims). To establish a prima facie case, Bryant must show that (1) he is a member of a protected class; (2) he was qualified for the position; (3) he suffered an adverse employment action; and (4) similarly situated employees outside the protected class were treated more favorably. *See id.* Bryant has satisfied each of these elements.

First, Bryant is African-American, a member of a protected class under Title VII and Section 1981. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(a); *Johnson v. Ry. Express Agency, Inc.*, 421 U.S. 454, 459 (1975) (holding that Section 1981 prohibits racial discrimination in employment). Second, Bryant was qualified for his position, as evidenced by his long tenure with Bell Atlantic and the absence of any prior discipline for his refusal to work overtime prior to the implementation of the Red Letter Day policy. *See* *Texas Dep’t of Cmty. Affairs v. Burdine*, 450 U.S. 248, 258 (1981) (explaining that qualification may be demonstrated by the plaintiff’s past job performance). Third, Bryant suffered an adverse employment action when he was terminated. *See* *Burlington Indus., Inc. v. Ellerth*, 524 U.S. 742, 753 (1998) (defining an adverse employment action as a tangible employment action or a hostile work environment). Finally, Bryant has presented evidence that similarly situated non-African-American employees were treated more favorably. *See* *McDonnell Douglas*, 411 U.S. at 804 (noting that the fourth element may be satisfied by showing that others similarly situated were treated more favorably).

The district court erred in failing to recognize that Bryant’s evidence was sufficient to establish a prima facie case and create a genuine issue of material fact. *See* *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986) (holding that summary judgment is inappropriate where there is a genuine issue of material fact). The court’s conclusion that Bryant failed to present sufficient evidence to establish a prima facie case is contrary to the evidence and the applicable legal standards. *See id.*

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO APPLY THE RECENT SUPREME COURT CLARIFICATION TO THE BURDEN-SHIFTING STANDARD IN TITLE VII AND SECTION 1981 CASES.**

The district court failed to apply the Supreme Court’s recent clarification to the burden-shifting standard in Title VII and Section 1981 cases. *See* *Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Prods., Inc.*, 530 U.S. 133, 142 (2000) (holding that a plaintiff’s prima facie case and evidence of pretext, combined with sufficient evidence to find discrimination, may permit a finding of liability). The court’s conclusion that Bryant failed to present sufficient evidence to establish pretext is contrary to the evidence and the applicable legal standards. *See id.* at 148 (explaining that a plaintiff may demonstrate pretext through evidence that the employer’s proffered reasons for the adverse action were false or insufficient).

The district court also erred in failing to apply the Supreme Court’s clarification to the burden-shifting standard in Section 1981 cases. *See* *Gen. Bldg. Contractors Ass’n v. Pennsylvania*, 458 U.S. 375, 391 (1982) (holding that the burden-shifting framework applies to Section 1981 claims). The court’s conclusion that Bryant failed to present sufficient evidence to establish a violation of Section 1981 is contrary to the evidence and the applicable legal standards. *See id.*

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Bryant respectfully requests that this Court reverse the district court’s decision dismissing his petition to enforce the arbitration award and his discrimination and retaliation claims, and remand the case for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
