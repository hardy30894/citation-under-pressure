The district court erred in granting summary judgment because material failures in disclosure and notice extended the rescission period to three years. A waiver of statutory rights is effective only when shown to be voluntary and intelligent. Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938). The record contains no such showing. The handwritten statement was executed at the direction of the closing agent on the day of closing, without evidence that the Mouas understood the rights surrendered or that any emergency was of their own making. See Grymes v. Sanders, 93 U.S. 55, 62 (1876) (rescission unavailable where party seeking to enforce acted with knowledge of the facts).

Notice of the right to cancel must be reasonably calculated to apprise interested parties of the pendency of the action and afford them an opportunity to present their objections. Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S. 306, 314 (1950). The two versions of the Notice, both signed on April 22, 2005, and the simultaneous execution of the confirmation clause before the three-day period expired, failed this standard. The lender cannot rely on a confirmation executed under these circumstances to extinguish the statutory period. See Hepburn v. Auld, 5 U.S. 262, 278 (1803) (instrument signed under misapprehension of its legal effect does not bind the signer).

A contract term that imposes a penalty for prepayment is unenforceable when it contravenes the protective purpose of the governing statute. Williams v. Walker-Thomas Furniture Co., 350 F.2d 445, 449 (D.C. Cir. 1965). The inclusion of such a penalty, together with the absence of the required advance HOEPA disclosure, rendered the disclosures materially deficient. Where a party has failed to make required disclosures, equity will not permit that party to enforce a shortened rescission period. Scott v. United States, 79 U.S. 423, 443 (1870).

The lender’s attempt to obtain a waiver based on an asserted financial emergency likewise fails. A waiver procured by economic pressure that leaves the borrower no reasonable alternative is not voluntary. Hume v. United States, 132 U.S. 406, 414 (1889). The pending redemption period and the lender’s own scheduling decisions created the asserted urgency; the Mouas cannot be held to have waived rights they were never given a meaningful opportunity to exercise. See The Floyd Acceptances, 74 U.S. 666, 676 (1868) (party cannot enforce an agreement obtained through its own failure to perform antecedent duties).

Because the disclosures and notice were defective, the three-year rescission period remained open. The district court’s contrary ruling must be reversed.
