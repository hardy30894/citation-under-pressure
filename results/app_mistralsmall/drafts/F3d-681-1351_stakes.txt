**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Board Erred in Concluding That Harari’s ’880 Application Is Entitled to the Benefit of the Filing Date of the ’566 Application Under 35 U.S.C. § 120**

The Board misapplied the continuity requirements of 35 U.S.C. § 120 by holding that the ’880 application is entitled to the benefit of the ’566 application’s filing date. Section 120 permits a later application to claim the benefit of an earlier filing date if the later application is a continuation, divisional, or continuation-in-part of the earlier application and discloses the same invention. *See* 35 U.S.C. § 120; *In re Zickendraht*, 319 F.2d 225, 231 (C.C.P.A. 1963). To satisfy § 120, the later application must comply with the written description requirement of 35 U.S.C. § 112(a) by disclosing the same invention as the earlier application. *See* *In re Wertheim*, 541 F.2d 257, 262 (C.C.P.A. 1976).

Here, the Board erred in finding that the ’838 and ’768 applications sufficiently incorporated the ’579 application by reference to satisfy § 120. The Board relied on the incorporation statement copied from the ’566 application, which referred to the ’579 application as being “filed on the same day” as the ’566 application. However, as this Court held in *Harari I*, 602 F.3d at 1352-53, the proper standard for evaluating incorporation by reference at the initial filing stage is whether the identity of the incorporated reference is clear to a reasonable examiner. The Board failed to apply this standard correctly on remand.

The incorporation statement in the ’566 application was ambiguous because it did not clearly identify the ’579 application by serial number or filing date. *See* J.A. 1204. The preliminary amendment in the ’880 application corrected this ambiguity by explicitly identifying the ’579 application by serial number and filing date. *Harari I*, 602 F.3d at 1353-54. However, the ’838 and ’768 applications did not include this clarifying amendment. Instead, they merely copied the ambiguous language from the ’566 application without further explanation. *See* J.A. 1204. Under *Harari I*, this ambiguity was not resolved until the filing of the ’880 application. Therefore, the ’838 and ’768 applications did not sufficiently incorporate the ’579 application by reference to satisfy the written description requirement of § 112(a).

The Board’s reliance on *In re Scheiber*, 587 F.2d 59 (C.C.P.A. 1978), is misplaced. In *Scheiber*, the court held that a later application could rely on an earlier application’s incorporation by reference if the earlier application clearly identified the incorporated material. *Id.* at 62. Here, the ’566 application’s incorporation statement was not clear, and the ’838 and ’768 applications did not remedy this ambiguity. Thus, *Scheiber* does not support the Board’s conclusion.

Because the ’838 and ’768 applications did not satisfy the written description requirement of § 112(a), the ’880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the ’566 application’s filing date under § 120. The Board’s judgment to the contrary should be reversed.

**II. The Board Erred in Holding That Harari’s ’880 Application Preceded Hollmer’s ’601 Patent Under 35 U.S.C. § 102(g)**

The Board further erred in holding that Harari’s ’880 application preceded Hollmer’s ’601 patent under 35 U.S.C. § 102(g). Section 102(g) provides that a patent claim is invalid if the invention was made by another who had not abandoned, suppressed, or concealed it before the patent applicant’s date of invention. *See* 35 U.S.C. § 102(g); *Mahurkar v. C.R. Bard, Inc.*, 79 F.3d 1572, 1577 (Fed. Cir. 1996).

Here, the Board found that Harari’s ’880 application was entitled to the benefit of the ’566 application’s filing date of April 13, 1989, and thus preceded Hollmer’s date of conception for the ’601 patent. However, as discussed above, the ’880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the ’566 application’s filing date. Without this benefit, Harari’s ’880 application is only entitled to the benefit of the ’708 application’s filing date of December 20, 1996. *See* J.A. 1204. Hollmer’s ’601 patent issued on October 27, 1998, and Hollmer’s date of conception predates the ’708 application’s filing date. Therefore, Harari’s ’880 application does not anticipate Hollmer’s ’601 patent under § 102(g).

The Board’s reliance on *In re Deckler*, 977 F.2d 1449 (Fed. Cir. 1992), is unavailing. In *Deckler*, the court held that a later application could rely on an earlier application’s filing date if the later application was a continuation-in-part of the earlier application and disclosed the same invention. *Id.* at 1452. Here, the ’880 application is not a continuation-in-part of the ’566 application, and it does not disclose the same invention as the ’566 application. Thus, *Deckler* does not support the Board’s conclusion.

Because Harari’s ’880 application does not precede Hollmer’s ’601 patent under § 102(g), the Board’s judgment should be reversed.
