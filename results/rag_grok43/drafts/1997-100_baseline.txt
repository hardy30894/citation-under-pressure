**ARGUMENT**

The Double Jeopardy Clause of the Fifth Amendment, made applicable to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment, *Benton v. Maryland*, 395 U.S. 784, 796 (1969), “protects against a second prosecution for the same offense after acquittal,” *North Carolina v. Pearce*, 395 U.S. 711, 717 (1969). That protection extends to a noncapital sentencing proceeding to determine the truth of a prior-conviction allegation when the proceeding “in all relevant respects [is] like the immediately preceding trial on the issue of guilt or innocence.” *Bullington v. Missouri*, 451 U.S. 430, 438 (1981).

California Penal Code § 1025 grants a statutory right to jury trial on prior-conviction allegations. The prosecution must prove the allegation beyond a reasonable doubt, *People v. Tenner*, 6 Cal. 4th 559, 566 (1993), the rules of evidence apply, *People v. Reed*, 13 Cal. 4th 217, 224 (1996), and the defendant enjoys the rights to remain silent and to confront adverse witnesses, *In re Yurko*, 10 Cal. 3d 857, 863 n.5 (1974). These procedures are indistinguishable from those that led this Court to apply double-jeopardy principles in *Bullington* and *Arizona v. Rumsey*, 467 U.S. 203, 209-210 (1984).

The Court of Appeal’s determination that the evidence was legally insufficient to prove the prior serious-felony allegation is therefore “the equivalent of an acquittal” for double-jeopardy purposes. *Bullington*, 451 U.S. at 445. Retrial is accordingly barred. *See Lockhart v. Nelson*, 488 U.S. 33, 40 (1988) (distinguishing insufficiency from trial error).

The suggestion in *Caspari v. Bohlen*, 510 U.S. 383, 392 (1994), that *Bullington*’s reasoning is limited to capital cases is not a holding and does not control. *Pennsylvania v. Goldhammer*, 474 U.S. 28, 30 (1985) (per curiam), likewise recognizes that noncapital sentencing ordinarily lacks “the qualities of constitutional finality that attend an acquittal,” but that principle yields when the State elects to impose trial-like procedures and burdens. Because California has done so here, the Double Jeopardy Clause precludes retrial of the prior-conviction allegation.
