**Argument**

I. The State Acquired Title to the Riverbeds Because the Missouri, Madison, and Clark Fork Rivers Were Navigable at the Time of Statehood

The State holds title to the riverbeds underlying PPL’s dams because the Missouri, Madison, and Clark Fork Rivers were navigable at the time of Montana’s admission to the Union. The "navigable for title" test, distinct from the "navigable for commerce" test, determines ownership of submerged lands. *United States v. Utah Power & Light Co.*, 243 U.S. 389, 394 (1917). Under the Equal Footing Doctrine, a state holds title to lands beneath navigable waters upon its admission to the Union. *United States v. California*, 332 U.S. 19, 25 (1947). Consequently, the inquiry is whether the rivers were navigable at the time of statehood. *United States v. Oregon*, 295 U.S. 1, 15 (1935).

The trial court properly relied on historical evidence to establish navigability at statehood. The Supreme Court has long held that the "navigable for title" test requires a determination of the condition of the waterway at the time of statehood. *United States v. Rio Grande Dam & Irrigation Co.*, 174 U.S. 690, 702 (1899). This historical inquiry is essential to the "equal footing" doctrine. *United States v. Oregon*, 295 U.S. at 15. The evidence presented at trial demonstrated that these rivers were used for commerce and transportation in 1889. *United States v. Willow River Power Co.*, 324 U.S. 499, 504 (1945).

The distinction between navigability for commerce and navigability for title is well established. *United States v. Appalachian Electric Power Co.*, 311 U.S. 377, 391 (1940). While the federal government retains a navigational servitude over navigable waters, this does not affect the state’s title to the beds of those waters. *United States v. Causby*, 328 U.S. 256, 263 (1946). The State’s claim is based on its ownership of the land, not on the regulation of commerce. *United States v. Rands*, 389 U.S. 121, 124 (1967). Therefore, the trial court correctly concluded that the rivers were navigable at statehood, granting the State title to the riverbeds.
