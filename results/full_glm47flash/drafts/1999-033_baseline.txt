**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE PROTECTS INDIVIDUALS FROM ARBITRARY STATE ACTION, EVEN IN THE ABSENCE OF A FORMAL CLASS.**

The Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment prohibits the State from invidiously discriminating against any person or class of persons. 528 U.S. 562, 565 (2000). While traditional equal protection analysis requires a plaintiff to identify a similarly situated class, the Supreme Court has recognized an exception for "class of one" claims where the plaintiff alleges that the state action was motivated by a "spiteful effort to 'get' him for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective." 424 U.S. 500, 504 (1976). This exception ensures that the Constitution protects individuals from arbitrary government action, even when the government has not singled out a specific group for disparate treatment.

**II. THE VILLAGE’S DEMAND FOR AN 18-FOOT LARGER EASEMENT CONSTITUTES THE "SPITEFUL EFFORT" REQUIRED TO SUSTAIN A CLASS OF ONE CLAIM.**

The District Court erred in dismissing Ms. Olech’s complaint for failure to state a cognizable claim. Ms. Olech alleges that the Village of Willowbrook conditioned the connection of her property to the municipal water supply on her granting of a 33-foot easement, while other property owners seeking access to the water supply were only required to grant a 15-foot easement. This differential treatment, without a legitimate reason, satisfies the "class of one" standard established by the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court has affirmed that a "class of one" claim is cognizable where a plaintiff alleges that the state action was motivated by a "spiteful effort to 'get' him for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective." 424 U.S. at 504. Here, Ms. Olech alleges that the Village’s demand for an additional 18-foot easement was arbitrary and unrelated to any legitimate state objective, such as public safety or engineering necessity. By treating Ms. Olech differently from other property owners without a rational basis, the Village’s conduct falls squarely within the scope of the "class of one" exception to the traditional equal protection requirement.

Accordingly, the District Court’s dismissal of Ms. Olech’s claim was improper. She has adequately alleged a violation of the Equal Protection Clause by asserting that the Village’s action was motivated by a "spiteful effort" to discriminate against her.
