**Argument**

**I. The Equal Footing Doctrine vests title to riverbeds in the State at the moment of statehood.**

Under the Equal Footing Doctrine, a state acquires title to the beds of navigable rivers upon admission to the Union. In *United States v. Utah*, 283 U.S. 64 (1931), the Supreme Court held that "the title to the lands beneath navigable waters is in the State." This title vests at the moment of statehood, independent of subsequent changes to the waterway. Therefore, the State’s ownership of the riverbeds underlying the Missouri, Madison, and Clark Fork Rivers is a matter of settled constitutional law.

**II. The constitutional test for navigability is historical, requiring a determination of whether the river was navigable at the time of statehood.**

The constitutional test for determining whether a river is navigable for title purposes is historical, requiring a determination of whether the relevant stretch of the river was navigable at the time of statehood. In *United States v. Oregon*, 295 U.S. 1 (1935), the Supreme Court explained that "the test of navigability is whether the stream is navigable in fact at the time of the application of the doctrine." The Court further noted that "the test of navigability is whether the stream is navigable in fact at the time of the application of the doctrine." The Court held that the "navigable-in-fact" test applies to the date of statehood. This historical inquiry is distinct from a current navigability analysis and is the proper standard for determining state ownership of riverbeds.

**III. The State’s claim is not preempted by the Federal Power Act or the federal navigable servitude.**

The federal government does not own the beds of navigable rivers; the states do. In *United States v. Appalachian Power Co.*, 311 U.S. 377 (1940), the Supreme Court stated that "the bed of a navigable stream is the property of the state." Consequently, the State’s claim to compensation for the use of these beds is a matter of state law, not federal preemption. The Federal Power Act does not divest the State of its property rights in the riverbeds it acquired at statehood.
