The government's installation of excess fiber-optic capacity and its subsequent lease of unused fibers to third parties effects a permanent physical occupation of Tuthill's land outside the 1953 easement, constituting a per se taking. Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419, 426 (1982) ("[A] permanent physical occupation authorized by government is a taking without regard to the public interests that it may serve.").

The easement conveyed only "a perpetual easement and right to enter and erect, operate, maintain, repair, rebuild, and patrol one or more electrical power transmission lines and appurtenant signal lines, poles, towers, wires, cables, and appliances necessary in connection therewith." The undisputed record establishes that the Bonneville Power Administration installed thirty-six-fiber cables when only a fraction were required for its transmission operations, then leased the remainder to a telecommunications company for commercial profit. This exceeds the easement's express limitation to signal lines "necessary in connection" with power transmission.

A physical invasion that exceeds the scope of an existing easement is compensable under the Fifth Amendment. See United States v. Causby, 328 U.S. 256, 261-62 (1946) (holding that government overflights "constitute an intrusion" beyond any implied permission and therefore effect a taking). The Court of Federal Claims erred in concluding that "the scope of the easement is not relevant in this court because it cannot give rise to a physical taking." When the government occupies property without authorization, the occupation itself supplies the requisite property interest taken. See Preseault v. Interstate Commerce Comm'n, 494 U.S. 1, 13 (1990) (recognizing that a taking occurs when government action "denies the owner of the property the right to exclude others").

Because the occupation is permanent and physical, it is per se compensable without regard to the government's asserted future needs or the absence of additional surface disturbance. Loretto, 458 U.S. at 432. Tuthill is therefore entitled to just compensation for the taking.
