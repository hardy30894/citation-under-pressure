**ARGUMENT**

This Court should affirm. The Due Process Clause permits specific jurisdiction when a defendant has purposefully established forum contacts and the suit “arise[s] out of or relate[s]” to those contacts. *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 316-17 (1945); *Helicopteros Nacionales de Colombia, S. A. v. Hall*, 466 U.S. 408, 414 n.8 (1984). Ford’s position—that the forum contact must be the but-for cause of the plaintiff’s injury—adds a causation requirement this Court has never imposed. The Constitution asks whether the defendant’s forum conduct makes it reasonable to require defense of the suit there, not whether the precise vehicle at issue first entered the forum because of the defendant’s own in-state sale.

Specific jurisdiction is grounded in “the relationship among the defendant, the forum, and the litigation.” *Shaffer v. Heitner*, 433 U.S. 186, 204 (1977). A defendant may be haled into court where it has “purposefully availed itself of the privilege of conducting activities within the forum State,” *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, 471 U.S. 462, 475 (1985), so long as the claims “arise out of or relate to” those activities, *Helicopteros*, 466 U.S. at 414 n.8. That standard is intentionally disjunctive. The Court has repeatedly described the inquiry in terms of connection, not strict causation. See *Ford Motor Co. v. Eighth Judicial Dist. Court*, 432 P.3d 1014, 1022-23 (Mont. 2018), rev’d, 592 U.S. ___ (2021). Ford’s forum-wide marketing, sales, and servicing of the very models at issue created the necessary relationship with Montana and Minnesota. See *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297-98 (1980) (purposeful availment turns on defendant’s conduct and forum connection).

This Court’s precedents confirm that due process does not demand a causal link between the forum contact and the precise injury. In *Keeton v. Hustler Magazine, Inc.*, 465 U.S. 770, 774-75 (1984), the Court upheld jurisdiction based on extensive forum sales even though the plaintiff was not a resident and the plaintiff’s claims were not caused by the forum sales themselves. In *Calder v. Jones*, 465 U.S. 783, 788-90 (1984), jurisdiction rested on forum effects and the defendant’s intentional exploitation of the forum, not on an in-state causative act. And in *Burnham v. Superior Court*, 495 U.S. 604, 619 (1990), the Court reaffirmed that jurisdictional rules turn on established constitutional limits, not mechanical causation formulas.

Ford relies on language from *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255, 264-65 (2017), but that case does not require a strict causal nexus either. *Bristol-Myers* held only that there must be “an affiliation between the forum and the underlying controversy,” and it expressly distinguished cases where the defendant engaged in extensive forum conduct tied to the same product and same type of injury. Id. at 262-63. Ford’s in-state conduct here is far more substantial than the attenuated forum contact rejected in *Bristol-Myers*. Ford advertised, sold, and serviced the same vehicle models in Montana and Minnesota, and plaintiffs were injured in those states by alleged defects in those same products.

The fairness factors also support jurisdiction. *Asahi Metal Industry Co. v. Superior Court*, 480 U.S. 102, 113-16 (1987), and *Burger King*, 471 U.S. at 476-78, instruct courts to consider the burden on the defendant, the forum state’s interest, the plaintiff’s interest in relief, and the interstate system’s interest in efficient resolution. Montana and Minnesota have a strong sovereign interest in adjudicating injuries suffered by their residents on their roads. Ford, a global manufacturer already doing substantial business in both states, faces no constitutionally significant burden in defending there. See *McGee v. International Life Insurance Co.*, 355 U.S. 220, 223 (1957) (recognizing a state’s manifest interest in providing effective redress to its residents).

Ford’s contrary rule would also be unworkable. Under *International Shoe* and *Burger King*, jurisdiction depends on practical fairness and the defendant’s deliberate forum affiliation, not on reassigning injury causation to the jurisdictional stage. The Montana Supreme Court correctly recognized that Ford’s extensive forum activities concerning the same vehicles and alleged defects gave rise to claims that “relate to” those activities. That conclusion accords with *World-Wide Volkswagen*, *Keeton*, *Calder*, *Burger King*, *Asahi*, *Shaffer*, *Helicopteros*, and *Bristol-Myers*.

The judgment should be affirmed.
