ARGUMENT

The district court abused its discretion by granting a preliminary injunction that compels appellants to depart from their neutral, first-come, first-served parking policy. A preliminary injunction requires a showing of irreparable harm and either a likelihood of success on the merits or sufficiently serious questions going to the merits with a balance of hardships tipping decidedly in the movant’s favor. Citibank, N.A. v. Citytrust, 756 F.2d 273, 275 (2d Cir. 1985); Jackson Dairy, Inc. v. H.P. Hood & Sons, Inc., 596 F.2d 70, 72 (2d Cir. 1979).

Shapiro cannot demonstrate likelihood of success under the Fair Housing Amendments Act. The statute makes it unlawful to refuse “reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services, when such accommodations may be necessary to afford [a handicapped] person equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling.” 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B). The Act does not require landlords to alter neutral allocation rules to confer an immediate preference. Huntington Branch, NAACP v. Town of Huntington, 844 F.2d 926, 936 (2d Cir. 1988). Appellants’ policy treats all residents alike; it neither singles out handicapped persons nor denies them access once their turn arrives.

Reasonable-accommodation analysis under analogous civil-rights statutes confirms that fundamental alterations are not required. Southeastern Community College v. Davis, 442 U.S. 397, 410 (1979); Alexander v. Choate, 469 U.S. 287, 300 (1985). Assigning Shapiro a space ahead of others on the list would displace the orderly waiting system that governs 400 units and only 202 spaces. Such displacement exceeds the statutory command. United States v. California Mobile Home Park Mgmt. Co., 29 F.3d 1413, 1418 (9th Cir. 1994).

The district court’s order also rests on an erroneous view of irreparable harm. Delay in obtaining a parking space, while inconvenient, does not constitute the “certain and great” injury needed for extraordinary relief. Sampson v. Murray, 415 U.S. 61, 90 (1974). Shapiro’s evidence of occasional incontinence does not establish that harm will occur before her name reaches the top of the list under ordinary operation of the policy.

Finally, the balance of equities favors appellants. The cooperative must manage scarce parking for hundreds of residents; granting one resident an immediate exception undermines the very fairness the statute seeks to preserve. Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Ins. Co., 409 U.S. 205, 211 (1972); City of Edmonds v. Oxford House, Inc., 514 U.S. 725, 732 (1995). Because Shapiro failed to satisfy the prerequisites for preliminary injunctive relief, the order should be reversed.
