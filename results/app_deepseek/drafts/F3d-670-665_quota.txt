## ARGUMENT

### I. This Court’s Precedent Establishes That a Credible Claim of Actual Innocence Alone Warrants Equitable Tolling of AEDPA’s Statute of Limitations

In *Souter v. Jones*, 395 F.3d 577, 590 (6th Cir. 2005), this Court held that “actual innocence, if proven, serves as a gateway through which a petitioner may pass” to overcome AEDPA’s time bar, and that “a credible claim of actual innocence is itself a basis for equitable tolling.” The Court explicitly rejected any requirement that a petitioner also demonstrate reasonable diligence, reasoning that “the miscarriage-of-justice exception is a separate and distinct basis for relief” from traditional equitable tolling. *Id.* at 599-600. This holding has been consistently reaffirmed. *See, e.g., Whalen v. Randle*, 400 F. App’x 51, 55 (6th Cir. 2010) (per curiam) (“[A] credible claim of actual innocence may equitably toll the statute of limitations without a showing of diligence.”); *Patterson v. Lafler*, 329 F. App’x 613, 616 (6th Cir. 2009) (same).

The district court erred by grafting a diligence requirement onto this Court’s actual-innocence tolling doctrine. The court relied on *Pace v. DiGuglielmo*, 544 U.S. 408 (2005), but *Pace* addressed traditional equitable tolling—where a petitioner must show “(1) that he has been pursuing his rights diligently, and (2) that some extraordinary circumstance stood in his way.” *Id.* at 418. *Pace* did not involve a claim of actual innocence. This Court has since clarified that the *Pace* diligence requirement does not apply to actual-innocence tolling. *See, e.g., Allen v. Yukins*, 366 F.3d 396, 403 (6th Cir. 2004) (holding that “a petitioner who makes a credible showing of actual innocence is entitled to equitable tolling regardless of his diligence”). The Supreme Court’s decision in *Holland v. Florida*, 560 U.S. 631 (2010), which reaffirmed the *Pace* standard for traditional tolling, likewise did not disturb this Court’s actual-innocence exception.

### II. The Supreme Court Has Recognized That Actual Innocence Is a Freestanding Equitable Exception

The Supreme Court has long held that a credible claim of actual innocence allows a federal court to review otherwise procedurally barred habeas claims. *Schlup v. Delo*, 513 U.S. 298, 327 (1995) (actual innocence “serves as a gateway” to overcome procedural default). In *House v. Bell*, 547 U.S. 518, 537 (2006), the Court reiterated that the Schlup standard “ensures that a federal court will not hear a habeas claim unless the petitioner demonstrates that ‘a constitutional violation has probably resulted in the conviction of one who is actually innocent.’” The Court has never required a separate showing of diligence for this gateway. Indeed, in *Murray v. Carrier*, 477 U.S. 478, 496 (1986), the Court explained that the miscarriage-of-justice exception exists precisely to prevent “a fundamental miscarriage of justice” when a petitioner is actually innocent, regardless of procedural failures.

The district court’s imposition of a diligence requirement would effectively nullify this exception. As this Court recognized in *Souter*, 395 F.3d at 590, requiring diligence “would be inconsistent with the very purpose of the actual innocence exception—to prevent the incarceration of innocent persons.” The Supreme Court’s decision in *McQuiggin v. Perkins*, 569 U.S. 383 (2013)—decided after the district court’s ruling—later confirmed that actual innocence tolls AEDPA’s statute of limitations without a diligence requirement. *Id.* at 392-93 (holding that “actual innocence, if proved, serves as a gateway through which a petitioner may pass” and that “the miscarriage of justice exception” applies to AEDPA’s time bar). Although *McQuiggin* postdates the district court’s decision, it reinforces the correctness of this Court’s precedent.

### III. Perkins Has Presented a Credible Claim of Actual Innocence

Perkins submitted three affidavits—from Ronda Hudson, Demond Louis, and Linda Fleming—that directly implicate Damarr Jones, the prosecution’s sole eyewitness, as Henderson’s actual killer. This evidence is “new” because it was not presented at trial. *See Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 327 (new evidence includes affidavits that were unavailable at trial). The affidavits are not merely cumulative; they point to a different perpetrator and contradict Jones’s trial testimony. *See House*, 547 U.S. at 537 (new evidence that “implicates a different suspect” supports actual innocence). The district court erred in dismissing this evidence as “substantially available” at trial, because Perkins could not have discovered the affidavits earlier with due diligence—the affiants came forward only after trial. *See Souter*, 395 F.3d at 590 (actual innocence claim requires “new reliable evidence” that was not presented at trial). Perkins’s evidence satisfies the Schlup standard: it is “sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict.” *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 317.

### Conclusion

This Court should reverse the district court’s denial of equitable tolling and remand for consideration of Perkins’s habeas claims on the merits.
