The discharge exception in 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A) reaches only “any debt ... for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by ... actual fraud.” This language confines nondischargeability to the sum actually procured by the fraud. Neal v. Clark, 95 U.S. 704, 709 (1877) (“fraud” denotes “positive fraud, or fraud in fact, involving moral turpitude or intentional wrong”).

Treble damages imposed by the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act were not obtained by fraud; they are a statutory penalty assessed after the fact. Crawford v. Burke, 195 U.S. 176, 193 (1904) (exception applies solely to debts “created by” the fraud). The same limitation appears in Friend v. Talcott, 228 U.S. 27, 37 (1913) (debts “not created by” fraud remain dischargeable).

Strict construction of the exception is required. Tinker v. Colwell, 193 U.S. 473, 488 (1904) (“The provisions of the bankruptcy act ... should be construed strictly”). Gleason v. Thaw, 236 U.S. 558, 562 (1915) likewise demands narrow reading of the statutory terms.

The Code’s dominant purpose is to afford the honest debtor a fresh start. Local Loan Co. v. Hunt, 292 U.S. 234, 244 (1934) (“One of the primary purposes of the bankruptcy act is to relieve the honest debtor from the weight of oppressive indebtedness”). Punitive or statutory additions fall outside that exception. McIntyre v. Kavanaugh, 242 U.S. 138, 141 (1916) (only liabilities arising directly from the fraudulent act are preserved).

Technical or constructive wrongdoing does not trigger the bar. Davis v. Aetna Acceptance Co., 293 U.S. 328, 332 (1934) (“A wilful and malicious injury does not follow as of course from every act of conversion”). Equitable principles likewise counsel against expanding the exception beyond its text. Pepper v. Litton, 308 U.S. 295, 306 (1939) (“Courts of bankruptcy are essentially courts of equity”).

Where one partner’s fraud is at issue, liability remains several. Strang v. Bradner, 114 U.S. 555, 559 (1885) (“The fraud of one partner ... will not prevent the discharge of the other”). The treble award therefore stands on a different footing from the compensatory sum and is dischargeable.
