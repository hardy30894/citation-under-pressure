ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act provides that “[a]ny claimant” may obtain review in the courts of appeals of “any final decision of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A Board order refusing to reopen a prior claim satisfies that statutory text. Rochester Telephone Corp. v. United States, 307 U.S. 125, 143 (1939) (“The order of the Commission is final.”). Such an order “determine[s] rights” and “impose[s] obligations,” Columbia Broadcasting System, Inc. v. United States, 316 U.S. 407, 417 (1942), and therefore constitutes agency action ripe for judicial scrutiny.

The statute does not distinguish between an initial denial and a later refusal to reopen. Where Congress has not carved out an exception, “the judicial function is exhausted when the bounds of the authority are not transgressed.” Myers v. Bethlehem Shipbuilding Corp., 303 U.S. 41, 50 (1938). The Board’s refusal to reopen therefore falls within the review provision.

This conclusion accords with settled principles governing review of administrative orders. An agency determination is reviewable when it “has a final and definitive impact upon the petitioner.” Leedom v. Kyne, 358 U.S. 184, 188 (1958). The hearing officer’s conclusion that the 2006 application lay beyond the regulatory reopening period produced precisely that impact. See Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136, 149 (1967) (“The regulations were made effective immediately.”).

The same result follows from the practical test of finality. An order is final when “nothing remain[s] to be done but the enforcement of the award.” Federal Power Commission v. Metropolitan Edison Co., 304 U.S. 375, 381 (1938). Once the Board declined to reopen, no further administrative steps were available. The decision therefore became “a definitive ruling” subject to review. Chicago & Southern Air Lines, Inc. v. Waterman S.S. Corp., 333 U.S. 103, 112 (1948).

The Second Circuit reached the identical conclusion under the same statute. Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Board, 370 F.2d 253, 254 (2d Cir. 1966) (“We hold that we have jurisdiction to review the Board’s refusal to reopen.”). That court recognized that the statutory language contains no limitation excluding reopening decisions from the class of reviewable orders.

Because the Board’s refusal to reopen is a final decision within the meaning of § 355(f), the petition for review should be entertained.
