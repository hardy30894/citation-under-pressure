# Citation Under Pressure: What Deployment Constraints Do to Legal Citation Integrity, and What Deterministic Verification Can Repair

*Draft v0.1, 2026-09-01. Every number in this draft is copied from a file in `results/`; the source file is named in square brackets at first use of each figure so the copy-never-retype check can run against it. Anonymized for submission.*

## Abstract

Language models fabricate legal citations, and the failures that reach courtrooms have so far been studied mainly after the fact, through detection benchmarks and sanctions dockets. We study the production side. Four models spanning a capability ladder, from a 30B open-weight model to two frontier systems, each drafted merits-brief argument sections for 48 leakage-screened U.S. Supreme Court matters under five deployment conditions: an unpressured baseline, a citation quota, a restriction to pre-1970 authority, a Rule 11 sanctions warning, and all three combined. Every citation in the resulting 960 drafts was adjudicated deterministically, with no LLM judge anywhere in the primary measurements: existence against an 18-million-row reporter database, quotation fidelity against a verbatim checker validated on seeded faults, and pinpoint pages against true star pagination recovered from freely available volume archives. Pressure dismantles citation integrity from the bottom of the ladder up. The 30B model loses fifteen points of citation existence under combined pressure (odds ratio 0.30, p < 0.0001, citation-level GEE clustered by matter), quotation fidelity degrades significantly at every tier except the highest, and in 960 pressured drafts no model abstained even once. At the top of the ladder the sign reverses: under the full stack of constraints including the sanctions warning, the strongest model's quotation fidelity significantly improves. A second experiment placed the verifier inside the drafting loop. Repair is capability-gated, ranging from no measurable improvement at 30B to near-perfect quotation fidelity at the top, and a scrambled-feedback control shows that false-positive verifier flags actively corrupt drafts at every tier, most severely for the models best at following feedback. What survives at frontier scale is not invented law: 55 percent of the strongest model's inaccurate quotations are paraphrases of the correct case wearing quotation marks, and over a hundred are verbatim passages of real opinions attributed to the wrong authority. We release the instruments, including the first page-level pinpoint-citation verifier built entirely on public data.

## 1. Introduction

The public record of language models in legal practice is a record of citations that did not exist. Courts have sanctioned filings built on invented authority, and a growing measurement literature has responded with detection benchmarks: given a brief containing errors, how many can a model or agent find (Liu et al., 2026, arXiv:2606.21155; Chen et al., 2026, arXiv:2605.10186). Detection is the right question for a court clerk. It is the wrong question for anyone deciding whether and how to deploy a drafting model, because it treats fabrication as a fixed property of text rather than a behavior with causes.

This paper asks the production-side questions. First, what conditions cause citation failure? Practitioners do not prompt models in the abstract; they impose quotas, restrict the allowable authority, and raise the stakes. A controlled study of scholarly citations found that such constraints, temporal restrictions above all, collapse citation existence even in capable models (Zhao et al., 2026, arXiv:2603.07287). No comparable study existed for law, the domain where the failures carry professional sanctions. Second, when a verifier that can check citations deterministically is placed inside the drafting loop, does the model repair its drafts honestly, fail to repair them, or learn to satisfy the verifier while degrading something the verifier cannot see?

Answering either question requires an instrument the field has said does not exist in public. The best published detection agents catch 18.2 percent of incorrect pinpoint citations, and their authors attribute the failure to page-level information living only behind commercial databases (Liu et al., 2026). We built the missing instrument from public sources: citation existence and name pairing against a local database derived from CourtListener bulk data, verbatim quotation checking validated on seeded faults at 100 of 100 planted checks, and page-level pinpoint verification against star pagination recovered from the Caselaw Access Project's free volume archives. The instrument, not any language model, produces every primary number in this paper.

Three findings organize the results. Pressure breaks citation integrity from the bottom of the capability ladder up, channel by channel, and it does so silently: across 960 pressured drafts, no model declined the task even once. Verification inside the loop repairs only the models that least need it, and imprecise verification is worse than none. And the failure that survives at frontier scale is misattribution rather than invention: real legal language bound to the wrong authority, and paraphrase presented as quotation.

## 2. Related work

**Legal citation reliability.** Liu et al. (2026, arXiv:2606.21155) construct a detection benchmark from real appellate briefs with injected errors grounded in a taxonomy of sanctioned filings; we adopt their five error categories as our outcome coding and measure, by elicitation rather than injection, the naturally occurring analogue their limitations section calls for. Chen et al. (2026, arXiv:2605.10186) show closed-book citation retrieval is near zero and that failing models almost never abstain, a pattern our pressure conditions reproduce in generation. Studies of hallucinated authority in deployed tools (Dahl et al., 2024; Magesh et al., 2024) established the stakes.

**Constraints and citation fabrication.** Zhao et al. (2026, arXiv:2603.07287) is the closest design: five prompting regimes over scholarly claims, with fuzzy bibliographic matching as the verifier. We transplant their factorial skeleton to law and replace their probabilistic verification, which left 36 to 61 percent of citations unresolved, with deterministic adjudication that left 0.5 percent unresolved in our pilot. Their headline mechanism, compliance without substance under temporal restriction, transfers to law only partway down the ladder, a difference we measure.

**Execution-grounded revision.** The loop experiment borrows its protocol shape from execution-feedback work in code generation (Gehring et al., 2024, arXiv:2410.02089), including the critical control: feedback whose form is preserved but whose content is uninformative, which separates the use of feedback content from the benefit of extra attempts.

**LLM judges.** Our negative result on relevance judging joins a literature documenting that agreement statistics overstate LLM-judge validity (Norman et al., 2026, arXiv:2606.19544). We add a calibration protocol with a pre-declared bar and report three failures against expert labels, at budget and frontier tiers.

## 3. Tasks, conditions, and instruments

**Matters.** Forty-eight U.S. Supreme Court cases, 1990 to 2020, drawn from a 500-case pool built for an earlier project: each packet contains the question presented, party names, a factual summary, and an excerpt of the lower-court opinion, with the Supreme Court's own decision excluded and every packet screened for outcome leakage. The model argues an assigned side. Nothing in the design predicts case outcomes, which keeps the memorization confound that afflicts Supreme Court prediction tasks out of scope by construction.

**Conditions.** Baseline; quota ("cite at least eight distinct case authorities"); temporal ("every authority you cite MUST have been decided before 1970"); stakes (the section will be filed, and miscited authority risks Rule 11 sanctions); and combo, the three stacked. One fixed template per condition, temperature 0. A robustness arm re-ran twelve matters under alternative phrasings of every condition.

**Models.** Qwen3-30B-A3B, DeepSeek V4 Flash, GPT-5.4-mini, and Claude Sonnet 5, all through one API gateway, chosen to span open-weight 30B through frontier while keeping two frontier-family vendors distinct.

**Adjudication.** Citations are extracted with eyecite and adjudicated three ways, never two: exists, not found, or unverifiable, because coverage gaps masquerading as fabrication is the recurring flaw of prior pipelines. Existence and name pairing resolve against an 18-million-row citation table; quotations are located by balanced quotation marks, attributed to the case named nearest the quote with proximity as fallback, and checked verbatim against the cited opinion's text from a local-first chain (cached texts, then Caselaw Access Project volume archives, then a legacy cache, with the CourtListener API as last resort). The quote instrument was validated by seeded faults before any measurement: across twenty synthetic drafts with planted genuine quotes, wrong-attribution quotes, single-word corruptions, fabrications, and formatting artifacts, it scored 100 of 100 [validate_q2]. Pinpoint pages are verified against star pagination parsed from CAP's HTML volumes, which give exact page spans and page-break positions for U.S. Reports. Vendor citations are excluded from fabrication counts by design.

**Human reference.** The same instrument scored 482 clean, pre-ChatGPT federal appellate brief excerpts from the benchmark of Liu et al. Human lawyers reach 0.407 strict quotation accuracy (578 scored quotations, stable at 0.400 on the subset whose citations all resolve) and 0.931 citation existence under our oracle [human_baseline]; the verbatim standard is severe for everyone, and all comparisons in this paper are within this one instrument. The frontier models sit at or just below this human level at rest, and the strongest reaches it only under the pressure condition that includes the sanctions warning.

**Pre-registration.** Hypotheses and decision rules were frozen before the full run; every later analysis decision, including two findings retracted by their own verification passes, is recorded in a forking-paths ledger shipped with the code.

## 4. Experiment 1: pressure

Table 1 reports the definitive re-scored rates [rescore_full]; inference is a citation-level logistic GEE with exchangeable correlation clustered by matter, fit separately per model [stats_gee], with a matter-level cluster bootstrap as the pre-registered provisional giving the same picture throughout [stats_h1_bootstrap].

**Existence collapses only at the bottom.** The 30B model's citation existence falls from 0.924 at baseline to 0.773 under combo, and every one of its four pressure contrasts is significant (combo OR 0.304, p < 0.0001). Every larger model holds existence between 0.950 and 0.998 in every cell, with no significant negative contrast anywhere.

**Quotation fidelity is pressure-sensitive at every tier except the highest.** Strict accuracy under temporal and combo falls significantly for the 30B model (0.175 to 0.072), for DeepSeek (0.281 to 0.120), and for GPT-5.4-mini (0.460 to 0.294), with odds ratios between 0.35 and 0.61. Only Sonnet 5 holds flat.

**At the top, the sign reverses.** Under combo, the condition stacking the quota, the temporal restriction, and the sanctions warning, Sonnet 5's quotation fidelity is its best cell, 0.427 against a 0.333 baseline, significant on both channels (quotes OR 1.475, p = 0.029; existence OR 4.556, p = 0.034). The warning that degrades or fails to move every other model appears to induce care in the one model with the capability to be careful. The paraphrase arm reproduces the direction, 0.684 under the alternative phrasing of combo [paraphrase_comparison].

**Compliance is its own gradient.** The temporal restriction is violated in roughly 20 percent of checked citations at 30B, 7 to 13 percent at the middle tiers, and once in 802 at the top. Zhao et al. found scholarly models obey the window and fill it with fabrications; in law that signature appears only above the bottom tier, where the 30B model both fabricates and violates.

**No one abstains.** Across all 960 pressured drafts there were zero refusals at three tiers and one at the fourth. Degradation under pressure is silent, consistent with the misleading-answer rates Chen et al. report in the closed-book setting.

**Robustness.** Under alternative phrasings of every condition on a twelve-matter subsample, no core claim reverses sign; existence patterns reproduce almost exactly, and per-cell quotation rates at that sample size are reported as directional checks only.

## 5. Experiment 2: the verifier in the loop

Combined-pressure drafts entered a revision loop: each round, the deterministic checker returned one structured line per failure (a citation that does not resolve; a quotation that does not appear verbatim in the cited opinion), the model revised, and the loop ended on a clean draft or after three revisions. The control arm received the same number of identically formatted lines pointed at items that were in fact correct.

**Repair is capability-gated** [rescore_loops]. Final strict quotation accuracy under true feedback climbs the ladder monotonically: 0.112 at 30B (from 0.103, no episode converging), 0.645 at DeepSeek, 0.719 at GPT-5.4-mini, and 0.974 at Sonnet 5, which converged in 23 of 24 episodes in a mean of 2.6 rounds. Citation existence repairs more easily than quotation at every tier; the 30B model can replace a fabricated citation when told which one it is, but cannot fix its quoting at all.

**Imprecise verification is worse than none.** Under scrambled feedback every model got worse, and the strongest models degraded most: Sonnet 5 fell from 0.471 to 0.266, GPT-5.4-mini from 0.360 to 0.241. Told that a correct quotation is wrong, a capable model dutifully rewrites it into something wrong. The models best at using feedback are the most damaged by false feedback, which makes verifier precision, not just recall, a deployment requirement. Our false-alarm audit puts the deterministic instrument's own precision in range: 6 to 15 spurious inaccuracy verdicts per model across all 960 drafts, each traceable to partial opinion text [provenance_report].

Together the two experiments frame an uncomfortable complementarity: the tier where fabrication lives cannot use the verifier's help, and the tier that can use it barely needed it. Verification in the loop is a frontier amenity, not a safety net for the cheap models that are actually deployed at volume.

## 6. What survives at scale

Three failure modes remain when existence is essentially solved, each measured deterministically.

**Paraphrase wearing quotation marks.** Decomposing every frontier inaccurate quotation against the cited case's own text: 55 percent of Sonnet 5's (413 of 746 decomposed) are recognizable paraphrases of the correct case presented as verbatim quotation, 27 percent match nothing substantial in the cited opinion, and the remainder cite authority outside the corpus's scope [notincorpus_decomposition]. The dominant frontier sin is not inventing law; it is putting quotation marks around a summary.

**Right words, wrong case.** Searching every inaccurate quotation against a segmented corpus of Supreme Court opinions yields 66, 120, 66, and 114 quotations per model (ladder order) whose distinctive language appears verbatim in a different case than the one cited [provenance_report]. The models know the words of the law better than they know which case said them. A screen for a more exotic possibility, models quoting the argued case's own later-decided opinion from memory, did not survive its own audit: of 41 candidates, 32 were record or statute text present in the prompt, and the packet-absent remainder proved to be famous doctrinal formulas that the argued opinions were themselves quoting from precedent [leak_screen]. We report this retraction because the screen that killed the finding is the same one that establishes the others.

**Right case, wrong page.** Where a pinpoint page and a quotation co-occur on a star-paginated authority, the quotation actually sits on the cited page 77 percent of the time for Sonnet 5 and 51 to 58 percent for the others, while pins almost never fall outside the case entirely (13 of roughly 4,000) [pincites]. This is exhaustive measurement, on public data, of the error class the best published detector catches at 18.2 percent recall.

## 7. The jury that could not be seated

One question resists deterministic checking: whether a real, correctly quoted case supports the proposition it is cited for. We attempted to calibrate an LLM panel against the expert-labeled content-misrepresentation items of Liu et al., with a pre-declared bar of 0.75 accuracy on decisive verdicts. A three-family budget panel judged binary support at 0.500, rejecting everything. Reframed with a three-way verdict and abstention, the same panel scored 0.700, accepting nearly everything, with misrepresentation recall of 2 of 11. A frontier panel of GPT-5.4, Sonnet 5, and Gemini 2.5 Pro with 15,000-character grounded excerpts scored 0.594, recall 4 of 17. No measurement verdict from any failed panel was interpreted. We report the three failures as a result: on the one channel that requires judgment rather than lookup, no jury we could assemble met a modest validity bar, which is the paper's thesis in miniature. In legal citation integrity, the layer you can trust is the layer you can verify deterministically.

## 8. Discussion

The results reorganize the safety question around capability rather than around models in general. "Do language models fabricate legal citations under pressure" has no single answer; it has a gradient, and the gradient runs opposite to deployment reality, since cost pushes production systems toward exactly the tier where a citation quota and a hard authority restriction triple the odds of fabrication. The finding that the sanctions warning improves the strongest model while doing nothing or worse below it suggests that stakes framing is not a safety lever, it is a capability amplifier, and it should not be relied on where it is needed most.

The loop results carry a concrete engineering prescription. A deterministic citation verifier is cheap to build from public data, it repairs frontier drafts almost completely in under three rounds, and its false positives are rare enough to matter little; an imprecise verifier, by contrast, is actively destructive in proportion to the target model's obedience. Systems that bolt probabilistic hallucination detectors into revision loops should measure the cost of their false-positive rate before shipping.

Finally, the decompositions relocate the frontier problem. Once fabrication is gone, what remains is a binding failure between language and source: paraphrase presented as quotation, doctrine attributed to the wrong case, quotations pinned to the wrong page. These errors survive because they are invisible to existence checks and to readers who recognize the words. They are exactly the errors our instrument stack was built to see, and all three verifiers run on public data.

## 9. Limitations

Single domain and single task: U.S. Supreme Court merits argument, one packet format, one fixed template per condition with a phrasing-robustness subsample rather than a full paraphrase factorial. The quote instrument's strict standard fails bracketed alterations and ellipses for humans and models alike; we therefore anchor to a human baseline under the same standard rather than to an absolute notion of accuracy. The provenance corpus covers 8,392 Supreme Court opinions and cannot adjudicate quotations from other courts, which we label out of scope rather than fabricated. The relevance channel is unmeasured, bounded by three documented calibration failures. Statistical inference is per-model at 48 matters; cross-model interaction claims rest on the visible gradient, not on a pooled test. The capability ladder confounds scale, family, and training recipe, as any four-model ladder must.

## 10. Ethics and release

All source material is public court data. Drafts were generated for measurement, are marked as machine output, and none was filed anywhere. We release the instrument stack (existence, pairing, verbatim quotation, page-level pinpoint verification), the seeded-fault validation harness, the forking-paths ledger, and per-citation records sufficient to reproduce every table, and we do not release anything that would function as a tool for making fabricated filings look verified.
