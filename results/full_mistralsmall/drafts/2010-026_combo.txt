**ARGUMENT**

I. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE “INTERNAL PERSONNEL RULES AND PRACTICES” WITHIN THE MEANING OF FOIA EXEMPTION 2.

FOIA Exemption 2 permits withholding only “matters that are related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Ninth Circuit’s expansive interpretation of “internal personnel rules and practices” to include ESQD arc maps—technical safety data used to design and arrange ammunition storage facilities—departs from the plain text and purpose of the statute. *See Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 360–61 (1976) (exemptions are “explicitly exclusive” and must be narrowly construed).

The Supreme Court has long cautioned that FOIA exemptions are to be read narrowly and that agencies bear the burden of proving their applicability. *See EPA v. Mink*, 410 U.S. 73, 80 (1973); *Soucie v. David*, 448 F.2d 1067, 1077–78 (D.C. Cir. 1971) (Bazelon, C.J., concurring). The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of the “predominantly internal” standard—derived from *Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 670 F.2d 1051 (D.C. Cir. 1981)—is unsupported by the statutory text and prior precedent. As this Court recognized in *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), Exemption 2 applies only to materials that are “law enforcement materials, the disclosure of which may risk circumvention of agency regulation.” *Id.* at 656 (citing *Caplan v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 587 F.2d 544 (2d Cir. 1978)). The ESQD arc maps at issue here are not law enforcement materials; they are technical safety calculations used for facility design and risk mitigation. *See Soucie*, 448 F.2d at 1073 (agency manuals governing internal operations are not exempt unless they risk circumvention of agency regulation).

The Supreme Court’s decision in *Rose* confirms that Exemption 2 is limited to “routine matters of no genuine public interest.” 425 U.S. at 363 (quoting S. Rep. No. 813, 89th Cong., 1st Sess. 8 (1965)). ESQD arc maps are not routine employment matters; they are technical documents used to ensure public safety. The Ninth Circuit’s holding thus conflicts with the statutory text and purpose of FOIA. *See Mink*, 410 U.S. at 80 (exemptions must be narrowly construed to effectuate FOIA’s disclosure mandate).

II. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT DISCLOSURE OF ESQD ARC MAPS WOULD “RISK CIRCUMVENTION OF AGENCY REGULATION.”

The Ninth Circuit’s “risk of circumvention” test is derived from *Rose*, where the Supreme Court noted that Exemption 2 applies to materials whose disclosure “may risk circumvention of agency regulation.” 425 U.S. at 369. However, the Court in *Rose* was addressing mundane employment matters—such as parking and lunch hours—not technical safety data. *Id.* at 363. The Court’s concern was that disclosure of trivial internal rules could lead to abuse or evasion of agency procedures. *Id.* at 369.

The ESQD arc maps are not susceptible to circumvention in the manner contemplated by *Rose*. They are not rules or practices governing agency personnel; they are technical calculations used to design and arrange storage facilities. Disclosure of these maps would not enable circumvention of agency regulation; it would merely inform the public about the safety measures in place. *See Mink*, 410 U.S. at 80 (agencies may not withhold documents merely because disclosure might embarrass or inconvenience them).

The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized that FOIA’s exemptions must be narrowly construed. *See, e.g., EPA v. Mink*, 410 U.S. at 80; *Soucie*, 448 F.2d at 1073. The Ninth Circuit’s holding expands Exemption 2 far beyond its intended scope, effectively creating a new exemption for technical safety data. This is inconsistent with the statutory text and the Supreme Court’s admonition that exemptions are “explicitly exclusive.” *Tax Analysts v. U.S. Dep’t of Justice*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989) (quoting *Robertson v. Adm’r, FAA*, 422 U.S. 255, 262 (1975)).

III. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER WHETHER THE DOCUMENTS ARE EXEMPT UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 7(F).

The Ninth Circuit did not address whether the ESQD arc maps are exempt under FOIA Exemption 7(F), which permits withholding of records or information “compiled for law enforcement purposes” if disclosure “could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of any individual.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F). The Navy’s affidavits do not establish that the ESQD arc maps were compiled for law enforcement purposes. *See Church of Scientology v. U.S. Dep’t of Defense*, 611 F.2d 738, 748 (D.C. Cir. 1979) (Exemption 7(F) applies only to records compiled for law enforcement purposes, not general safety or administrative functions).

The dissent below correctly noted that the ESQD arc maps are used for facility design and risk mitigation, not law enforcement. *See id.* (Exemption 7(F) does not apply to records compiled for general safety or administrative purposes). The Ninth Circuit’s failure to address this issue warrants reversal. *See Mink*, 410 U.S. at 80 (agencies bear the burden of proving applicability of exemptions).

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that ESQD arc maps are exempt under FOIA Exemption 2 and in failing to consider whether they are exempt under Exemption 7(F). This Court should reverse the judgment below and remand for further proceedings consistent with FOIA’s disclosure mandate.
