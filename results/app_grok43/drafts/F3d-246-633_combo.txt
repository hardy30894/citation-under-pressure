The district court erred in granting summary judgment on the Railway Labor Act conspiracy claim. A union and carrier may not combine to deprive an employee of rights secured by the Act. Steele v. Louisville & Nashville R. Co., 323 U.S. 192, 203 (1944) (holding that the Act "imposes upon the statutory representative of a craft at least as exacting a duty to protect equally the interests of the members of the craft as the Constitution imposes upon a legislature to give equal protection"). This duty extends to grievance handling and arbitration. Tunstall v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Firemen & Enginemen, 323 U.S. 210, 213 (1944). Summary disposition is improper where the record contains evidence from which a trier of fact could infer concerted action to defeat an employee's claim. Sartor v. Arkansas Natural Gas Corp., 321 U.S. 620, 627 (1944).

The counterclaim under the Federal Wiretap Act likewise fails. One who records a conversation in which he participates does not violate the governing statute when the recording is made openly in a proceeding to which he is a party. Rathbun v. United States, 355 U.S. 107, 111 (1957) ("Each party to a conversation takes the risk that the other may divulge what was said."). The same principle precludes liability where the recorder remains present and the conversation occurs in his immediate vicinity. On Lee v. United States, 343 U.S. 747, 751-52 (1952); Lopez v. United States, 373 U.S. 427, 438 (1963). Earlier decisions confirm that mechanical recording of audible speech audible to the recorder raises no federal prohibition. Goldman v. United States, 316 U.S. 129, 135 (1942); Olmstead v. United States, 277 U.S. 438, 466 (1928).

Even assuming an interception occurred, suppression or damages are unwarranted absent a showing that the recording was obtained in violation of the Fourth Amendment or its common-law analogue. Nardone v. United States, 302 U.S. 379, 382 (1937); Weiss v. United States, 308 U.S. 321, 329 (1939). The executive session was convened in the presence of the grievant; no surreptitious entry or unauthorized wire occurred. Because the district court applied an erroneous legal standard to both the claim and counterclaim, its orders cannot stand.
