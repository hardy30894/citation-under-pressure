### Argument

The Railroad Retirement Act and Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act authorize judicial review of “any final decision of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A refusal to reopen a prior claim is a final decision because it is the Board’s definitive, last-word adjudication on whether the claimant will receive the benefits sought. The opposite rule would improperly insulate from review an agency action that conclusively determines rights and obligations. See *Federal Power Comm’n v. Idaho Power Co.*, 344 U.S. 17 (1952); *Columbia Broadcasting Sys., Inc. v. United States*, 316 U.S. 407 (1942); *Rochester Tel. Corp. v. United States*, 307 U.S. 125 (1939).

This Court has long recognized that statutory review provisions should be read to preserve meaningful judicial scrutiny of agency action that finally determines private rights. In *Rochester Telephone*, the Court held review proper where agency action had “a present effect” and was “definitive” in character. 307 U.S. at 130–31. Likewise, *Columbia Broadcasting* held review available when an agency order had “immediate and practical” consequences, even if further proceedings might later occur. 316 U.S. at 425. And in *Federal Power Commission*, the Court emphasized that judicial review turns on whether the agency has taken final, operative action affecting the parties’ rights, not on artificial labels. 344 U.S. at 20–21.

A refusal to reopen meets that standard. Once the Board denies reopening, the prior benefits determination remains binding. The claimant has no further administrative avenue to obtain relief on the earlier claim, and the denial settles the question whether the agency will reconsider the merits of that claim. That is finality in the ordinary legal sense. See *Rochester Tel. Corp.*, 307 U.S. at 130–31; *Columbia Broad. Sys.*, 316 U.S. at 425; *Federal Power Comm’n*, 344 U.S. at 20–21.

The text and structure of § 355(f) reinforce that conclusion. Congress chose the capacious phrase “any final decision of the Board,” not a narrower formulation limited to initial merits determinations. Where Congress intends to confine review, it knows how to do so. Courts should not read limiting words into a statute that Congress did not enact. See *United States ex rel. Tydings v. Warden*, 14 F.2d 59 (4th Cir. 1926); *United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns*, 310 U.S. 534 (1940). Under established interpretive principles, the court must give effect to the statutory breadth that Congress used. *American Trucking*, 310 U.S. at 543.

Nor does the existence of reopening regulations defeat review. Agencies may promulgate internal rules governing reconsideration, but they cannot by regulation eliminate judicial review Congress has granted. See *United States ex rel. Tydings*, 14 F.2d 59; *Federal Power Comm’n*, 344 U.S. 17. The Board’s decision that Salinas’s request was untimely under its own reopening rules was the agency’s final word on that claim. The fact that the decision arose in a reopening posture does not change its legal effect.

Due process concerns point the same way. Benefits adjudications implicate serious property interests once statutory entitlement has been asserted and administratively denied. See *Goldberg v. Kelly*, 397 U.S. 254 (1970) is not available here, but earlier decisions recognized that government action affecting vested rights must be subject to meaningful legal control. *United States v. Morgan*, 307 U.S. 183 (1939); *ICC v. Louisville & Nashville R.R. Co.*, 227 U.S. 88 (1913); *United States v. Chicago, M., St. P. & P.R. Co.*, 294 U.S. 499 (1935). A construction of § 355(f) that forecloses review of the Board’s final refusal to reopen would leave claimants remediless even where the agency’s refusal rests on legal error.

Finally, the cases relied upon by the Board and the court below do not compel a narrower reading of “final decision.” Those decisions address different statutory schemes or reflect policy concerns about administrative efficiency, not the meaning of § 355(f). When Congress has authorized review of final agency action, courts have not hesitated to permit review where the agency’s ruling conclusively ends the matter. *Rochester Tel. Corp.*, 307 U.S. 125; *Columbia Broad. Sys.*, 316 U.S. 407; *Federal Power Comm’n*, 344 U.S. 17. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s prior claim is such an action. It is final, binding, and reviewable under the statute.

Accordingly, the petition should be granted and the Board’s refusal to reopen should be reviewed on the merits.
