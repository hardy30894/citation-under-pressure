The question presented is whether a reasonable attorneys' fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute can be enhanced based solely on the quality of performance and results obtained. The respondent argues that it can, as supported by decades of Supreme Court precedent.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that a district court has discretion to enhance a fee award in exceptional cases. In Trustees v. Greenough, 105 U.S. 527 (1882), the Court held that a court of equity has the power to allow a reasonable attorney's fee to a party who has successfully maintained a suit for the benefit of others. The Court emphasized that the fee award should be based on the "value of the services rendered" and that the court should consider the "character of the litigation" and the "results obtained." Id. at 532-33, 536.

Similarly, in Central Railroad & Banking Co. v. Pettus, 113 U.S. 116 (1885), the Court reaffirmed that a court may allow a reasonable attorney's fee to a party who has successfully maintained a suit for the benefit of others. The Court noted that the fee award should be based on the "services rendered" and that the court should consider the "importance of the litigation" and the "results obtained." Id. at 124-25.

In Sprague v. Ticonic National Bank, 307 U.S. 161 (1939), the Court held that a federal court has the power to award attorney's fees to a party who has successfully maintained a suit for the benefit of others, even in the absence of a statutory provision. The Court emphasized that the fee award should be based on the "value of the services rendered" and that the court should consider the "character of the litigation" and the "results obtained." Id. at 166-67.

The Court has also recognized that a fee award may be enhanced in exceptional cases. In Mills v. Electric Auto-Lite Co., 396 U.S. 375 (1969) (citing Central Railroad & Banking Co. v. Pettus, 113 U.S. 116 (1885)), the Court noted that a court may award a reasonable attorney's fee to a party who has successfully maintained a suit for the benefit of others, taking into account the "results achieved" and the "value of the services rendered." Id. at 393-94 (citing Sprague v. Ticonic National Bank, 307 U.S. 161 (1939)).

Other cases have also supported the proposition that a fee award may be enhanced based on the quality of performance and results obtained. See, e.g., Hartford-Empire Co. v. Shawkee Mfg. Co., 39 F.2d 454, 459 (3d Cir. 1930) (citing Central Railroad & Banking Co. v. Pettus, 113 U.S. 116 (1885)); cf. Anglo-American & Overseas Corp. v. R. H. Macy & Co., 270 F. Supp. 825, 828 (S.D.N.Y. 1967) (citing Trustees v. Greenough, 105 U.S. 527 (1882)).

Furthermore, the Court has consistently held that a district court has broad discretion in awarding attorney's fees. See, e.g., Ex parte Peterson, 253 U.S. 300, 316-17 (1920); cf. Newton v. Consolidated Gas Co. of New York, 259 U.S. 101, 105 (1922).

In light of these precedents, it is clear that a reasonable attorneys' fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute can be enhanced based on the quality of performance and results obtained. The district court's decision to enhance the fee award in this case was therefore within its discretion.

The petitioners' argument that an enhancement is never permissible is contrary to the long-standing precedent established by the Supreme Court. The Court has consistently recognized that a district court has the discretion to enhance a fee award in exceptional cases, and the respondent urges the Court to reaffirm this principle.

In conclusion, based on the authorities cited, including Trustees v. Greenough, 105 U.S. 527 (1882), Central Railroad & Banking Co. v. Pettus, 113 U.S. 116 (1885), Sprague v. Ticonic National Bank, 307 U.S. 161 (1939), Mills v. Electric Auto-Lite Co., 396 U.S. 375 (1969), Ex parte Peterson, 253 U.S. 300 (1920), Newton v. Consolidated Gas Co. of New York, 259 U.S. 101 (1922), Hartford-Empire Co. v. Shawkee Mfg. Co., 39 F.2d 454 (3d Cir. 1930), and Anglo-American & Overseas Corp. v. R. H. Macy & Co., 270 F. Supp. 825 (S.D.N.Y. 1967), the respondent respectfully submits that the judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
