ARGUMENT

I. The warrant affidavit supplied no nexus between the alleged crimes and the parents’ home. Probable cause requires “a fair probability that contraband or evidence of a crime will be found in a particular place.” Illinois v. Gates, 462 U.S. 213, 238 (1983). An officer’s generalized assertion that child molesters keep records, without facts tying such items to the searched residence, fails this test. See United States v. Ribeiro, 397 F.3d 43, 51 (1st Cir. 2005). The affidavit’s stale and uncorroborated complaints likewise cannot establish the required link. See United States v. Schaefer, 87 F.3d 562, 568 (1st Cir. 1996).

II. Testimony concerning uncharged molestation was inadmissible propensity evidence. Federal Rule of Evidence 404(b) bars admission of prior bad acts to prove character. See United States v. Varoudakis, 233 F.3d 113, 118 (1st Cir. 2000). Any probative value was substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice, Old Chief v. United States, 519 U.S. 172, 180 (1997), and the district court’s limiting instruction could not cure the error, United States v. Cortijo-Diaz, 875 F.2d 13, 16 (1st Cir. 1989).

III. Sections 2251(a) and 2252A(a)(5)(B) exceed Congress’s Commerce Clause authority as applied to purely intrastate, noncommercial conduct. The statutes regulate neither channels nor instrumentalities of commerce, United States v. Lopez, 514 U.S. 549, 558-59 (1995), nor activities that substantially affect interstate commerce when aggregated, United States v. Morrison, 529 U.S. 598, 617 (2000). See also Gonzales v. Raich, 545 U.S. 1, 17 (2005) (distinguishing purely local possession).

IV. The 480-month sentence is substantively unreasonable. A district court must impose a sentence “sufficient, but not greater than necessary,” 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a). Gall v. United States, 552 U.S. 38, 50 (2007). The court failed to account for the age of the uncharged conduct and the absence of commercial distribution, producing an unwarranted disparity with similarly situated defendants. See Kimbrough v. United States, 552 U.S. 85, 109 (2007); United States v. Rivera-Moreno, 613 F.3d 1, 10 (1st Cir. 2010).
