**ARGUMENT**

**I. FEDERAL COURTS POSSESS EXCLUSIVE JURISDICTION OVER STATE LEGAL MALPRACTICE CLAIMS THAT REQUIRE RESOLUTION OF A SUBSTANTIAL QUESTION OF FEDERAL PATENT LAW.**

The Supreme Court has long held that federal courts have exclusive jurisdiction over cases "arising under" federal patent law. *See Christianson v. Colt Indus. Operating Corp.*, 486 U.S. 800, 808-09 (1988) (construing 28 U.S.C. § 1338(a)). This jurisdiction extends to state-law claims that "necessarily raise a stated federal issue, actually disputed and substantial." *Grable & Sons Metal Prods., Inc. v. Darue Eng’g & Mfg.*, 545 U.S. 308, 314 (2005). The Federal Circuit has applied this principle to legal malpractice claims arising from patent litigation, holding that such claims trigger exclusive federal jurisdiction when they require resolution of a substantial question of federal patent law. *See Air Measurement Techs., Inc. v. Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld, L.L.P.*, 504 F.3d 1262, 1273 (Fed. Cir. 2007); *Immunocept, LLC v. Fulbright & Jaworski, LLP*, 504 F.3d 1281 (Fed. Cir. 2007).

Here, Minton’s legal malpractice claim arises from Gunn’s alleged failure to raise the experimental use exception in the underlying patent infringement litigation. *See* Pet. App. 12-13. The experimental use exception is a judicially created doctrine under federal patent law that excuses certain pre-patent sales from invalidating a patent under the on-sale bar. *See Electromotive Div. of Gen. Motors Corp. v. Tramp. Sys. Div. of Gen. Elec. Co.*, 417 F.3d 1203, 1210 (Fed. Cir. 2005). Resolution of whether Gunn’s failure to raise this exception proximately caused Minton’s injury requires a determination of whether Minton’s earlier sale of TEXCEN qualified for the experimental use exception—a question of federal patent law. *See id.* This question is both substantial and disputed, as it implicates the scope and application of a federal patent doctrine. *See Air Measurement*, 504 F.3d at 1273.

The Texas Supreme Court erred in concluding that this case does not trigger exclusive federal jurisdiction. The court acknowledged that the experimental use exception plays a "substantial role" in Minton’s malpractice claim but incorrectly held that federal jurisdiction would "disturb the balance of federal and state judicial responsibilities." *See* Pet. App. 20-21. This conclusion misapplies *Grable*, which requires courts to consider whether federal jurisdiction would "disturb any congressionally approved balance of federal and state judicial responsibilities." *Grable*, 545 U.S. at 314. Here, federal resolution of the experimental use exception will not disrupt that balance, as it is a purely legal question that does not implicate broader patent policy concerns. *See id.* at 315-16.

Moreover, the Texas Supreme Court’s reliance on non-patent cases to cabin federal jurisdiction is misplaced. *See* Pet. App. 18-19. The Supreme Court has made clear that the "arising under" analysis under § 1338(a) is informed by the same principles governing § 1331. *See Christianson*, 486 U.S. at 808-09. Thus, the Federal Circuit’s well-reasoned decisions in *Air Measurement* and *Immunocept* correctly apply *Grable* to legal malpractice claims arising from patent litigation. *See Air Measurement*, 504 F.3d at 1273; *Immunocept*, 504 F.3d at 1281.

**II. THE EXPERIMENTAL USE EXCEPTION IS A NECESSARY ELEMENT OF MINTON’S MALPRACTICE CLAIM.**

To prevail on his legal malpractice claim, Minton must establish that Gunn’s failure to raise the experimental use exception proximately caused his injury. *See Alexander v. Turtur & Assocs.*, 146 S.W.3d 113, 117 (Tex. 2004). This requires a showing that, but for Gunn’s negligence, Minton would have prevailed in the underlying patent infringement litigation. *Id.* Resolution of this causation question necessarily requires a determination of whether TEXCEN’s sale qualified for the experimental use exception—a question of federal patent law. *See Electromotive*, 417 F.3d at 1210.

The Texas Supreme Court’s conclusion that the experimental use exception is not a "necessary element" of Minton’s malpractice claim is erroneous. *See* Pet. App. 20. The court’s reliance on *Coastal Oil & Gas Corp. v. Garza Energy Trust*, 268 S.W.3d 1 (Tex. 2008), is misplaced, as that case did not involve a legal malpractice claim arising from patent litigation. *See id.* at 9. Here, the experimental use exception is not merely "incidental" to Minton’s malpractice claim; it is the linchpin of his theory of causation. *See Electromotive*, 417 F.3d at 1210.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the judgment of the Texas Supreme Court and hold that federal courts possess exclusive jurisdiction over Minton’s legal malpractice claim. The experimental use exception is a substantial and disputed question of federal patent law that is necessary to the resolution of Minton’s claim. Federal jurisdiction over this claim will not disturb the congressionally approved balance of federal and state judicial responsibilities.
