ARGUMENT

The district court erred in holding that BCR’s Rule 68 offer mooted Lucero’s individual claims and thereby deprived the court of subject-matter jurisdiction over the uncertified class action. “A suit brought as a class action must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified.” Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir. 2009) (internal quotation marks omitted). That general statement, however, does not control when the named plaintiff has timely moved for class certification and the defendant’s offer is made before the court rules on that motion.

In Sosna v. Iowa, 419 U.S. 393 (1975), the Supreme Court recognized that “the class of unnamed persons described in the complaint acquired a legal status separate from the interest asserted by” the named plaintiff once the district court certified the class. Id. at 399. The Court therefore held that mootness of the named plaintiff’s claim after certification did not moot the action. Id. at 401. U.S. Parole Commission v. Geraghty, 445 U.S. 388 (1980), extended the principle to the denial of certification: a named plaintiff retains a sufficient personal stake to appeal an adverse certification ruling even after his individual claim has expired. Id. at 404.

These precedents establish that the “personal stake” requirement in class litigation is measured by reference to the class, not solely the named plaintiff, once the complaint is filed as a class action and a certification motion is pending. See Geraghty, 445 U.S. at 402. Allowing a pre-certification Rule 68 offer to terminate the entire action would permit defendants to “buy off” named plaintiffs and thereby frustrate the class mechanism Congress authorized in Rule 23. The district court’s contrary conclusion—that jurisdiction vanishes the moment the individual claim is satisfied—contradicts the relation-back doctrine applied in analogous contexts. See County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 500 U.S. 44, 51-52 (1991) (holding that class claims relate back to the filing of the complaint when the district court has not had a reasonable time to rule on certification).

Because Lucero filed his motion for class certification within the court-ordered deadline and before any ruling on BCR’s offer, the action retained a live controversy between the putative class and BCR. The district court therefore possessed subject-matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 and should have proceeded to decide the certification motion.
