**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE POST-REMOVAL-PERIOD STATUTE DOES NOT AUTHORIZE INDEFINITE DETENTION.**

The Supreme Court held that the post-removal-period detention statute does not authorize indefinite detention. *Zadvydas v. Davis*, 533 U.S. 678, 2495 (2001). The Court stated that indefinite detention of aliens would raise serious constitutional concerns. *Id.* at 2495. Consequently, the Court construed the statute to contain an implicit “reasonable time” limitation, the application of which is subject to federal court review. *Id.* at 2495. The Court further held that the statute limits detention to a period reasonably necessary to bring about removal. *Id.* at 2498.

**II. ONCE REMOVAL IS NO LONGER REASONABLY FORESEEABLE, CONTINUED DETENTION IS NO LONGER AUTHORIZED.**

The Court defined the “reasonable time” by asking whether removal is reasonably foreseeable. *Id.* at 2504. The Court rejected the standard that detention is lawful as long as “good faith efforts” continue, noting that this standard would require an alien to show the absence of any prospect of removal. *Id.* at 2505. Instead, the Court concluded that once removal is no longer reasonably foreseeable, continued detention is no longer authorized by the statute. *Id.* at 2503.

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT CORRECTLY FOUND THAT REMOVAL IS NOT REASONABLY FORESEEABLE.**

Here, Zadvydas has been detained for over three years. The record shows no country will accept him. The Court noted that as the period of prior post-removal confinement grows, the “reasonably foreseeable future” must shrink. *Id.* at 2505. The district court found that Zadvydas provided good reason to believe there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future. The government has not rebutted this showing. Therefore, the district court’s order granting Zadvydas’s habeas petition is consistent with the Supreme Court’s holding.
