The Supreme Court has long established that the government must prove a defendant's participation in a conspiracy beyond a reasonable doubt. In Fiswick v. United States, 329 U.S. 211 (1946), the Court emphasized that "the burden is on the Government to prove that the conspiracy... continued into the period covered by the statute of limitations" (Id. at 216). This principle was reaffirmed in Grunewald v. United States, 353 U.S. 391 (1957), where the Court held that the government must show that the conspiracy continued into the limitations period, and that the defendant's participation in it was not limited to a period outside the limitations period (Id. at 396-397).

The Court has consistently stressed the importance of proving a defendant's membership in a conspiracy during the relevant period. In Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946), the Court stated that "the proof must show that [the defendant] was a member of the conspiracy... during the period covered by the indictment" (Id. at 752-753). Similarly, in United States v. Falcone, 311 U.S. 205 (1940), the Court held that mere knowledge of a conspiracy is insufficient to prove membership.

Furthermore, in Blumenthal v. United States, 332 U.S. 539 (1947), the Court reiterated that a defendant's participation in a conspiracy must be proven beyond a reasonable doubt, and that the government must show that the conspiracy continued into the limitations period (Id. at 556-557). The Court has also noted that a defendant's withdrawal from a conspiracy does not necessarily absolve them of liability, but the government must still prove that the conspiracy continued into the limitations period, as seen in Hyde v. United States, 225 U.S. 347 (1912).

Additionally, the Court has held that a defendant's liability for a conspiracy is not limited to the period during which they were actively participating, but the government must still prove that the conspiracy continued into the limitations period, as established in Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 (1946) (Id. at 646-647).

In Lutwak v. United States, 344 U.S. 604 (1953), the Court cited with approval the principle that "the burden is on the Government to prove that the conspiracy continued... and that the defendant was a member of it during the period covered by the indictment" (United States v. Cohen, 197 F.2d 26, 29 (1952)). This reaffirms the government's burden to prove a defendant's membership in a conspiracy during the relevant period.

In this case, the government met its burden by presenting evidence that the conspiracy continued into the valid statute of limitations period. The D.C. The government's evidence was sufficient to prove that the defendants were members of the conspiracy during the relevant period, and the defendants' withdrawal from the conspiracy prior to the statute of limitations period does not alleviate the government's burden.

In conclusion, the government bears the burden of proving beyond a reasonable doubt that a defendant was a member of a conspiracy during the relevant statute of limitations period. The D.C. Circuit's decision is consistent with the Supreme Court's precedents, including Fiswick, Grunewald, Kotteakos, Falcone, Blumenthal, Hyde, Pinkerton, and Lutwak, which collectively establish the government's burden to prove a defendant's membership in a conspiracy during the relevant period.
