The Railroad Retirement Act authorizes judicial review of a “final decision” of the Board, and a refusal to reopen a closed benefits claim fits that description. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The ordinary rule is that agency action is reviewable when it finally determines rights and liabilities; finality does not turn on the label the agency chooses for its action. See Dismuke v. United States, 297 U.S. 167 (1936); Dodge v. Board of Ed. of Chicago, 302 U.S. 74 (1937). And where Congress has provided for judicial review of administrative action, courts do not lightly infer that a later agency order is insulated from review merely because it addresses reopening rather than the original merits. See SEC v. Louisiana Pub. Serv. Comm’n, 353 U.S. 368 (1957); Robertson v. Chambers, 341 U.S. 37 (1951).

The Board’s denial of reopening is the operative, consummated agency action that finally determines whether a claimant may revive a benefits award. That kind of decision is not provisional or ministerial; it resolves conclusively whether the agency will disturb its prior judgment. This Court has treated analogous agency action as reviewable when it marks the end of the administrative road on the issue presented. See Cheng Fan Kwok v. Immigration & Naturalization Serv., 392 U.S. 206 (1968) (recognizing review of agency determinations made in the course of administrative proceedings and of a motion to reopen); SEC v. Louisiana Pub. Serv. Comm’n, 353 U.S. 368 (1957) (reviewing denial of petition to reopen); Robertson v. Chambers, 341 U.S. 37 (1951) (reviewing board action on rehearing-related request); Wragg v. Federal Land Bank of New Orleans, 317 U.S. 325 (1943) (review available after an administrative proceeding had been terminated); Dismuke, 297 U.S. 167 (same principle in the benefits context).

The Government will argue, as the Fifth Circuit did, that reopening is merely an ancillary request and therefore not a “final decision.” But this Court has never adopted such a categorical rule. To the contrary, the Court has recognized that where Congress provides a right to review final administrative action, the courts may review the agency’s disposition of a reopening request if that disposition itself finally settles the claimant’s rights as to the reopened matter. See SEC v. Louisiana Pub. Serv. Comm’n, 353 U.S. 368 (1957); Cheng Fan Kwok, 392 U.S. 206 (1968). The Board’s order here did exactly that: it concluded that Salinas’s 2006 claim could not be reopened because the regulatory time limit had expired. That was the Board’s last word on the subject.

Nothing in the statutory text supports an artificial distinction between an original denial and a later refusal to reopen. Section 355(f) speaks broadly of review of “final decisions,” not just initial merits determinations. When Congress has intended to restrict review to a narrower class of agency actions, it has done so expressly. See Pennsylvania R.R. v. Day, 360 U.S. 548 (1959); Joint Indus. Bd. of Elec. Indus. v. United States, 391 U.S. 224 (1968); International Union of Mine, Mill & Smelter Workers v. Eagle-Picher Mining & Smelting Co., 325 U.S. 335 (1945). The Board’s position would require adding a judicially invented exception for reopening orders that the statute does not contain.

Moreover, finality and reviewability are especially appropriate here because the denial of reopening carries immediate legal consequences. Salinas is not asking the Court to supervise an internal administrative housekeeping step. He seeks review of an order that foreclosed any further consideration of his 2006 claim. This Court has repeatedly recognized that final agency action with such consequences is fit for judicial review. See Dodge, 302 U.S. 74; Wragg, 317 U.S. 325; Robertson, 341 U.S. 37; SEC, 353 U.S. 368.

At a minimum, the statute should be construed to permit review of a reopening denial where, as here, the Board has finally adjudicated the claimant’s entitlement to relief and refused to alter that adjudication. That reading best accords with § 355(f)’s text, the presumption favoring review of final agency action, and this Court’s decisions treating conclusive agency refusals to reconsider as reviewable. See Dismuke, 297 U.S. 167; Robertson, 341 U.S. 37; SEC, 353 U.S. 368; Cheng Fan Kwok, 392 U.S. 206.

The petition should therefore be held reviewable and the judgment below reversed.
