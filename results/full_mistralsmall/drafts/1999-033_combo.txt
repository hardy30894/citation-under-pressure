**ARGUMENT**

I. THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE RECOGNIZES A “CLASS OF ONE” THEORY OF LIABILITY WHERE STATE ACTION IS MOTIVATED BY ILL WILL OR ARBITRARINESS.

The Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment proscribes arbitrary and irrational state action. *Yick Wo v. Hopkins*, 118 U.S. 356, 369 (1886) (“[T]he equal protection of the laws is a pledge of the protection of equal laws.”). This Court has long recognized that the Clause reaches not only discrimination against discrete and insular minorities, but also invidious treatment of individuals motivated by hostility or malice. *See Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Dev. Corp.*, 429 U.S. 252, 265 (1977) (citing *Yick Wo*, 118 U.S. at 373–74). Where a plaintiff alleges that state action was taken “solely because of [his] personal animosity” or “spiteful effort to ‘get’ him,” the plaintiff states a cognizable claim under the Equal Protection Clause. *Snowden v. Hughes*, 321 U.S. 1, 8 (1944) (holding that discriminatory refusal to certify plaintiff’s election results violated Equal Protection where motivated by personal animosity); *cf. Home Tel. & Tel. Co. v. City of Los Angeles*, 227 U.S. 278, 296–97 (1913) (invalidating municipal ordinance enforced with “oppressive discrimination” against plaintiff).

This Court’s decision in *Yick Wo* is particularly instructive. There, the Court struck down a facially neutral ordinance that was enforced discriminatorily against Chinese laundry operators. 118 U.S. at 374. The Court held that the Equal Protection Clause forbids “a denial of equal justice” where “[t]he facts shown establish an administration [of the law] … practically discriminating against [a] class.” *Id.* at 373–74. The same principle applies where the discrimination is directed at an individual rather than a class. *See Snowden*, 321 U.S. at 8 (“[T]he illegality of the discrimination does not depend upon the existence of a class or group which is adversely affected.”).

II. THE SEVENTH CIRCUIT CORRECTLY CONCLUDED THAT OLECH ALLEGED A VIOLATION OF THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE BY ALLEGING THAT THE VILLAGE ACTED WITH ILL WILL TOWARD HER.

The Seventh Circuit properly recognized that Olech’s complaint stated a claim under the Equal Protection Clause by alleging that the Village’s demand for an 18-foot easement was motivated by “spiteful effort to ‘get’ her for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective.” *Olech v. Village of Willowbrook*, 160 F.3d 386, 387 (7th Cir. 1998) (quoting *Snowden*, 321 U.S. at 8). This Court has repeatedly held that the Equal Protection Clause forbids state action taken for reasons of personal animosity or ill will. *See, e.g., Home Tel. & Tel. Co.*, 227 U.S. at 296–97; *Yick Wo*, 118 U.S. at 373–74. The Seventh Circuit’s decision is consistent with this precedent.

The Village’s reliance on *City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center*, 473 U.S. 432 (1985), is misplaced. *Cleburne* addressed whether a zoning ordinance was rationally related to a legitimate state interest, not whether state action motivated by ill will violates the Equal Protection Clause. *Id.* at 446–48. The Village’s attempt to distinguish *Snowden* and *Yick Wo* on the ground that those cases involved facial discrimination is unavailing. *Snowden*, 321 U.S. at 8; *Yick Wo*, 118 U.S. at 373–74. The Equal Protection Clause forbids invidious treatment whether directed at a class or an individual. *Id.*

III. THE VILLAGE’S PROFFERED JUSTIFICATIONS FOR ITS ACTIONS ARE IRRELEVANT WHERE THE MOTIVATION WAS ILL WILL.

Where state action is motivated by ill will, the Village’s proffered justifications for its actions are irrelevant. *See Snowden*, 321 U.S. at 8 (“[T]he illegality of the discrimination does not depend upon the existence of a legitimate justification for the action.”). This Court has long held that the Equal Protection Clause forbids state action taken for reasons of personal animosity, regardless of whether the action could otherwise be justified. *See Home Tel. & Tel. Co.*, 227 U.S. at 296–97; *Yick Wo*, 118 U.S. at 373–74. The Village’s attempt to justify its actions on the ground that the easement was required for safety or drainage is beside the point where the Village’s demand was motivated by ill will. *Snowden*, 321 U.S. at 8.

IV. THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE DOES NOT REQUIRE OLECH TO ALLEGE MEMBERSHIP IN A CLASS OR GROUP.

The Equal Protection Clause does not require a plaintiff to allege membership in a class or group. *See Snowden*, 321 U.S. at 8 (“[T]he illegality of the discrimination does not depend upon the existence of a class or group which is adversely affected.”). This Court has repeatedly held that the Clause forbids invidious treatment of individuals, regardless of whether the discrimination is directed at a class. *See, e.g., Home Tel. & Tel. Co.*, 227 U.S. at 296–97; *Yick Wo*, 118 U.S. at 373–74. The Village’s reliance on cases involving facially neutral laws or discriminatory classifications is misplaced. *See, e.g., Washington v. Davis*, 426 U.S. 229 (1976); *McLaughlin v. Florida*, 379 U.S. 184 (1964). Those cases do not address the “class of one” theory of liability, which this Court has never foreclosed. *See Snowden*, 321 U.S. at 8.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Seventh Circuit’s decision should be affirmed. The Equal Protection Clause recognizes a “class of one” theory of liability where state action is motivated by ill will or arbitrariness. *Yick Wo*, 118 U.S. at 373–74; *Snowden*, 321 U.S. at 8. Olech’s complaint states such a claim, and the Village’s proffered justifications for its actions are irrelevant where the motivation was ill will. *Id.* The Equal Protection Clause does not require Olech to allege membership in a class or group. *Id.*
