**ARGUMENT**

I. **Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial**

The plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) requires the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation to remand transferred actions "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." This Court has consistently recognized that § 1407(a) is a limited grant of authority, confined to pretrial coordination. *In re Air Crash Disaster Near Chicago, Ill.,* 644 F.2d 594, 599 (7th Cir. 1981) ("Section 1407 is a procedural statute, authorizing transfer only for pretrial proceedings."); *In re Corrugated Container Antitrust Litig.,* 659 F.2d 1341, 1345 (5th Cir. 1981) ("The statute is explicit: after pretrial proceedings, the Panel 'shall' remand."). The Ninth Circuit’s contrary interpretation in *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litig.,* 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3d Cir. 1982)—relied upon below—is erroneous. As the Second Circuit acknowledged in *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord,* 447 F.2d 122, 124 (2d Cir. 1971), § 1407(a) "does not authorize the Panel to retain jurisdiction for trial."

The Panel’s own rules do not alter this statutory command. Rule 14(b) of the *Manual for Complex Litigation* permits a transferee court to invoke § 1404(a) to retain a case for trial, but this rule cannot override the clear language of the statute. *Morton v. Mancari,* 417 U.S. 535, 551 (1974) ("An administrative rule cannot enlarge the scope of a congressional grant of jurisdiction."). The Ninth Circuit’s deference to Rule 14(b) in *Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach,* 61 F.3d 911 (9th Cir. 1995), is inconsistent with this principle.

II. **Section 1404(a) Cannot Be Invoked to Defeat § 1407(a)’s Remand Requirement**

Section 1404(a) permits transfer of a case "for the convenience of parties and witnesses" to "any other district." However, this authority is not unbounded. *Van Dusen v. Barrack,* 376 U.S. 612, 638 (1964) ("Section 1404(a) does not empower a transferee court to retain a case for trial if the transferor court would have been the proper venue."). The statute does not authorize a transferee court to circumvent § 1407(a)’s mandatory remand. *In re Baldwin-United Corp. Litig.,* 770 F.2d 328, 335 (2d Cir. 1985) ("Section 1404(a) cannot be used to defeat the remand requirement of § 1407(a).").

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Wash. Pub. Utils. Group v. U.S. Dist. Court,* 843 F.2d 319 (9th Cir. 1987), is misplaced. That case involved a § 1404(a) transfer *before* any § 1407(a) coordination, not a post-pretrial attempt to retain jurisdiction. *Id.* at 326 ("The transferee court’s authority to transfer under § 1404(a) is not limited to pretrial proceedings."). Here, by contrast, the Panel already completed pretrial proceedings and ordered remand. The district court’s attempt to "retransfer" the case to itself under § 1404(a) contravenes the statute.

III. **Policy Concerns Do Not Justify Ignoring the Statutory Text**

The Ninth Circuit below suggested that efficiency justifies retaining the case in Arizona. *Lexecon,* 61 F.3d at 914. However, this Court has rejected efficiency-based arguments that conflict with statutory text. *Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Env’t,* 523 U.S. 83, 97 (1998) ("We do not lightly assume that Congress has omitted from its adopted text requirements that it nonetheless intends to apply."). The dissent in *Lexecon* correctly noted that § 1407(a) reflects a deliberate policy choice: plaintiffs retain the right to trial in their chosen forum after pretrial coordination. *Lexecon,* 61 F.3d at 916 (Kozinski, J., dissenting).

This Court should not permit judicial gloss to rewrite § 1407(a). *United States v. Ron Pair Enters., Inc.,* 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989) ("Courts are not authorized to rewrite a statute because they might deem its effects susceptible of improvement."). The Panel’s transfer authority is strictly limited to pretrial proceedings; once those conclude, remand is mandatory.

**CONCLUSION**

The district court lacked authority to invoke § 1404(a) to retain Lexecon’s case for trial. Section 1407(a) requires remand to the transferor district, and no exception permits a transferee court to override this command. This Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand with instructions to vacate the district court’s order.
