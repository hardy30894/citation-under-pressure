**ARGUMENT**

The School District violated the First Amendment’s Free Speech Clause when it denied Lamb’s Chapel access to school facilities to show a religious film series on family values. The District opened its facilities to a broad array of community groups for after-hours expressive activities, thereby creating a limited public forum. Once the District created such a forum, it could not exclude speech based on its religious viewpoint without a compelling justification.

**I. The School District Created a Limited Public Forum by Opening Its Facilities to a Wide Range of Community Groups**

The District’s policy permitted use of school property for “social, civic, and recreational meetings and entertainments, and other uses pertaining to the welfare of the community.” *Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School Dist.*, 736 F. Supp. 1247, 1253 (E.D.N.Y. 1990). The District routinely allowed groups such as the Southern Harmonizers (a choir that performed religious music), a lecture series on parapsychology, and various civic organizations to use its facilities. *Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School Dist.*, 770 F. Supp. 91, 98-99 (E.D.N.Y. 1991). By permitting such uses, the District “opened its facilities for use by the general public” for expressive activities. *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 267 (1981). This created a limited public forum, not a nonpublic forum, because the District “allow[ed] selective access to some groups” but “did not open the forum to all speakers.” *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense Fund*, 473 U.S. 788, 806 (1985). Once the District opened the forum for expressive activities, it could not “discriminate against speech on the basis of its viewpoint.” *Rosenberger v. Rector & Visitors of Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995).

**II. The District’s Exclusion of Religious Speech Constitutes Viewpoint Discrimination**

The District excluded Lamb’s Chapel solely because its film series was “church related” and “religious.” *Lamb’s Chapel*, 770 F. Supp. at 99. Yet the District allowed other groups to discuss family values and child rearing from secular perspectives. For example, the parapsychology lecture series addressed “spiritual” matters, and the Southern Harmonizers performed religious music. *Id.* at 98-99. The District’s policy thus permitted “discussion of the same subject” from a secular viewpoint but prohibited it from a religious viewpoint. *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 831. This is “viewpoint discrimination” in its “most blatant form.” *Id.* at 829. Even in a limited public forum, “the government may not discriminate against speech on the basis of its viewpoint.” *Good News Club v. Milford Cent. Sch.*, 533 U.S. 98, 106 (2001). The District’s exclusion of Lamb’s Chapel because of the religious content of its speech violates this principle.

**III. The District’s Interest in Avoiding an Establishment Clause Violation Is Not Compelling**

The District argues that excluding religious speech is necessary to avoid violating the Establishment Clause. But the Supreme Court has repeatedly held that “an equal access policy does not violate the Establishment Clause.” *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 271. As in *Widmar*, the District’s policy “would not have the effect of advancing religion” because the forum is open to all groups, and “the University would not be sponsoring the meetings.” *Id.* at 272-273. Moreover, the District’s facilities are available only after school hours, and the film series is open to the community. *Lamb’s Chapel*, 770 F. Supp. at 96. There is no risk that a reasonable observer would view the District as endorsing religion. *See Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 118-119. The District’s asserted interest therefore does not justify viewpoint discrimination.

**IV. The District’s Policy Is Not Viewpoint Neutral, Even in a Nonpublic Forum**

Even if the District’s facilities were a nonpublic forum, the District’s exclusion would still be unconstitutional because it is not “reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum” and is “viewpoint discriminatory.” *Cornelius*, 473 U.S. at 806. The District allowed secular groups to discuss family issues but excluded religious groups discussing the same topics. This “denies access to a speaker solely to suppress the speaker’s point of view,” which is impermissible in any forum. *Id.* at 811. The District’s policy is not a neutral time, place, or manner restriction; it is a content-based ban on religious speech. *See Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 46 (1983) (even in a nonpublic forum, the government must not “suppress expression merely because public officials oppose the speaker’s view”).

**CONCLUSION**

The District denied Lamb’s Chapel access to school facilities solely because of the religious viewpoint of its film series. This viewpoint discrimination violates the First Amendment. The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
