**ARGUMENT**

**I. The reinstatement statute applies to pre-1997 orders.**

The Tenth Circuit erred in concluding that § 241(a)(5) does not apply to deportation orders issued prior to the statute’s enactment. The statute is a "clean slate" provision designed to address the problem of illegal reentry. *INS v. Lopez-Mendoza*, 468 U.S. 1032, 1040 (1984), held that a prior deportation order has preclusive effect, and the reinstatement statute merely enforces that preclusion. *INS v. St. Cyr*, 533 U.S. 289, 311 (2001), confirmed that IIRIRA’s provisions, including reinstatement, apply to proceedings that began before its enactment. Because the statute is a general application of the law to all illegal reentries, it must apply to those who re-entered before 1997.

**II. The application is impermissibly retroactive.**

The Tenth Circuit’s conclusion that the statute is not retroactive because Petitioner lacked a "protectable expectation" of relief is incorrect. *INS v. St. Cyr*, 533 U.S. at 311, held that an alien has a "protectable interest" in adjustment of status. *Matter of S-P-*, 21 I&N Dec. 486, 489 (BIA 1996), established that aliens had a reasonable expectation of relief under the pre-IIRIRA regime. Because Petitioner had a vested right to apply for adjustment, the reinstatement statute retroactively alters that right. *INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca*, 480 U.S. 421, 438 (1987), requires that a statute be applied retroactively if it "impairs a vested right." *INS v. Jimenez-Arcila*, 520 U.S. 397, 410 (1997), and *INS v. Pimentel-Mendoza*, 518 U.S. 876, 878 (1996), further held that IIRIRA’s retroactive application to pre-IIRIRA orders is unconstitutional. The Tenth Circuit’s finding that Petitioner had no expectation of relief is contrary to binding Supreme Court precedent.

**III. The statute is a substantive change.**

Even if the statute is procedural, it affects substantial rights. *INS v. St. Cyr*, 533 U.S. at 311, noted that reinstatement is a procedural mechanism, but it "substantially affects" the alien’s rights. *Klein v. INS*, 313 U.S. 467, 472 (1941), held that statutes of general applicability apply retroactively unless they affect substantial rights. Here, the statute denies relief that was previously available, constituting a substantial change. *Boumediene v. Bush*, 553 U.S. 723, 742 (2008), reaffirmed that the Constitution protects a "protectable interest" in liberty. By retroactively reinstating a prior order and denying relief, the statute infringes upon Petitioner’s vested rights.
