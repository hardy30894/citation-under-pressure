**Argument**

Milford’s exclusion of the Good News Club from after-hours use of school facilities violated the Club’s free speech rights. When a school creates a limited public forum and allows community groups to use its property after hours for “social, civic and recreational meetings and entertainment events and other uses pertaining to the welfare of the community,” it may not discriminate against speech because of its religious viewpoint. *Good News Club v. Milford Central School*, 533 U.S. 98, 106-07 (2001); *Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District*, 508 U.S. 384, 390-93 (1993); *Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia*, 515 U.S. 819, 829-31 (1995). Milford’s policy was viewpoint discrimination because the Club sought to address morality and character from a Christian perspective, not to introduce a new subject outside the forum. *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 107-12; *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 831-32; *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 393-95.

The school’s reliance on the Establishment Clause cannot justify the exclusion. The Supreme Court has repeatedly held that a public school does not violate the Establishment Clause by allowing equal access to religious speech in a limited public forum after hours. *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 274-75 (1981); *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 395-97; *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 845-46. The Constitution does not permit a public entity to suppress private religious speech “in order to avoid a potential Establishment Clause violation” where the school’s policy is evenhanded and the speech is private. *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 113-19; *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 394-97.

First, Milford opened a limited public forum. A limited public forum exists when the government intentionally permits certain classes of speakers or subjects to use public property after hours. *Perry Education Assn. v. Perry Local Educators’ Assn.*, 460 U.S. 37, 45-46 (1983); *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 106-07. Milford invited community residents to use school premises for a range of nonexclusive community activities. *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 106-07. Once it did so, it was bound by the rule that restrictions must be reasonable and viewpoint neutral. *Perry Education Assn.*, 460 U.S. at 46; *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 788, 806 (1985).

Second, the exclusion turned on viewpoint, not subject matter. Milford allowed groups such as the Boy Scouts and Girl Scouts to teach morals and character development, yet denied the Good News Club because its perspective was Christian. *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 108-10. That is classic viewpoint discrimination. In *Lamb’s Chapel*, the Court held unconstitutional the denial of school access to a church that wanted to show a film on family values from a religious perspective because the school had already opened the forum to discussions of family issues. 508 U.S. at 393-94. Likewise, in *Rosenberger*, the University could not deny funding to a student publication because it expressed a religious viewpoint on otherwise permissible subjects. 515 U.S. at 829-31. Milford’s position is no different: it permitted moral instruction, but not moral instruction “from a religious standpoint.” *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 112.

Third, permitting the Club’s meetings would not violate the Establishment Clause. The Court has rejected the notion that equal access for religious speech in a limited public forum amounts to endorsement. *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 271-75; *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 395-97. The relevant inquiry is whether the speech is private, whether access is evenhanded, and whether the setting makes clear that the speech is not government-sponsored. *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 841-46; *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 113-19. Here, the Club’s meetings were after school, open to district residents, and conducted by a private organization. *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 101-03, 113-19. Under those circumstances, “there is no realistic danger” that observers would view the Club’s activities as government endorsement. *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 395.

Finally, Milford’s attempt to label the Club’s meetings “religious worship” does not alter the analysis. The Court has made clear that when private speakers use a forum to discuss morals, character, and family from a religious viewpoint, the government may not exclude them by recasting that viewpoint as a forbidden subject. *Good News Club*, 533 U.S. at 108-12; *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 831-32. To hold otherwise would permit officials to discriminate “because of disagreement with the message” and would “allow[] discrimination against speech because of its message.” *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 828-29.

For these reasons, the judgment should be reversed.
