**Argument**

I. The Due Process Clause requires a heightened standard for waivers of fundamental rights, distinct from the standard for competency to stand trial.

A waiver of counsel is a "fundamental right" that cannot be presumed. *Cole v. Arkansas, 333 U.S. 196, 203 (1948)*. The Due Process Clause mandates that a defendant's waiver of such rights be made "knowing and intelligent." *Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 462 (1938)*. This standard requires a "reasoned choice" among the alternatives available to the defendant. *Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238, 243 (1969)*. By contrast, competency to stand trial requires only that a defendant have a rational and factual understanding of the proceedings and the ability to assist counsel. *Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S. 402, 402 (1960)*; *Pate v. Robinson, 383 U.S. 375, 387 (1966)*.

II. The state court failed to apply the heightened standard, instead relying on the lower competency to stand trial standard.

The state court erroneously applied the lower competency to stand trial standard to Moran's waiver of counsel. *Holland v. United States, 348 U.S. 121, 141 (1954)*. The state court's reliance on psychiatric evaluations for competency to stand trial was insufficient to support a finding of competency to waive rights. *Specht v. Patterson, 386 U.S. 605, 610 (1967)*. The state court did not ensure Moran had the capacity for a "reasoned choice" before accepting his waiver. *United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218, 226 (1967)*.

III. The record supports a finding of incompetence under the heightened standard.

Moran was heavily medicated and suicidal at the time of his waiver. *Malloy v. Hogan, 378 U.S. 1, 7 (1964)*. The state court failed to inquire into the effects of medication on Moran's decision-making capacity. *Cole v. Arkansas, 333 U.S. at 203*. Because Moran lacked the capacity for a "reasoned choice," his waiver was involuntary and unconstitutional. *Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. at 243*.
