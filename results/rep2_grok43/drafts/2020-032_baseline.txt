**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE ELEVENTH CIRCUIT CORRECTLY HELD THAT THE CLAIMS FOR NOMINAL DAMAGES DO NOT PRESENT A LIVE CONTROVERSY ONCE THE CHALLENGED POLICIES WERE REVISED.**

A federal court lacks jurisdiction under Article III when a case becomes moot. *Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc.*, 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013). Mootness occurs when the issues presented are no longer live or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome. *Id.* (quoting *Murphy v. Hunt*, 455 U.S. 478, 481 (1982) (per curiam)). Here, Georgia Gwinnett College revised its Freedom of Expression Policy and Student Code of Conduct in February 2017, superseding the Prior Policies and eliminating the challenged restrictions. These changes rendered the requests for declaratory and injunctive relief moot, as the district court found after applying the voluntary-cessation analysis.

The remaining request for nominal damages does not preserve a live controversy. In *Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc), the court held that a prayer for nominal damages generally will not save an otherwise moot challenge to an allegedly unconstitutional policy or law. *Id.* at 1263–64. The court explained that nominal damages, standing alone, do not redress an actual injury sufficient to satisfy Article III when the underlying conduct has ceased. *Id.* at 1265. Appellants’ complaint alleged no concrete injury—such as monetary loss, physical pain, or emotional distress—beyond the abstract violation of constitutional rights. *See Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (requiring compensatory damages in § 1983 suits to rest on actual injury rather than the abstract value of the right).

Appellants’ attempt to distinguish *Flanigan’s* on the ground that this case involves an as-applied challenge fails. The complaint sought only nominal damages and contained no well-pleaded allegations of compensable harm. Under the prior-precedent rule, the panel was bound to follow *Flanigan’s* unless overruled en banc or by the Supreme Court. *United States v. Vega-Castillo*, 540 F.3d 1235, 1236 (11th Cir. 2008). The district court therefore properly dismissed the action as moot.

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT DID NOT ABUSE ITS DISCRETION IN DENYING LEAVE TO AMEND.**

A district court’s denial of leave to amend is reviewed for abuse of discretion. *Santiago v. Wood*, 904 F.2d 673, 675 (11th Cir. 1990). Requests for amendment embedded in opposition memoranda are not properly raised. *Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018). Appellants failed to file a motion under Rule 15 or to specify the substance of any proposed amendment, including factual allegations supporting compensatory damages. *Newton v. Duke Energy Fla., LLC*, 895 F.3d 1270, 1277 (11th Cir. 2018). After dismissal without prejudice, they could have sought relief under Rules 59(e) or 60(b), yet did not. *Czeremcha v. Int’l Ass’n of Machinists & Aerospace Workers*, 724 F.2d 1552, 1556 (11th Cir. 1984). The district court acted within its discretion.
