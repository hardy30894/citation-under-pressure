**ARGUMENT**

Section 1407(a) states that actions transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the Panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). This command is "clear" and "mandatory." *Anderson v. Yungkau*, 329 U.S. 482, 485 (1947) (interpreting identical "shall" language as leaving "no discretion"). Once pretrial proceedings end, the statute "requires" return to the transferor court. *See Van Dusen v. Barrack*, 376 U.S. 612, 636-37 (1964) (emphasizing that § 1407 transfers are limited to pretrial matters and do not alter the parties' ultimate forum rights).

Section 1404(a) supplies no authority for a transferee court to circumvent this directive. That provision permits transfer only "to any other district or division where it might have been brought," but it operates against the backdrop of § 1407's express limitation. *See Morton v. Mancari*, 417 U.S. 535, 550-51 (1974) ("[A] specific statute controls over a general one."). Allowing a transferee judge to invoke § 1404(a) to retain the case for trial would nullify the Panel's exclusive remand power and the statutory policy that plaintiffs retain their chosen forum once pretrial coordination concludes. *See Hoffman v. Blaski*, 363 U.S. 335, 342-43 (1960) (rejecting constructions that expand venue statutes beyond their terms).

The Judicial Panel's Rule 14(b) cannot enlarge the statute. *See Sibbach v. Wilson & Co.*, 312 U.S. 1, 10 (1941) (rules may not "abridge, enlarge or modify any substantive right"). Nor do policy considerations justify departure from the text. *See Caminetti v. United States*, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917) ("[W]hen the statute's language is plain, the sole function of the courts is to enforce it according to its terms.").

The district court's self-assignment under § 1404(a) therefore violated § 1407(a). The judgment should be reversed.
