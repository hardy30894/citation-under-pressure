I. The district court erred in granting summary judgment on Epps’s ADA and MHRA claims. Summary judgment is proper only when the record shows no genuine issue of material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322 (1986); Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986). The evidence, viewed in the light most favorable to Epps, creates a triable question whether Pine Lawn regarded him as disabled.

An individual is regarded as disabled when an employer “mistakenly believes that [the] person has a substantially limiting impairment.” Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., 527 U.S. 471, 489 (1999). Chief Hardy’s memoranda expressly cited Dr. Mirkin’s note that Epps suffered degenerative disk disease and “would very likely need to seek a different type of occupation,” together with Epps’s workers’ compensation history, to conclude that Epps “would never be able to function as a regular officer.” This evidence permits the inference that Pine Lawn perceived Epps’s impairment as substantially limiting the major life activity of working. See Murphy v. United Parcel Serv., Inc., 527 U.S. 516, 523 (1999); Toyota Motor Mfg., Ky., Inc. v. Williams, 534 U.S. 184, 197–98 (2002). Because the MHRA employs the same analytical framework, the same factual dispute precludes summary judgment on that claim. See Medley v. Valentine Radford Commc’ns, Inc., 173 S.W.3d 315, 320 (Mo. Ct. App. 2005).

II. The district court erred in holding that sovereign immunity bars Epps’s retaliation claim under Mo. Rev. Stat. § 287.780. Missouri municipalities enjoy no immunity from statutory causes of action that expressly create liability. Hansen v. City of St. Louis, 807 S.W.2d 245, 248 (Mo. Ct. App. 1991). Section 287.780 provides that an employer “shall not discharge or in any way discriminate against” an employee for filing a workers’ compensation claim. The statute supplies an independent basis for municipal liability that sovereign immunity does not override. See State ex rel. Bd. of Trs. v. Russell, 843 S.W.2d 353, 358–59 (Mo. 1992). Epps presented evidence that Hardy recommended termination immediately after reviewing Epps’s workers’ compensation file and that the Board acted on that recommendation; this satisfies the causation element. Hansome v. Nw. Cooperage Co., 679 S.W.2d 273, 275 (Mo. 1984).

III. The same statutory exception defeats immunity on the intentional-infliction claim. Missouri courts have recognized that sovereign immunity yields when a statute creates a private right of action. See Jungerman v. City of Raytown, 925 S.W.2d 202, 204 (Mo. 1996). Epps’s evidence that Pine Lawn terminated him after he pursued protected medical treatment raises a genuine issue whether the City’s conduct was extreme and outrageous. Nazeri v. Mo. Valley Coll., 860 S.W.2d 303, 316 (Mo. 1993).

The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for trial.
