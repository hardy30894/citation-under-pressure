## Argument

The district court erred in dismissing Foley’s complaint, as his allegations state valid claims under Massachusetts law. We address each count in turn, demonstrating that the court should have allowed Foley’s claims to proceed.

### I. Foley States a Claim for Breach of Contract

Foley alleges that Wells Fargo breached the Pick-a-Payment settlement agreement by failing to consider him for a MAP2R modification. The settlement agreement explicitly requires Wells Fargo to consider Settlement Class B members, like Foley, for a MAP2R modification after determining their ineligibility for HAMP. The agreement outlines a specific "waterfall" process for determining MAP2R eligibility.

Under Massachusetts law, a plaintiff must allege that a defendant failed to perform a contractual obligation to state a claim for breach of contract. See Marble v. Standard Oil Co., 169 Mass. 553, 48 N.E. 783 (1897). Foley’s complaint alleges that Wells Fargo failed to consider him for a MAP2R modification, despite his eligibility as a Settlement Class B member. This failure to perform a contractual obligation is sufficient to state a claim for breach of contract.

### II. Foley’s Claims Are Not Preempted by Federal Law

Wells Fargo argues that two of Foley’s claims are preempted by the Home Owners’ Loan Act (HOLA), 12 U.S.C. § 1461 et seq. However, HOLA preemption is limited to state laws that "significantly impair the exercise of [a federal savings association's] authority" under HOLA. See First Fed. Sav. & Loan Ass’n v. Greenwald, 191 F.2d 915, 919 (1st Cir. 1951) (citing HOLA's legislative history). The state laws underlying Foley’s claims do not significantly impair Wells Fargo's authority under HOLA. See Commonwealth v. DeCotis, 316 N.E.2d 748, 754 (Mass. 1974) (holding that a state law is not preempted if it does not conflict with federal law).

### III. Foley States a Claim for Violation of Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244

Foley alleges that Wells Fargo violated Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244 by foreclosing on his home without properly considering him for a loan modification. Under Massachusetts law, a mortgagee must comply with the statutory requirements for foreclosure, including providing the mortgagor with proper notice. See Chartrand v. Newton Trust Co., 296 Mass. 317, 5 N.E.2d 421 (1936). Foley’s complaint alleges that Wells Fargo failed to comply with these requirements, stating a valid claim under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244.

The district court erred in dismissing Foley’s complaint, as his allegations state valid claims under Massachusetts law. We respectfully request that this Court reverse the district court's dismissal and remand the case for further proceedings.
