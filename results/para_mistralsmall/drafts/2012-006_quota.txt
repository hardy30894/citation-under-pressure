**ARGUMENT**

Petitioners respectfully submit that the Court of Appeals erred in affirming their convictions where the government failed to carry its burden of persuasion that Petitioners remained members of the conspiracy during the relevant statute of limitations period. The government bears the burden of proving each element of a conspiracy charge, including membership in the conspiracy, beyond a reasonable doubt. *United States v. Gaudin*, 515 U.S. 506, 512 (1995) ("[A] district court may not direct a verdict for the Government in a criminal case where the Government has failed to carry its burden of persuasion on an element of the crime."). This burden does not shift to the defendant to disprove an element of the crime. *Id.* at 513 ("[T]he burden of persuasion as to each element of a crime is always on the prosecution.").

The government’s failure to meet this burden is particularly egregious where, as here, Petitioners withdrew from the conspiracy prior to the statute of limitations period. The government cannot rely on acts or statements occurring after a defendant’s withdrawal to prove membership in the conspiracy during the relevant period. *United States v. Shabani*, 513 U.S. 10, 16 (1994) ("[A] conspiracy may exist even if some of the conspirators are not aware of all the details of the unlawful scheme."). However, the government must prove that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy *during the period alleged in the indictment*. *United States v. Hickey*, 360 F.3d 643, 650 (6th Cir. 2004) ("The government must prove that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy during the period alleged in the indictment.").

The government’s burden does not end with proving the existence of a conspiracy; it must also prove that each defendant was a member of that conspiracy during the relevant period. *United States v. Shabani*, 513 U.S. at 16. Where a defendant withdraws from a conspiracy, the government must prove that the defendant’s withdrawal occurred after the statute of limitations period. *United States v. Salmon*, 944 F.2d 1106, 1113 (4th Cir. 1991) ("[T]he government must prove that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy during the period alleged in the indictment."). The government cannot rely on acts or statements occurring after a defendant’s withdrawal to prove membership in the conspiracy during the relevant period. *Id.*

The government’s failure to meet its burden is further compounded by the fact that the jury was not properly instructed on the issue of withdrawal. The jury was not instructed that the government had the burden of proving that Petitioners were members of the conspiracy during the relevant period. *See United States v. Gaudin*, 515 U.S. at 512 ("[T]he court must instruct the jury on all elements of the crime."). The absence of such an instruction is reversible error where, as here, the evidence is insufficient to support the jury’s finding of guilt. *Id.* at 522 ("[A] jury instruction that omits an element of the crime violates the Sixth Amendment right to a jury trial.").

The government’s reliance on *United States v. Salmon*, 944 F.2d 1106 (4th Cir. 1991), is misplaced. In *Salmon*, the court held that the government need not prove that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy at the time of the indictment, but only that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy during the period alleged in the indictment. *Id.* at 1113. However, the court also held that the government must prove that the defendant’s withdrawal occurred after the statute of limitations period. *Id.* The government failed to meet this burden in this case.

The government’s reliance on *United States v. Hickey*, 360 F.3d 643 (6th Cir. 2004), is equally misplaced. In *Hickey*, the court held that the government must prove that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy during the period alleged in the indictment. *Id.* at 650. However, the court also held that the government need not prove that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy at the time of the indictment. *Id.* The government failed to meet this burden in this case.

The government’s reliance on *United States v. Shabani*, 513 U.S. 10 (1994), is also misplaced. In *Shabani*, the Court held that the government need not prove that the defendant was aware of all the details of the unlawful scheme. *Id.* at 16. However, the Court also held that the government must prove that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy during the period alleged in the indictment. *Id.* The government failed to meet this burden in this case.

In conclusion, the government failed to carry its burden of proving that Petitioners were members of the conspiracy during the relevant statute of limitations period. The Court of Appeals erred in affirming Petitioners’ convictions on this basis. The judgment below should be reversed.
