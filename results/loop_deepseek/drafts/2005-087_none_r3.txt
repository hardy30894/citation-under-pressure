**ARGUMENT**

**I. EXPERT EVIDENCE OF MENTAL DISEASE MAY CONSTITUTIONALLY BE CONFINED TO THE INSANITY DEFENSE WITHOUT VIOLATING DUE PROCESS.**

The Due Process Clause does not require Arizona to permit a defendant to use expert evidence of mental disease to negate *mens rea* when the State has chosen to channel such evidence exclusively to the insanity defense. This Court has long recognized that "the definition of the elements of a criminal offense is a matter of state law," *Patterson v. New York*, 432 U.S. 197, 201 (1977), and that States may craft distinct rules for affirmative defenses. In *Leland v. Oregon*, 343 U.S. 790 (1952), the Court upheld Oregon's requirement that a defendant prove insanity beyond a reasonable doubt, rejecting the argument that due process compelled the State to allow the same evidence to contest specific intent. *Id.* at 795–96. The Court reasoned that "the defense of insanity is separate from the issue of specific intent," *id.* at 795, and that Oregon could place the burden of proving insanity on the defendant without shifting the prosecution's burden on intent.

Arizona has likewise defined the mental element of first-degree murder as a cognitive capacity distinct from the abnormal mental condition that grounds an insanity plea.
