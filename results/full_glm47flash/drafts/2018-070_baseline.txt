**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE OCSLA REQUIRES STATE LAW TO APPLY ONLY TO FILL GAPS IN FEDERAL LAW**

The Ninth Circuit erred by interpreting the OCSLA to allow state law to apply whenever it is “applicable” and “not inconsistent” with federal law, regardless of whether federal law is comprehensive. The correct interpretation, established by the Supreme Court and the Fifth Circuit, requires state law to apply only to fill gaps in federal law.

The Supreme Court in *Rodrigue v. Aetna Casualty & Surety Co.* held that the OCSLA incorporates state law “to the extent that they are applicable and not inconsistent with this subchapter.” 395 U.S. 352, 355 (1969). The Court explained that the OCSLA was designed to supplement federal law where it was inadequate, not to supplant it where it was comprehensive. *Id.* at 355–56. This principle was further articulated by the Fifth Circuit in *Continental Oil Co. v. London S.S. Owners’ Mut. Ins. Ass’n*, which established that state law is adopted as surrogate federal law “to the extent that it is necessary to fill a significant void or gap in federal law.” 417 F.2d 1030, 1036 (5th Cir. 1969). This “gap filling” test has been consistently applied by the Fifth Circuit. *See* *Union Texas Petroleum Corp. v. PLT Engineering, Inc.*, 895 F.2d 1043, 1050 (5th Cir. 1990).

**II. THE FLSA IS A COMPREHENSIVE STATUTORY SCHEME THAT LEAVES NO GAPS FOR STATE WAGE AND HOUR LAWS**

The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) provides a comprehensive framework for wage and hour regulation on the Outer Continental Shelf. *Shell Oil Co. v. Iowa Dep’t of Revenue*, 488 U.S. 19, 26 (1988). The Supreme Court noted that Congress intended the OCSLA to provide a body of law for the seabed, but federal law was intended to be the primary source. *Id.* at 26–27. Because the FLSA is comprehensive, there is no “significant void or gap” for state law to fill. *Continental Oil*, 417 F.2d at 1036. The FLSA’s savings clause allows for more protective state laws, but only where the federal scheme is silent or incomplete. *See* *Breton Energy, L.L.C. v. Mariner Energy Res., Inc.*, 764 F.3d 394, 398 (5th Cir. 2014). As the district court correctly found, the FLSA leaves no room for state law to address wage and hour grievances arising on the OCS. Therefore, California’s wage and hour laws do not apply.
