ARGUMENT

The exhaustion requirement, rooted in principles of comity and federalism, bars a state prisoner from obtaining federal habeas relief on any claim not first presented to the state courts in accordance with state procedural rules. Ex parte Royall, 117 U.S. 241, 251 (1886); New York v. Eno, 155 U.S. 89, 92-93 (1894). This doctrine demands that a petitioner "exhaust the remedies available in the courts of the State." Ex parte Hawk, 321 U.S. 114, 116 (1944). A claim omitted from a direct appeal or post-conviction proceeding is therefore defaulted and unavailable as a ground for relief. Darr v. Burford, 339 U.S. 200, 204 (1950).

A procedurally defaulted assertion of ineffective assistance of appellate counsel cannot supply the cause necessary to overcome the default of a separate claim. Brown v. Allen, 344 U.S. 443, 486-87 (1953). The requirement that all available state remedies be pursued applies with equal force to every constitutional claim a prisoner seeks to raise. Fay v. Noia, 372 U.S. 391, 419-20 (1963). Permitting one defaulted claim to revive another would nullify the independent and adequate state ground doctrine that has long governed federal review of state convictions. Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293, 317-18 (1963).

The Sixth Amendment right to counsel, made applicable to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment, does not create an exception to these exhaustion rules. Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 467-68 (1938). A prisoner who fails to present an ineffective-assistance claim in the manner prescribed by state law has not preserved any federal right to use that claim as cause. Mooney v. Holohan, 294 U.S. 103, 115 (1935). State procedural requirements remain binding unless the prisoner demonstrates both cause and prejudice arising from a properly exhausted federal claim. Frank v. Mangum, 237 U.S. 309, 329-30 (1915).

Because Carpenter's ineffective-assistance claim was itself dismissed by the Ohio courts as untimely, it supplies no basis for excusing the separate default of his sufficiency-of-the-evidence claim. The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
