**ARGUMENT**

The Railroad Retirement Act, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), authorizes judicial review of “a final decision of the Board.” A Board order denying reopening satisfies that statutory text. The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding rests on an erroneous extension of Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977).

Section 355(f) contains no “after a hearing” limitation. Califano construed the Social Security Act’s narrower phrase—“final decision of the Secretary made after a hearing”—to exclude reopening denials because those denials “may be denied without a hearing” and because allowing review “would frustrate the congressional purpose . . . to impose a 60-day limitation upon judicial review of the Secretary’s final decision on the initial claim.” 430 U.S. at 108. The Railroad Retirement Act lacks both the hearing qualifier and the 60-day bar. The textual differences preclude importing Califano’s construction.

Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala, 525 U.S. 449 (1999), confirms that reopening regulations are creatures of agency discretion, not statutory command. That case held only that an intermediary’s refusal to reopen was unreviewable by the Provider Reimbursement Review Board under Medicare’s distinct scheme; it did not address circuit-court jurisdiction under a statute phrased like § 355(f). Henderson v. Shinseki, 562 U.S. 428 (2011), further instructs that filing deadlines and review limitations are claim-processing rules unless Congress “clearly states” they are jurisdictional. Section 355(f) contains no such clear statement.

The Board’s denial of Salinas’s reopening request therefore constitutes a final decision subject to review under § 355(f). The petition should be reinstated for consideration on the merits.
