**ARGUMENT**

**I.  The Government Need Not Prove the Predicate Offense Beyond a Reasonable Doubt**

The Ninth Circuit erred in applying *United States v. Ant* to 18 U.S.C. § 117(a). Under *Nichols v. United States*, the government may use an uncounseled conviction to establish a predicate offense in a recidivist statute by proving the prior conviction by a preponderance of the evidence. *Nichols v. United States*, 511 U.S. 738, 746-47 (1994).

Section 117(a) is a recidivist statute that "penal-iz[e] only the last offense committed by the defendant." *United States v. Rodriquez*, 553 U.S. 377, 386 (2008). Because the statute punishes the final act rather than the defendant’s status as a recidivist, the predicate offense requirement functions as a sentencing factor rather than an element of the crime. *Id.* Consequently, the government is not required to prove the prior tribal court convictions beyond a reasonable doubt. *Nichols*, 511 U.S. at 748. Instead, the government need only prove the existence of the prior convictions by a preponderance of the evidence. *Id.* This standard is consistent with the reasoning of the Tenth and Eighth Circuits, which have held that uncounseled tribal court convictions may be used as predicates under *Nichols*. *United States v. Shavanaux*, 647 F.3d 993, 997-98 (10th Cir. 2011); *United States v. Cavanaugh*, 643 F.3d 592, 595 (8th Cir. 2011).

**II.  Tribal Court Convictions Are Presumptively Reliable Under the Indian Civil Rights Act**

The Sixth Amendment does not apply to tribal court proceedings. *United States v. Wheeler*, 435 U.S. 313, 323-24 (1978). As the Supreme Court has recognized, the federal government has plenary power to legislate regarding Indian tribes. *United States v. Lara*, 541 U.S. 193, 200 (2004). In enacting § 117(a), Congress exercised this power to address the unique problem of domestic violence on tribal lands. *Id.* The Indian Civil Rights Act (“ICRA”) governs the procedures in tribal courts, and tribal convictions are presumptively valid so long as they comply with ICRA. *United States v. Ant*, 882 F.2d 1389, 1395 (9th Cir. 1989) (O’Scannlain, J., dissenting). Federal courts must respect tribal jurisprudence and avoid unnecessary judicial paternalism. *Wilson v. Marchington*, 127 F.3d 805, 810 (9th Cir. 1997).

**III.  *Ant* Is Inconsistent with Supreme Court Precedent**

The Ninth Circuit’s decision in *Ant* is inconsistent with *Nichols* and *Rodriquez*. *Ant* created a bright-line rule that uncoupled the validity of a conviction from the validity of the sentence, a rule that has no basis in Supreme Court jurisprudence. *United States v. Acuna-Reyna*, 677 F.3d 1282, 1284-85 (11th Cir. 2012). In *Alabama v. Shelton*, the Supreme Court held that a prison sentence may be invalid, but the underlying misdemeanor conviction remains valid. *Alabama v. Shelton*, 535 U.S. 654, 661-62 (2002). *Ant* improperly extended the Sixth Amendment’s right to counsel to tribal court proceedings, a violation of tribal sovereignty. *Ant*, 882 F.2d at 1397. The government respectfully requests this Court reverse the Ninth Circuit and reinstate the conviction.
