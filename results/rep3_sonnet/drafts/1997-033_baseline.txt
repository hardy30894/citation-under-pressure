# ARGUMENT

## SECTION 1407(a) UNAMBIGUOUSLY REQUIRES REMAND OF TRANSFERRED ACTIONS BEFORE TRIAL, AND SECTION 1404(a) CANNOT BE USED TO CIRCUMVENT THAT MANDATE

### A. The Plain Text of Section 1407(a) Commands Remand and Admits No Exception

The starting point for statutory interpretation is the text itself. *Connecticut Nat'l Bank v. Germain*, 503 U.S. 249, 253-54 (1992) ("courts must presume that a legislature says in a statute what it means and means in a statute what it says there"). Section 1407(a) provides that any action transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). This Court has repeatedly held that "shall" is mandatory, not permissive. *Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach*, 523 U.S. 26, 35 (1998) (recognizing that "the word 'shall' generally indicates a command that admits of no discretion on the part of the person instructed to carry out the directive"). Congress placed remand authority exclusively in the Panel, and conditioned it only on the conclusion of pretrial proceedings—not on the transferee court's convenience or its unilateral decision to invoke another statute.

Judge Kozinski's dissent below correctly recognized that this statutory command is unambiguous: "It's hard to imagine a clearer statutory command, or one more widely ignored." *Lexecon Inc. v. United States Dist. Court*, 61 F.3d 911 (9th Cir. 1995) (Kozinski, J., dissenting). As he explained, "there is nothing in the language of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) or § 1404(a) which directly allows, or even suggests, that the transferee judge has the power to transfer cases to his district, or any district, for purposes of trial," quoting *In re Air Crash Disaster Near Hanover, New Hampshire*, 342 F. Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H. 1971).

### B. Section 1404(a) Cannot Override the Specific Mandate of Section 1407(a)

Respondents' position—that a transferee court may invoke § 1404(a) to transfer a case to itself, thereby avoiding § 1407(a)'s remand requirement—cannot be squared with settled principles of statutory construction. Where a specific statutory provision conflicts with a general one, the specific provision governs. *Morton v. Mancari*, 417 U.S. 535, 550-51 (1974) ("Where there is no clear intention otherwise, a specific statute will not be controlled or nullified by a general one."). Section 1407(a) speaks specifically and directly to the disposition of multidistrict litigation cases, mandating remand upon conclusion of pretrial proceedings. Section 1404(a), by contrast, is a general venue-transfer provision applicable to ordinary civil litigation. The specific command of § 1407(a) must control.

Moreover, this Court has cautioned against interpretations that would render statutory language "insignificant, if not wholly superfluous." *TVA v. Hill*, 437 U.S. 153, 173 (1978). If a transferee court could simply invoke § 1404(a) to transfer a case to itself whenever it wished to retain a case for trial, the remand mandate of § 1407(a) would become a nullity—triggered only when a transferee judge declined to exercise self-serving transfer authority. Such a reading cannot be reconciled with Congress's use of mandatory language.

### C. The Ninth Circuit's "Clear Erroneous" Standard Improperly Deferred to an Unlawful Practice

Although mandamus review below applied a deferential "clearly erroneous" standard, *Bauman v. United States Dist. Court*, 557 F.2d 650, 653 (9th Cir. 1977), the panel majority itself acknowledged that "upon review on direct appeal, we might well reach the opposite result." *Lexecon*, 61 F.3d 911. The majority's reluctance stemmed not from confidence in the transferee court's statutory authority, but from the proliferation of contrary authority beginning with *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971), and codified in Multidistrict Litigation Rule 14(b). But an erroneous practice, however longstanding, does not acquire the force of law merely through repetition. As Judge Kozinski observed, "[m]ost have simply taken their place at the head of an ever-growing string cite," and "[s]eldom have so many been so consistently wrong." *Id.* (Kozinski, J., dissenting).

The *Pfizer* line of cases justified departure from the statutory text on policy grounds—chiefly judicial efficiency and the supposed absurdity of delaying venue transfer motions until after remand. *Pfizer*, 447 F.2d at 124-25. But policy preferences, however sensible in the abstract, cannot override the plain text of a validly enacted statute. *TVA v. Hill*, 437 U.S. at 194-95 ("Congress has spoken in the plainest of words... under these circumstances a court has no power to sanction a violation of federal law even upon a showing of equitable considerations."). Congress made a considered choice: transferee courts receive cases only for coordinated pretrial proceedings, and the "ancient right" of plaintiffs to select their forum for trial remains undisturbed except through the ordinary operation of § 1404(a) in the *original* transferor court. *Lexecon*, 61 F.3d 911 (Kozinski, J., dissenting).

### D. Practical Consequences Confirm the Correctness of This Reading

The facts of this case illustrate precisely the abuse Congress sought to prevent. Petitioner was transferred to Arizona solely for coordinated pretrial proceedings in the underlying class action. Once those claims were dismissed and Petitioner's own suit against Respondents was later swept into the same MDL proceeding, Petitioner "participated in no common proceedings" justifying continued presence in Arizona, and "benefitted from no coordinated discovery" relevant to its separate claims. *Lexecon*, 61 F.3d 911 (Kozinski, J., dissenting). Nonetheless, the district court invoked § 1404(a) to transfer the case to itself for trial—converting a temporary pretrial assignment into a permanent appropriation of the case, in direct contravention of § 1407(a)'s remand mandate.

This Court should hold that § 1407(a)'s remand requirement is mandatory and cannot be avoided through resort to § 1404(a) by the very court to which the case was transferred for pretrial purposes only. The judgment below should be reversed.
