**ARGUMENT**

This case is moot. Although petitioners sought nominal damages, that request cannot revive an otherwise dead controversy when the challenged policies have been rescinded, the sole plaintiff with standing to seek prospective relief has graduated, and petitioners suffered no actual, compensable injury. The Eleventh Circuit correctly affirmed dismissal.

**I. The Policy Revisions and Uzuegbunam’s Graduation Eliminated Any Ongoing Controversy**

A case becomes moot “when the issues presented are no longer ‘live’ or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome.” *Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc.*, 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013). Here, Georgia Gwinnett College rescinded both challenged policies before the district court ruled, implementing a new Freedom of Expression Policy that allows students to speak anywhere on campus without a permit. Petitioners do not contest that this change was “unambiguous” and that there is “no reasonable basis to expect” a return to the old rules. App. 7. Voluntary cessation of challenged conduct moots a case unless the defendant “could reasonably be expected to revert to its old ways.” *United States v. W.T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. 629, 632 (1953). Petitioners have never alleged—and cannot allege—any likelihood of reversion. Equally dispositive, Uzuegbunam’s graduation eliminated any claim for prospective relief. *DeFunis v. Odegaard*, 416 U.S. 312, 319-20 (1974) (holding challenge to law school admissions moot after plaintiff graduated). Because no live controversy remains, the case must be dismissed.

**II. A Claim for Nominal Damages Does Not Salvage an Otherwise Moot Challenge**

Petitioners’ sole remaining prayer is for nominal damages. But “a prayer for nominal damages generally will not save an otherwise moot challenge to an allegedly unconstitutional policy or law.” *Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248, 1268 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc). This principle follows from the bedrock requirement that federal courts decide only “actual cases or controversies.” *Already*, 568 U.S. at 90. When a challenged policy has been rescinded and no continuing violation exists, a request for nominal damages—which merely vindicates a “technical” right—does not create a live dispute. *See Carey v. Piphus*, 435 U.S. 247, 266-67 (1978) (nominal damages are “a small amount of money” awarded without proof of actual injury). Indeed, in *City of Erie v. Pap’s A.M.*, 529 U.S. 277, 288 (2000), this Court held that a case becomes moot when “the challenged conduct has ceased and there is no reasonable expectation that it will recur.” That standard is satisfied here.

Petitioners rely on *Powell v. McCormack*, 395 U.S. 486 (1969), but *Powell* is distinguishable. There, the plaintiff sought back pay—compensatory damages—for a past wrong, and this Court held that such a claim prevented mootness. *Id.* at 496-500. By contrast, petitioners consistently stated that they sought only nominal damages. Their belated efforts to characterize their request as compensatory are unavailing because they never alleged any actual injury: no monetary loss, no physical harm, no emotional distress. *See Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (compensatory damages under §1983 require proof of actual injury). Absent a well-pled claim for compensatory damages, nominal damages alone cannot sustain a case after the underlying policy has been eliminated.

**III. The First Amendment Does Not Immunize This Claim from Mootness**

To be sure, public university students enjoy First Amendment protections. *Healy v. James*, 408 U.S. 169, 180 (1972) (“state colleges and universities are not enclaves immune from the sweep of the First Amendment”). But that principle does not override Article III’s case-or-controversy requirement. Once the challenged policy is gone, and there is no live dispute over its application, federal courts lack jurisdiction regardless of the constitutional right asserted. *Already*, 568 U.S. at 91. The policy revisions here were a complete and unambiguous repudiation of the old rules, mooting the challenge. Petitioners had every opportunity to file a procedurally proper motion to amend their complaint to add compensatory damages; they failed to do so. The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying leave to amend when petitioners merely embedded a request in a response brief, *Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018), and did not specify what new factual allegations they would include, *Newton v. Duke Energy Fla., LLC*, 895 F.3d 1270, 1277 (11th Cir. 2018).

**CONCLUSION**

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
