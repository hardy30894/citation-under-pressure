The School District’s denial of Lamb’s Chapel’s applications to exhibit films addressing family values and child-rearing from a religious perspective violated the First Amendment. The facilities constitute a limited public forum. Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983). In such a forum, “the State may reserve the forum for its intended purposes, communicative or otherwise,” but “may not exclude speech where its distinction is not reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum” and may not “discriminate against speech on the basis of its viewpoint.” Id. at 46, 49 (internal quotation marks omitted); see Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educ. Fund, Inc., 473 U.S. 788, 806 (1985).

The District permitted after-hours use of its facilities by numerous community organizations for lectures, performances, and discussions on topics including family life, psychology, and social issues. These uses included musical performances containing religious elements and lectures addressing parapsychology and metaphysics. The District nevertheless rejected Lamb’s Chapel’s applications solely because the proposed films presented the same subject matter “from a Christian perspective.” This exclusion turns on the speaker’s viewpoint, not on any neutral characteristic of the speech or any incompatibility with the forum’s purposes. See Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263, 277 (1981) (holding that a university could not “discriminate against groups based on the religious content of their speech” once it had created a forum open to student groups).

The District’s reliance on New York Education Law § 414 and Local Rule 7 does not render the restriction viewpoint-neutral. Those provisions bar religious uses while permitting secular speech on identical subjects. Such a line “reflects an impermissible discrimination” against religious perspectives. See Board of Educ. of the Westside Community Schs. v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, 250 (1990) (plurality opinion) (noting that once a limited forum exists, equal access cannot be denied on the basis of religious content). The District’s asserted interest in avoiding an Establishment Clause violation supplies no justification, because “the Establishment Clause does not require” the exclusion of religious speech from a forum otherwise open to discussion of the same topics. Widmar, 454 U.S. at 271.

Because the denial rested on the religious character of Lamb’s Chapel’s message rather than any reasonable, viewpoint-neutral criterion, it abridged the First Amendment guarantee of freedom of speech.
