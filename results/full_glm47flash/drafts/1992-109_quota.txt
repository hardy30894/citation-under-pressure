**Argument**

I. The Due Process Clause requires a heightened "reasoned choice" standard to determine competency to waive counsel or plead guilty, which is distinct from the "competency to stand trial" standard.

In *Boykin v. Alabama*, 395 U.S. 238, 243 (1969), the Supreme Court held that a waiver of fundamental rights must be "knowing, intelligent, and voluntary." This requirement extends to the decision to waive counsel or plead guilty. *See United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218, 226 (1967) (waiver of counsel must be knowing and intelligent). A defendant must possess the capacity for "reasoned choice" among the alternatives available to him. *Holland v. United States*, 448 U.S. 209, 223 (1980). This is a higher level of mental functioning than that required for competency to stand trial.

II. The "reasoned choice" standard requires a higher level of mental functioning than the "competency to stand trial" standard.

By contrast, competency to stand trial requires only that a defendant has a "rational and factual understanding" of the proceedings and is capable of assisting counsel. *Dusky v. United States*, 362 U.S. 402, 402 (1960); *Drope v. Missouri*, 420 U.S. 162, 172 (1975); *Pate v. Robinson*, 383 U.S. 375, 387 (1966). *Parham v. J.R.*, 442 U.S. 584, 602 (1979) (defining the standard as "rational and factual understanding"). This standard focuses on the defendant's ability to understand the nature of the charges and assist in his defense, not on his ability to make complex strategic choices.

III. The state court erred by applying the lower standard to Moran's case.

Moran was heavily medicated with four different drugs, including Inderal and Dilantin, at the time of his plea. *See* *Holland*, 448 U.S. at 223 (waiver must be knowing and intelligent). His responses were monosyllabic, and he sought to waive counsel solely to prevent the introduction of mitigating evidence. *See* *Faretta v. California*, 422 U.S. 806, 835 (1975) (right to self-representation requires understanding the risks). The record does not support a finding that Moran was capable of the reasoned choice required for a valid waiver.
