ARGUMENT

The Board erred by extending the “reasonable examiner” standard from *Harari I* to evaluate incorporation by reference in the intervening applications. That standard was expressly limited to the “initial filing stage” of the *’880 application*—where the applicant simultaneously filed a transmittal sheet and preliminary amendment clarifying an ambiguous incorporation statement. *Harari v. Harari*, 602 F.3d 1348, 1352-53 (Fed. Cir. 2010). The Board’s application of that same standard to the *’838* and *’768* applications, which were filed years earlier without any contemporaneous clarifying documents, contravenes this Court’s mandate and well-settled precedent requiring that incorporation by reference be “clear and unambiguous” from the face of the application itself. *Zenon Environmental, Inc. v. U.S. Filter Corp.*, 506 F.3d 1370, 1378 (Fed. Cir. 2007); *In re de Blauwe*, 736 F.2d 699, 701 (Fed. Cir. 1984).

**A. The Board Misapplied *Harari I*’s Narrow Holding**

In *Harari I*, this Court held that an ambiguous incorporation statement in the *’880 application* could be clarified by the transmittal sheet and preliminary amendment filed *simultaneously* with that application. 602 F.3d at 1353-54. The Court emphasized that its holding was “narrow” and turned on the unique circumstances of the “initial filing stage.” *Id.* at 1352. The Board, however, ignored that limitation and applied the same “reasonable examiner” standard to the *’838* and *’768* applications—applications that were filed *without* any transmittal sheet or preliminary amendment identifying the *’579* application. The only incorporation language in those applications was the copied statement from the *’566* application: “filed on the same day as the present application.” J.A. 1204. That language is ambiguous because it does not identify the *’579* application by serial number, filing date, or title. A reasonable examiner reviewing the *’838* or *’768* application alone would have no way to determine which “copending” application was being incorporated. The Board’s reliance on *Harari I* to cure this deficiency was error.

**B. Incorporation by Reference Requires Clear Identification on the Face of the Application**

This Court has consistently held that incorporation by reference must be “clear and unambiguous” to satisfy the written description requirement of 35 U.S.C. § 112, first paragraph. *Zenon*, 506 F.3d at 1378. The purpose of this requirement is to ensure that the public and the Patent Office can ascertain the full disclosure of the application without resort to extrinsic evidence. *See In re de Blauwe*, 736 F.2d at 701. Here, the *’838* and *’768* applications merely state that “two copending U.S. patent applications” are incorporated, one of which is identified only as “filed on the same day as the present application.” That is not a clear identification. Unlike the *’880 application*, where the applicant simultaneously filed a preliminary amendment specifying the serial number and filing date of the *’579* application, no such clarifying document accompanied the *’838* or *’768* applications. The Board therefore erred in finding that those applications “sufficiently incorporated” the *’579* application.

**C. The Board’s Decision Undermines the Statutory Continuity Requirement**

Under 35 U.S.C. § 120, an application is entitled to the benefit of an earlier filing date only if the earlier application “discloses the invention in the manner provided by the first paragraph of section 112.” That disclosure must be present in each application in the priority chain. *See In re Hogan*, 559 F.2d 595, 604 (CCPA 1977). Because the *’838* and *’768* applications did not clearly incorporate the *’579* application, they cannot serve as a bridge to the *’566* application’s filing date. The Board’s contrary conclusion effectively allows an applicant to cure an ambiguous incorporation statement years later by filing a clarifying amendment in a later application—a result that violates the prohibition against new matter in 35 U.S.C. § 132. *See In re Rasmussen*, 650 F.2d 1212, 1214 (CCPA 1981) (new matter cannot be added after filing).

**D. The Board’s Decision Should Be Reversed**

The Board’s judgment that Harari’s *’880 application* is entitled to the benefit of the *’566* application’s filing date must be reversed. The *’838* and *’768* applications did not clearly incorporate the *’579* application by reference. Consequently, the priority chain is broken, and the *’880 application* is entitled only to the filing date of the *’708 application* (December 20, 1996). Because Hollmer’s *’601 patent* has a conception date prior to that date, the Board should have entered judgment in favor of Hollmer. This Court should reverse and remand with instructions to award priority to Hollmer.
