ARGUMENT

The Civil Service Reform Act channels judicial review of MSPB decisions in “mixed cases” to district court. 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2). A mixed case exists when an employee “has been affected by an action which [she] may appeal to the Merit Systems Protection Board” and “alleges that a basis for the action was discrimination” prohibited by enumerated statutes. 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1). Kloeckner v. Solis, 568 U.S. 41, 133 S. Ct. 596, 184 L. Ed. 2d 433 (2012), holds that this forum rule applies whenever the Board dismisses a mixed-case appeal without reaching the merits, whether the ground is procedural or jurisdictional.

Kloeckner’s reasoning turns on the statutory definition of a mixed case, not on the Board’s ultimate disposition. The Court explained that “mixed cases—those appealable to the MSPB and alleging discrimination—shall be filed in district court.” Id. at 604. Nothing in § 7702(a)(1) or § 7703(b)(2) conditions district-court review on the Board first confirming its own jurisdiction. Once an employee presents an adverse action within the Board’s potential reach and alleges discrimination, the case is mixed and review lies in district court.

Powell v. Department of Defense, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), cannot survive this holding. Powell treated a jurisdictional dismissal as removing the case from the mixed-case track. Kloeckner rejected precisely that premise for procedural dismissals, and its logic applies equally to jurisdictional ones. Both types of dismissal occur before the Board reaches the merits of the discrimination claim; both leave the employee with an unresolved allegation of discrimination tied to an action the Board could have reviewed had it found jurisdiction or timeliness. The statute supplies no textual basis for distinguishing the two.

The Federal Circuit’s contrary view in Conforto v. Merit Systems Protection Board, 713 F.3d 1111 (Fed. Cir. 2013), rests on the same distinction Kloeckner discarded. Uniform interpretation of the Board’s jurisdictional boundaries is a policy concern, not a statutory command that overrides the plain text of §§ 7702 and 7703. See Kloeckner, 568 U.S. at 607.

Accordingly, the petition for review should have been heard in district court.
