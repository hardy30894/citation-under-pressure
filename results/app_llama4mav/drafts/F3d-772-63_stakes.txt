ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DISMISSING FOLEY'S CLAIMS UNDER MASS. GEN. LAWS CH. 93A AND CH. 244.

The district court improperly dismissed Foley’s claims under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A and ch. 244 for failure to state a claim. To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain sufficient factual matter to state a claim that is plausible on its face. Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009). The court must accept as true all well-pleaded facts and draw all reasonable inferences in the plaintiff's favor. Medina-Velázquez v. Hernández-Gregorat, 767 F.3d 103, 108 (1st Cir. 2014).

Foley’s complaint alleges that Wells Fargo failed to consider him for a MAP2R modification as required by the Pick-a-Payment settlement agreement, and instead proceeded with foreclosure. This failure to consider Foley for a modification constitutes a violation of Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A, which prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in trade or commerce. See Commonwealth v. Fremont Inv. & Loan, 452 Mass. 476, 489-90 (2008) (holding that a lender's failure to consider a borrower's ability to repay a loan was an unfair practice under ch. 93A). Moreover, Wells Fargo's failure to provide Foley with a clear, written explanation of his modification denial, as required by the settlement agreement, is a further violation of ch. 93A. See id. at 490.

Foley’s claim under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244, § 35A, also survives dismissal. Section 35A requires a mortgagee to provide a borrower with a right to cure a default before accelerating the mortgage. The complaint alleges that Wells Fargo failed to provide Foley with the required right to cure, and instead proceeded with foreclosure. See U.S. Bank Nat’l Ass’n v. Schumacher, 467 Mass. 421, 429-30 (2014) (holding that a mortgagee's failure to provide a borrower with a right to cure under § 35A renders a foreclosure sale void).

II. FOLEY'S CLAIMS ARE NOT PREEMPTED BY FEDERAL LAW.

Wells Fargo argues that Foley’s claims under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A and ch. 244 are preempted by the Home Owners’ Loan Act (HOLA), 12 U.S.C. § 1461 et seq. However, HOLA preemption is limited to state laws that "significantly impair the exercise of [a federal savings association's] authority" under HOLA. 12 C.F.R. § 560.2(b). The First Circuit has held that HOLA preemption is not applicable to claims that are "incidental" to the lending process. See In re Citigroup, Inc., 699 F.3d 78, 84-85 (1st Cir. 2012). Foley's claims, which arise from Wells Fargo's failure to consider him for a modification and its foreclosure practices, are incidental to the lending process and therefore not preempted by HOLA.

The district court's dismissal of Foley’s claims was improper, and this Court should reverse and remand for further proceedings.
