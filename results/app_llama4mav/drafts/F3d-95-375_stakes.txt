The district court erred in striking the testimony of four of Plaintiffs' expert witnesses as a sanction for failing to comply with the court's discovery orders. The court's decision to strike the experts was too harsh a sanction, given the circumstances of the case. Under the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, a district court has the authority to impose sanctions for failure to comply with discovery orders, but the sanction must be proportionate to the offense. See National Hockey League v. Metropolitan Hockey Club, Inc., 427 U.S. 639, 642-43 (1976) (per curiam). The court's warning to Plaintiffs on June 3, 1993, that noncompliant experts would be struck, while stern, did not justify the extreme sanction ultimately imposed.

Furthermore, the district court's decision to strike the experts without considering lesser sanctions was an abuse of discretion. See Batson v. Neal Spelce Associates, Inc., 765 F.2d 511, 516 (5th Cir. 1985). The court should have considered alternative sanctions, such as a monetary fine or a continuance, before striking the experts. The court's failure to do so was an abuse of discretion.

The district court also erred in finding the testimony of Dr. Schroeder and Dr. Watson inadmissible under Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, 509 U.S. 579 (1993). The court's application of Daubert was overly restrictive, and it failed to properly consider the relevance and reliability of the experts' testimony. Under Daubert, the trial judge acts as a gatekeeper, ensuring that expert testimony is both relevant and reliable. Id. at 589. However, the court's gatekeeping function does not permit it to exclude expert testimony simply because it is not convincing. See id. at 596. The court's decision to exclude Dr. Schroeder and Dr. Watson's testimony was not based on a thorough analysis of the experts' methodologies and the relevance of their opinions to the case at hand.

The district court's grant of summary judgment to Defendants was also improper, as it was based on the erroneous exclusion of Plaintiffs' expert testimony. Without the testimony of the struck experts, Plaintiffs were unable to establish a genuine issue of material fact, and summary judgment was granted. However, if the expert testimony is reinstated, it is clear that a genuine issue of material fact exists, and summary judgment is not warranted. See Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986).

Finally, Appellants' notice of appeal, filed on October 19, 1994, was effective, even though the district court did not enter a final order until March 1, 1995. Under Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 4(a)(2), a premature notice of appeal is effective if it is filed after the district court has announced its decision, but before the entry of judgment. See FirsTier Mortgage Co. v. Investors Mortgage Ins. Co., 498 U.S. 269, 273-74 (1991). Therefore, Appellants' notice of appeal was timely, and this Court has jurisdiction to hear the appeal.
