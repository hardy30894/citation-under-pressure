**ARGUMENT**

The District Court correctly concluded that the failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal under Rule 32(a)(2) did not entitle him to habeas relief. A Rule 32 violation is not structural error but is subject to harmless-error review, and the record here establishes that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal and chose not to pursue one. This Court should affirm.

**I. A Rule 32(a)(2) violation is subject to harmless-error review.**

Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) requires the district court to advise a defendant of his right to appeal. The Courts of Appeals have uniformly treated violations of Rule 32 as subject to harmless-error review. *See United States v. Dansker*, 537 F.2d 40, 58 (3d Cir. 1976) (en banc) (Rule 32 violation is not structural error and is subject to harmless-error analysis); *United States v. Rosales*, 484 F.2d 1301, 1303 (9th Cir. 1973) (same); *United States v. Morgan*, 472 F.2d 510, 512 (5th Cir. 1973) (same). This Court has long recognized that harmless-error review applies to procedural violations that do not implicate fundamental rights. *See Kotteakos v. United States*, 328 U.S. 750, 764-65 (1946) (holding that errors not affecting substantial rights are harmless). Because Rule 32(a)(2) is a procedural rule designed to ensure informed decision-making rather than a constitutional guarantee, its violation is not immune from harmless-error review. *See Dansker*, 537 F.2d at 58.

**II. The record establishes that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal and elected not to pursue one.**

The District Court found, after an evidentiary hearing, that Peguero knew of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing. This factual finding is supported by the record and is entitled to deference. *See United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 333 U.S. 364, 395 (1948) (factual findings in habeas proceedings are entitled to deference unless clearly erroneous). Peguero’s awareness of his right to appeal defeats any claim that the Rule 32(a)(2) violation prejudiced him. *See Morgan*, 472 F.2d at 512 (holding that a defendant’s knowledge of his right to appeal cures any Rule 32 violation).

Moreover, the purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that a defendant is informed of his right to appeal so that he may make an intelligent decision about whether to pursue one. *See Dansker*, 537 F.2d at 58. Here, the District Court’s failure to advise Peguero did not deprive him of the opportunity to appeal; it merely failed to remind him of a right he already possessed. Because Peguero was aware of his right and chose not to appeal, the Rule 32 violation was harmless. *See Morgan*, 472 F.2d at 512.

**III. The harmless-error standard applies even where the defendant’s knowledge is established after the fact.**

The Courts of Appeals have applied harmless-error review to Rule 32 violations even where the defendant’s knowledge of his right to appeal is established post-sentencing. *See Rosales*, 484 F.2d at 1303 (applying harmless-error review where the defendant’s knowledge was established at an evidentiary hearing). This approach is consistent with the principle that harmless-error review focuses on whether the error affected the defendant’s substantial rights, not the timing of that determination. *See Kotteakos*, 328 U.S. at 764-65.

Here, the District Court’s factual finding that Peguero knew of his right to appeal is sufficient to conclude that the Rule 32 violation did not prejudice him. The error, if any, was harmless, and habeas relief is unwarranted.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
