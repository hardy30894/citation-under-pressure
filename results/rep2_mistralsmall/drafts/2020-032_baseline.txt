**ARGUMENT**

I. A live controversy remains only if the challenged policy continues to affect the plaintiff’s rights.

A plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages cannot resurrect a constitutional challenge to a repealed policy. Already, Ltd. v. Nike, Inc., 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013) (“A defendant’s voluntary cessation of a challenged practice does not moot a case if the defendant could resume the practice ‘at any time.’”). When the defendant repeals the policy and disavows any intent to reenact it, the controversy is moot. Already, 568 U.S. at 91; see also City of Mesquite v. Aladdin’s Castle, Inc., 455 U.S. 283, 289 n.10 (1982) (“[I]f the challenged conduct has ceased and there is no reasonable expectation that it will recur, the case is moot.”). Here, Georgia Gwinnett College (GGC) repealed the challenged policies and disclaimed any intent to reimpose them. App. 12a-13a. The Eleventh Circuit therefore correctly held that the claims for declaratory and injunctive relief are moot. 18-1267-6 (11th Cir. July 1, 2019) (per curiam).

II. Nominal damages cannot revive a moot controversy.

A request for nominal damages does not preserve a live controversy when the underlying constitutional claim is moot. Flanigan’s Enters., Inc. of Ga. v. City of Sandy Springs, 868 F.3d 1248, 1256 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc) (“[A] prayer for nominal damages cannot save an otherwise moot controversy.”). The Supreme Court has repeatedly recognized that nominal damages are not an adequate substitute for a live controversy. Already, 568 U.S. at 91; Lewis v. Continental Bank Corp., 494 U.S. 472, 482-83 (1990). Nominal damages merely acknowledge a technical violation without redressing any cognizable injury. Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247, 266-67 (1978). Because the plaintiffs here seek only nominal damages for a conceded violation of their rights, their claims are moot.

III. The plaintiffs’ belated attempt to plead compensatory damages is untimely and futile.

The plaintiffs’ attempt to recharacterize their damages claim as compensatory is both procedurally improper and substantively unsupported. A plaintiff may not transform a nominal-damages claim into a compensatory-damages claim on appeal. Flanigan’s, 868 F.3d at 1256. Moreover, the plaintiffs never alleged any cognizable injury. Slicker v. Jackson, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (compensatory damages in § 1983 suits require proof of actual injury). Their conclusory allegations of “irreparable harm” are insufficient. App. 10a-11a. The district court therefore did not abuse its discretion in denying leave to amend. Santiago v. Wood, 904 F.2d 673, 675 (11th Cir. 1990).

IV. The plaintiffs’ as-applied challenge is also moot.

Even if the plaintiffs’ facial challenge were not moot, their as-applied challenge fails because they never attempted to speak under the revised policy. Already, 568 U.S. at 91. The plaintiffs’ self-censorship—rather than any ongoing enforcement—drives their claim. But a plaintiff cannot manufacture standing by declining to engage in conduct permitted by the revised policy. Clapper v. Amnesty Int’l USA, 568 U.S. 398, 414 (2013). The Eleventh Circuit therefore correctly held that the plaintiffs’ claims are moot.
