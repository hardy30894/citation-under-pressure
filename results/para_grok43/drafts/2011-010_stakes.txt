**ARGUMENT**

Section 1333(b) of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act extends Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act coverage only to “disability or death of an employee resulting from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The statutory text requires both that the injury occur on the Shelf and that it arise from Shelf operations. The Fifth Circuit correctly held that the provision “imposes a situs-of-injury requirement.” Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs, 877 F.2d 356, 362 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc).

The Supreme Court has recognized that “the general scope of OCSLA’s coverage … would be determined principally by locale, not by the status of the individual injured or killed.” Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207, 219 (1986). Although the footnote in Tallentire addressed a different subsection, its observation that only § 1333(b) “superimposes a status requirement on the otherwise determinative OCSLA situs requirement” confirms that locale remains the controlling limit. Id. at 219 n.2. The Court’s statement is consistent with the geographic focus of the surrounding provisions. See 43 U.S.C. § 1333(a)(1) (applying federal law to the Shelf “and artificial islands and fixed structures erected thereon”).

Congress modeled § 1333(b) on the LHWCA’s own situs requirement, 33 U.S.C. § 903(a), which the Court has described as “explicit [and] geographic.” Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray, 470 U.S. 414, 426 (1985). Nothing in the text or history of § 1333(b) suggests that Congress intended to abandon that geographic limit when it incorporated the LHWCA remedy. The Fifth Circuit’s construction therefore respects “the explicit geographic limitation to [OCSLA’s] incorporation of the LHWCA.” Id. at 427.

Petitioner’s proposed “substantial nexus” test would sever the remedy from the Shelf entirely, allowing recovery for any land-based injury that bears some attenuated connection to Shelf operations. That reading finds no support in the statutory language or in Tallentire’s emphasis on locale. Because Juan Valladolid’s fatal injury occurred at an onshore facility in California, not on the outer Continental Shelf, the Benefits Review Board correctly denied benefits under § 1333(b).

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
