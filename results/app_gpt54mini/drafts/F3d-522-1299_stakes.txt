## ARGUMENT

### I. The district court erred in denying the asserted claims the benefit of the original 1997 filing date.

Under 35 U.S.C. § 120, a later application is entitled to the filing date of an earlier application if the earlier application “discloses the invention claimed in the later application in the manner provided by the first paragraph of section 112.” *In re Wertheim*, 646 F.2d 527, 537 (C.C.P.A. 1981); *Tronzo v. Biomet, Inc.*, 156 F.3d 1154, 1158 (Fed. Cir. 1998). The written-description inquiry asks whether the earlier application “reasonably conveys to those skilled in the art that the inventor had possession of the claimed subject matter as of the filing date.” *Ariad Pharms., Inc. v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 598 F.3d 1336, 1351 (Fed. Cir. 2010) (en banc). Possession may be shown without literal recitation of every later claim limitation; the question is whether the original disclosure identifies the invention with sufficient clarity. *Vas-Cath Inc. v. Mahurkar*, 935 F.2d 1555, 1563-64 (Fed. Cir. 1991).

The district court departed from that standard by treating the later-added 2000 CIP language as necessary to support the asserted claims. But later-added matter cannot be used to supply written-description support for an earlier date. *Lockwood v. Am. Airlines, Inc.*, 107 F.3d 1565, 1571-72 (Fed. Cir. 1997). The relevant question is whether the 1997 Original Application itself supported the asserted claims as construed, not whether the later CIP introduced a clearer, more convenient articulation. *In re Rasmussen*, 650 F.2d 1212, 1214-15 (C.C.P.A. 1981); *LizardTech, Inc. v. Earth Res. Mapping, Inc.*, 424 F.3d 1336, 1343-44 (Fed. Cir. 2005).

Here, the original application disclosed a vending machine for telecommunications access. That disclosure, together with the claim language, supports the asserted claims. The court’s narrowing construction of “customer interface” to require an interface located on the vending machine itself was not compelled by the original disclosure and improperly imported a limitation from the accused-structure dispute into the priority analysis. Claim construction must be grounded in the intrinsic record, but it cannot be used to rewrite the written-description requirement. *Phillips v. AWH Corp.*, 415 F.3d 1303, 1312-13 (Fed. Cir. 2005) (en banc); *Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc.*, 517 U.S. 370, 389 (1996). Once the claims are read in light of the specification, the original application adequately shows possession of the claimed vending-machine system, including its customer-facing interface. *Ariad*, 598 F.3d at 1351; *Vas-Cath*, 935 F.2d at 1563-64.

The district court’s contrary conclusion also conflicts with the principle that § 120 priority depends on whether the earlier application supports the later claims, not whether it contains the exact later phrasing. *In re Ziegler*, 992 F.2d 1197, 1200-01 (Fed. Cir. 1993). PowerOasis’s original application described the same basic invention chain that culminated in the asserted patents. The addition of further explanatory detail in the 2000 CIP did not create a new invention; it elaborated on an already-disclosed system. *In re Wertheim*, 646 F.2d at 537-38. Because the asserted claims are entitled to the 1997 filing date, MobileStar’s 1999 activities are not § 102(b) prior art. *Hazeltine Research, Inc. v. Brenner*, 382 U.S. 252, 255 (1965).

### II. Even if the 2000 CIP date controlled, the court’s anticipation ruling cannot stand.

Anticipation requires that a single prior-art reference disclose every claim limitation, arranged as in the claim. *Scripps Clinic & Research Found. v. Genentech, Inc.*, 927 F.2d 1565, 1576 (Fed. Cir. 1991); *In re Gleave*, 560 F.3d 1331, 1334 (Fed. Cir. 2009). Anticipation is a question of fact, and summary judgment is proper only when no reasonable factfinder could find otherwise. *AquaTex Indus., Inc. v. Techniche Solutions*, 479 F.3d 1320, 1328 (Fed. Cir. 2007).

The district court’s anticipation ruling depended entirely on its erroneous construction of “customer interface.” Once that term is construed consistent with the intrinsic evidence from the original application, the MobileStar Network does not disclose the claimed interface “on” the customer’s device in the way required by the claim construction adopted below. A prior-art system anticipates only if it meets the claim as properly construed. *Net MoneyIN, Inc. v. VeriSign, Inc.*, 545 F.3d 1359, 1369-70 (Fed. Cir. 2008). Because the court’s claim construction drove its anticipation analysis, the summary judgment cannot survive. *Brown v. 3M, 265 F.3d 1349, 1351-52 (Fed. Cir. 2001)*.

### III. The judgment should be reversed.

The asserted claims were supported by the original 1997 application and therefore are entitled to that filing date under § 120. *Ariad*, 598 F.3d at 1351; *Vas-Cath*, 935 F.2d at 1563-64. MobileStar’s pre-1999 activities cannot anticipate claims with a 1997 priority date. *Hazeltine Research*, 382 U.S. at 255. At minimum, genuine disputes remain as to written description, claim construction, and anticipation, precluding summary judgment. *AquaTex*, 479 F.3d at 1328. The Court should reverse.
