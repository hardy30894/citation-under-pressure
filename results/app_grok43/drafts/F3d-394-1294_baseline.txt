ARGUMENT

Section 2056(b)(7) permits a marital deduction for qualified terminable interest property only when the surviving spouse possesses a qualifying income interest for life. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). That interest exists solely when the spouse “is entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals.” Id. The Supreme Court has long required strict compliance with this statutory language. See Commissioner v. Estate of Church, 335 U.S. 632, 644 (1949).

The Amendment fails this test. It directs the trustee to distribute “all of the net income from the trust estate as the trustee, in the trustee’s reasonable discretion, shall determine to be proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare” of the spouse. This formulation conditions receipt on the trustee’s discretionary assessment of need rather than conferring an unqualified right to every dollar of income. See Estate of Heim v. Commissioner, 914 F.2d 1322, 1328 (9th Cir. 1990) (deduction unavailable where distribution standard is limited to support). The further instruction that the trustee “may consider any other income or resources” reinforces that the spouse holds no absolute claim to the entire income stream. See Morgan v. Commissioner, 309 F.3d 1003, 1008 (7th Cir. 2002).

Because the trust instrument withholds from Mrs. Davis the statutory right to all income, the property does not constitute qualified terminable interest property. The Tax Court therefore correctly disallowed the claimed deduction.
