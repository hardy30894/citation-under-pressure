The federal courts do not have exclusive jurisdiction over every case that merely involves a patent issue. Congress gave the federal courts original jurisdiction over civil actions “arising under” the patent laws, 28 U.S.C. § 1338(a), but the Supreme Court has repeatedly cautioned that not every dispute touching federal law belongs in federal court. In Christianson v. Colt Industries Operating Corp., the Court held that a patent case “arises under” § 1338(a) only if federal patent law creates the cause of action or the plaintiff’s right to relief necessarily depends on resolution of a substantial question of patent law. 486 U.S. 800, 808-09 (1988).
