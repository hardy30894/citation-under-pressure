I. The Text and Structure of §§ 1254a(f)(4) and 1255(a) Establish That a TPS Grant Constitutes an Admission.

Statutory interpretation begins with the plain meaning of the text. INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421, 431 (1987). Section 1254a(f)(4) provides that a TPS recipient “shall be considered as being in, and maintaining, lawful status as a nonimmigrant.” Nonimmigrant status under § 1101(a)(15) presupposes lawful admission. See United States v. Ron Pair Enters., Inc., 489 U.S. 235, 242 (1989) (plain language controls absent absurdity). Congress’s use of “admitted” in § 1255(a) therefore encompasses the inspection and authorization inherent in conferring that status. See FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp., 529 U.S. 120, 133 (2000) (statutes must be read as a whole).

Section 1255(k)(1) reinforces this reading by requiring only “lawful admission” on the filing date, a condition satisfied once TPS is granted. Where Congress wished to distinguish admission from status, it did so expressly. Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co., 534 U.S. 438, 452 (2002). The absence of any such distinction in § 1254a(f)(4) confirms that TPS satisfies § 1255(a)’s threshold requirement.

II. Context and Purpose Confirm Eligibility for Adjustment.

The TPS statute shields aliens already present from removal during crises in their homelands. 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(b)(1). Requiring physical reentry to obtain adjustment would frustrate that humanitarian purpose. See Whitman v. American Trucking Ass’ns, 531 U.S. 457, 468 (2001) (statutes interpreted to effectuate congressional design). Congress created explicit exceptions to the admission requirement for certain classes, 8 U.S.C. § 1255(h), (i), yet declined to exclude TPS recipients, indicating inclusion. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. at 432.

III. The Third Circuit’s Distinction Between Admission and Status Is Inconsistent with Precedent.

The court below treated “admission” and “status” as mutually exclusive. But adjustment of status itself constitutes an admission. See In re Espinosa-Guillot, 25 I. & N. Dec. 653, 654 (BIA 2011). Parallel provisions in § 1255(m)(1) equate “admitted” with “otherwise provided nonimmigrant status,” confirming the terms overlap. See King v. Burwell, 576 U.S. 473, 486 (2015) (context resolves apparent tension). The contrary view would render § 1254a(f)(4) superfluous, contrary to Hibbs v. Winn, 542 U.S. 88, 101 (2004), and Utility Air Regulatory Group v. EPA, 573 U.S. 302, 321 (2014).

Petitioners are therefore eligible for adjustment of status.
