ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DISMISSING FOLEY'S CLAIMS UNDER MASS. GEN. LAWS CH. 93A AND CH. 244, § 35A.

The district court improperly dismissed Foley’s claims under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A and ch. 244, § 35A, for failure to state a claim. To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain sufficient factual matter to state a claim that is plausible on its face. See Medina-Velázquez v. Hernández-Gregorat, 767 F.3d 103, 108 (1st Cir. 2014). Here, Foley’s complaint alleges that Wells Fargo failed to consider him for a MAP2R modification as required by the Pick-a-Payment settlement agreement, and instead proceeded with foreclosure. This failure to consider Foley for a modification constitutes a violation of Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A, which prohibits unfair or deceptive business practices. See Mass. Eye & Ear Infirmary v. QLT Phototherapeutics, Inc., 552 F.3d 47, 69 (1st Cir. 2009) (noting that a violation of a statutory duty can be considered an unfair or deceptive practice under Chapter 93A).

Furthermore, Foley’s complaint alleges that Wells Fargo failed to provide him with a right-to-cure notice as required by Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244, § 35A, before accelerating the mortgage and publishing a notice of sale. The statute requires that a creditor provide a homeowner with a 90-day right-to-cure notice before accelerating the mortgage. See Eaton v. Fannie Mae, 969 N.E.2d 1118, 1129 (Mass. 2012). Foley’s complaint plausibly alleges that Wells Fargo failed to comply with this requirement.

II. FOLEY'S CLAIMS ARE NOT PREEMPTED BY THE HOME OWNERS' LOAN ACT (HOLA).

Wells Fargo argues that Foley’s claims under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A and ch. 244, § 35A are preempted by HOLA, 12 U.S.C. § 1461 et seq. However, HOLA preemption is limited to state laws that "more than incidentally affect the lending operations or other operations" of federal savings associations. See 12 C.F.R. § 560.2(a). The First Circuit has held that HOLA preemption is not applicable to state laws that "only incidentally affect the lending operations" of federal savings associations. See Silvas v. E*Trade Mortg. Corp., 514 F.3d 1001, 1005 (9th Cir. 2008) (cited with approval in In re: Citigroup, Inc., 699 F.3d 598, 604 (1st Cir. 2012)). Here, Foley’s claims under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A and ch. 244, § 35A are not preempted by HOLA because they do not directly regulate the lending operations of Wells Fargo, but rather impose general duties on creditors.

In conclusion, the district court erred in dismissing Foley’s claims under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A and ch. 244, § 35A. Foley’s complaint plausibly alleges that Wells Fargo failed to consider him for a MAP2R modification and failed to provide him with a right-to-cure notice, in violation of state law. Furthermore, Foley’s claims are not preempted by HOLA. Therefore, this Court should reverse the district court’s dismissal of Foley’s claims.
