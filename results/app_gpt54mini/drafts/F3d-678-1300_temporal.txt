**Argument**

The judgment should be reversed because Facebook did not carry the heavy burden of proving invalidity by clear and convincing evidence. *Radio Corp. of America v. Radio Engineering Laboratories, Inc.*, 293 U.S. 1, 7–8 (1934); *Smith v. Hall*, 301 U.S. 216, 226–27 (1937). A jury may invalidate a patent only on proof that is “clear, satisfactory and convincing.” *United States v. General Electric Co.*, 272 U.S. 476, 489 (1926). On this record, the evidence at most showed that Leader had developed and demonstrated an earlier software product. It did not show that every limitation of the asserted claims was anticipated by a single prior-art system in public use or on sale before December 10, 2002. *Westinghouse v. Boyden Power-Brake Co.*, 170 U.S. 537, 568–69 (1898); *The Barbed Wire Patent*, 143 U.S. 275, 284–85 (1892).

Section 102(b) requires proof that the claimed invention was in public use or on sale and that the prior use disclosed the same invention as later claimed. *Egbert v. Lippmann*, 104 U.S. 333, 336–37 (1881); *City of Elizabeth v. American Nicholson Pavement Co.*, 97 U.S. 126, 136–37 (1878). But anticipation demands identity, not resemblance. The earlier device or process must “embod[y]” the same invention, with all its elements present and operating as the claims require. *Seymour v. Osborne*, 78 U.S. (11 Wall.) 516, 555–56 (1871); *Topliff v. Topliff*, 145 U.S. 156, 161–62 (1892). “It is not enough that the prior art may show something similar.” *Cimiotti Unhairing Co. v. American Fur Refining Co.*, 198 U.S. 399, 410 (1905).

Facebook’s evidence did not meet that standard. Leader’s white paper and demonstrations showed a commercial effort and a platform in development, but an offer to sell software is not proof that the later-asserted claim limitations were already in public use. *Egbert*, 104 U.S. at 336–37; *McClurg v. Kingsland*, 42 U.S. (1 How.) 202, 208–09 (1843). Nor does evidence that a product was “operational now with low user volumes” establish anticipation unless the product, as used, contained the precise combination recited in the claims. *Seymour*, 78 U.S. at 555–56; *Topliff*, 145 U.S. at 161–62. Facebook was required to prove that the Leader2Leader® system practiced the claimed computerized steps of dynamically associating metadata with data, tracking movement between environments, and dynamically updating the association when the user moved. Those are specific, interrelated limitations; a generalized showing of collaboration software does not satisfy anticipation. *Westinghouse*, 170 U.S. at 568–69.

The district court’s ruling also cannot be sustained by treating individual disclosures in Leader’s materials as if they were a single anticipatory reference. Anticipation must be found in one prior device or publication, not in a mosaic assembled from separate pieces of evidence. *The Barbed Wire Patent*, 143 U.S. at 284–85; *Cimiotti*, 198 U.S. at 410. To the extent Facebook relied on the white paper, the “Big Board,” testimony about the “underlying engine,” and later recollections about development, that evidence may suggest that Leader had a sophisticated prototype. But proof of a prototype is not proof that the prototype practiced the full claimed method in public use before the critical date. *City of Elizabeth*, 97 U.S. at 136–37; *Consolidated Fruit-Jar Co. v. Wright*, 94 U.S. 92, 96–97 (1876).

The clear-error problem is especially acute because the asserted claims are directed to a specific software architecture that dynamically links metadata to data and user environments. Anticipation cannot rest on functional similarity or on a broad characterization of the invention as “communication and collaboration.” *Tilghman v. Proctor*, 102 U.S. 707, 711–12 (1881). The prior use must disclose the same means arranged in the same way to accomplish the same result. *Westinghouse*, 170 U.S. at 568–69. Facebook’s proof did not establish that the pre-December 10, 2002 Leader2Leader® system performed the claimed automatic metadata updates when users moved between environments, or that it did so in the manner required by the claims. At most, Facebook showed development toward that result. Development is not anticipation. *Topliff*, 145 U.S. at 161–62.

Because invalidity had to be proved by clear and convincing evidence, and because anticipation requires a single prior use or sale that discloses every claim limitation, the verdict cannot stand. *Radio Corp.*, 293 U.S. at 7–8; *Smith*, 301 U.S. at 226–27; *Seymour*, 78 U.S. at 555–56. The Court should reverse the judgment of invalidity and remand for entry of judgment in Leader’s favor, or at minimum for a new trial.
