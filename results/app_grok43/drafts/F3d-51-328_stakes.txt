ARGUMENT

The district court abused its discretion by granting a preliminary injunction. A party seeking such relief must demonstrate, among other things, a likelihood of success on the merits. Jackson Dairy, Inc. v. H.P. Hood & Sons, Inc., 596 F.2d 70, 72 (2d Cir. 1979). Shapiro cannot make that showing under the Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988.

Section 3604(f)(3)(B) requires only “reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services” when “necessary to afford” equal opportunity to handicapped persons. 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B). The statute does not mandate that every neutral allocation rule yield on demand. See Southeastern Community College v. Davis, 442 U.S. 397, 410-11 (1979) (Rehabilitation Act requires reasonable, not fundamental, alterations). Cadman Towers’ first-come, first-served policy is applied uniformly to all 423 apartments; it is not a rule “because of” handicap. See International Brotherhood of Teamsters v. United States, 431 U.S. 324, 335 n.15 (1977).

Requiring immediate assignment of one of the 66 spaces at 101 Clark Street would displace applicants who have waited longer under the published procedure. Such displacement exceeds the “reasonable” limit. See Alexander v. Choate, 469 U.S. 287, 300 (1985) (accommodation not required where it would impose “undue hardship”). The cooperative already exempts three employee spaces and six grandfathered units; further ad hoc exceptions threaten the waiting-list system that allocates a scarce resource among hundreds of owners.

Even assuming Shapiro ultimately prevails on the merits, the injunction entered on January 25, 1994 prematurely altered the status quo by compelling assignment before any space became available through ordinary operation of the policy. Because Shapiro failed to establish a likelihood of success on the statutory claim, the preliminary injunction should be vacated.
