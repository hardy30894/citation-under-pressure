ARGUMENT

Section 355(f) of the Railroad Retirement Act authorizes judicial review of any “final decision of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A Board order denying reopening satisfies that statutory text. The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139, 141 (5th Cir. 2003), rests on an erroneous reading of Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), and conflicts with the presumption that agency action is reviewable, Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136, 140 (1967).

The plain language of § 355(f) contains no exception for reopening decisions. “Under the plain language of § 355(f), the jurisdiction of the federal courts of appeals is limited to the review of Board decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits after administrative appeals have been exhausted.” Roberts, 346 F.3d at 140. Yet the statute does not define “final decision” by reference to the nature of the underlying request; it turns on whether the Board has concluded its administrative process. See Bennett v. Spear, 520 U.S. 154, 178 (1997) (finality requires consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process).

That conclusion occurred here. The hearing officer’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application left no further avenue of administrative relief. The order therefore constitutes agency action “by which rights or obligations have been determined.” Bennett, 520 U.S. at 178. The D.C. Circuit reached the same result under identical statutory language. Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016). The Eighth and Second Circuits likewise held that § 355(f) encompasses reopening denials. Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991); Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254 (2d Cir. 1966).

Sanders does not compel a different outcome. That decision construed the Social Security Act’s more restrictive review provision and emphasized the absence of any statutory basis for reopening review. 430 U.S. at 107-08. Section 355(f) supplies the missing statutory hook. Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians, 476 U.S. 667, 670 (1986), confirms the “strong presumption that Congress intends judicial review of administrative action.”

The contrary decisions of other circuits rest on policy considerations rather than statutory text. See Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999); Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995); Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990); Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 708 F.2d 250, 255 (7th Cir. 1983). Those policy concerns cannot override the unambiguous language of § 355(f). See Connecticut Nat’l Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249, 253-54 (1992).

Because the Board’s denial of reopening is a final decision within the meaning of § 355(f), the petition for review should be reinstated.
