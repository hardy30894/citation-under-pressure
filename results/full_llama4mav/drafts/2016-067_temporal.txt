The petitioners argue that Santander Consumer USA, Inc. (Santander) is a "debt collector" under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) because it acquired their defaulted debts from CitiFinancial Auto. However, the FDCPA's definitions of "debt collector" and "creditor" make clear that Santander is not a debt collector when collecting debts it owns.

The FDCPA defines a "debt collector" as a person who "regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed or due or asserted to be owed or due another." 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). The Supreme Court has interpreted this language to mean that a debt collector is one who collects debts on behalf of another. See Pollock v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 157 U.S. 429, 559 (1895) (distinguishing between a principal and an agent). When Santander collects debts it owns, it is not acting as a debt collector.

The petitioners rely on the exclusion from the definition of "creditor" for a person who receives an assignment or transfer of a debt in default "solely for the purpose of facilitating collection of such debt for another." 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(4). However, this exclusion does not apply to Santander because it acquired the debts for its own account, not solely for the purpose of collecting for another. See Louisville & N.R. Co. v. United States, 282 U.S. 740, 757-58 (1931) (distinguishing between an assignment for collection and an assignment for ownership).

The FDCPA's purpose is to regulate the conduct of debt collectors, not creditors. See FTC v. Gratz, 253 U.S. 421, 427 (1920) (interpreting a statute according to its plain meaning). When Santander collects its own debts, it is acting as a creditor, not a debt collector. The petitioners' argument that Santander's status as a debt collector before purchasing the debts makes it a debt collector after purchasing the debts is unpersuasive. See Porto Rico Ry., Light & Power Co. v. Mor, 253 U.S. 345, 348 (1920) (rejecting an overly broad interpretation of a statute).

The Fourth Circuit correctly held that Santander is not a debt collector when collecting debts it owns. The judgment should be affirmed.
