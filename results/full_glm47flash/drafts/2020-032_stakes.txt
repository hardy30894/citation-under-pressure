**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Case Is Moot Because the Challenged Policies Have Been Revised and the Plaintiff Has Graduated.**

A case is moot when an event occurs that renders it impossible for the court to grant any effectual relief to the prevailing party. *United States v. W.T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. 629, 632 (1953). Here, Georgia Gwinnett College (GGC) voluntarily revised its policies to allow free speech anywhere on campus, and Uzuegbunam graduated. These changes eliminate the live controversy necessary for judicial review. *Id.* at 632–33. Furthermore, even if the policies remained, Appellants’ request for nominal damages cannot cure the lack of standing. *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife*, 504 U.S. 555, 560 (1992).

First, GGC’s policy revision moots the claims. *W.T. Grant* holds that a defendant’s voluntary cessation of an illegal act does not cure a case’s mootness. 345 U.S. at 632. GGC unambiguously terminated the challenged “Freedom of Expression Policy” and replaced it with a policy permitting speech anywhere on campus. The district court correctly found there is no reasonable basis to expect GGC will return to the prior policies. *Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248, 1255 (11th Cir. 2017). Consequently, no live controversy exists regarding the enforcement of the prior policies.

**II. Nominal Damages Cannot Cure a Moot Constitutional Challenge.**

Even assuming the prior policies remained in effect, Appellants’ claim for nominal damages cannot save the case. To have standing, a plaintiff must allege an “injury in fact” that is concrete and particularized. *Lujan*, 504 U.S. at 560. Nominal damages are merely a formality to acknowledge a legal right was violated; they do not constitute an actual injury. *Id.* at 560–61. The Eleventh Circuit has held that a “prayer for nominal damages generally will not save an otherwise moot challenge to an allegedly unconstitutional policy or law.” *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1255. Appellants failed to allege any actual injury, such as monetary loss or physical harm, and instead rested their claim on the abstract value of their constitutional rights. *Id.* at 1255–56. Therefore, the case is moot and must be dismissed.
