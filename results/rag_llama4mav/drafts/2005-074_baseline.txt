The reinstatement statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(5), applies to aliens who re-entered the United States before its effective date, April 1, 1997. The plain language of the statute indicates that it applies to any alien who "has reentered the United States illegally," without regard to the timing of the re-entry. See 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(5). The statute's broad language and the absence of any explicit temporal limitation support this interpretation. See Lehmann v. United States Ex Rel. Carson, 353 U.S. 685, 690 (1957) (holding that a similar provision in the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1952 applied to aliens who had entered the United States prior to the date of enactment).

Furthermore, the application of the reinstatement statute to pre-enactment re-entries is not impermissibly retroactive. The Supreme Court has held that a statute is not retroactive merely because it is applied to events that occurred before its enactment. See Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244, 269-70 (1994). Rather, a statute is retroactive only if it "attaches new legal consequences to events completed before its enactment." Id. at 270. In this case, the reinstatement statute does not impose new legal consequences on Fernandez-Vargas' pre-enactment re-entry. Instead, it governs the consequences of his continued presence in the United States after the statute's effective date. See Fernandez-Vargas v. Gonzales, 427 F.3d 803, 815-16 (10th Cir. 2005) (rejecting a similar retroactivity challenge).

The Tenth Circuit correctly held that Fernandez-Vargas had no "protectable expectation of being able to adjust his status" because his re-entry was illegal and he had not taken any steps to legalize his status before the reinstatement statute took effect. See Fernandez-Vargas, 427 F.3d at 820. The reinstatement statute did not impair any vested rights or settled expectations that Fernandez-Vargas had prior to its enactment. See Landgraf, 511 U.S. at 280 (noting that a statute does not operate retroactively if it does not impair vested rights or settled expectations).

In sum, the reinstatement statute applies to aliens who re-entered the United States before its effective date, and its application to Fernandez-Vargas is not impermissibly retroactive. The Tenth Circuit's decision should be affirmed.
