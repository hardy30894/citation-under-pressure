The continuity requirements of 35 U.S.C. § 120 are satisfied only when each intermediate application in the chain contains a specific reference to every earlier application whose filing date is claimed. See *In re de Seze*, 21 C.C.P.A. 911, 914, 70 F.2d 576, 578 (1934); *In re Lund*, 54 C.C.P.A. 1361, 1368, 376 F.2d 982, 988 (1967). Incorporation by reference supplies that specific reference only when the host document identifies the incorporated material “with detailed particularity.” *Advanced Display Sys., Inc. v. Kent State Univ.*, 212 F.3d 1272, 1282 (Fed. Cir. 2000).

The Board applied the incorporation standard announced in *Harari v. Hollmer*, 602 F.3d 1348, 1352-53 (Fed. Cir. 2010), to the ’838 and ’768 applications. That standard, however, was expressly limited to “this stage of the proceedings”—the initial filing of the ’880 application—where the examiner possessed both the transmittal sheet and the preliminary amendment. *Id.* at 1352. The intervening applications received no such contemporaneous clarification.

Without the preliminary amendment, the incorporation language copied from the ’566 application refers only to an application “filed on the same day as the present application.” J.A. 1204. That phrase cannot identify the ’579 application when the host application was filed years later. The language therefore fails the particularity requirement of *Advanced Display Systems*. Because the ’838 and ’768 applications do not themselves supply a compliant specific reference, the chain is broken and the ’880 application is entitled only to the December 20, 1996 filing date of the ’708 application.

The Board’s contrary conclusion rests on an unwarranted extension of *Harari I* beyond its stated temporal and procedural limits. Reversal is required.
