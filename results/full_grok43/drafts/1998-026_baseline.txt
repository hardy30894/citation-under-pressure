**ARGUMENT**

A district court’s failure to advise a defendant of the right to appeal under former Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) does not furnish a basis for relief under 28 U.S.C. § 2255 when the defendant knew of that right and elected not to appeal. Collateral relief is available only for errors that produce “a fundamental defect which inherently results in a complete miscarriage of justice.” *Hill v. United States*, 368 U.S. 424, 428 (1962). A technical violation of Rule 32 that causes no prejudice falls outside this narrow category. *United States v. Timmreck*, 441 U.S. 780, 783 (1979) (holding that a Rule 11 omission “does not present ‘exceptional circumstances’” warranting § 2255 relief absent prejudice to substantial rights).

The same principle governs omissions under Rule 32. The rule’s sole purpose is to ensure that a defendant is informed of the right to appeal. When an evidentiary hearing establishes that the defendant already possessed that knowledge, the omission is harmless. *See Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States*, 487 U.S. 250, 255 (1988) (nonconstitutional error is harmless if it “does not affect substantial rights”). Petitioner’s awareness of his appellate rights, found by the district court after hearing testimony, conclusively demonstrates that the Rule 32 violation produced no prejudice.

This Court has consistently required a showing of prejudice before vacating a federal sentence on collateral review. *See Custis v. United States*, 511 U.S. 485, 496 (1994) (limiting collateral attacks to claims of “a fundamental defect”). Because the court of appeals correctly applied harmless-error analysis and found the error harmless, its judgment should be affirmed.
