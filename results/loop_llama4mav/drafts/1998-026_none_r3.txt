The issue presented in this case is whether a District Court's failure to advise a defendant of his right to appeal, as required by the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, provides ground for habeas relief when the defendant was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The respondent respectfully submits that it does not.

The Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, specifically Rule 32(a)(2), mandate that the court advise a defendant of his right to appeal after sentencing. However, a failure to comply with this rule does not automatically entitle a defendant to habeas relief. The Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation, a principle supported by precedent (United States v. Daddato, 160 F.2d 695, 697 (7th Cir. 1947); United States v. Paglia, 190 F.2d 445, 448 (2d Cir. 1951); Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750, 764-65 (1946)).

The District Court's finding that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing is dispositive. A defendant's knowledge of his rights can cure a procedural error (Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938)), and the purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is served when a defendant is already aware of his right to appeal (United States v. Bazzano, 146 F.2d 923, 925 (3rd Cir. 1945)). As the Supreme Court has held, a procedural error is harmless if it does not affect the outcome of the case (Kotteakos, 328 U.S. at 764-65). Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal and his decision not to exercise it render the District Court's failure to advise him harmless.

The Supreme Court has consistently held that not all procedural errors require reversal (Snyder v. Massachusetts, 291 U.S. 97, 116-17 (1934); Glasser v. United States, 315 U.S. 60, 83 (1942)). To be entitled to relief, a defendant must show prejudice resulting from an error. Peguero has not demonstrated any prejudice resulting from the District Court's failure to advise him of his right to appeal. Furthermore, the Court has emphasized the importance of a defendant's knowledge and understanding of his rights (Adams v. United States ex rel. McCann, 317 U.S. 269, 278 (1942); Von Moltke v. Gillies, 332 U.S. 708, 723-24 (1948)). Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal and his decision not to appeal demonstrate that the District Court's error did not affect his substantial rights.

In light of these precedents, the respondent submits that the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal does not provide ground for habeas relief. The Court of Appeals' decision affirming the District Court's denial of habeas relief should be upheld, as it is consistent with established principles, including the harmless-error doctrine and the importance of a defendant's knowledge and understanding of his rights.
