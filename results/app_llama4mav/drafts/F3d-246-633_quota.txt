The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's claim of conspiracy in violation of the Railway Labor Act ("RLA"), 45 U.S.C. § 153. The RLA requires that unions fairly represent their members in disputes with employers. See Steele v. Louisville & Nashville R.R. Co., 323 U.S. 192, 204 (1944) (holding that a union's duty of fair representation is "imposed upon it by the statute"). A union breaches this duty when its actions are "arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith." Vaca v. Sipes, 386 U.S. 171, 190 (1967). 

Here, Plaintiff presented evidence that UTU representative Earley conspired with CSX to deny Plaintiff the thirty shares of CSX stock. Specifically, the tape recording of the executive session, although obtained in violation of the Federal Wiretap Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2510 et seq., as found by the district court, suggests that Earley may have acted in bad faith by arguing against Plaintiff's claim during the executive session. See Earley v. Executive Bd. of the UTU, 957 F.Supp. 997, 999 (N.D.Ohio 1996) (noting that Ruck alleged Earley argued against the official position of the UTU in the executive session). This evidence raises a genuine issue of material fact as to whether Earley breached his duty of fair representation, and consequently, whether UTU and CSX conspired to deny Plaintiff the CSX stock. See Garrison v. Cassens Transport Co., 334 F.3d 528, 538 (6th Cir. 2003) (holding that a union's breach of its duty of fair representation is a necessary element of a claim against an employer for breach of a collective bargaining agreement).

Furthermore, the district court's grant of summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's RLA claim was premature, as discovery was not yet complete. See White's Landing Fisheries, Inc. v. Buchholzer, 29 F.3d 229, 231-32 (6th Cir. 1994) (holding that summary judgment is premature when discovery is not yet complete). The court should have allowed Plaintiff to conduct further discovery to uncover evidence of the alleged conspiracy. See Plott v. General Motors Corp., 71 F.3d 1190, 1195 (6th Cir. 1995) (noting that a court should consider the availability of additional discovery when determining whether to grant summary judgment).

The district court also erred in awarding Defendants statutory damages, punitive damages, and attorneys' fees and costs under the Federal Wiretap Act. The Act provides for damages only when a person intercepts a wire, oral, or electronic communication "intentionally." 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(a). While the district court found that Plaintiff intentionally recorded the executive session, the court's finding is not dispositive of the issue. See United States v. Councilman, 418 F.3d 67, 80-81 (1st Cir. 2005) (en banc) (noting that the Act requires a showing of intent to intercept a communication). Moreover, the Act provides an exception for recordings made in the ordinary course of business or with prior consent. 18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d). Plaintiff's recording of the executive session may have been permissible if he had a legitimate reason for doing so, such as to ensure that the PLB members were acting impartially. See United States v. Murdock, 63 F.3d 1391, 1398-99 (6th Cir. 1995) (noting that the Act's exceptions are to be construed broadly).

Additionally, the award of punitive damages was not justified, as the Act requires a showing of "willful, wanton, or reckless disregard for the law." Bess v. Bess, 929 F.2d 1332, 1335 (8th Cir. 1991). The district court's finding that Plaintiff intentionally recorded the executive session does not necessarily establish that he acted with the requisite level of culpability. See Reynolds v. Spears, 93 F.3d 428, 435-36 (8th Cir. 1996) (noting that punitive damages under the Act require a showing of "aggravated circumstances").

The award of attorneys' fees and costs was also improper. The Act provides for an award of fees and costs only when a court finds that a party has violated the Act "in bad faith, vexatiously, wantonly, or for oppressive reasons." 18 U.S.C. § 2520(b)(3). See Dorris v. Absher, 179 F.R.D. 508, 512 (M.D.Tenn. 1997) (noting that the Act's fee-shifting provision is discretionary). The district court's finding that Plaintiff intentionally recorded the executive session is not sufficient to establish bad faith or vexatious conduct. See In re Ford Motor Co., 110 F.3d 954, 967-68 (3d Cir. 1997) (noting that a court's award of fees and costs under the Act must be based on a finding of bad faith or other egregious conduct).

In conclusion, the district court erred in granting summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's RLA claim and in awarding Defendants damages and fees under the Federal Wiretap Act. The court's decisions should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings. See Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 255 (1986) (noting that summary judgment is only appropriate when there is no genuine issue of material fact).
