**ARGUMENT**

**I. FLORIDA’S DEATH SENTENCING SCHEME DOES NOT VIOLATE THE SIXTH AMENDMENT’S JURY TRIAL GUARANTEE.**

The Sixth Amendment’s jury trial guarantee does not require that a jury unanimously find every fact necessary to impose the death penalty. *Walton v. Arizona*, 497 U.S. 639, 647-48 (1990) (plurality opinion) (holding that Arizona’s capital sentencing scheme, which permitted the trial judge to find aggravating factors by a preponderance of the evidence, did not violate the Sixth Amendment). The Supreme Court has repeatedly recognized that sentencing determinations are not subject to the same jury trial protections as elements of the offense. *Spaziano v. Florida*, 468 U.S. 447, 459-60 (1984) (upholding Florida’s scheme where the jury’s recommendation was merely advisory and the trial judge made the ultimate sentencing decision). Florida’s scheme is consistent with these precedents.

Florida’s death penalty statute requires the jury to find at least one statutory aggravating factor before recommending death. Fla. Stat. § 921.141(2) (1974). The jury’s recommendation is then considered by the trial judge, who makes the ultimate sentencing determination. *Hildwin v. Florida*, 490 U.S. 638, 640-41 (1989) (per curiam) (upholding Florida’s scheme where the trial judge, not the jury, made the final sentencing decision). This Court has long held that the Sixth Amendment does not require jury unanimity in sentencing determinations. *Spaziano*, 468 U.S. at 460 (citing *McGautha v. California*, 402 U.S. 183, 214-15 (1971), which upheld a capital sentencing scheme where the jury’s role was advisory).

**II. FLORIDA’S DEATH SENTENCING SCHEME DOES NOT VIOLATE THE EIGHTH AMENDMENT’S PROHIBITION AGAINST CRUEL AND UNUSUAL PUNISHMENT.**

The Eighth Amendment does not require jury unanimity in capital sentencing. *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242, 252 (1976) (plurality opinion) (upholding Florida’s death penalty scheme, including the trial judge’s role in sentencing and the jury’s advisory recommendation). The Supreme Court has consistently deferred to state legislatures in structuring their capital sentencing schemes, so long as they provide sufficient guidance and safeguards. *Gregg v. Georgia*, 428 U.S. 153, 195 (1976) (plurality opinion) (upholding Georgia’s guided discretion statute).

Florida’s scheme provides clear statutory aggravating factors and requires the jury to find at least one before recommending death. Fla. Stat. § 921.141(5) (1974). The trial judge then considers the jury’s recommendation, along with other statutory mitigating factors, before imposing sentence. *Proffitt*, 428 U.S. at 251-52 (approving Florida’s scheme). This Court has repeatedly upheld Florida’s death penalty statute against Eighth Amendment challenges. *Hildwin*, 490 U.S. at 640 (upholding Florida’s scheme where the trial judge, not the jury, made the final sentencing decision).

**III. FLORIDA’S TREATMENT OF MENTAL RETARDATION DOES NOT VIOLATE THE EIGHTH AMENDMENT.**

The Supreme Court has held that the Eighth Amendment forbids the execution of mentally retarded defendants. *Ford v. Wainwright*, 477 U.S. 399, 405-06 (1986) (plurality opinion). However, the Court has left to the states the manner in which this constitutional restriction is enforced. *Atkins v. Virginia*, 536 U.S. 304, 317 (2002). Florida’s statute provides a clear and workable standard for determining mental retardation, requiring proof by clear and convincing evidence. Fla. Stat. § 921.137(4) (2001). This Court has consistently upheld Florida’s mental retardation statute against constitutional challenges. *Nixon v. State*, 2 So. 3d 137, 145 (Fla. 2009) (upholding Florida’s mental retardation statute).

Florida’s scheme does not violate the Eighth Amendment because it provides a meaningful opportunity to prove mental retardation and prohibits the execution of those who meet the statutory criteria. *Ford*, 477 U.S. at 410 (holding that the Eighth Amendment prohibits the execution of the insane, but leaving the procedures for determining insanity to the states). The Supreme Court has repeatedly deferred to state procedures in this area. *Panetti v. Quarterman*, 551 U.S. 930, 949-50 (2007) (upholding Texas’s procedures for determining competency to be executed).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Florida’s death sentencing scheme does not violate the Sixth Amendment’s jury trial guarantee or the Eighth Amendment’s prohibition against cruel and unusual punishment. The judgment of the Florida Supreme Court should be affirmed.
