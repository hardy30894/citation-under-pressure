## ARGUMENT

### Standard of Review

Whether a case is moot is a question of law reviewed de novo. The district court dismissed Mr. Lucero's class complaint for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction on the theory that BCR's unaccepted Rule 68 offer extinguished any live controversy before class certification could be decided. That ruling rests on a mistaken and unduly rigid application of the mootness doctrine that this Court's predecessors have long rejected.

### I. A Defendant's Tender of Relief to the Named Plaintiff Does Not Automatically Divest a Court of Jurisdiction Over the Underlying Controversy.

Mootness is not a mechanical inquiry triggered the instant a defendant offers to pay a plaintiff off. The Supreme Court has long recognized that a defendant's own voluntary conduct cannot be permitted to manipulate the jurisdiction of the courts. In *United States v. W.T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. 629 (1953), the Court held that voluntary cessation of the challenged conduct does not moot a case, because "the defendant is free to return to his old ways." *Id.* at 632. That reasoning applies with equal force here: BCR's tender was not a judicially enforced remedy but a self-selected litigation tactic designed to terminate the suit before the class could be certified. Nothing prevented BCR from continuing the very debt-collection practices Mr. Lucero challenged on behalf of the class.

This Court's forerunners likewise recognized that mootness of the named party's individual demand does not necessarily terminate a suit brought to vindicate the rights of a group. In *Oskoian v. Canuel*, 269 F.2d 311 (1st Cir. 1959), the First Circuit held that where a class-type suit has been filed, the interests of those associated with the litigation survive even where the individual claim asserted by the nominal plaintiff no longer presents a live dispute. *Id.* at 314. The same logic controls here: Mr. Lucero filed his class complaint, invoked the class-action machinery of the Federal Rules, and diligently pursued certification according to the schedule the district court itself adopted. BCR's tender came only after the case was already structured, by joint agreement of the parties and order of the court, around a bifurcated discovery plan devoted first to certification. Permitting BCR's tactical offer to terminate the suit before that process concluded elevates form over the substance that *Oskoian* protects.

Nor can BCR rely on the fact that satisfaction of the individual claim, standing alone, ends all live disputes. *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), teaches that mootness principles exist to preserve, not to defeat, the orderly resolution of live controversies, and that courts must be alert to the possibility that a party's own conduct—rather than any genuine disappearance of the dispute—has produced the claimed mootness. The timing of BCR's offer, filed the same day as its answer and long before any certification ruling was possible, reveals exactly the kind of manufactured mootness *Munsingwear* cautions against.

### II. Mr. Lucero Retained a Personal Stake Sufficient to Satisfy Article III Pending Resolution of Class Certification.

Article III requires only that a plaintiff retain "a personal stake in the outcome of the controversy." *Baker v. Carr*, 369 U.S. 186, 204 (1962). Mr. Lucero's stake in obtaining a ruling on class certification—an issue wholly independent of the value of his individual claim—remained live throughout the proceedings below. The Supreme Court has repeatedly declined to find mootness where a live procedural or collateral issue persists even after the plaintiff's primary claim has become moot. In *Powell v. McCormack*, 395 U.S. 486 (1969), the Court held that the expiration of Powell's congressional term did not moot his case because a continuing dispute over collateral relief remained. The same principle applies here: even accepting that BCR's offer satisfied Mr. Lucero's individual damages demand, the class certification question—and with it the interests of absent class members—remained unresolved and very much alive.

### III. The Timing of BCR's Tender Created Precisely the Evasion-of-Review Problem That Mootness Doctrine Forecloses.

Long-standing doctrine holds that a controversy is not moot where the underlying dispute is "capable of repetition, yet evading review." *Southern Pacific Terminal Co. v. ICC*, 219 U.S. 498, 515 (1911). The Supreme Court applied that principle to protect a class of similarly situated litigants in *Moore v. Ogilvie*, 394 U.S. 814, 816 (1969), holding that the passage of the election underlying the named plaintiffs' claims did not moot the controversy because the same legal question would recur for other members of the class. And in *Carroll v. President & Comm'rs of Princess Anne*, 393 U.S. 175 (1968), the Court refused to treat the expiration of a temporary order as mooting the case, recognizing that mootness cannot be permitted to insulate recurring practices from review. So too here: if a defendant may always extinguish a class action simply by tendering the named plaintiff's individual damages the moment suit is filed, FDCPA class claims will forever evade appellate review, and defendants will be free to "pick off" plaintiffs one by one while continuing the challenged practice against the class at large.

For these reasons, the district court's dismissal for want of subject-matter jurisdiction should be reversed.
