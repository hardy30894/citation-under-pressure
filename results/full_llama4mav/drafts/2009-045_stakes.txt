The district court's award of a fee enhancement based on the quality of counsel's representation and the exceptional results obtained is consistent with Supreme Court precedent and should be affirmed. The Supreme Court has held that, in "rare" and "exceptional" cases, a district court may enhance a lodestar fee award to reflect the quality of representation and results obtained. Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984); Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens' Council for Clean Air, 478 U.S. 546, 565 (1986). 

In Blum, the Court indicated that an upward adjustment may be justified when the fee applicant offers "specific evidence" to demonstrate that the quality of service rendered was superior to that one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged and that the success was "exceptional." 465 U.S. at 899. The Court reaffirmed this principle in Delaware Valley I, stating that "upward adjustments of the lodestar figure are still permissible ... in certain 'rare' and 'exceptional' cases, supported by both 'specific evidence' on the record and detailed findings by the lower courts." 478 U.S. at 565.

The district court's decision to enhance the lodestar fee in this case was based on its own observations and experience with the case, as well as specific evidence in the record. The court detailed the exceptional results obtained by counsel and the superior quality of their representation, which far exceeded what could reasonably be expected given the hourly rates charged. Kenny A. v. Perdue, 454 F.Supp.2d 1260, 1286, 1290 (N.D.Ga. 2006). This is precisely the type of "specific evidence" and "detailed findings" contemplated by the Supreme Court in Blum and Delaware Valley I.

Furthermore, every circuit court that has considered this issue has agreed that a district court may provide an enhancement for exceptional performance and results obtained. See, e.g., Kenny A. v. Perdue, 532 F.3d 1209, 1242 (11th Cir. 2008); Geier v. Sundquist, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co., 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000). The unanimity among the circuits on this issue underscores the correctness of the district court's decision.

The petitioners' argument that the district court's enhancement was improper because it was based on the judge's personal observations and experience is unpersuasive. The district judge is uniquely positioned to evaluate the quality of counsel's representation and the results obtained, and his observations and experience are relevant to this determination. See Kenny A., 532 F.3d at 1242 (Wilson, J., concurring). The judge's detailed findings and explanation for the enhancement demonstrate that his decision was not arbitrary or capricious.

In conclusion, the district court's award of a fee enhancement based on the quality of counsel's representation and the exceptional results obtained is consistent with Supreme Court precedent and should be affirmed. The decision is supported by specific evidence in the record and detailed findings by the district court, and is in line with the unanimous view of the circuit courts on this issue.
