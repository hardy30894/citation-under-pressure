ARGUMENT

The rule announced in *Johnson v. United States*, 576 U.S. 591 (2015), applies retroactively on collateral review. In *Johnson*, this Court held that the ACCA residual clause “denies fair notice to defendants and invites arbitrary enforcement by judges” because it requires courts to assess “the hypothetical risk posed by an abstract generic version” of an offense. *Id.* at 597-98.

Under *Teague v. Lane*, 489 U.S. 288 (1989), new rules of criminal procedure generally do not apply retroactively to cases that became final before the rule was announced. *Id.* at 310. Substantive rules, however, “apply retroactively because they ‘necessarily carry a significant risk that a defendant stands convicted of an act that the law does not make criminal.’” *Schriro v. Summerlin*, 542 U.S. 348, 352 (2004) (quoting *Bousley v. United States*, 523 U.S. 614, 620 (1998)). A rule is substantive when it “alters the range of conduct or the class of persons that the law punishes.” *Schriro*, 542 U.S. at 353.

*Johnson* announced such a substantive rule. By invalidating the residual clause, it “narrow[ed] the scope of a criminal statute by interpreting its terms,” *Bousley*, 523 U.S. at 620, and thereby “place[d] particular conduct or persons covered by the statute beyond the State’s power to punish,” *Schriro*, 542 U.S. at 352. The decision therefore applies retroactively. *See Penry v. Lynaugh*, 492 U.S. 302, 330 (1989); *Saffle v. Parks*, 494 U.S. 484, 495 (1990).

This Court has repeatedly applied analogous holdings retroactively. *See Griffith v. Kentucky*, 479 U.S. 314, 328 (1987) (new rules apply to all cases pending on direct review); *Danforth v. Minnesota*, 552 U.S. 264, 266 (2008) (states may give broader retroactive effect); *Montgomery v. Louisiana*, 577 U.S. 190, 206 (2016) (substantive rules apply on collateral review). Procedural rules, by contrast, do not. *See Chaidez v. United States*, 568 U.S. 342, 347 (2013); *Sawyer v. Smith*, 497 U.S. 227, 241-42 (1990).

Because Welch’s ACCA sentence rests on the now-invalid residual clause, his sentence is unlawful. The judgment should be reversed.
