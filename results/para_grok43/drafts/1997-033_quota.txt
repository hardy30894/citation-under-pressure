The plain language of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) commands that actions transferred for pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded by the Panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” This directive is “clear” and “mandatory.” See Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971). Section 1404(a) supplies no exception. A transferee court’s authority is limited to the pretrial phase; once that phase ends, the statute restores the transferor forum. See In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litig., 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3d Cir. 1982).

Statutory text controls over judicially created efficiencies. “When the statute’s language is plain, the sole function of the courts is to enforce it according to its terms.” See United States v. Ron Pair Enters., Inc., 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989). Courts may not “ignore the statutory mandate” merely because remand appears inconvenient. See Bauman v. United States Dist. Court, 557 F.2d 650, 654 (9th Cir. 1977). The multidistrict rules cannot enlarge the statute; J.P.M.L.R.P. 14(b) is “without force” where it conflicts with the congressional command. See Washington Pub. Util. Group v. United States Dist. Court, 843 F.2d 319, 326 (9th Cir. 1988).

Section 1404(a) transfers remain available, but only before the Panel’s order or after remand. Allowing a transferee judge to retain the case for trial would “subvert the Panel’s responsibility” and nullify the statutory limitation to “coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings.” See In re Air Crash Disaster Near Hanover, N.H., 342 F. Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H. 1971). Plaintiffs retain their “ancient right to select the forum” for trial. See Hoffman v. Blaski, 363 U.S. 335, 344 (1960).

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary conclusion rested on an erroneous view that the transfer order was not “clearly erroneous as a matter of law.” See United States v. Harper, 729 F.2d 1216, 1222 (9th Cir. 1984). That standard cannot excuse departure from an unambiguous remand command. See Sunshine Beauty Supplies, Inc. v. United States Dist. Court, 872 F.2d 310, 311 (9th Cir. 1989). Nor can the absence of prejudice justify retention when the statute itself supplies the rule. See Varsic v. United States Dist. Court, 607 F.2d 245, 252 (9th Cir. 1979).

Because the Arizona court lacked authority to assign the case to itself for trial, the judgment must be reversed and the action remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
