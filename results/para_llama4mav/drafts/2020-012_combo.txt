ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act (RRA) and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (RUIA) grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final" decisions of the United States Railroad Retirement Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The Fifth Circuit below held that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application, relying on its prior decision in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Board, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003). However, Roberts was wrongly decided, as it misinterpreted the Supreme Court's holding in Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), which is not controlling here because it was decided after the relevant period.

Instead, this Court should follow the reasoning of the Second Circuit in Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Board, 370 F.2d 253 (2d Cir. 1966), which held that a Board decision denying a request to reopen a prior claim is reviewable. The Szostak court relied on the Supreme Court's decision in St. Joseph Stock Yards Co. v. United States, 298 U.S. 38 (1936), which established that an agency's refusal to reopen a prior proceeding is subject to judicial review.

Furthermore, the Eighth Circuit in Sones v. R.R. Retirement Board, 933 F.2d is not the only pre-1970 case; however,  Sones was decided after 1970. Instead, we can look to the dissenting opinion in Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Board, 708 F.2d 250 (7th Cir. 1983), which cited Szostak. Although Steebe was decided after 1970, its dissent referenced pre-1970 cases. The dissenting judge in Steebe relied on the Supreme Court's decision in United States v. Interstate Commerce Commission, 337 U.S. 426 (1949), which held that an agency's order denying a petition to reopen is reviewable.

Additionally, the Supreme Court's decision in United States v. Morgan, 307 U.S. 183 (1939), established that an agency's decision is final and reviewable when it has exhausted its administrative remedies. Similarly, in Federal Communications Commission v. Pottsville Broadcasting Co., 309 U.S. 134 (1940), the Court held that an agency's decision is reviewable when it has taken final action on a matter.

The Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a final decision subject to judicial review. The RRA and RUIA grant circuit courts jurisdiction to review final Board decisions, and the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's prior claim is a final decision within the meaning of the statute.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts was incorrect, and this Court should reconsider its holding in light of the pre-1970 case law. The Second Circuit's decision in Szostak and the Supreme Court's decisions in St. Joseph Stock Yards Co., United States v. Interstate Commerce Commission, United States v. Morgan, and Federal Communications Commission v. Pottsville Broadcasting Co. all support the conclusion that a Board decision denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim is reviewable.

Moreover, the Supreme Court's decision in Rochester Telephone Corp. v. United States, 307 U.S. 125 (1939), established that the finality of an agency's decision is determined by the statutory scheme governing the agency. In this case, the RRA and RUIA grant circuit courts jurisdiction to review final Board decisions, and the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's prior claim is a final decision within the meaning of the statute.

Finally, the Supreme Court's decision in Gray v. Powell, 314 U.S. 402 (1941), and Alstate Construction Co. v. Durkin, 345 U.S. 13 (1953), are not directly relevant, but other cases like Shields v. Utah Idaho R. Co., 305 U.S. 177 (1938) are. 

In conclusion, the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a final decision subject to judicial review, and this Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision dismissing Salinas's petition for lack of jurisdiction.
