**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s Decision Denying Reopening Is a Final, Reviewable Agency Action

The Railroad Retirement Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 disability application is a “final decision” subject to judicial review under the plain text of the Railroad Retirement Act (“RRA”) and the Administrative Procedure Act (“APA”). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding in *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), misreads the statute and conflicts with this Court’s precedents defining finality.

A. The Railroad Retirement Act Permits Review of “Final” Board Decisions

Section 355(f) of the RRA provides that "[a]ny employee or annuitant aggrieved by a final decision of the Board may obtain review of such decision in the appropriate court of appeals." 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The statute does not limit review to decisions on the merits of a benefits claim; it reaches any "final decision" of the Board. The Board’s refusal to reopen a prior claim is a decision that conclusively determines Salinas’s rights and obligations under the RRA. See *Bennett v. Spear*, 520 U.S. 154, 177–78 (1997) (finality exists where an agency action "mark[s] the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process" and "determine[s] rights or obligations").

B. The Board’s Reopening Decision Is Final Under the APA

The APA defines a "final agency action" as one that is "final" within the meaning of 5 U.S.C. § 704. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 704. This Court has held that an agency action is final when it "impose[s] an obligation, deny[s] a right, or fix[es] some legal relationship." *Associated Builders & Contractors, Inc. v. Herman*, 166 F.3d 1247, 1252 (D.C. Cir. 1999) (quoting *Port of Boston Marine Terminal Ass’n v. Rederiaktiebolaget Transatlantic*, 400 U.S. 62, 71 (1970)). The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application fixed his legal status—he remains ineligible for benefits based on that application—and thus constitutes final agency action.

C. The Fifth Circuit’s Precedent in *Roberts* Is Wrong and Should Be Rejected

The Fifth Circuit in *Roberts* held that § 355(f) does not permit review of Board decisions declining to reopen prior claims. 346 F.3d at 140. That decision rests on a misreading of *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), which addressed reopening decisions under the Social Security Act, not the RRA. *Sanders* held only that the Social Security Act’s judicial review provision did not authorize review of reopening decisions, not that no such review was ever available. *Id.* at 107–08. The Court in *Sanders* did not address the RRA or its text.

Moreover, *Roberts* ignored this Court’s decision in *Darby v. Cisneros*, 509 U.S. 137 (1993), which held that § 704 of the APA provides a separate, independent basis for judicial review of final agency actions. *Id.* at 154. The Fifth Circuit’s refusal to apply *Darby* to the RRA’s review scheme is error.

II. The Board’s Decision Is Reviewable Under the APA’s Waiver Provision

Even if § 355(f) were read to exclude reopening decisions, the APA’s waiver provision, 5 U.S.C. § 702, independently authorizes review. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 702 ("A person suffering legal wrong because of agency action, or adversely affected or aggrieved by agency action within the meaning of a relevant statute, is entitled to judicial review thereof."). The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s claim caused him legal wrong by foreclosing his ability to seek benefits based on that application. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 702; *Webster v. Doe*, 486 U.S. 592, 603 (1988) (review available where agency action causes legal wrong).

III. The Board’s Decision Is Arbitrary and Capricious

Even if the Court were to conclude that the Board’s decision is not final, the Board’s refusal to reopen is arbitrary and capricious. The Board denied reopening based on its regulation, 20 C.F.R. § 261.2, which permits reopening for "new and material evidence" or "administrative error." The Board’s refusal to consider whether Salinas’s prior counsel’s failure to timely file constituted administrative error was arbitrary and capricious. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 706(2)(A); *Motor Vehicle Mfrs. Ass’n v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co.*, 463 U.S. 29, 43 (1983) (agency action is arbitrary and capricious if it "relie[s] on factors which Congress has not intended it to consider").

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit’s judgment and remand for review of the Board’s decision denying reopening.
