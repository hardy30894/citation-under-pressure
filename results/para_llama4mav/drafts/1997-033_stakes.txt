The plain language of 28 U.S.C. section 1407(a) mandates that the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation "shall" remand any action transferred for pretrial proceedings to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The statute's clear directive is that the Panel's authority is limited to transferring cases for pretrial purposes, and that the original district retains jurisdiction for trial. 

The District of Arizona's invocation of section 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the statutory scheme established by section 1407(a). As Judge Kozinski noted in his dissent below, "Section 1407(a) authorizes the Multidistrict Panel to transfer cases for pretrial proceedings; after those proceedings are completed, and before trial, '[e]ach action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel.'" (emphasis added). Lexecon Inc. v. United States Dist. Court, 61 F.3d 911 (9th Cir. 1995) (Kozinski, J., dissenting).

The courts of appeals that have addressed this issue have largely disregarded the statutory mandate, instead relying on policy or convenience grounds to justify their departure from the statute's plain meaning. See, e.g., Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971); In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litig., 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3d Cir. 1982). However, as Judge Kozinski observed, "Seldom have so many been so consistently wrong." Lexecon, 61 F.3d 911 (Kozinski, J., dissenting).

The statutory text and legislative history of section 1407(a) support the conclusion that Congress intended to limit the Panel's authority to transferring cases for pretrial purposes, while preserving the original district's jurisdiction for trial. Allowing a transferee court to invoke section 1404(a) to retain a case for trial would subvert this statutory scheme and undermine the policy choice reflected in section 1407(a).

In light of the clear statutory language and the importance of respecting Congress's policy choices, this Court should reverse the judgment of the Court of Appeals and hold that a federal district court conducting "pretrial proceedings" under 28 U.S.C. section 1407(a) may not invoke section 1404(a) to assign a transferred case to itself for trial.
