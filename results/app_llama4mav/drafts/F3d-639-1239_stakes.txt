ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Lucero's Class Action Complaint for Lack of Subject Matter Jurisdiction Based on Mootness.

The district court's dismissal of Lucero's complaint was based on its conclusion that, because BCR offered to satisfy Lucero's entire individual claim, there was no longer a justiciable dispute. However, this conclusion ignores the Supreme Court's holdings in Sosna v. Iowa, 419 U.S. 393 (1975), and United States Parole Commission v. Geraghty, 445 U.S. 388 (1980), which establish that a class action is not rendered moot by the satisfaction of the named plaintiff's individual claims if the plaintiff has a continuing personal stake in the litigation.

In Sosna, the Court held that a class action "may continue to be litigated" even after the named plaintiff's individual claim has been resolved, as long as the plaintiff has a "personal stake in the class action's continued vitality." 419 U.S. at 401, 402-03. The Court emphasized that the "class action device was designed to permit litigation in which 'the interest of the named plaintiff is relatively small but there is a large number of persons in a similar situation.'" Id. at 399 (quoting Advisory Committee's Note to Rule 23, 39 F.R.D. 69, 103 (1966)).

Similarly, in Geraghty, the Court held that a plaintiff's "personal stake" in a class action is not extinguished by the mootness of their individual claim, as long as they continue to press a class certification motion. 445 U.S. at 404. The Court noted that the "relation back" doctrine, which allows a class certification motion to relate back to the filing of the complaint, is not the only means by which a class action can survive the mootness of the named plaintiff's individual claim. Id. at 398-99, n. 7.

In this case, Lucero filed his class action complaint on April 20, 2009, and BCR served him with a Rule 68 offer of judgment on June 2, 2009. However, Lucero continued to press his class certification motion, which was filed on February 22, 2010. Under Sosna and Geraghty, Lucero's continued pursuit of class certification demonstrates that he retains a personal stake in the litigation, even after BCR's offer of judgment.

The Tenth Circuit has recognized that a defendant cannot "pick off" a named plaintiff by tendering an offer of judgment for the full amount of their individual claim, thereby frustrating the class action mechanism. See Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 590 F.3d 1134, 1138-39 (10th Cir. 2009) (noting that "a defendant cannot moot a class action by picking off the named plaintiff"). While Clark ultimately held that the plaintiff's individual claims were not moot because the defendant had not made an offer of judgment that fully satisfied the plaintiff's claims, the court's discussion suggests that a defendant's attempt to "buy off" a named plaintiff can be seen as an attempt to frustrate the class action process.

In light of Sosna, Geraghty, and Clark, the district court erred in dismissing Lucero's complaint for lack of subject matter jurisdiction based on mootness. Lucero's continued pursuit of class certification demonstrates that he retains a personal stake in the litigation, and the district court should have allowed the class action to proceed.

II. The District Court's Dismissal of Lucero's Complaint Frustrates the Class Action Mechanism.

Allowing defendants to "pick off" named plaintiffs by tendering an offer of judgment for the full amount of their individual claims would undermine the class action device. As the Supreme Court noted in Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper, 445 U.S. 326 (1980), "the class action device was designed to permit litigation in which 'the interest of the named plaintiff is relatively small but there is a large number of persons in a similar situation.'" Id. at 338 (quoting Advisory Committee's Note to Rule 23, 39 F.R.D. 69, 103 (1966)). By dismissing Lucero's complaint, the district court effectively allowed BCR to "buy off" Lucero, thereby frustrating the class action mechanism.

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the district court's dismissal of Lucero's complaint and remand for further proceedings.
