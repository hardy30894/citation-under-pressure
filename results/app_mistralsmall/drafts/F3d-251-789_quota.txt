**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT APPELLANT CONSENTED TO THE REMOVAL AND RETENTION OF DANELSY UNDER ARTICLE 13(A) OF THE HAGUE CONVENTION.**

The Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction, incorporated into U.S. law by the International Child Abduction Remedies Act, 42 U.S.C. §§ 11601–11610, requires the prompt return of a child wrongfully removed or retained from his or her habitual residence, unless the respondent establishes one of the narrow exceptions set forth in Article 13. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 11601(a)(4); *Mozes v. Mozes*, 239 F.3d 1067, 1070 (9th Cir. 2001). One such exception is consent to the removal or retention. *See* Hague Convention, art. 13(a); *Baxter v. Baxter*, 423 F.3d 363, 370 (3d Cir. 2005). Consent must be proven by clear and convincing evidence and will not be inferred from equivocal statements or ambiguous conduct. *See* *Baxter*, 423 F.3d at 370; *Whiting v. Krus*, 391 F.3d 1, 8 (1st Cir. 2004). The party asserting consent bears the burden of proof. *See* *Whiting*, 391 F.3d at 8.

Here, the district court erred in finding that Appellant consented to Danelsy’s removal and retention. The record reveals no clear and convincing evidence of consent. Appellant’s statements to Mena were ambiguous and inconsistent. She initially claimed that she agreed to allow Mena to take Danelsy for two weeks until she could obtain proper paperwork to join her daughter. At other times, she claimed that she allowed Mena to remove and retain Danelsy provided he help her immigrate to the United States. Such equivocal statements cannot constitute clear and convincing evidence of consent. *See* *Baxter*, 423 F.3d at 370; *Whiting*, 391 F.3d at 8.

Moreover, the district court erred in relying on Appellant’s provision of Danelsy’s legal documents as evidence of consent. The Ninth Circuit has held that the transfer of a child’s documents does not, without more, constitute consent to removal or retention. *SeeMiller v. Miller*, 240 F.3d 392, 400 (4th Cir. 2001) (citing *Friedrich v. Friedrich*, 983 F.2d 1396, 1401 (6th Cir. 1993)). Here, Appellant provided Danelsy’s documents to facilitate Mena’s travel, not to authorize his retention of Danelsy. *See id.*

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT APPELLANT ACQUIESCED TO THE REMOVAL AND RETENTION OF DANELSY UNDER ARTICLE 13(B) OF THE HAGUE CONVENTION.**

The Hague Convention also permits a court to refuse to order the return of a child if the applicant “was not actually exercising the custody rights at the time of the removal or retention” or “had consented to or subsequently acquiesced in the removal or retention.” Hague Convention, art. 13(b); *Mozes*, 239 F.3d at 1070. Acquiescence requires conduct that is “unequivocal” and “clearly inconsistent with a desire to insist upon the return of the child.” *Id.* at 1071 (quoting *Friedrich*, 983 F.2d at 1401). Silence or inaction, without more, is insufficient. *Id.*

Here, the district court erred in finding that Appellant acquiesced to Danelsy’s removal and retention. Appellant’s actions immediately after Danelsy’s removal—consulting Panamanian authorities, the police, and the Institute for Children—demonstrate her unequivocal objection to the removal. *See* *Mozes*, 239 F.3d at 1071. Her subsequent efforts to secure Danelsy’s return, including traveling to the United States to pursue legal remedies, further demonstrate her lack of acquiescence. *See id.*

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT DANELSY’S BEST INTERESTS FAVORED HER REMOVAL AND RETENTION.**

The Ninth Circuit has held that the best interests of the child are not a factor in determining whether to order a child’s return under the Hague Convention. *See* *Mozes*, 239 F.3d at 1076. The Convention’s purpose is to deter international child abduction and restore the status quo ante litem. *Id.* at 1075. Thus, the district court erred in considering Danelsy’s best interests in denying Appellant’s petition. *See id.*

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court’s denial of Appellant’s petition to return Danelsy to her custody in Panama should be reversed. The district court erred in finding that Appellant consented to or acquiesced in Danelsy’s removal and retention, and in considering Danelsy’s best interests. The case should be remanded with instructions to order Danelsy’s return to Panama.
