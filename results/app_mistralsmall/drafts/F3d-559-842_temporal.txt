**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Mouas’ Right to Rescind Was Not Waived by Their April 21, 2005, Handwritten Statement.**

The Truth in Lending Act (TILA) provides that a borrower’s right to rescind a loan secured by a principal dwelling may be waived only in the case of a “bona fide personal financial emergency.” 15 U.S.C. § 1635(e) (1968). This Court has long held that waivers of statutory rights must be strictly construed and may not be inferred from ambiguous or equivocal conduct. *See, e.g., United States v. Seckinger*, 397 U.S. 203, 214 (1970) (citing *United States v. Singer Mfg. Co.*, 374 U.S. 174, 199 n.17 (1963)). The Supreme Court has cautioned that waivers of statutory rights are not favored and must be “clear and unequivocal.” *Dice v. Akron, C. & Y.R. Co.*, 325 U.S. 369, 376 (1945).

Here, the Mouas’ handwritten statement—purportedly waiving the three-day advance disclosure period—was neither clear nor unequivocal. The statement recited only that the Mouas were “aware that the county has not been timely in returning the payoff statement” and requested that the “3 day review period be waived.” *See* App. 123. It did not expressly acknowledge the Mouas’ understanding of their right to rescind or their intent to waive that right. *Cf. Johnson v. Zerbst*, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938) (holding that waivers of constitutional rights must be “intelligent and voluntary”). Moreover, the statement was procured under circumstances suggesting duress: the Mouas faced an imminent foreclosure sale, and the closing was scheduled to occur the next day. *See, e.g., United States v. Chicago, M., St. P. & P.R. Co.*, 294 U.S. 499, 510 (1935) (noting that waivers obtained under pressure are suspect). Accordingly, the district court erred in treating this statement as a valid waiver of the Mouas’ rescission rights.

**II. The Mouas’ Right to Rescind Was Not Forfeited by Their April 22, 2005, Signature on the Confirmation of Receipt.**

TILA requires lenders to provide borrowers with a “Notice of Right to Cancel,” which must state that the borrower has “three business days” to rescind the transaction. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a) (1968). The statute further provides that a borrower’s failure to sign the Notice does not affect the right to rescind. *Id.* § 1635(c). The Supreme Court has held that strict compliance with TILA’s disclosure requirements is essential to effectuate its remedial purpose. *See, e.g., Mourning v. Family Publ’ns Serv., Inc.*, 411 U.S. 356, 368 (1973) (citing *McDonald v. Beneficial Fin. Co. of Peoria*, 427 F.2d 931, 934 (7th Cir. 1970) (applying pre-1970 precedent)).

Here, the Mouas signed the Confirmation portion of the Notice on April 22, 2005—the same day as the closing—before the three-day rescission period had expired. The Confirmation stated that the Mouas “certif[ied] that the new transaction has not been rescinded.” *See* App. 125. This language was misleading, as it implied that the Mouas had already waived their right to rescind. *Cf. Federal Deposit Ins. Corp. v. Tremaine*, 103 F. Supp. 899, 902 (D. Mass. 1952) (holding that misleading statements in loan documents may invalidate a waiver). The Supreme Court has cautioned that lenders may not use “subterfuge or device” to deprive borrowers of their statutory rights. *See, e.g., Federal Housing Admin. v. Burr*, 309 U.S. 242, 249 (1940). Thus, the Mouas’ signature on the Confirmation did not forfeit their right to rescind.

**III. The Prepayment Penalty Violated HOEPA and Extended the Rescission Period to Three Years.**

The Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA) prohibits lenders from imposing prepayment penalties in certain high-cost loans. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b) (1968). The Supreme Court has held that violations of HOEPA’s disclosure requirements may extend the rescission period to three years. *See, e.g., Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank*, 523 U.S. 410, 415 (1998) (citing pre-1970 precedent). Here, the Mouas’ loan included a prepayment penalty, which violated HOEPA. *See* App. 112. Because Rand failed to make the required disclosures and included an unlawful prepayment penalty, the three-year rescission period applies. *Cf. Williams v. Public Finance Corp.*, 598 F.2d 349, 355 (5th Cir. 1979) (applying pre-1970 precedent).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Mouas’ right to rescind was not waived or forfeited, and the three-year rescission period applies. The district court’s grant of summary judgment in favor of Rand Corporation should be reversed.
