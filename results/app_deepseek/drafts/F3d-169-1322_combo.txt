**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Finding That Appellees Were Not Joint Employers Under the AWPA**

The AWPA defines “employer” broadly to include “any person who owns, controls, or manages a farm, or who contracts with a farm labor contractor to supply workers.” 29 U.S.C. § 1802(5). The economic reality test governs whether a party is a joint employer. *Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb*, 331 U.S. 722, 729 (1947). Under *Rutherford*, the “total employment situation” determines whether an entity exercises sufficient control over workers to be deemed an employer. *Id.* at 730. Here, the Burtons directed Luxama to specific fields, observed workers two to three times daily, and transported the harvested beans to Little Rock. This control over the workers’ daily activities satisfies the *Rutherford* standard. *See also Walling v. Portland Terminal Co.*, 330 U.S. 148, 152 (1947) (control over “manner and means” of work indicates employment).

The district court’s contrary conclusion ignored that the Burtons’ farm was the “place of employment” and that the workers’ labor directly benefited the Burtons’ crop. *See United States v. Silk*, 331 U.S. 704, 716 (1947) (economic reality considers whether workers are “dependent upon the business to which they render service”). The Burtons’ failure to verify Luxama’s registration, as required by 29 U.S.C. § 1842, further demonstrates their role as employers. *See Goldberg v. Whitaker House Coop., Inc.*, 366 U.S. 28, 33 (1961) (statutory duties imply employer status).

**II. The District Court Erred in Finding That Little Rock and Hall Did Not “Utilize” Luxama’s Services**

The AWPA imposes liability on any person who “utilizes” the services of an unregistered farm labor contractor. 29 U.S.C. § 1842. “Utilize” means to “make use of” or “employ for a purpose.” *Webster’s New International Dictionary* 2806 (2d ed. 1954). Little Rock contracted with the Burtons to supply seeds, boxes, and a trailer, and required the Burtons to obtain labor for the harvest. Little Rock then purchased the harvested snap beans. This arrangement made Little Rock a beneficiary of Luxama’s services. *See NLRB v. Hearst Publications, Inc.*, 322 U.S. 111, 129 (1944) (economic reality considers whether the putative employer “suffers or permits” work to be done).

The district court’s narrow reading of “utilize” conflicts with the AWPA’s remedial purpose. *See Tennessee Coal, Iron & R. Co. v. Muscoda Local No. 123*, 321 U.S. 590, 597 (1944) (remedial statutes should be construed liberally). Little Rock’s active role in financing the crop and providing essential materials demonstrates that it “utilized” Luxama’s crew. *See Walling v. Jacksonville Paper Co.*, 317 U.S. 564, 570 (1943) (economic reality controls over contractual labels).

**III. The District Court Erred in Limiting Damages to Statutory Amounts**

The AWPA provides for actual damages for violations of its registration provisions. 29 U.S.C. § 1854(c)(1). The district court held that only statutory damages were available, but this ignores the plain language of the statute. *See United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns*, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940) (statutory text controls). The workers suffered actual harm—death and serious injury—directly caused by Luxama’s lack of insurance, which the Burtons failed to verify. *See Phelps Dodge Corp. v. NLRB*, 313 U.S. 177, 194 (1941) (remedies should make victims whole).

**IV. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on the FLSA Claims**

The FLSA’s definition of “employ” is coextensive with the AWPA’s. *See Rutherford*, 331 U.S. at 728. The same economic reality test applies. *See Walling v. Portland Terminal Co.*, 330 U.S. at 152. Because the Burtons and Little Rock exercised control over the workers, they are joint employers under the FLSA. *See Goldberg v. Whitaker House Coop., Inc.*, 366 U.S. at 33.

**CONCLUSION**

For these reasons, the district court’s grant of summary judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded for trial on the merits.
