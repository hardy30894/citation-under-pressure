**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE TAX COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT MERUELO FAILED TO ESTABLISH A "BONA FIDE INDEBTEDNESS" RUNNING DIRECTLY TO HIM.**

A. **A Shareholder’s Basis in an S Corporation Includes Indebtedness Running Directly to the Shareholder.**

Section 1366(d)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code limits a shareholder’s deduction for losses to the sum of his basis in the S corporation’s stock and any indebtedness of the S corporation to the shareholder. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 1366(d)(1). Treasury Regulation § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i) clarifies that a shareholder’s basis includes “money and other property” contributed to the corporation and “indebtedness of the S corporation to the shareholder.” *See* Treas. Reg. § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i). This Court has recognized that “[a] shareholder’s basis in an S corporation is increased by any loans he makes to the corporation.” *Gilday v. Comm’r*, 103 F.3d 202, 204 (11th Cir. 1997) (citing *Perlman v. Comm’r*, 54 T.C. 1419, 1426 (1970)).

B. **The Tax Court Erred in Requiring That the Indebtedness Run Directly from Merco to Meruelo.**

The Tax Court held that Meruelo failed to establish a bona fide indebtedness running directly to him because the funds were transferred through intermediaries. This was error. The regulation does not require that the indebtedness run *directly* from the S corporation to the shareholder. Instead, it requires only that the indebtedness be *to the shareholder*. *See* Treas. Reg. § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i) (“indebtedness of the S corporation to the shareholder”). The Tax Court’s interpretation conflates the shareholder’s basis in the corporation’s indebtedness with the source of the funds used to create that indebtedness. The regulation does not impose such a limitation.

This Court has recognized that a shareholder may increase his basis by contributing capital or lending money to the corporation, regardless of the source of the funds. *SeeGilday*, 103 F.3d at 204. The Tax Court’s requirement that the indebtedness run *directly* from the S corporation to the shareholder is unsupported by the regulation and conflicts with the purpose of Subchapter S, which is to pass through losses to shareholders in proportion to their ownership. *SeeBufferd v. Comm’r*, 506 U.S. 523, 525 (1993) (“a pass-through system under which corporate income, losses, deductions, and credits are attributed to individual shareholders”).

C. **Meruelo Established a Bona Fide Indebtedness Running to Him.**

The Tax Court also erred in holding that Meruelo failed to establish a bona fide indebtedness. The record shows that Meruelo’s CPA, Luis Carreras, drafted a promissory note in March 2004 purporting to establish a $10 million unsecured line of credit for Meruelo to Merco at a six percent interest rate. Carreras further testified that, for tax years 2004 to 2008, he annually charged Merco’s line of credit for an amount equal to Meruelo’s calculated share of Merco’s net accounts payable to its affiliates. This evidence is sufficient to establish a bona fide indebtedness running to Meruelo.

The Tax Court’s reliance on *Perlman v. Comm’r*, 54 T.C. 1419 (1970), is misplaced. In *Perlman*, the Tax Court held that a shareholder’s basis was not increased by loans made by a partnership in which the shareholder held an interest because the indebtedness did not run to the shareholder. *Id.* at 1426. Here, by contrast, the indebtedness ran to Meruelo, as evidenced by the promissory note and Carreras’s testimony. The Tax Court’s reliance on *Perlman* is therefore inapposite.

II. **THE TAX COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT MERUELO FAILED TO ESTABLISH AN "ACTUAL ECONOMIC OUTLAY."**

A. **A Shareholder’s Basis Includes Funds He Contributes to the Corporation or Lends to It.**

Section 1366(d)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code and Treasury Regulation § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i) provide that a shareholder’s basis includes money and other property contributed to the corporation and indebtedness of the S corporation to the shareholder. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 1366(d)(1); *Treas. Reg. § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i)*. This Court has recognized that a shareholder’s basis is increased by any loans he makes to the corporation. *SeeGilday*, 103 F.3d at 204.

B. **Meruelo Made an Actual Economic Outlay.**

The Tax Court erred in holding that Meruelo failed to establish an actual economic outlay. Meruelo personally borrowed $4,985,035 and transferred those funds to Merco Group at Akoya, an S corporation in which he held a 50 percent interest. Akoya then transferred $5 million to Merco’s escrow account, including Meruelo’s loan proceeds. The Commissioner does not dispute that this transfer gave Meruelo a shareholder basis in that amount in Merco.

The Tax Court’s reliance on *Oren v. Comm’r*, 357 F.3d 889 (8th Cir. 2004), is misplaced. In *Oren*, the Eighth Circuit held that a shareholder did not make an actual economic outlay where the funds were borrowed by an entity in which the shareholder held an interest and then transferred to the S corporation. *Id.* at 895. Here, by contrast, Meruelo personally borrowed the funds and transferred them to an entity in which he held an interest, which then transferred the funds to Merco. This is sufficient to establish an actual economic outlay.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Tax Court erred in holding that Meruelo failed to establish a bona fide indebtedness running directly to him and failed to establish an actual economic outlay. The decision of the Tax Court should be reversed.
