**ARGUMENT**

The Internal Revenue Code requires that an assessment identify “the taxpayer” whose liability is recorded. 26 U.S.C. § 6203; 26 C.F.R. § 301.6203-1. A “taxpayer” is “any person subject to any internal revenue tax,” and a “person” expressly includes both an “individual” and a “partnership.” 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14), (a)(1). Because each Debtor is a distinct “individual” taxpayer, the timely assessments against the Partnership extended the ten-year collection period of 26 U.S.C. § 6502(a) only as to the Partnership itself. See Francis v. McNeal, 228 U.S. 695, 700 (1913) (“the partnership and individual estates both were to be administered”).

The Ninth Circuit correctly held that no assessment was made against Debtors within the three-year period of § 6501(a). The IRS therefore cannot invoke the ten-year extension to collect directly from them. Begier v. Internal Revenue Service, 496 U.S. 53, 57-58 (1990), confirms that trust-fund taxes are collected only after proper assessment against the person liable; the same principle governs here.

California partnership law supplies no alternative. Under Cal. Corp. Code § 16307(c), “a judgment against a partnership is not by itself a judgment against a partner.” The IRS obtained no judgment against any Debtor, and the three-year period for doing so has expired. Liberty Nat. Bank of Roanoke, Va. v. Bear, 276 U.S. 215, 219 (1928), and Meek v. Centre County Banking Co., 264 U.S. 499, 503 (1924), likewise treat partnership and individual liabilities as procedurally distinct.

The IRS’s reliance on Young v. Riddell, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir. 1960), is misplaced. That decision permitted a refund action only after voluntary payment; it did not authorize collection without either an individual assessment or a judgment. United States v. Coson, 286 F.2d 453, 458 (9th Cir. 1961), reinforces that an assessment naming only the partnership fails to create a lien against an unassessed partner.

Accordingly, the bankruptcy court properly disallowed the claims under 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1). The judgment should be affirmed.
