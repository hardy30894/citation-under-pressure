**ARGUMENT**

The lodestar figure for a reasonable attorney’s fee under a federal fee-shifting statute may be enhanced when the fee applicant presents “specific evidence” that “the quality of service rendered was superior to that one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged” and produced “exceptional” success. *Blum v. Stenson*, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984). The district court’s detailed findings, grounded in its direct observation of counsel’s performance over years of litigation, satisfied that standard.

In *Blum*, the Court explained that quality-of-representation and results-obtained factors “normally” are subsumed in the lodestar, yet it expressly left open the possibility of enhancement “in the rare case” supported by specific evidence. *Id.* at 899-900. The Court reiterated the point the following Term: “where a plaintiff has obtained excellent results, his attorney should recover a fully compensatory fee. Normally this will encompass all hours reasonably expended on the litigation, and indeed in some cases of exceptional success an enhancement award may be justified.” *Id.* at 901 (quoting *Hensley v. Eckerhart*, 461 U.S. 424, 435 (1983)).

*Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air*, 478 U.S. 546 (1986), confirmed rather than foreclosed that possibility. The Court again stated that “upward adjustments of the lodestar figure are still permissible … in certain ‘rare’ and ‘exceptional’ cases, supported by both ‘specific evidence’ on the record and detailed findings by the lower courts.” *Id.* at 565 (quoting *Blum*, 465 U.S. at 898-901). It conducted a case-specific inquiry rather than adopting a categorical bar.

Every circuit to address the question has read these decisions to permit enhancement on the grounds of superior performance and exceptional results when the requisite showing is made. *See, e.g.*, *Geier v. Sundquist*, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); *Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co.*, 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000); *Hyatt v. Apfel*, 195 F.3d 188, 192 (4th Cir. 1999); *Roe v. Cheyenne Mountain Conference Resort, Inc.*, 124 F.3d 1221, 1233 n.8 (10th Cir. 1997); *Shipes v. Trinity Indus.*, 987 F.2d 311, 320 (5th Cir. 1993). The Eleventh Circuit’s decision is consistent with this uniform authority.

Here the district court made precisely the findings *Blum* and *Delaware Valley I* require. It compared the hourly rates used in the lodestar calculation with the prevailing Atlanta market, noted counsel’s documented commitment of more than 30,000 hours over four years, and described the breadth and durability of the injunctive relief obtained—relief the court characterized as “historic” and “unprecedented.” Those findings rested on the court’s own observation of counsel’s work from the filing of the complaint through entry of the consent decree. Because the record contains the “specific evidence” and “detailed findings” the governing precedents demand, the enhancement was within the district court’s discretion.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
