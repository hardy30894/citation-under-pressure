The district court erred in granting partial summary judgment to Ybarra on four of the seven calls placed from the 8047 number. The TCPA imposes liability only for a call that actually employs an artificial or prerecorded voice. 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A). A plaintiff must therefore adduce evidence that such a voice was used during the call itself. See Satterfield v. Simon & Schuster, Inc., 569 F.3d 946, 949 (9th Cir. 2009); Meyer v. Portfolio Recovery Assocs., LLC, 707 F.3d 1037, 1043 (9th Cir. 2012); Breslow v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A., 755 F.3d 1265, 1267 (11th Cir. 2014).

Summary judgment is appropriate only when the movant shows the absence of a genuine dispute of material fact. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322 (1986). The court must view the evidence in the light most favorable to the nonmoving party and may not draw inferences unsupported by the record. Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 255 (1986); Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp., 475 U.S. 574, 587 (1986). Here, the Cisco Dialer played a prerecorded message solely upon detection of a positive voice. The record establishes that no positive voice was detected on calls two through five. Those calls therefore could not have used a prerecorded voice.

The district court’s contrary conclusion rested on an admission that DISH used the dialer on the 8047 line, not that every call from that line delivered a prerecorded message. Such an inference exceeds the scope of the admission and contradicts the call-detail evidence. See Osorio v. State Farm Bank, F.S.B., 746 F.3d 1242, 1251 (11th Cir. 2014); Mais v. Gulf Coast Collection Bureau, Inc., 768 F.3d 1110, 1124 (11th Cir. 2014). Because Ybarra bore the burden of proving each statutory element, the absence of evidence that a prerecorded voice was used on four calls required entry of summary judgment for DISH on those calls. Mims v. Arrow Fin. Servs., LLC, 565 U.S. 368, 373 (2012); Soppet v. Enhanced Recovery Co., LLC, 679 F.3d 637, 642 (7th Cir. 2012).

DISH concedes liability on the three calls during which a positive voice was detected. On the remaining four calls, however, the judgment must be reversed.
