ARGUMENT

The Board erred in according the '880 application the benefit of the '566 filing date. Under the statutory scheme governing continuing applications, each intermediate application must itself contain a sufficient disclosure of the subject matter claimed in the later application. Chapman v. Wintroath, 252 U.S. 126, 137 (1920) (a continuation "must be for the same invention" disclosed in the parent). The '838 and '768 applications contain only the original incorporation statement referring to an application "filed on the same day" as the '566 application. That statement does not identify the '579 application with the particularity required to incorporate its disclosure. Seymour v. Osborne, 78 U.S. 516, 547 (1870) (the specification must "put the public in possession of the invention").

An incorporation clause that depends on a same-day filing date cannot operate to bring forward material from a distinct application filed on a different date. The clause therefore fails to satisfy the continuity requirement. Smith & Griggs Mfg. Co. v. Sprague, 123 U.S. 249, 257 (1887) (the application must "disclose the invention in such full, clear, and exact terms" as to enable one skilled in the art to practice it). Because the '838 and '768 applications do not themselves disclose the '579 material, the chain is broken. The '880 application is entitled at most to the filing date of the '708 application.

The Board's contrary conclusion rests on an erroneous view that an ambiguous reference may be clarified retroactively. That view conflicts with the rule that the disclosure must be complete within the four corners of each application as filed. Schriber-Schroth Co. v. Cleveland Trust Co., 305 U.S. 47, 57 (1938) (new matter may not be introduced to enlarge the original disclosure). The judgment should be reversed.
