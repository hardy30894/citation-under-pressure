**ARGUMENT**

Federal jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1338(a) extends only to cases in which federal patent law creates the cause of action or the plaintiff’s right to relief necessarily depends on resolution of a substantial question of federal patent law. *Christianson v. Colt Indus. Operating Corp.*, 486 U.S. 800, 808-09 (1988). Minton’s malpractice claim arises under Texas law and requires only that he prove a “case within a case” by showing that timely assertion of the experimental-use exception would have altered the outcome of the underlying infringement suit. *Alexander v. Turtur & Assocs.*, 146 S.W.3d 113, 117 (Tex. 2004).

The four-part test of *Grable & Sons Metal Prods., Inc. v. Darue Eng’g & Mfg.*, 545 U.S. 308, 314 (2005), is not satisfied. Although the on-sale bar and experimental-use exception are disputed, they are not substantial within the meaning of *Grable*. Resolution of those issues would affect only the parties’ private rights and would not control numerous other cases. *Empire Healthchoice Assurance, Inc. v. McVeigh*, 547 U.S. 677, 700-01 (2006); *Merrell Dow Pharm. Inc. v. Thompson*, 478 U.S. 804, 814 (1986).

Federal adjudication of every state malpractice claim that touches patent law would upset the congressionally approved balance between federal and state judicial responsibilities. *Grable*, 545 U.S. at 314. State courts routinely decide such claims without threatening uniformity in patent administration. *Singh v. Duane Morris LLP*, 538 F.3d 384, 340 (5th Cir. 2008); *New Tek Mfg., Inc. v. Beehner*, 702 N.W.2d 336, 347 (Neb. 2005); *Roof Technical Servs., Inc. v. Hill*, 679 F. Supp. 2d 749, 753-54 (N.D. Tex. 2010); *Genelink Biosciences, Inc. v. Colby*, 722 F. Supp. 2d 592, 602 (D.N.J. 2010).

Because the patent issues embedded in Minton’s malpractice action are neither substantial nor capable of disturbing the federal-state balance, exclusive federal jurisdiction does not lie. The judgment of the Texas Supreme Court should be reversed.
