ARGUMENT

The text of 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4) provides that a TPS recipient “shall be considered as being in, and maintaining, lawful status as a nonimmigrant.” That statutory command satisfies the “inspected and admitted or paroled” requirement of 8 U.S.C. § 1255(a). See Ramirez v. Brown, 852 F.3d 954, 960 (9th Cir. 2017) (“By the very nature of obtaining lawful nonimmigrant status, the alien goes through inspection and is deemed admitted.”). The INA’s definition of “admission” as “the lawful entry of the alien into the United States after inspection and authorization by an immigration officer,” 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(13)(A), is met once the government confers nonimmigrant status through TPS.

This reading respects the distinction between “admission” and “status” drawn in Hanif v. Att’y Gen., 694 F.3d 479, 485 (3d Cir. 2012), and Taveras v. Att’y Gen., 731 F.3d 281, 290 (3d Cir. 2013). Section 1254a(f)(4) does not merely preserve status; it deems the recipient to occupy the precise nonimmigrant posture that § 1255(a) requires. See also Gomez v. Lynch, 831 F.3d 652, 658 (5th Cir. 2016) (recognizing that status ordinarily follows a procedurally regular inspection). Congress’s choice to equate TPS with nonimmigrant status therefore supplies the missing procedural element.

The structure of § 1255 confirms the point. Subsection (k)(1) permits adjustment when an alien is “present in the United States pursuant to a lawful admission.” TPS supplies that lawful admission by operation of § 1254a(f)(4). Had Congress wished to withhold this benefit from TPS recipients, it would have done so expressly, as it did for other categories in § 1255(c)(2). See I.N.S. v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421, 432 (1987).

Finally, the purpose of TPS—to permit qualified aliens to remain lawfully without removal—would be frustrated if the same grant of status were simultaneously deemed insufficient to invoke the adjustment mechanism Congress made available to nonimmigrants. The Third Circuit’s contrary conclusion cannot be reconciled with the plain language of § 1254a(f)(4) or with the holdings of the Sixth and Ninth Circuits.
