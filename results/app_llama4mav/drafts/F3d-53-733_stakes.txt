## Argument

### I. The District Court Erred in Allowing American Steamship's Expert to Testify as to the Cause of the Line's Parting

The district court abused its discretion when it permitted American Steamship's expert to offer an opinion on the cause of the line's parting. The expert's testimony was not based on specialized knowledge, skill, or experience, and thus did not meet the requirements for expert testimony under Federal Rule of Evidence 702. As the Supreme Court held in Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., 509 U.S. 579, 589 (1993), expert testimony must be grounded in reliable principles and methods. Here, the expert's opinion was speculative and lacked a foundation in scientific or technical expertise. The admission of this testimony was prejudicial, as it influenced the jury's determination of comparative negligence. See Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, 526 U.S. 137, 152 (1999) (expert testimony must be based on a reliable methodology).

### II. The District Court Erred in Denying Cook's Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law on the Issue of Unseaworthiness

The district court should have granted Cook's motion for judgment as a matter of law on the issue of unseaworthiness because the evidence presented at trial established that the vessel was unseaworthy as a matter of law. A vessel is considered unseaworthy if it lacks proper equipment or if its equipment is not properly maintained. See Mitchell v. Trawler Racer, Inc., 362 U.S. 539, 550 (1960). Here, the line supporting the bos'n's chair was not properly maintained, as it had been left rigged during the summer months and was not inspected or replaced. Furthermore, the crew's haste in rigging the boom and the bos'n's chair, due to the rough sea and Captain Derry's orders, contributed to the unseaworthy condition of the vessel. The jury's finding of unseaworthiness was therefore not a matter of dispute, and the district court should have granted Cook's motion. See Ferrara v. A. & V. Fish, Inc., 509 F.2d 369, 371 (2d Cir. 1975) (unseaworthiness is a question of law when the evidence is undisputed).

### III. The District Court Did Not Err in Admitting Evidence of Cook's Alcohol-Related Ailments, but this Issue is Moot in Light of the Errors Committed by the District Court

The district court's admission of evidence regarding Cook's alcohol-related ailments was within its discretion, as it was relevant to Cook's damages claim. See Fitzgerald v. A.L. Burbank & Co., 451 F.2d 670, 681 (2d Cir. 1971) (evidence of pre-existing conditions is admissible to mitigate damages). However, given the errors committed by the district court in admitting the expert's testimony and denying Cook's motion for judgment as a matter of law on unseaworthiness, this issue will likely be revisited on remand.

### IV. The Judgment Should be Vacated and the Case Remanded for a New Trial

Given the errors committed by the district court, the judgment entered below must be vacated, and the case remanded for a new trial. See Havens v. Gross & Co., 681 F.2d 457, 466 (6th Cir. 1982) (reversal and remand for new trial is warranted when errors affect the outcome of the trial). On remand, the district court should ensure that expert testimony is properly vetted and that the issue of unseaworthiness is resolved as a matter of law.
