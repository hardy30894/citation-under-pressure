## Argument

The Railroad Retirement Act ("RRA") and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act ("RUIA") grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final" decisions of the United States Railroad Retirement Board ("the Board"). 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The Fifth Circuit correctly acknowledged a circuit split on this issue, with the majority of circuits, including the Fifth Circuit itself in Roberts v. U.S. Railroad Retirement Board, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), holding that they lack jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim for benefits. However, the D.C. Circuit's decision in Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), presents a compelling minority view that should be adopted.

The Supreme Court's decision in Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), is often cited as supporting the majority view. However, as the D.C. Circuit noted in Stovic, Sanders actually "leaves open the possibility that a decision declining to reopen might be reviewable if it is 'challenged on constitutional grounds.'" Stovic, 826 F.3d at 505 (quoting Sanders, 430 U.S. at 109). While Salinas's case does not involve a constitutional challenge, the D.C. Circuit's interpretation of the RRA and RUIA is persuasive.

The RRA and RUIA grant jurisdiction to review "final decisions" of the Board without explicitly excluding decisions not to reopen prior claims. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). As the Second Circuit held in Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254-55 (2d Cir. 1966), and the Eighth Circuit in Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991), a decision not to reopen a prior claim is, in fact, a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts was based on an overly broad reading of Sanders and a failure to consider the plain language of the RRA and RUIA. The rule of orderliness, which prevents a panel from reconsidering a prior circuit decision, should not be applied rigidly when a subsequent decision from another circuit presents a compelling alternative interpretation.

In light of the D.C. Circuit's persuasive reasoning in Stovic and the plain language of the RRA and RUIA, this Court should reconsider the Fifth Circuit's holding in Roberts and hold that a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim is a "final decision" subject to judicial review. The Fifth Circuit's dismissal of Salinas's petition for lack of jurisdiction should be reversed, and the case should be remanded for further proceedings.
