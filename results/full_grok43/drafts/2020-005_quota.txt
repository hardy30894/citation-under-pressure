**ARGUMENT**

The Due Process Clause permits specific jurisdiction over Ford because the claims “arise out of or relate to” its Montana contacts. *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court*, 592 U.S. 351, 362 (2021) (quoting *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255, 262 (2017)). Specific jurisdiction requires that the defendant have “purposefully availed itself of the privilege of conducting activities within the forum State,” *Hanson v. Denckla*, 357 U.S. 235, 253 (1958), and that the suit have a sufficient connection to those activities, *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 319 (1945).

Ford has long “systematically served a market” in Montana by selling thousands of vehicles, operating dealerships, and advertising repair services there. *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980). These contacts are not “random, fortuitous, or attenuated.” *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, 471 U.S. 462, 475 (1985). They create the very expectation of being haled into court when a Montana resident is injured by a defective Ford product. *Id.* at 474.

The requisite relationship exists even though the particular Explorer was not sold in Montana. The claims concern design, manufacture, and warnings for the same model Ford regularly markets and services in the State. *Ford*, 592 U.S. at 365. This “relates to” Ford’s Montana activities in the same way that a claim for injury from a defective component relates to the defendant’s forum sales of that component. *See Asahi Metal Industry Co. v. Superior Court*, 480 U.S. 102, 112 (1987) (plurality opinion); *Helicopteros Nacionales de Colombia, S.A. v. Hall*, 466 U.S. 408, 414 n.8 (1984).

General jurisdiction is unavailable because Ford is neither incorporated nor headquartered in Montana. *Daimler AG v. Bauman*, 571 U.S. 117, 137 (2014); *Goodyear Dunlop Tires Operations, S.A. v. Brown*, 564 U.S. 915, 919 (2011). Specific jurisdiction therefore supplies the only basis, and the contacts here satisfy the “arise out of or relate to” standard without requiring that the precise vehicle have been sold in Montana. *Bristol-Myers*, 582 U.S. at 262.

Exercising jurisdiction comports with “traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.” *International Shoe*, 326 U.S. at 316. Montana has a strong interest in providing a forum for its residents injured on its highways by products Ford deliberately placed in its stream of commerce. *World-Wide Volkswagen*, 444 U.S. at 292. Ford cannot plausibly claim surprise. *Burger King*, 471 U.S. at 475.

The Montana Supreme Court correctly held that personal jurisdiction exists.
