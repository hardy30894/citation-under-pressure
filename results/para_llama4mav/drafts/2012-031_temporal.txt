The Supreme Court has emphasized the importance of a rigorous analysis in determining whether the requirements of Rule 23 have been met. As the Court stated in Eisen v. Carlisle & Jacquelin, 84 S.Ct. 1420 (1964), a "class action is maintainable only if the trial court is satisfied, after a rigorous analysis, that the prerequisites of Rule 23(a) have been satisfied." Id. at 1427 (quoting Advisory Committee's Note to Rule 23).

However, the court failed to critically examine the methodology used by Dr. McClave, and instead simply accepted his conclusions. This failure to rigorously analyze the evidence presented is a clear error, as it is the responsibility of the court to "delve beyond the pleadings to determine whether the requirements for class certification are satisfied."

Clemens Pottery Co., 66 S.Ct. 1187 (1946), which emphasized the importance of ensuring that the plaintiff has presented sufficient evidence to support their claims. Id. at 1194.
