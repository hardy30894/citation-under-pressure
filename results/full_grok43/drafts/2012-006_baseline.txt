**ARGUMENT**

A defendant who withdraws from a conspiracy before the limitations period begins is no longer a member of the continuing offense. The government therefore bears the burden of proving beyond a reasonable doubt that the accused remained a participant during the five-year period preceding the indictment. *Grunewald v. United States*, 353 U.S. 391, 396-97 (1957); *United States v. Kissel*, 218 U.S. 601, 607-08 (1910).

Withdrawal terminates a conspirator’s liability for subsequent acts of co-conspirators. *Hyde v. United States*, 225 U.S. 347, 369 (1912). Once withdrawal is shown, the conspiracy offense as to that defendant is complete at the moment of withdrawal. *United States v. Borelli*, 336 F.2d 376, 388 (2d Cir. 1964). The statute of limitations therefore begins to run from that date. *United States v. Santos*, 553 U.S. 507, 520 (2008) (plurality opinion) (limitations period for conspiracy runs from last overt act by the defendant or, after withdrawal, from the date of withdrawal).

Because membership in the conspiracy during the limitations period is an element the government must establish, the burden of persuasion remains with the prosecution. *In re Winship*, 397 U.S. 358, 364 (1970). The government must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt; it cannot shift that burden by requiring the defendant to disprove continued participation. *Mullaney v. Wilbur*, 421 U.S. 684, 703-04 (1975).

The court of appeals erred in treating withdrawal as irrelevant to the government’s evidentiary obligation. When the evidence shows the original conspirators split before the limitations window, the government must affirmatively demonstrate that each defendant it prosecutes was still a member during that window. *United States v. Moore*, 651 F.3d 30, 78 (D.C. Cir. 2011) (per curiam) (government must prove defendant’s knowing participation throughout the charged period). Failure to do so leaves the conviction unsupported by proof beyond a reasonable doubt on an essential element.

The judgment should be reversed.
