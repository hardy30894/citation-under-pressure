The Ninth Circuit erred in exempting documents relating to the effects of explosions from disclosure under the FOIA because they are "predominantly" for internal use and present a risk of circumventing agency regulation. The court's adoption of the "predominantly internal" standard for Exemption 2 is unfounded and contravenes the statutory text and Supreme Court precedent.

Exemption 2 shields matters "related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency." 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Supreme Court has interpreted this exemption narrowly, emphasizing that it applies to materials that are not of "genuine and significant public interest" and pose a risk of circumvention of agency regulation. Dep't of the Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S. 352, 369 (1976). The Ninth Circuit's prior caselaw, including Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), limited Exemption 2 to "law enforcement materials" that risk circumvention of agency regulation. The court's recent expansion to a "predominantly internal" standard, borrowed from the D.C. Circuit's decision in Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 670 F.2d 1051 (D.C. Cir. 1981), is not supported by the statutory text or Supreme Court precedent.

The "predominantly internal" standard is overly broad and threatens to swallow the rule of narrow construction for FOIA exemptions. See U.S. Dep't of Justice v. Tax Analysts, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989); Rose, 425 U.S. at 361. The Navy's ESQD arc maps, which are used to design and construct ammunition storage facilities, are not "related solely" to internal personnel rules and practices. Rather, they contain information relevant to the safety of the surrounding community, which is of genuine and significant public interest.

Furthermore, Exemption 7(F), which protects information compiled for law enforcement purposes that could reasonably be expected to endanger life or physical safety, is inapplicable here. The ESQD arc maps were not compiled for law enforcement purposes, and the Navy's affidavits do not establish a credible risk of harm. See Church of Scientology v. Dep't of the Army, 611 F.2d 738, 748 (9th Cir. 1979).

The Navy's reluctance to disclose the ESQD arc maps may be driven by a desire to avoid political controversy, rather than a genuine concern for national security. The Navy's voluntary disclosure of similar maps for the Bangor base, which is located farther from populated areas, suggests that the agency is selectively withholding information based on local politics rather than a legitimate security concern. If the Navy truly believes that disclosure of the ESQD arc maps poses a national security risk, it should classify the documents under Exemption 1, rather than relying on an overly broad interpretation of Exemption 2. The Ninth Circuit's decision should be reversed, and the Navy should be required to disclose the ESQD arc maps under FOIA.
