**ARGUMENT**

The First Amendment prohibits the government from discriminating against speech based on its viewpoint. *Rosenberger v. Rector & Visitors of Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995). Here, the School District opened its facilities to the general public for secular use but excluded religious speech, constituting impermissible viewpoint discrimination.

**I. The School District’s Facilities Constitute a Limited Public Forum**

The School District’s facilities are a "limited public forum" designated by the government for use by the public for expressive activity. *Hazelwood School Dist. v. Kuhlmeier*, 484 U.S. 260, 273 (1988). The Supreme Court has identified schools as "limited public forums" where the government has designated the property for use by the public for expressive activity. *Perry Education Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983). The District’s own rules allowed the use of facilities for "non-religious purposes" and permitted the use of the auditorium for secular events, such as volleyball. This designation clearly classifies the property as a limited public forum.

**II. The District Opened the Forum to the General Public**

The District’s actions demonstrate that it opened the forum to the general public. The District allowed the use of its facilities for secular purposes while maintaining a policy that specifically excluded religious use. *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 273 (1981). In *Widmar*, the Court held that a state university could not deny access to religious meetings on campus if it allowed secular meetings. The Court reasoned that "a rule against religious worship cannot be justified by the mere fact that it is neutral with respect to religion." *Id.* at 273. Similarly, in *Mergens v. Bd. of Educ.*, 496 U.S. 226, 247 (1990), the Court held that a high school created a limited open forum by allowing noncurriculum-related student groups to use school facilities. The District’s allowance of secular groups constitutes the opening of the forum.

**III. Excluding Religious Speech Violates the First Amendment**

The exclusion of religious speech violates the First Amendment. In *Rosenberger*, the Court held that a university could not exclude religious publications from a student activities fund. The Court stated that "the government may not refuse to subsidize a speaker merely because the speaker's views are religious." *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 829. This principle applies with equal force here. The District’s policy of allowing secular use while banning religious use is a classic example of viewpoint discrimination. *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*, 491 U.S. 781, 791 (1989). The District’s actions cannot be justified as a reasonable regulation of a nonpublic forum. *International Soc’y For Krishna Consciousness v. Lee*, 925 F.2d 576, 580 (2d Cir. 1991). The facilities here were not nonpublic forums; they were limited public forums opened to the general public. *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 788, 802 (1985). Therefore, the District’s denial of access to Lamb's Chapel was unconstitutional.
