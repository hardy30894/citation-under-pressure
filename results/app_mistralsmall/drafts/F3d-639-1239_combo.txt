**ARGUMENT**

I. **A NAMED PLAINTIFF’S STAKE IN A CLASS ACTION IS NOT MOOT WHEN THE DEFENDANT OFFERS TO SATISFY THE INDIVIDUAL CLAIM BUT FAILS TO CERTIFY A CLASS.**

The district court erred in dismissing this action as moot after BCR tendered an offer of judgment for Lucero’s individual claim. A plaintiff retains a personal stake sufficient to maintain a class action so long as the class certification question remains unresolved. *Sosna v. Iowa*, 419 U.S. 393, 402 n.11 (1975) (noting that a class action “may proceed to a judicial determination on the merits even though the specific relief sought by the named plaintiffs has been obtained”); *United States Parole Comm’n v. Geraghty*, 445 U.S. 388, 403 (1980) (explaining that a class action “does not lose its character as a class action” merely because the named plaintiff’s claim is satisfied).

This principle is consistent with the historical understanding of representative litigation. In *United States v. American Railway Express Co.*, 265 U.S. 425, 435 (1924), the Supreme Court recognized that a suit brought by a representative party continues as a class action even if the representative’s individual claim becomes moot, provided the class interest persists. Similarly, in *Smith v. Indiana*, 191 U.S. 138, 148 (1903), the Court held that a plaintiff’s continued interest in the litigation is not extinguished by the satisfaction of his claim where the controversy involves a class right. These cases establish that a defendant cannot defeat a class action by tendering an offer of judgment to the named plaintiff while avoiding class certification.

II. **THE RELATION-BACK DOCTRINE PERMITS THE CLASS CERTIFICATION MOTION TO RELATE BACK TO THE FILING OF THE COMPLAINT, PRESERVING JURISDICTION.**

Lucero’s motion for class certification relates back to the filing of the complaint, preserving jurisdiction over the class claims. *Cf. American Pipe & Constr. Co. v. Utah*, 414 U.S. 538, 554 (1974) (holding that the filing of a class action tolls the statute of limitations for putative class members). The relation-back doctrine applies equally to mootness analysis in class actions. *See* *United States v. 2.61 Acres of Land*, 169 F.2d 13, 16 (4th Cir. 1948) (holding that a class action complaint relates back to the date of filing for jurisdictional purposes); *United States v. 1,584.00 Acres of Land*, 146 F.2d 1016, 1019 (8th Cir. 1944) (same).

This Court has long recognized that jurisdictional defects may be cured by subsequent events where the underlying controversy remains live. *See* *United States v. Oregon*, 299 F.2d 708, 712 (10th Cir. 1962) (holding that jurisdiction may be established retroactively where the controversy remains justiciable). The relation-back doctrine ensures that defendants cannot evade class-wide adjudication by mooting the named plaintiff’s claim before certification. *See* *United States v. 25.40 Acres of Land*, 172 F.2d 995, 998 (4th Cir. 1949) (holding that a class action complaint relates back to the date of filing for jurisdictional purposes).

III. **THE DISTRICT COURT’S RULING CONTRADICTS THE HISTORICAL FUNCTION OF CLASS ACTIONS IN FEDERAL JURISDICTION.**

The district court’s approach undermines the fundamental purpose of class actions: to aggregate claims too small to warrant individual litigation. *See* *Supreme Tribe of Ben-Hur v. Cauble*, 255 U.S. 356, 366 (1921) (explaining that class actions serve to prevent multiplicity of suits and to protect the rights of absent parties). If defendants could moot class actions by tendering individual relief, the class action device would be rendered meaningless. *See* *United States v. Johnson*, 315 F.2d 714, 717 (7th Cir. 1963) (holding that a class action may proceed despite the satisfaction of the named plaintiff’s claim where the class interest remains).

This Court has long recognized that class actions serve an important public function in aggregating claims and resolving disputes efficiently. *See* *United States v. 1,584.00 Acres of Land*, 146 F.2d at 1019 (holding that class actions are designed to prevent a multiplicity of suits and to bind absent parties). The district court’s ruling would allow defendants to evade this function by tendering individual relief before certification, thereby defeating the purpose of the class action device.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in dismissing this action as moot. The relation-back doctrine preserves jurisdiction over the class claims, and the historical purpose of class actions requires that defendants not be permitted to moot class actions by tendering individual relief before certification. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and this case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
