ARGUMENT

The decision by the Railroad Retirement Board ("the Board") denying Manfredo Salinas's request to reopen his prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The plain language of the statute does not explicitly limit review to decisions on the merits after administrative appeals have been exhausted. However, as the D.C. Circuit correctly held in Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), the Railroad Retirement Act grants the Court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Board, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), which held that the court lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim, was wrongly decided. The Roberts court relied on Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), which interpreted a different statute, the Social Security Act. The Supreme Court's holding in Sanders was based on the specific language of 42 U.S.C. § 405(g), which explicitly limits review to "final decisions" made after a hearing. In contrast, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) does not contain similar language, and therefore, the Court should not follow the same restrictive interpretation.

Furthermore, the majority of circuits that have followed Roberts, including the Fourth, Sixth, Seventh, and Tenth Circuits, have done so without fully considering the differences between the Social Security Act and the Railroad Retirement Act. As the D.C. Circuit noted in Stovic, "the RRA's text, structure, and purpose all support the conclusion that the Board's denial of a request to reopen is reviewable." Stovic, 826 F.3d at 504.

The Court should reconsider the holding in Roberts and follow the reasoning of the D.C. Circuit in Stovic. The Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a final decision subject to judicial review, and the Court has jurisdiction to review it under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). 

In Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253 (2d Cir. 1966), the Second Circuit held that a decision by the Board refusing to reopen a claim was reviewable. The Court should adopt a similar approach, as it is more consistent with the language and purpose of the Railroad Retirement Act. By doing so, the Court will ensure that the Board's decisions are subject to meaningful review and that claimants like Salinas have a fair opportunity to challenge the Board's determinations.
