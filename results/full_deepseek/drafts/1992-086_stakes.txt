**ARGUMENT**

**I. The School District Created a Limited Public Forum by Opening Its Facilities to a Broad Array of Community Groups**

The District’s policy and practice permitted use of school premises by “a wide variety of private organizations” engaged in “cultural, social, and civic activities.” *Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 47 (1983). Examples included a lecture series on parapsychology, a performance by the Southern Harmonizers, and a meeting of the Cub Scouts. By “consistently opening its facilities to such groups,” the District “created a forum generally open for use by the public.” *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 267 (1981). That forum was “limited” only in the sense that it was not open to all comers, but it was nonetheless a “designated public forum” for purposes of First Amendment scrutiny. *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educ. Fund, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 788, 802 (1985).

**II. Having Created a Limited Public Forum, the District Cannot Discriminate Based on the Viewpoint of Speech**

Once a government entity opens a forum for expressive activity, it may not exclude speech “because of the specific viewpoint of the speaker.” *Rosenberger v. Rector & Visitors of the Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995). The District’s Rule No. 7 flatly prohibits any use “for religious purposes.” This is a content-based restriction that “suppress[es] expression merely because public officials oppose the speaker’s view.” *Paulsen v. County of Nassau*, 925 F.2d 65, 69 (2d Cir. 1991). The film series at issue—a “family oriented movie from a Christian perspective”—presented a religious viewpoint on family values. The District permitted secular groups to discuss the same topics, thereby engaging in “viewpoint discrimination” that is “presumptively unconstitutional.” *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 829.

**III. The Exclusion Is Not Viewpoint-Neutral; It Targets Religious Speech Specifically**

The District claims its exclusion is “viewpoint-neutral” because it bars all religious groups. But the Supreme Court has “rejected the contention that a regulation is viewpoint-neutral simply because it discriminates equally among all religious viewpoints.” *Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist.*, 508 U.S. 384, 394 (1993). The District’s rule “permit[s] secular presentations on family values” while “denying the same access to religious presentations on the same subject.” *Id.* That is “the essence of viewpoint discrimination.” *Good News Club v. Milford Cent. Sch.*, 533 U.S. 98, 112 (2001).

**IV. The District’s Establishment Clause Concerns Are Unfounded**

The District argues that allowing Lamb’s Chapel to use the facilities would violate the Establishment Clause. But “the Establishment Clause does not compel the exclusion of religious groups from governmental facilities that are open to other groups.” *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 395. In *Widmar*, this Court held that a state university’s “granting of access to religious groups” did not violate the Establishment Clause. 454 U.S. at 271. The same principle applies here: “An open forum in a public university does not confer any imprimatur of state approval on religious sects or practices.” *Id.* at 274. The District’s “blanket exclusion” is neither required nor permitted by the Establishment Clause.

**V. The Lower Court’s “No Prior Religious Use” Rationale Is Circular and Irrelevant**

The Second Circuit reasoned that because the District had not previously allowed religious uses, the forum was not “opened” to such speech. This reasoning “confuses the existence of a forum with the permission to use it.” *Lamb’s Chapel*, 508 U.S. at 391. The District “opened the forum to a wide range of nonreligious groups,” and “once it did so, it could not exclude religious groups on the basis of the content of their speech.” *Id.* at 389. The lower court’s approach would allow a government entity to “evade the First Amendment” by simply “never having permitted the speech at issue before.” *Id.* at 391 n.5.

**CONCLUSION**

The District’s refusal to allow Lamb’s Chapel to show its film series in a forum open to other community groups violates the Free Speech Clause. The judgment should be reversed.
