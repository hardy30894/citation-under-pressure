**ARGUMENT**

**I. The National Park Service possesses broad authority to regulate activities within the Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve.**

The NPS is the manager of the Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve, a unit of the National Park System. The federal government has broad power to control activities within its jurisdiction. *United States v. Causby*, 328 U.S. 256, 261 (1946) (holding that the federal government has the power to control the airspace over private land). Similarly, the federal government has authority over national forests, which are analogous to national parks. *United States v. New Mexico*, 438 U.S. 696, 701 (1978) (recognizing the federal government's power to manage national forests). Because the Preserve is a federal entity, the NPS has the authority to regulate activities within its boundaries, including the use of hovercrafts.

**II. ANILCA § 103(c) does not limit the NPS’s authority to federal lands.**

Sturgeon argues that ANILCA § 103(c) limits the NPS’s authority to "public lands." This interpretation is foreclosed by the plain text of the statute and the agency’s reasonable interpretation thereof. *United States v. Mead Corp.*, 533 U.S. 218, 226 (2001) (stating that the starting point for statutory interpretation is the text of the statute). The NPS’s interpretation of its own regulations is entitled to deference. *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 139 S. Ct. 2400, 2415 (2019) (requiring courts to give effect to an agency's reasonable interpretation of its own regulations). *Chevron U.S.A., Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, 842-43 (1984) (holding that courts must defer to an agency's reasonable interpretation of ambiguous statutes). The NPS’s authority to regulate the Preserve is not limited to federal lands; it extends to the entire unit, including state-owned lands and waters.

**III. The NPS’s authority over navigable waters within the unit is valid.**

The Nation River is a navigable waterway. The federal government possesses a "navigational servitude" over navigable waters, which allows it to regulate activities on those waters. *United States v. California*, 332 U.S. 19, 23 (1947) (recognizing the federal government's power to regulate navigable waters). *United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes, Inc.*, 474 U.S. 121, 126 (1985) (affirming the federal government's power to regulate navigable waters). This authority allows the NPS to ban the operation of hovercrafts on the Nation River, even if the submerged lands are state-owned.

**IV. The NPS’s authority over state lands within the unit is valid.**

The NPS’s authority extends to state lands within the Preserve. *Alaska v. Native Village of Venetie Tribal Government*, 522 U.S. 526, 540 (1998) (holding that the federal government has authority over Indian lands within units of the National Park System). Furthermore, ANILCA does not strip the NPS of authority over state lands within the system. *United States v. John (Katie John III)*, 720 F.3d 1214, 1220 (9th Cir. 2013) (recognizing that ANILCA does not strip the NPS of authority over state lands within the system). The NPS’s hovercraft ban is a valid exercise of its regulatory control over the Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve.
