ARGUMENT

A district court’s failure to advise a defendant of the right to appeal, in violation of former Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2), does not furnish a basis for collateral relief when the defendant knew of that right and elected not to appeal. In Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424 (1962), this Court held that an omission at sentencing supplies a ground for habeas relief only if it constitutes “a fundamental defect which inherently results in a complete miscarriage of justice.” Id. at 428. The same standard governs violations of the Federal Rules in collateral proceedings. United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780, 783–84 (1979); United States v. Addonizio, 442 U.S. 178, 185 (1979).

The evidentiary hearing established that Peguero was aware of his appellate rights at sentencing. That finding removes any possibility of prejudice. Where the defendant already possesses the information the rule requires the court to convey, the rule’s purpose has been fulfilled and the omission is harmless. See Timmreck, 441 U.S. at 784 (collateral relief unavailable absent a showing that the error “worked to his actual and substantial disadvantage”).

The court of appeals therefore correctly subjected the Rule 32 violation to harmless-error review and denied relief. Because Peguero suffered no prejudice, the judgment should be affirmed.
