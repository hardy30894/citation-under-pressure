**ARGUMENT**

I. The Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment requires a trial court to ensure that a defendant who seeks to waive counsel and plead guilty possesses the capacity for a "reasoned choice" among the available alternatives. *Godinez v. Moran*, 509 U.S. 389, 399-400 (1993) (citing *Harding v. Lewis*, 834 F.2d 853, 856 (9th Cir. 1987)); *Evans v. Raines*, 800 F.2d 884, 887 (9th Cir. 1986) (citing *Boykin v. Alabama*, 395 U.S. 238, 243 (1969)). This standard is more exacting than the minimal showing required for competency to stand trial. *Drope v. Missouri*, 420 U.S. 162, 172 (1975) (citing *Pate v. Robinson*, 383 U.S. 375, 387 (1966)); *Dusky v. United States*, 362 U.S. 402, 402 (1960) (per curiam). A defendant competent to stand trial may still lack the mental capacity to make a voluntary, knowing, and intelligent waiver of constitutional rights. *Sieling v. Eyman*, 478 F.2d 211, 214 (9th Cir. 1973) (citing *Johnson v. Zerbst*, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938)).

II. The trial court’s failure to apply the heightened "reasoned choice" standard violated due process. The record contained substantial evidence of Moran’s incompetence at the time he waived counsel and pleaded guilty. *Harding*, 834 F.2d at 856 (citing *United States v. Veatch*, 674 F.2d 1217, 1223 (9th Cir. 1981)); *Chavez v. United States*, 656 F.2d 512, 518 (9th Cir. 1981). Moran had attempted suicide months earlier, was heavily medicated, and gave monosyllabic responses to the court’s inquiries. *Moran v. Godinez*, 972 F.2d 263, 267 (9th Cir. 1992). Under these circumstances, the trial court had a constitutional obligation to conduct a competency hearing sua sponte. *Sieling*, 478 F.2d at 214 (citing *Pate*, 383 U.S. at 384); *Evans v. Raines*, 705 F.2d 1479, 1480 (9th Cir. 1983).

III. The state court’s post-conviction review did not cure the error. The state applied the wrong legal standard—competency to stand trial rather than "reasoned choice"—and relied on psychiatric evaluations that addressed only the former. *Moran*, 972 F.2d at 270-71 (citing *Townsend v. Sain*, 372 U.S. 293, 315 (1963)). The federal habeas court must independently review the record under the correct standard. *Harding*, 834 F.2d at 856 (citing *Miller v. Fenton*, 474 U.S. 104, 116 (1985)); *Terrovona v. Kincheloe*, 852 F.2d 424, 427 (9th Cir. 1988).

IV. The Ninth Circuit correctly concluded that the record did not support a finding that Moran possessed the requisite capacity for a "reasoned choice." *Moran*, 972 F.2d at 272-73. The psychiatric reports focused on Moran’s ability to assist counsel and understand the proceedings—factors relevant to competency to stand trial, not to waiver of constitutional rights. *Drope*, 420 U.S. at 172; *Dusky*, 362 U.S. at 402. The reports did not address the effects of medication or Moran’s ability to make a voluntary, knowing, and intelligent waiver. *Moran*, 972 F.2d at 271-72.

V. The trial court’s failure to apply the heightened standard and conduct a competency hearing violated due process. The judgment below should be affirmed.
