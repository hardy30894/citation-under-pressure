**ARGUMENT**

I. The case is not moot because a request for nominal damages keeps a live controversy in court.

A case becomes moot only when it is “impossible for a court to grant any effectual relief whatever to the prevailing party.” *Knox v. Service Employees International Union, Local 1000*, 567 U.S. 298, 307 (2012). That demanding standard is not satisfied here. Petitioners allege that respondent violated their First Amendment rights, and they seek nominal damages for that completed constitutional injury. This Court has long recognized that a damages claim supplies the necessary live controversy even when equitable relief is no longer available. *Powell v. McCormack*, 395 U.S. 486, 496-500 (1969); *Memphis Community School District v. Stachura*, 477 U.S. 299, 307-08 (1986).

The Eleventh Circuit’s contrary rule cannot be reconciled with settled mootness doctrine. A retrospective damages claim seeks redress for a past wrong, and nominal damages are the classic vehicle for vindicating the deprivation of an absolute right where actual damages may be difficult to prove. *Carey v. Piphus*, 435 U.S. 247, 266-67 (1978). As this Court explained in *Farrar v. Hobby*, “nominal damages” are the appropriate award when a plaintiff establishes a violation but cannot prove actual injury. 506 U.S. 103, 112-13 (1992). That is precisely why a nominal-damages claim is not rendered moot by later policy changes: the completed violation remains redressable by at least symbolic relief. *See* *Uzuegbunam v. Preczewski*, 592 U.S. ___, slip op. at 8-10 (2021).

II. Repeal of the challenged policy during litigation does not eliminate liability for the prior constitutional violation.

Governmental cessation of challenged conduct ordinarily moots requests for prospective relief, but not claims for redress of past injury. *City of Mesquite v. Aladdin’s Castle, Inc.*, 455 U.S. 283, 289 (1982); *Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc.*, 528 U.S. 167, 189-94 (2000). The voluntary-cessation doctrine prevents a defendant from evading judicial review by temporarily altering conduct. *Friends of the Earth*, 528 U.S. at 189. Even where cessation moots an injunction request, a claim for nominal damages preserves the case because it addresses the completed injury, not future conduct. *Carey*, 435 U.S. at 266-67; *Farrar*, 506 U.S. at 112-13.

Respondent’s revised policy thus does not erase the alleged constitutional violation that occurred before repeal. The relevant question is whether the court can still grant effectual relief for that past injury. It can: nominal damages. *Uzuegbunam*, slip op. at 8-10. Petitioners were allegedly stopped from engaging in protected speech under the old rule; if that rule was unconstitutional, they suffered a completed injury that remains judicially cognizable notwithstanding later policy revision.

III. The Eleventh Circuit’s categorical rule conflicts with Article III and this Court’s damages precedents.

The Eleventh Circuit held that nominal damages cannot save an otherwise moot case unless the complaint also pleads compensatory damages. That rule is inconsistent with Article III because it makes the existence of a live controversy depend on the amount and type of damages pleaded, rather than on whether the plaintiff alleges a completed legal wrong capable of redress. This Court has rejected such formalism. In *Memphis Community School District v. Stachura*, the Court explained that damages in § 1983 cases are designed to compensate for “the injury caused by the constitutional deprivation,” 477 U.S. at 307, and in *Carey*, it held that even absent proof of actual injury, a plaintiff may recover nominal damages for a completed constitutional violation, 435 U.S. at 266-67.

The rule below also undermines the “honest and actual antagonistic assertion of rights” that gives federal courts jurisdiction. *Muskrat v. United States*, 219 U.S. 346, 361 (1911). Where, as here, a plaintiff alleges a past First Amendment violation and requests nominal damages, the dispute is concrete and adversarial. Federal courts therefore retain jurisdiction even after a defendant changes the challenged policy. *Powell*, 395 U.S. at 496-500; *Friends of the Earth*, 528 U.S. at 189-94.

IV. The judgment should be reversed.

Because nominal damages remain available to redress petitioners’ alleged past injury, the case is not moot. The decision below should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings on the merits of petitioners’ constitutional claims. *Uzuegbunam*, slip op. at 10-13.
