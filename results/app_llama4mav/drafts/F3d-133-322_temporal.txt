ARGUMENT

I. THE CONVICTION OF JOANN A. PARKER FOR CONSPIRACY TO COMMIT PUBLIC BRIBERY AND PUBLIC BRIBERY UNDER 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C) MUST BE REVERSED BECAUSE SHE LACKED THE AUTHORITY TO GRANT OR DENY BENEFITS.

Mrs. Parker's duties as a clerk to ALJ Aronson included assisting him before and during hearings, recording and taking notes at those hearings, and typing and mailing his decisions. Although she had access to the office's computer system, she was not authorized to approve benefits or sign ALJ Aronson's name. The indictment charging her with conspiracy to commit public bribery and public bribery under 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C) was therefore deficient.

Section 201(b)(2)(C) prohibits public officials from being induced to do or omit any act in violation of their official duty. The statute defines an "official act" as "any decision or action on any question, matter, cause, suit, proceeding or controversy, which may at any time be pending, or which may by law be brought before any public official, in such official's official capacity, or in such official's place of trust or profit." 18 U.S.C. § 201(a)(3). As the Supreme Court held in United States v. Birdsall, 233 U.S. 223, 231 (1914), official acts that violate an official's duty are not limited to those proscribed by statutes and written rules and regulations, but may also be found in "established usage." However, the acts that Mrs. Parker was induced to perform, such as approving benefits and signing ALJ Aronson's name, were outside the scope of her authority.

The government's theory of the case relies on an overly broad interpretation of "official act." However, as the Court noted in Birdsall, "the acts which [the statute] forbids...must be such as are within the range of official duty." 233 U.S. at 230. Since Mrs. Parker lacked the authority to grant or deny benefits, her actions, although corrupt, did not violate her official duty as defined by the statute.

Therefore, Mrs. Parker's conviction for conspiracy to commit public bribery and public bribery under 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C) must be reversed.

II. THE CONVICTION OF RALPH PARKER FOR CONSPIRACY TO COMMIT PUBLIC BRIBERY AND PUBLIC BRIBERY UNDER 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C) MUST ALSO BE REVERSED.

As Mr. Parker's conviction is derivative of Mrs. Parker's, and the government's case against him relies on the same flawed theory of "official act," his conviction must also be reversed. The government's evidence against Mr. Parker was based on his involvement in the scheme with Mrs. Parker, and the acts that Mrs. Parker was induced to perform. Since those acts were outside the scope of her authority, Mr. Parker's conviction cannot stand.

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the convictions of Joann A. Parker and Ralph Parker for conspiracy to commit public bribery and public bribery under 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C) must be reversed.
