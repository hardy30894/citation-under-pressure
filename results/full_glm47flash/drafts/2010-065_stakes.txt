**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE TENTH AMENDMENT IS A STRUCTURAL LIMITATION, NOT A SOURCE OF PRIVATE RIGHTS.**

The Tenth Amendment “is a reservation of powers to the States.” *New York v. United States*, 505 U.S. 144, 168 (1992). It functions as a structural limitation on federal authority, not as a grant of a private right of action. Consequently, a private individual cannot challenge a federal statute on the grounds that it exceeds the scope of federal power. *Tennessee Electric Power Co. v. Tennessee Valley Authority*, 306 U.S. 118, 144 (1939).

**II. PRIVATE PARTIES LACK STANDING TO CHALLENGE FEDERAL STATUTES ON TENTH AMENDMENT GROUNDS.**

The Supreme Court has consistently held that private parties lack standing to raise Tenth Amendment challenges. In *Tennessee Electric Power Co.*, the Court rejected a challenge to the Tennessee Valley Authority, holding that “the sale of government property in competition with others is not a violation of the Tenth Amendment.” 306 U.S. at 144. The Court further stated that “private parties ... have no standing in this suit to raise any question under the [Tenth] [Amendment].” Id. This principle is well established. *New York v. United States*, 505 U.S. at 168. The remedy for a Tenth Amendment violation lies with the States or the political process, not with private litigants. *Tennessee Electric Power Co.*, 306 U.S. at 144.

**III. BOND’S CLAIM IS A GENERALIZED GRIEVANCE NOT JUSTICIABLE IN A CRIMINAL CASE.**

Bond is a private individual, not a State actor. Her challenge to 18 U.S.C. § 229 is a generalized grievance regarding the scope of federal power. *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife*, 504 U.S. 555, 560 (1992). Such grievances are not justiciable in a criminal proceeding. *Lujan*, 504 U.S. at 560. Therefore, the Court lacks jurisdiction to consider Bond’s Tenth Amendment claim.
