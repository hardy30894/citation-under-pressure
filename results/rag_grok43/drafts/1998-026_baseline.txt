The District Court’s failure to comply with former Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) supplies no ground for habeas relief. In Hill v. Lockhart, 474 U.S. 52 (1985), this Court held that a prisoner seeking collateral relief must allege “the kind of prejudice from the allegedly incompetent advice of counsel that would have entitled him to a hearing.” Id. at 60. The same requirement governs here. The District Court found after an evidentiary hearing that Peguero “knew of his right to appeal when the sentencing hearing occurred.” That finding eliminates any possibility of prejudice.

The purpose of the Rule is to ensure that a defendant is informed of his appellate rights. Where the defendant already possesses that information, the omission is harmless. Lane v. Williams, 455 U.S. 624 (1982), illustrates the point: once a defendant is aware of the consequences of his conviction, the failure to repeat that information in a later proceeding does not create a live controversy or entitle him to relief. Id. at 631–33. The same logic applies to the right to appeal.

Canizio v. New York, 327 U.S. 82 (1946), confirms that a sentencing court’s omission of a required advisement does not automatically invalidate the judgment when the defendant was otherwise aware of his rights. There the Court examined whether the absence of counsel advice at sentencing violated due process; it focused on whether the defendant had in fact been deprived of a meaningful opportunity to exercise his rights. Id. at 85–86. No such deprivation occurred here.

Blackledge v. Allison, 431 U.S. 63 (1977), further supports this conclusion. The Court there required an evidentiary hearing only when allegations “related primarily to purported occurrences outside the courtroom and upon which the record could, therefore, cast no real light.” Id. at 74. Here the record contains an express finding, after hearing, that Peguero knew of and elected not to exercise his right to appeal. That finding is conclusive.

Because Peguero suffered no prejudice, the Rule 32 violation does not furnish a basis for vacating his sentence under 28 U.S.C. § 2255. The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
