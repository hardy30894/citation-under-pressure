## ARGUMENT

The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that this case became moot when Georgia Gwinnett College revised the challenged policies and Petitioners’ only live claim was for nominal damages. Article III requires a live case or controversy at all stages of litigation. *Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc.*, 568 U.S. 85, 90-91 (2013); *Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc.*, 528 U.S. 167, 189-90 (2000). Where the challenged policy has been withdrawn and there is no reasonable expectation of recurrence, equitable claims are moot. *City of Mesquite v. Aladdin’s Castle, Inc.*, 455 U.S. 283, 289 (1982); *County of Los Angeles v. Davis*, 440 U.S. 625, 631 (1979). Petitioners do not contest that their claims for declaratory and injunctive relief are moot. The only question is whether a request for nominal damages alone can preserve jurisdiction.

It cannot, at least on the record and pleading posture here. Nominal damages are a form of damages, but they are not a substitute for a concrete, ongoing Article III controversy. This Court has repeatedly held that a plaintiff must show a completed injury traceable to the defendant and redressable by the relief sought. *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife*, 504 U.S. 555, 560-61 (1992); *Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment*, 523 U.S. 83, 102-03 (1998). When a claim for damages is not properly pleaded as compensation for actual injury, a belated nominal-damages request does not keep a dead constitutional challenge alive. See *Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248, 1264-66 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc).

The complaint here did not plead compensatory injury. It asked for “nominal damages,” “damages in an amount to be determined,” and other generic relief, but alleged no concrete injury that would support a compensatory award. That matters because compensatory damages under § 1983 require proof of actual injury. *Carey v. Piphus*, 435 U.S. 247, 254-55 (1978); *Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230-31 (11th Cir. 2000). Where a pleading contains only abstract constitutional injury and no actual harm, nominal damages cannot be used to manufacture jurisdiction after mootness has otherwise attached. *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1265-66.

Petitioners’ contrary rule would also conflict with the principle that federal courts do not issue advisory opinions. *North Carolina v. Rice*, 404 U.S. 244, 246 (1971) (per curiam); *Preiser v. Newkirk*, 422 U.S. 395, 401-02 (1975). Once GGC revised its policy, no injunction or declaration could alter Petitioners’ present legal relations. A nominal-damages claim, untethered to pleaded actual injury, would transform Article III into a mechanism for obtaining retrospective pronouncements about defunct policies. This Court’s prior precedent rule properly forecloses that result. *United States v. Vega-Castillo*, 540 F.3d 1235, 1236 (11th Cir. 2008).

Nor does the First Amendment context change the analysis. This Court has distinguished between cases seeking damages for completed constitutional violations and cases in which only moot equitable relief remains. *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1264-66. Petitioners rely on the unconstitutional-policy label, but that does not answer the jurisdictional question. The Supreme Court has long required a concrete injury even in constitutional cases. *Lujan*, 504 U.S. at 573-74; *Raines v. Byrd*, 521 U.S. 811, 819-20 (1997). And where plaintiffs have ceased to engage in the challenged conduct and the policy has been repealed, mootness follows absent a live damages claim for actual injury. *Friends of the Earth*, 528 U.S. at 189-90; *Already*, 568 U.S. at 91.

The Eleventh Circuit also properly affirmed the denial of leave to amend. A district court does not abuse its discretion by declining to entertain a request to amend embedded in a response brief rather than in a proper motion. *Rosenberg v. Gould*, 554 F.3d 962, 967 (11th Cir. 2009); *Cita Trust Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018). And after judgment, amendment must proceed through Rule 59 or Rule 60, not an informal request in briefing. *Jacobs v. Tempur-Pedic International, Inc.*, 626 F.3d 1327, 1344 (11th Cir. 2010); *Czeremcha v. International Association of Machinists & Aerospace Workers, AFL-CIO*, 724 F.2d 1552, 1556 (11th Cir. 1984). Petitioners never filed such a motion, and the district court expressly left that avenue open. There was no abuse of discretion. *Santiago v. Wood*, 904 F.2d 673, 675 (11th Cir. 1990).

In short, the case was moot once GGC repealed the challenged policy, and Petitioners’ pleading did not preserve jurisdiction through a cognizable compensatory-damages claim. The judgment should be affirmed.
