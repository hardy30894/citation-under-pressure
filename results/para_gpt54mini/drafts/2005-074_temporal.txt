**ARGUMENT**

I. Section 241(a)(5) Does Not Apply to Fernandez-Vargas’s Pre-1997 Reentry Absent Clear Congressional Direction

A statute will not be construed to operate on past conduct, or to attach new legal consequences to completed acts, unless Congress has plainly required that result. *United States v. Heth*, 7 U.S. (3 Cranch) 399, 413-14 (1806); *Shwab v. Doyle*, 258 U.S. 529, 534-35 (1922). The Court has repeatedly applied that principle in the immigration context. In *United States v. Menasche*, 348 U.S. 528, 531-39 (1955), the Court emphasized that immigration statutes should not be read to defeat settled rights absent unmistakable congressional command. Likewise, in *Mulcahey v. Catalanotte*, 353 U.S. 692, 696-97 (1957), the Court construed deportation legislation according to its text, but only after finding the statutory language clearly governed the case before it.

Here, Section 241(a)(5) became effective in 1997, after Fernandez-Vargas’s illegal reentry in 1982. His entry, continued presence, and eventual marriage-based adjustment effort all occurred before the statute’s enactment. Nothing in the statutory text compels the conclusion that Congress meant to reach aliens who had completed the operative act—illegal reentry—years before the statute existed. Under settled interpretive principles, the Court should not presume that Congress silently displaced the legal consequences attached to conduct that had long since occurred. *Shwab*, 258 U.S. at 534-35; *Heth*, 7 U.S. at 413-14.

II. Applying Section 241(a)(5) to Fernandez-Vargas Would Be Impermissibly Retroactive

A law is retroactive when it “affixes a new disability, in respect to transactions or considerations already past.” *Society for the Propagation of the Gospel in Foreign Parts v. Wheeler*, 22 F. Cas. 756, 767 (C.C.D.N.H. 1814) (Story, J.). The Court has long condemned such legislation where it alters the legal effect of completed conduct. *Calder v. Bull*, 3 U.S. (3 Dall.) 386, 390-91 (1798); *Sturges v. Crowninshield*, 17 U.S. (4 Wheat.) 122, 196-97 (1819). A statute is especially suspect when it “takes away or impairs vested rights acquired under existing laws, or creates a new obligation, imposes a new duty, or attaches a new disability” to prior acts. *Society for the Propagation of the Gospel*, 22 F. Cas. at 767.

That is exactly what Section 241(a)(5) does here. Before 1997, Fernandez-Vargas’s illegal reentry did not automatically extinguish every avenue by which he might regularize his presence. By applying the reinstatement statute to him, the government transforms his past reentry into a present ground of automatic removal and forecloses relief otherwise available under the INA. This is not merely prospective regulation of ongoing conduct; it is a new legal disability imposed because of a past act. *Calder*, 3 U.S. at 390-91; *Sturges*, 17 U.S. at 196-97.

The government may argue that Fernandez-Vargas’s application for adjustment was filed after 1997, but that misses the retroactivity problem. The critical event is not the filing of the application; it is the antecedent reentry that Section 241(a)(5) uses as the trigger for a wholly new and harsher legal consequence. A statute is retroactive when it “attaches a new disability” to conduct already completed. *Society for the Propagation of the Gospel*, 22 F. Cas. at 767. That is what happened here.

III. The Government’s Construction Also Conflicts with the Rule of Lenity in Deportation Law

Although deportation proceedings are civil, this Court has long recognized that statutes authorizing expulsion should be strictly construed in favor of the person affected. *Fong Yue Ting v. United States*, 149 U.S. 698, 732-33 (1893); *Mahler v. Eby*, 264 U.S. 32, 44-45 (1924). When Congress speaks ambiguously in such a grave area, doubts should be resolved against the harsher construction. *Fong Yue Ting*, 149 U.S. at 732-33. At minimum, ambiguity here must be resolved against applying Section 241(a)(5) to reentries that occurred before its effective date.

IV. Fernandez-Vargas Is Entitled to Relief

Because Section 241(a)(5) does not plainly reach pre-1997 reentries, and because applying it to Fernandez-Vargas would impose a new disability on completed conduct, the petition should be granted. This Court should hold that the reinstatement statute does not apply to aliens who reentered before its effective date, or, alternatively, that such application is impermissibly retroactive. *Shwab*, 258 U.S. at 534-35; *Calder*, 3 U.S. at 390-91; *Society for the Propagation of the Gospel*, 22 F. Cas. at 767.
