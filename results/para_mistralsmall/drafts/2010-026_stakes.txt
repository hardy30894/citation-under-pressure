**ARGUMENT**

I. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE “INTERNAL PERSONNEL RULES AND PRACTICES” UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 2.

The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test for Exemption 2 was erroneous and conflicts with this Court’s precedent. *See Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 360-61 (1976) (holding that FOIA exemptions must be narrowly construed and explicitly limited to the statutory text). Exemption 2 permits withholding only matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Ninth Circuit’s expansion of this exemption to include ESQD arc maps—documents used to assess external safety risks—is inconsistent with the statutory text and this Court’s instruction that exemptions must be narrowly construed. *Tax Analysts v. U.S. Dep’t of Justice*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989) (quoting *Adm’r, FAA v. Robertson*, 422 U.S. 255, 262 (1975)).

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), was misplaced. *Hardy* addressed law enforcement manuals used internally by agency personnel, not documents that assess external safety risks. *Id.* at 656. The court in *Hardy* did not endorse a “predominantly internal” test; rather, it held that law enforcement materials that risk circumvention of agency regulation are exempt. *Id.* The Ninth Circuit’s subsequent adoption of the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test in *Milner v. Dep’t of the Navy*, 563 F.3d 1111, 1118 (9th Cir. 2009), was a departure from *Hardy* and conflicts with this Court’s instruction that FOIA exemptions must be narrowly construed. *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 360-61.

Moreover, the ESQD arc maps at issue are not “internal personnel rules and practices.” They are technical safety calculations used to assess the external risks posed by explosions at a naval munitions depot. *See* Joint Appendix (“JA”) 45-46 (Commander Whitbred’s affidavit stating that ESQD arcs are used to design storage facilities and organize operations for safety). These maps are not rules governing agency personnel; they are tools used to assess risks to the surrounding community. The Ninth Circuit’s holding that these maps are “predominantly internal” is unsupported by the statutory text or precedent. *See Robertson*, 422 U.S. at 262 (holding that exemptions must be narrowly construed).

II. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT DISCLOSURE OF ESQD ARC MAPS WOULD “RISK CIRCUMVENTION OF AGENCY REGULATION.”

The Ninth Circuit further erred in holding that disclosure of ESQD arc maps would risk circumvention of agency regulation. *Milner*, 563 F.3d at 1118. This Court has held that the risk of circumvention must be “real and not merely remote or speculative.” *FBI v. Abramson*, 456 U.S. 615, 630 (1982). The Navy’s assertion that disclosure would allow terrorists to plan attacks is speculative and unsupported by evidence. *See id.* (holding that the risk of circumvention must be “real and not merely remote or speculative”).

The Navy’s affidavits do not establish a real risk of circumvention. Commander Whitbred’s affidavit states only that disclosure “could” allow terrorists to “reverse engineer” the maps to identify ammunition types and plan attacks. JA 46. This is insufficient to meet the Navy’s burden under FOIA. *See Ray v. Turner*, 587 F.2d 1187, 1195 (D.C. Cir. 1978) (holding that agency affidavits must provide specific details explaining why disclosure would harm the interest protected by the exemption). The Navy’s failure to classify the ESQD arc maps under Exemption 1 further undermines its claim that disclosure would jeopardize national security. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(1) (permitting withholding of classified information). If the maps were truly sensitive, the Navy would have classified them. *See* *Milner* dissent (noting that the Navy did not classify the maps despite claiming national security risks).

III. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE NOT “COMPILED FOR LAW ENFORCEMENT PURPOSES” UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 7(F).

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that ESQD arc maps are not “compiled for law enforcement purposes” under Exemption 7(F). *Milner*, 563 F.3d at 1121. Exemption 7(F) permits withholding records “compiled for law enforcement purposes” if disclosure “could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of any individual.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F). The Ninth Circuit held that the maps were not compiled for law enforcement purposes because they were used for safety and storage, not enforcement. *Milner*, 563 F.3d at 1121. This holding conflicts with this Court’s instruction that FOIA exemptions must be narrowly construed. *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 360-61.

The ESQD arc maps are compiled for law enforcement purposes because they assess risks to the surrounding community, including law enforcement and emergency responders. *See* JA 45-46 (Commander Whitbred’s affidavit stating that the maps are used to organize operations for safety and risk mitigation). The maps identify areas where explosions could endanger lives, including those of law enforcement and emergency personnel. Disclosure of these maps could reasonably be expected to endanger their lives by allowing terrorists to plan attacks. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F). The Ninth Circuit’s holding that the maps are not compiled for law enforcement purposes is unsupported by the statutory text or precedent.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Ninth Circuit erred in holding that ESQD arc maps are exempt from disclosure under FOIA Exemptions 2 and 7(F). The petition for a writ of certiorari should be granted.
