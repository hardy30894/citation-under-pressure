ARGUMENT

I.

The Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 does not reach appointed state judges. The statute excludes from its definition of “employee” any “appointee on the policymaking level.” 29 U.S.C. § 630(f). Missouri judges selected under the Non-Partisan Court Plan are such appointees. Their decisions declare and shape the common law for the entire State. See Erie R.R. Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64, 78 (1938) (“There is no federal general common law.”); Swift v. Tyson, 41 U.S. (16 Pet.) 1, 18 (1842) (judicial decisions “are, at most, only evidence of what the laws are”). Because the statute itself withholds coverage, no question of pre-emption arises. See McCulloch v. Maryland, 17 U.S. (4 Wheat.) 316, 426 (1819) (“the powers of the States” remain “supreme” where Congress has not acted).

II.

The mandatory retirement provision also satisfies the Equal Protection Clause. Age classifications in state law are measured by the rational-basis standard. McGowan v. Maryland, 366 U.S. 420, 425 (1961) (“A statutory discrimination will not be set aside if any state of facts reasonably may be conceived to justify it.”); Williamson v. Lee Optical of Okla., Inc., 348 U.S. 483, 489 (1955) (“It is enough that there is an evil at hand for correction, and that it might be thought that the particular legislative measure was a rational way to correct it.”). Missouri could rationally conclude that compulsory retirement at seventy eliminates the risk of incapacity on the bench, promotes orderly succession, and opens judicial positions to younger lawyers. See Lindsley v. Natural Carbonic Gas Co., 220 U.S. 61, 78 (1911) (classification valid if it rests on “some reasonable ground”). The same principle sustains analogous restrictions on public office. See, e.g., Pope v. Williams, 193 U.S. 621, 632 (1904) (State may prescribe qualifications for its officers). No stricter scrutiny applies. See Barbier v. Connolly, 113 U.S. 27, 31 (1885) (Fourteenth Amendment “does not interfere with the police power of the State”).

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
