The exclusion of the Good News Club from meeting after hours at Milford Central School did not violate the Club's First Amendment free speech rights. The school's community use policy, which prohibits use of its facilities for religious purposes, is a reasonable restriction on speech in a limited public forum. The policy is viewpoint-neutral and is designed to avoid violating the Establishment Clause.

The Court has long recognized that the government may create a limited public forum, opening its property to certain groups or for certain purposes, without being required to allow all speech. See Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941). In such a forum, the government may impose reasonable restrictions on speech, as long as they are viewpoint-neutral.

The Milford Central School's community use policy prohibits use of school facilities for "religious purposes." The school has allowed other groups to use its facilities for various purposes, including the Boy Scouts, the Girl Scouts, and the 4-H Club, but has excluded the Good News Club because its activities are deemed to be "quintessentially religious," as stated in the Court of Appeals opinion. Good News Club v. Milford Central School, 202 F.3d 502 (2d Cir. 2000).

The Club's activities, which include singing songs, hearing Bible lessons, memorizing scripture, and praying, are not simply a viewpoint on a secular subject, but rather are a form of religious worship. See Zorach v. Clauson, 343 U.S. 306 (1952). The school's exclusion of the Club is not viewpoint discrimination, but rather is a reasonable application of its community use policy.

The Court has recognized that the government has a legitimate interest in avoiding Establishment Clause violations. See McCollum v. Board of Education, 333 U.S. 203 (1948). In this case, permitting the Good News Club to use school facilities would have raised concerns about the school's entanglement with religion.

In conclusion, Milford Central School did not violate the Good News Club's First Amendment free speech rights by excluding it from meeting after hours at the school. The school's community use policy is a reasonable restriction on speech in a limited public forum, and is designed to avoid violating the Establishment Clause. The Club's activities are not simply a viewpoint on a secular subject, but rather are a form of religious worship that is excluded by the school's policy.

The Court should affirm the judgment of the Court of Appeals, holding that Milford Central School did not violate the Good News Club's First Amendment free speech rights. The school's exclusion of the Club is a reasonable application of its community use policy, and is justified by its concern about avoiding Establishment Clause violations.
