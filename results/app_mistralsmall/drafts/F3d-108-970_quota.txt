**ARGUMENT**

I. **FELTMANN FAILED TO PRESENT SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE TO ESTABLISH A PRIMA FACIE CASE OF SEX DISCRIMINATION UNDER TITLE VII.**

To prevail on a disparate treatment claim under Title VII, a plaintiff must first establish a prima facie case by showing: (1) she is a member of a protected class; (2) she was qualified for the position; (3) she suffered an adverse employment action; and (4) similarly situated employees outside the protected class were treated more favorably. *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792, 802 (1973). Here, Feltmann’s evidence falls short of the *McDonnell Douglas* standard.

First, Feltmann’s sales performance—while initially strong—declined after the incentive program ended, and her gross profit margins were consistently the lowest among consultants. *See* *Texas Dep’t of Cmty. Affairs v. Burdine*, 450 U.S. 248, 253 (1981) (employer need not retain an employee who cannot perform satisfactorily). Second, the decision not to include her in the Chicago sales competition was not an adverse action, as she was not deprived of any compensation, benefits, or job status. *See* *Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins*, 507 U.S. 604, 610 (1993) (adverse action must materially affect terms and conditions of employment). Third, the memo restricting her demo car use was justified by legitimate business concerns—cost control and sales performance—and was not applied differently than to similarly situated male employees. *See* *Hopkins v. Price Waterhouse*, 490 U.S. 228, 251 (1989) (employer may justify actions based on legitimate, non-discriminatory reasons).

II. **FELTMANN’S RETALIATION CLAIM FAILS BECAUSE SHE DID NOT ENGAGE IN PROTECTED ACTIVITY UNDER TITLE VII.**

Title VII’s retaliation provision protects employees who oppose unlawful employment practices. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-3(a). However, Feltmann’s complaint about a vulgar comment did not constitute opposition to sex discrimination, as it was not reasonably calculated to protest discriminatory treatment. *See* *Clark Cty. Sch. Dist. v. Breeden*, 532 U.S. 268, 271 (2001) (employee must have a reasonable belief that the conduct complained of is unlawful). The record shows that Feltmann herself did not believe the comment was motivated by sex discrimination, as she declined to request Anzalone’s termination. *See* *Hedrick v. Hercules, Inc.*, 65 F.3d 1412, 1418 (8th Cir. 1995) (employee’s subjective belief must be objectively reasonable).

III. **FELTMANN’S STATE LAW CLAIMS FAIL FOR THE SAME REASONS AS HER TITLE VII CLAIMS.**

Under the Missouri Human Rights Act (MHRA), a plaintiff must prove discriminatory intent. *See* Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.055; *Holloway v. Potter*, 228 S.W.3d 474, 480 (Mo. Ct. App. 2007). Feltmann presented no evidence that Sieben’s actions were motivated by sex discrimination or retaliation. Similarly, her common law claims fail because Sieben’s actions were justified by legitimate business reasons. *See* *Johnson v. McDonnell Douglas Corp.*, 745 S.W.2d 661, 666 (Mo. Ct. App. 1987) (employer may terminate for poor performance).

**CONCLUSION**

Feltmann failed to establish a prima facie case of discrimination or retaliation under Title VII, and her state law claims likewise lack merit. The district court’s judgment should be reversed.
