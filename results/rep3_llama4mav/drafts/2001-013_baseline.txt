ARGUMENT

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has jurisdiction under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (Act) to issue citations to uninspected vessels, which are subject to minimal Coast Guard regulation. The Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit erred in holding that the Coast Guard's exclusive jurisdiction over the regulation of seamen's working conditions aboard vessels precludes OSHA's regulation under section 4(b)(1) of the Act, and that this pre-emption encompasses both inspected and uninspected vessels.

Section 4(b)(1) of the Act provides that the Act does not apply to "working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health." 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). However, the Coast Guard's authority to regulate working conditions on vessels is not as comprehensive as the Fifth Circuit suggested. While the Coast Guard has authority to regulate certain aspects of vessel safety, its regulations do not cover all working conditions on uninspected vessels.

In Clary v. Ocean Drilling and Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), the Fifth Circuit held that OSHA regulations do not apply to working conditions of seamen on vessels in navigation. However, these cases do not control the outcome here because they did not specifically consider whether their holdings applied equally to uninspected and inspected vessels. Moreover, the vessels in those cases were operating on the high seas or navigable waters, whereas the MR. BELDON was operating in Louisiana's territorial waters.

Furthermore, the Coast Guard's regulations for uninspected vessels are limited. 46 U.S.C. § 4102 authorizes the Coast Guard to issue safety regulations for uninspected vessels, but these regulations are restricted to specific areas such as fire extinguishers, life preservers, and ventilation of engine and fuel tank compartments. The Coast Guard has issued regulations in these areas, but they do not comprehensively cover all working conditions on uninspected vessels. See 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01 et seq; 46 C.F.R. §§ 26.01 et seq.

The Fifth Circuit's holding that the Coast Guard's jurisdiction is exclusive and pre-empts OSHA's regulation is overly broad. Section 4(b)(1) of the Act requires that another federal agency "exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health" in order to pre-empt OSHA's jurisdiction. 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). The Coast Guard's limited regulations for uninspected vessels do not meet this standard.

In Gannett v. Merchants Mutual Insurance Co., 430 F.2d 603 (8th Cir. 1970), the court held that a federal agency's limited authority to regulate certain aspects of an industry does not pre-empt OSHA's jurisdiction over the entire industry. Similarly, in this case, the Coast Guard's limited regulations for uninspected vessels do not pre-empt OSHA's jurisdiction over the working conditions on those vessels.

Therefore, OSHA has jurisdiction to issue citations to uninspected vessels, such as the MR. BELDON, under the Act. The Fifth Circuit's holding to the contrary should be reversed, and the citation issued against Mallard should be reinstated.
