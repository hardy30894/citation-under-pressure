The exclusion of the Good News Club from meeting after hours at Milford Central School did not violate the Club's First Amendment free speech rights. The school's community use policy, which prohibits use of its facilities for religious purposes, is a constitutional restriction on speech in a limited public forum. The policy is reasonable in light of the forum's purpose and is viewpoint-neutral.

The Court of Appeals correctly held that the subject matter of the Club's activities was "quintessentially religious" and thus fell outside the scope of the limited public forum created by Milford's community use policy. While the Club purports to teach morals and character, its activities, including prayer, Bible lessons, and scripture memorization, are inherently religious. The fact that the Club's moral teachings are couched in religious terms and involve religious devotions distinguishes them from the secular moral instruction provided by other groups permitted to use the school's facilities, such as the Boy Scouts and 4-H Club.

The Club's argument that its activities represent a "religious viewpoint" on the secular subject of morality is unpersuasive. As the Court of Appeals noted, the Club's activities are not simply a discussion of morals and character from a religious perspective, but rather are "religious instruction and prayer" that go beyond the bounds of the limited public forum. The Supreme Court has recognized that "there is a difference between discussion of religious matters and religious worship or proselytizing." Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263, 269 n.6 (1981). The Club's activities fall into the latter category.

Furthermore, even if the Club's activities could be characterized as a "religious viewpoint" on morality, the school's exclusion of the Club is still justified. The Establishment Clause concerns that would arise from permitting the Club to use the school's facilities outweigh the Club's free speech interests. Allowing the Club to meet at the school would create a risk of perceived endorsement of the Club's religious activities, which would be inconsistent with the school's obligation to maintain a neutral stance on religious matters. See Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, 508 U.S. 384, 395 (1993).

In sum, Milford Central School did not violate the Good News Club's First Amendment free speech rights by excluding it from meeting after hours at the school. The school's community use policy is a reasonable and viewpoint-neutral restriction on speech in a limited public forum, and the Club's activities fall outside the scope of that forum. The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
