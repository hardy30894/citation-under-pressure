**ARGUMENT**

I. **Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial, and Section 1404(a) Cannot Be Invoked to Defeat That Command**

The plain text of § 1407(a) requires that, "[e]ach action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) (emphasis added). This Court has long held that "[w]here the words of a statute are unambiguous, the judicial inquiry is complete." *Ratzlaf v. United States*, 510 U.S. 135, 147–48 (1994) (quoting *United States v. Ron Pair Enters., Inc.*, 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989)). The statute’s command is clear: after pretrial proceedings conclude, the Panel *shall* remand the case to the transferor district. The Ninth Circuit’s contrary conclusion—that a transferee court may invoke § 1404(a) to retain the case for trial—ignores this statutory mandate and contravenes the statute’s plain meaning.

This Court has repeatedly emphasized that § 1407(a) is a *limited* grant of authority, confined to pretrial coordination. *See, e.g., Van Dusen v. Barrack*, 376 U.S. 612, 638 (1964) (noting that § 1407 "was not intended to permit transfer for trial"). The statute’s structure confirms this: it authorizes transfer only for "coordinated or consolidated *pretrial* proceedings," id. § 1407(a) (emphasis added), and explicitly requires remand "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." Id. § 1407(a). The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation (JPML) has no authority to extend pretrial proceedings into trial, nor may a transferee court arrogate that power by invoking § 1404(a).

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810 (3d Cir. 1982), and *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), is misplaced. Those cases erroneously concluded that § 1404(a) permits a transferee court to retain a case for trial, but they did so without grappling with the plain text of § 1407(a). *See Fine Paper*, 685 F.2d at 820; *Pfizer*, 447 F.2d at 124–25. As Judge Kozinski noted in dissent, these cases "simply disregarded the statutory mandate" and justified their departure on grounds of "policy or convenience." *Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach*, 61 F.3d 911, 916 (9th Cir. 1995) (Kozinski, J., dissenting). This Court should reject such an approach. *See, e.g., United States v. Locke*, 471 U.S. 84, 95–96 (1985) (rejecting judicial rewriting of unambiguous statutory text).

II. **The Judicial Panel’s Rule 14(b) Cannot Expand the Statutory Authority Granted by § 1407(a)**

The JPML’s Rule 14(b) purports to authorize transferee courts to "transfer" cases to themselves for trial under § 1404(a). *See* J.P.M.L. Rule 14(b). However, this rule cannot override the clear command of § 1407(a). *See, e.g., United States v. Mendoza-Lopez*, 481 U.S. 828, 837 (1987) ("[A]n administrative agency’s interpretation of a statute is not entitled to deference when it conflicts with the plain language of that statute."). The JPML’s rulemaking authority is limited to procedural rules governing pretrial proceedings, not to expanding the substantive scope of § 1407(a). *See* 28 U.S.C. § 1407(d) (limiting JPML’s rulemaking to "procedures for the conduct of such pretrial proceedings").

This Court has repeatedly rejected attempts by lower courts or agencies to expand their authority beyond the limits set by statute. *See, e.g., INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca*, 480 U.S. 421, 432 (1987) ("[W]e do not defer to an agency interpretation that is contrary to the plain language of the statute."). The JPML’s Rule 14(b) cannot serve as a basis for a transferee court to retain a case for trial after the conclusion of pretrial proceedings.

III. **Permitting Transferee Courts to Retain Cases for Trial Undermines the Statutory Scheme and Harms Plaintiffs**

The statutory scheme of § 1407(a) reflects Congress’s careful balance between efficiency and plaintiffs’ right to select their forum. *See Van Dusen*, 376 U.S. at 638. Permitting transferee courts to retain cases for trial under § 1404(a) subverts this balance by allowing plaintiffs to be forced into a remote forum for trial, despite the statute’s clear command that they be remanded. As Judge Kozinski observed, this practice "undercut[s] the important policy of section 1407" and "glom[s] onto" cases for purposes beyond those authorized by statute. *Lexecon*, 61 F.3d at 917 (Kozinski, J., dissenting).

This Court should reject the Ninth Circuit’s erroneous interpretation and hold that § 1407(a) mandates remand to the transferor district for trial. Any other conclusion would permit transferee courts to expand their authority beyond the limits set by statute, in derogation of Congress’s clear command.
