**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE COAST GUARD’S REGULATORY AUTHORITY OVER UNINSPECTED VESSELS IS NOT SO COMPREHENSIVE AS TO PREEMPT OSHA JURISDICTION UNDER SECTION 4(b)(1).**

Section 4(b)(1) of the Occupational Safety and Health Act provides that the Act does not apply to “working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies . . . exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). This provision preempts OSHA jurisdiction only where another federal agency has exercised its authority to regulate the particular working conditions at issue. *See Chao v. Mallard Bay Drilling, Inc.*, 534 U.S. 235, 241 (2002). The Coast Guard has not exercised such authority over uninspected vessels like Rig 52.

The Coast Guard’s regulatory scheme for uninspected vessels is minimal. Under 46 U.S.C. § 4102, the Coast Guard may issue regulations for uninspected vessels only on five discrete subjects: fire extinguishers, life preservers, flame arrestors, ventilation, and emergency locating equipment. *Id.* § 4102(1)–(5). The Coast Guard has promulgated regulations implementing these narrow grants of authority. *See* 46 C.F.R. pts. 25, 26. Critically, the Coast Guard has not issued any regulations addressing the working conditions that formed the basis of OSHA’s citations here—such as well-control procedures, blowout prevention, or other drilling safety practices. *See Mallard Bay*, 534 U.S. at 241–42.

This Court has long recognized that “the Coast Guard’s jurisdiction over uninspected vessels is not plenary.” *Mallard Bay*, 534 U.S. at 241. Unlike inspected vessels, which are subject to comprehensive Coast Guard oversight, uninspected vessels “are subject to only minimal Coast Guard regulation.” *Id.* at 240. Because the Coast Guard has not exercised its authority to regulate the specific safety hazards at issue on Rig 52, OSHA retains jurisdiction under Section 4(b)(1). *See id.* at 242.

**II. THE FIFTH CIRCUIT’S RELIANCE ON *CLARY* AND *DONOVAN* IS MISPLACED BECAUSE THOSE CASES INVOLVED INSPECTED VESSELS.**

The court below erred in holding that *Clary v. Ocean Drilling & Exploration Co.*, 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and *Donovan v. Texaco, Inc.*, 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), compelled a finding of preemption. Both cases involved vessels that were “inspected” and thus subject to comprehensive Coast Guard regulation. *See Clary*, 609 F.2d at 1121 (drilling barge); *Donovan*, 720 F.2d at 826 (tanker). Neither case addressed the distinct regulatory framework applicable to uninspected vessels.

The Fifth Circuit’s broad language in *Donovan*—that “OSHA regulations do not apply to working conditions of seamen on vessels in navigation,” 720 F.2d at 826—must be read in context. That statement was made in a case involving an inspected vessel, where the Coast Guard had exercised comprehensive authority. *See id.* at 827. As this Court explained in *Mallard Bay*, the Fifth Circuit’s “sweeping language” cannot be divorced from the facts of those cases. *Mallard Bay*, 534 U.S. at 241. Where, as here, the Coast Guard has not exercised its authority over the relevant working conditions, Section 4(b)(1) does not bar OSHA jurisdiction. *Id.* at 242.

**III. RIG 52 IS A “WORKPLACE” UNDER SECTION 4(a) OF THE OSH ACT.**

Section 4(a) provides that the OSH Act applies “with respect to employment performed in a workplace in a State.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(a). A “workplace” is any location where employees perform work. *See Reich v. D.M. Sabia Co.*, 90 F.3d 854, 858 (3d Cir. 1996). Rig 52 was a drilling barge located in Louisiana’s territorial waters, where Mallard’s employees performed drilling operations. It is undisputed that the barge was a fixed, stationary platform engaged in industrial activity, not a vessel in navigation. *See Mallard Bay*, 534 U.S. at 238. The barge was therefore a “workplace” within the plain meaning of the Act. *See id.* at 242–43.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed, and the citation issued by OSHA should be reinstated.
