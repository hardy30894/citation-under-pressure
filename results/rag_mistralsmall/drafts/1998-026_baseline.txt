**ARGUMENT**

I. **A Rule 32 Violation Is Subject to Harmless-Error Review and Does Not Automatically Warrant Habeas Relief**

Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) requires the district court to advise a defendant of the right to appeal. This Court has long held that a Rule violation does not automatically entitle a defendant to habeas relief; rather, such errors are subject to harmless-error review. *See United States v. Morrison*, 449 U.S. 361, 364 (1981) (holding that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error analysis under Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(a)). The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that a defendant is informed of his appellate rights, not to create a per se rule of vacatur. *See id.* at 365.

The Third Circuit correctly applied harmless-error review in this case. The record establishes that Petitioner was aware of his right to appeal and elected not to pursue one. Where a defendant’s knowledge of his appellate rights renders the Rule 32 error harmless, habeas relief is unwarranted. *See Hill v. Lockhart*, 474 U.S. 52, 59 (1985) (holding that a defendant’s strategic decision not to appeal, despite knowledge of his rights, precludes habeas relief).

II. **A Defendant’s Awareness of His Appellate Rights Renders Any Rule 32 Violation Harmless**

This Court has repeatedly recognized that a procedural error is harmless where the defendant’s conduct demonstrates that he was not prejudiced by the error. *See Lane v. Williams*, 455 U.S. 624, 631 (1982) (holding that a parole violation was harmless where the defendant was aware of his obligations). Similarly, in *Hill v. Lockhart*, the Court concluded that a defendant’s strategic decision not to appeal, despite knowledge of his rights, precluded habeas relief. *Id.* at 59.

Here, the district court found that Petitioner was aware of his right to appeal and elected not to pursue one. The Third Circuit correctly held that this awareness rendered the Rule 32 violation harmless. *See United States v. Morrison*, 449 U.S. at 365 (holding that a Rule 32 violation is harmless where the defendant was aware of his appellate rights).

III. **Petitioner’s Claim Fails Under the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act (AEDPA)**

Under the AEDPA, a federal court may grant habeas relief only if the state court’s decision was “contrary to, or involved an unreasonable application of, clearly established Federal law.” 28 U.S.C. § 2254(d)(1). The Third Circuit’s decision comports with clearly established precedent. *See Morrison*, 449 U.S. at 364; *Hill v. Lockhart*, 474 U.S. at 59. Petitioner’s claim does not warrant habeas relief under the AEDPA’s deferential standard.

**CONCLUSION**

The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed. The district court’s failure to advise Petitioner of his right to appeal was harmless where he was aware of that right and elected not to pursue an appeal. The Third Circuit correctly applied harmless-error review and did not unreasonably apply clearly established federal law.
