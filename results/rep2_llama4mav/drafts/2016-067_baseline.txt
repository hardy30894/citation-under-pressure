The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) regulates the conduct of "debt collectors," not "creditors." 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). The petitioners argue that Santander Consumer USA, Inc. (Santander) is a "debt collector" because it acquired their defaulted debts from CitiFinancial Auto. However, the FDCPA's definitions of "debt collector" and "creditor" make clear that the determining factor is not the default status of the debt, but rather whether the entity collects debts on behalf of others or for its own account.

The FDCPA defines a "debt collector" as a person who (1) has a principal purpose of collecting debts, (2) regularly collects debts owed to another, or (3) collects its own debts using a name other than its own. 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). The petitioners' argument relies on a flawed interpretation of the statute, ignoring the fact that the exclusion from the definition of "creditor" applies only to a person who receives defaulted debt "solely for the purpose of facilitating collection... for another." 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(4).

The Fourth Circuit correctly held that the default status of a debt has no bearing on whether a person qualifies as a "debt collector" under the threshold definition of § 1692a(6). Henson v. Santander Consumer USA, Inc., 817 F.3d 131, 136 (4th Cir. 2016). The court noted that the material distinction between a "debt collector" and a "creditor" is whether a person regularly collects debts for itself or for others. Id. at 137. Santander, having purchased the petitioners' debts from CitiFinancial Auto, was collecting the debts for its own account, not on behalf of another. Therefore, it is not a "debt collector" under the FDCPA.

The petitioners' argument that Santander remained a "debt collector" after purchasing their debts is unpersuasive. As the Eleventh Circuit held in Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A., 797 F.3d 1309, 1315-16 (11th Cir. 2015), a person must regularly collect or attempt to collect debts for others to qualify as a "debt collector." Santander's collection activities on its own behalf do not render it a "debt collector."

The FDCPA's purpose is to eliminate abusive debt collection practices by debt collectors. 15 U.S.C. § 1692(e). Congress did not intend to regulate creditors collecting their own debts. The Fourth Circuit's decision is consistent with the statutory text and the FDCPA's purpose. This Court should affirm the judgment, as Santander was not acting as a "debt collector" when it collected on the petitioners' debts.
