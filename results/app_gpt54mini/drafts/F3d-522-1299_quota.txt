PowerOasis’s asserted claims are entitled to the February 6, 1997 filing date of the Original Application under 35 U.S.C. § 120. A later-filed continuation or continuation-in-part receives the benefit of an earlier filing date when the earlier application satisfies the written-description requirement of 35 U.S.C. § 112 for the subject matter later claimed. *In re Wertheim*, 646 F.2d 527, 536-37 (C.C.P.A. 1981); *Lockwood v. Am. Airlines, Inc.*, 107 F.3d 1565, 1571-72 (Fed. Cir. 1997); *Capon v. Eshhar*, 418 F.3d 1349, 1357-58 (Fed. Cir. 2005). The written description inquiry asks whether the original disclosure conveyed to persons of ordinary skill that the inventor had possession of the later-claimed invention as of the earlier date. *Vas-Cath Inc. v. Mahurkar*, 935 F.2d 1555, 1563-64 (Fed. Cir. 1991); *Ariad Pharms., Inc. v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 598 F.3d 1336, 1351-52 (Fed. Cir. 2010) (en banc). That standard does not require verbatim disclosure of the later claim language, only that the specification reasonably describe the claimed subject matter. *Ruschig v. Hartz*, 376 F.2d 238, 245-46 (C.C.P.A. 1967); *Gentry Gallery, Inc. v. Berkline Corp.*, 134 F.3d 1473, 1479-80 (Fed. Cir. 1998).

The district court erred by treating the 2000 CIP’s added language as the only disclosure relevant to claim construction and priority. Priority under § 120 turns on the original application’s support, not on later-added matter. *Tronzo v. Biomet, Inc.*, 156 F.3d 1154, 1158-60 (Fed. Cir. 1998); *Lockwood*, 107 F.3d at 1572. Here, the Original Application disclosed the very architecture later claimed: a vending machine for telecommunications access, with a payment mechanism, customer interface, access circuit, connector, and control unit. That disclosure is enough if, viewed through the lens of the skilled artisan, it shows possession of the later-claimed arrangement. *Vas-Cath*, 935 F.2d at 1563-64; *Capon*, 418 F.3d at 1357-58. The district court’s cramped reading improperly required the Original Application to anticipate the exact dispute over whether the customer interface could be located on the user’s laptop. But written description does not demand that an inventor foresee and answer every later claim construction dispute. *Ariad*, 598 F.3d at 1351-52; *Capon*, 418 F.3d at 1358.

Because the asserted claims are supported by the Original Application, the critical prior-art date is February 6, 1997, not June 15, 2000. Under § 102(b), a reference qualifies only if it was in public use, on sale, or otherwise publicly available more than one year before the relevant filing date. *Pfaff v. Wells Elecs., Inc.*, 525 U.S. 55, 67-68 (1998); *In re Klopfenstein*, 380 F.3d 1345, 1350-52 (Fed. Cir. 2004). MobileStar’s alleged public use and sales before June 15, 1999 therefore do not matter if the asserted claims can reach back to February 6, 1997. T-Mobile’s anticipation theory fails for that reason alone. *Titanium Metals Corp. of Am. v. Banner*, 778 F.2d 775, 780-81 (Fed. Cir. 1985).

Even if this Court were to examine anticipation on the merits, summary judgment was inappropriate. Anticipation requires that a single prior-art reference disclose every claim limitation, arranged as in the claim. *Verdegaal Bros., Inc. v. Union Oil Co. of Cal.*, 814 F.2d 628, 631 (Fed. Cir. 1987); *Net MoneyIN, Inc. v. VeriSign, Inc.*, 545 F.3d 1359, 1371 (Fed. Cir. 2008). The parties’ dispute over the location and nature of the “customer interface” is a material issue because, under PowerOasis’s proper priority date, the MobileStar Network is not prior art at all. And where priority depends on written description, the issue is ordinarily one of fact reviewed for substantial evidence or tested against competing inferences, not resolved by an overbroad summary judgment ruling. *Lockwood*, 107 F.3d at 1571-72; *Ariad*, 598 F.3d at 1351-52.

The judgment should therefore be reversed. The Original Application sufficiently described the claimed invention to support the asserted claims under § 120, the MobileStar Network does not qualify as § 102(b) prior art against a February 6, 1997 filing date, and T-Mobile was not entitled to summary judgment of anticipation. *Wertheim*, 646 F.2d at 536-37; *Vas-Cath*, 935 F.2d at 1563-64; *Pfaff*, 525 U.S. at 67-68.
