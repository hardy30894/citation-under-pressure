**ARGUMENT**

Section 1407(a) provides that actions transferred for pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded by the Panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). The word “shall” creates a mandatory obligation. See, e.g., Anderson v. Yungkau, 329 U.S. 482, 485 (1947) (“shall” is “the language of command”). Once pretrial proceedings end, the statute leaves the transferee court with no authority to retain the case for trial.

Section 1404(a) supplies no exception. It authorizes transfer only when venue is proper in the receiving district and the transfer serves convenience and justice. 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). It does not repeal or qualify the express remand command of § 1407(a). Where Congress has enacted a specific procedural rule, a general venue statute cannot override it. See, e.g., Fourco Glass Co. v. Transmirra Prods. Corp., 353 U.S. 222, 228-29 (1957) (specific venue provision controls over general transfer statute).

The decisions permitting self-transfer rest on policy rather than text. Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971), and In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litig., 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3d Cir. 1982), acknowledged that nothing in § 1407(a) or § 1404(a) “directly allows” a transferee judge to assign the case to itself for trial, yet upheld the practice for reasons of efficiency. Such considerations cannot displace the statute’s plain directive. See, e.g., United States v. Ron Pair Enters., Inc., 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989) (courts must enforce clear statutory language regardless of policy preferences).

Multidistrict Litigation Rule 14(b) likewise cannot enlarge judicial power beyond the statute. See, e.g., Sibbach v. Wilson & Co., 312 U.S. 1, 10 (1941) (rules may not “abridge, enlarge or modify any substantive right”). The Panel’s remand duty remains absolute.

The judgment below should be reversed and the case remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
