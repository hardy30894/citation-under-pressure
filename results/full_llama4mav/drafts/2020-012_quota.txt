The Railroad Retirement Act (RRA) and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (RUIA) grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final" decisions of the United States Railroad Retirement Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review. The Fifth Circuit, bound by its own precedent in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), held that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Manfredo Salinas's 2006 application for a disability annuity. However, this Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision because the Board's refusal to reopen Salinas's prior claim is a final, reviewable decision under § 355(f).

The RRA and RUIA do not define "final decision," but the Supreme Court has interpreted similar language in the Social Security Act to permit judicial review of decisions that are "final" and "made after a hearing." Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99, 108 (1977). While Sanders held that a decision not to reopen a prior claim is not reviewable under the Social Security Act's "judicial review" provision, 42 U.S.C. § 405(g), it did not address the RRA's distinct statutory framework. See id. at 107-08. The D.C. Circuit has persuasively held that the RRA's "final decision" language is broader than the Social Security Act's, and encompasses decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations. Stovic v. R.R. Ret. Bd., 826 F.3d 500, 504-05 (D.C. Cir. 2016).

The majority of circuits, including the Fifth Circuit in Roberts, have held that decisions not to reopen prior claims are not reviewable under § 355(f). See, e.g., Harris v. R.R. Ret. Bd., 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999); Abbruzzese v. R.R. Ret. Bd., 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995); Gutierrez v. R.R. Ret. Bd., 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990); Steebe v. R.R. Ret. Bd., 708 F.2d 250, 254-55 (7th Cir. 1983). However, this Court should adopt the minority view, as articulated in Stovic and Szostak v. R.R. Ret. Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254-55 (2d Cir. 1966), which holds that decisions not to reopen prior claims are reviewable.

The RRA's statutory framework and legislative history support a broader interpretation of "final decision." See, e.g., United States v. Ptasynski, 462 U.S. 74, 81 (1983) (emphasizing the importance of considering statutory text and legislative history). The Board's own regulations provide that a decision not to reopen a prior claim is a "final" decision for purposes of the Board's own procedures. 20 C.F.R. § 261.2. Furthermore, the Supreme Court has recognized that "finality" is a flexible concept that depends on the context and purpose of the statute. See, e.g., Abbott Labs. v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136, 149-50 (1967), abrogated on other grounds by Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977); see also Bell v. New Jersey, 461 U.S. 773, 778 (1983) (noting that "finality" is not a "mechanical" concept).

In light of the RRA's statutory text, legislative history, and the Board's own regulations, this Court should hold that the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a "final decision" subject to judicial review under § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings. By adopting the minority view, this Court will ensure that the RRA is interpreted consistently with its purpose of providing a comprehensive system for reviewing Board decisions. See Sones v. R.R. Ret. Bd., 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991) (noting the importance of consistency with the RRA's purpose).
