**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT THE ASSERTED CLAIMS ARE NOT ENTITLED TO THE BENEFIT OF THE ORIGINAL APPLICATION’S FILING DATE UNDER 35 U.S.C. § 120.**

The district court erred in concluding that the asserted claims of the ’658 and ’400 patents are not entitled to the benefit of the Original Application’s filing date under 35 U.S.C. § 120. Section 120 permits a patentee to claim the benefit of an earlier filing date if the later application is a continuation or continuation-in-part of the earlier application and the later application complies with the written description requirement of 35 U.S.C. § 112. *See* 35 U.S.C. § 120; *Lockwood v. Am. Airlines, Inc.*, 107 F.3d 1565, 1572 (Fed. Cir. 1997) (“Section 120 allows a patentee to claim the benefit of an earlier filing date if the later application is a continuation or continuation-in-part of the earlier application and the later application complies with the written description requirement of § 112.”).

The district court’s analysis improperly conflated the written description requirement of § 112 with the enablement requirement. *See* *PowerOasis, Inc. v. T-Mobile USA, Inc.*, No. 05-cv-42-PB, 2007 WL 962937, at *2 (D.N.H. Mar. 30, 2007). The written description requirement is satisfied if the earlier application “reasonably conveys to those skilled in the art that the inventor had possession of the claimed subject matter as of the filing date.” *Ariad Pharm., Inc. v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 598 F.3d 1336, 1351 (Fed. Cir. 2010) (en banc) (quoting *Regents of the Univ. of Cal. v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 119 F.3d 1559, 1566 (Fed. Cir. 1997)). The district court’s reliance on the addition of new language in the 2000 CIP Application was misplaced, as the asserted claims do not recite any of the allegedly new matter. *See* *Lockwood*, 107 F.3d at 1572 (“The fact that a later application adds new matter does not preclude it from claiming the benefit of an earlier filing date for claims that are fully supported in the earlier application.”).

The asserted claims are directed to a vending machine for selling telecommunications access, a concept fully disclosed in the Original Application. The Original Application describes a vending machine with a payment mechanism, a customer interface, an electronic circuit, a telecommunications channel access circuit, a telecommunications channel access connector, and a control unit—each of which is recited in independent claim 1. *See* Original Application, filed Feb. 6, 1997, at 3:10-20. The district court’s conclusion that the Original Application lacked written description for the asserted claims is unsupported by the record and contrary to law.

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT OF INVALIDITY UNDER 35 U.S.C. § 102(B).**

The district court erred in granting summary judgment that the asserted claims are invalid as anticipated under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b). Anticipation requires that a prior art reference disclose every limitation of the claimed invention, either expressly or inherently. *See* *Netcraft Corp. v. eBay, Inc.*, 549 F.3d 1321, 1325 (Fed. Cir. 2008). The MobileStar Network, upon which T-Mobile relied, does not anticipate the asserted claims because it lacks a “customer interface” as properly construed.

The district court adopted PowerOasis’s construction of “customer interface” as encompassing an interface located on the customer’s laptop. *See* PowerOasis, 2007 WL 962937, at *2. Under this construction, the MobileStar Network does not anticipate the asserted claims because it did not include a customer interface on a laptop. The MobileStar Network required users to connect to the Internet via a wireless access point, but it did not provide an interface on the user’s laptop for interacting with the vending machine. *See* *In re Schreiber*, 128 F.3d 1473, 1477 (Fed. Cir. 1997) (“A prior art reference cannot anticipate a claim if it does not disclose each and every limitation of the claim.”).

Moreover, the MobileStar Network’s public use and sale occurred prior to June 15, 1999, which is more than one year before the filing date of the 2000 CIP Application. However, because the asserted claims are entitled to the Original Application’s filing date of February 6, 1997, the MobileStar Network does not qualify as prior art under § 102(b). *See* 35 U.S.C. § 102(b); *Amgen Inc. v. Hoechst Marion Roussel, Inc.*, 314 F.3d 1313, 1354 (Fed. Cir. 2003) (“A reference is prior art under § 102(b) only if it was publicly disclosed more than one year before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.”).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, PowerOasis respectfully submits that the district court erred in granting summary judgment of invalidity. The asserted claims are entitled to the Original Application’s filing date under § 120, and the MobileStar Network does not anticipate the asserted claims under § 102(b). The judgment of the district court should be reversed.
