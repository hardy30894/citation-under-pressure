**ARGUMENT**

**I. BOND LACKS STANDING TO ASSERT A TENTH AMENDMENT CHALLENGE TO THE VALIDITY OF 18 U.S.C. § 229.**

A criminal defendant may not invoke the Tenth Amendment to challenge the constitutionality of a federal statute under which she has been prosecuted. *Tennessee Electric Power Co. v. Tennessee Valley Authority*, 306 U.S. 118, 143–44 (1939) (“[P]rivate companies, absent the states or their officers, have no standing in this suit to raise any question under the [Tenth] [Amendment.”); *United States v. Parker*, 362 F.3d 1279, 1284–85 (10th Cir. 2004) (defendant lacked standing to raise Tenth Amendment challenge to federal prosecution); *Brooklyn Legal Servs. Corp. v. Legal Servs. Corp.*, 462 F.3d 219, 234–35 (2d Cir. 2006) (same), *cert. denied*, 548 U.S. 904 (2007); *Medeiros v. Vincent*, 431 F.3d 25, 33–36 (1st Cir. 2005) (same), *cert. denied*, 548 U.S. 904 (2006); *Oregon v. Legal Servs. Corp.*, 552 F.3d 965, 971–72 (9th Cir. 2009) (same). The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized that the Tenth Amendment “is not a source of rights enforceable by an individual.” *South Carolina v. Katzenbach*, 383 U.S. 301, 323–24 (1966); *see also United States v. Darby*, 312 U.S. 100, 124 (1941) (“The amendment states but a truism that all is retained which has not been surrendered.”). Thus, a private party may not invoke the Tenth Amendment to invalidate a federal statute on federalism grounds. *Tennessee Elec. Power Co.*, 306 U.S. at 143–44.

Bond’s reliance on *Gillespie v. City of Indianapolis*, 185 F.3d 693, 703–04 (7th Cir. 1999), is misplaced. The Seventh Circuit’s decision in *Gillespie* is an outlier, and this Court has recognized that the weight of authority rejects private-party standing to raise Tenth Amendment claims. *See supra* pp. 10–11. Moreover, *Gillespie* did not involve a criminal prosecution, and its reasoning has been undermined by subsequent Supreme Court decisions clarifying the jurisdictional limits of federal courts. *See Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Env’t*, 523 U.S. 83, 101–02 (1998) (courts must address standing before reaching the merits).

**II. 18 U.S.C. § 229 IS A VALID EXERCISE OF CONGRESS’S TREATY POWER AND THE NECESSARY AND PROPER CLAUSE.**

Congress acted well within its constitutional authority in enacting § 229 to implement the United States’ obligations under the Chemical Weapons Convention. *Missouri v. Holland*, 252 U.S. 416, 432 (1920) (“If [a] treaty is valid there can be no dispute about the validity of the statute [implementing it] under Article I, § 8, as a necessary and proper means to execute the powers of the Government.”); *United States v. Lara*, 541 U.S. 193, 201 (2004) (upholding federal prosecution under the Indian Civil Rights Act as a valid exercise of the treaty power). The Chemical Weapons Convention is a valid treaty, and § 229 is a necessary and proper means of implementing it. *Holland*, 252 U.S. at 432.

Bond’s argument that § 229 violates the Tenth Amendment is foreclosed by *Holland*. The Supreme Court has long recognized that the treaty power permits Congress to legislate in areas otherwise reserved to the states when necessary to implement a valid treaty. *Id.* at 432–33; *see also United States v. Lue*, 134 F.3d 79, 84 (2d Cir. 1998) (applying *Holland* to uphold prosecution under § 229). The Tenth Amendment does not bar such legislation. *Holland*, 252 U.S. at 432–33.

**III. 18 U.S.C. § 229 IS NOT UNCONSTITUTIONALLY VAGUE.**

A statute is void for vagueness only if it fails to provide a person of ordinary intelligence fair notice of what conduct is prohibited. *United States v. Powell*, 423 U.S. 87, 92 (1975). Section 229 clearly prohibits the knowing use or possession of toxic chemicals for non-peaceful purposes. 18 U.S.C. § 229(a)(1). Bond’s conduct—repeatedly spreading toxic chemicals on her victim’s property with the intent to harm—falls squarely within the statute’s prohibition. *Powell*, 423 U.S. at 92. The statute provides fair notice and does not violate due process. *Id.*

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the court below should be affirmed.
