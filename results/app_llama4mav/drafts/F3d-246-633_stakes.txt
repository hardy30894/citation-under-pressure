The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's claim of conspiracy in violation of the Railway Labor Act ("RLA"), 45 U.S.C. § 153. The RLA requires that a union fairly represent its members in disputes with employers. See Steele v. Louisville & Nashville R.R. Co., 323 U.S. 192, 204 (1944). A union breaches its duty of fair representation when its actions are "arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith." Air Line Pilots Ass'n, Int'l v. O'Neill, 499 U.S. 65, 67 (1991) (quoting Vaca v. Sipes, 386 U.S. 171, 190 (1967)). 

Here, Plaintiff presented evidence that UTU representative Earley conspired with CSX to deny Plaintiff the thirty shares of CSX stock. Specifically, the secretly recorded executive session tape suggests that Earley may have argued against Plaintiff's claim, potentially in collusion with CSX representative Emerick. The district court improperly weighed the evidence and resolved disputed facts in favor of Defendants when it granted summary judgment. See Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 249 (1986) ("[A]t the summary judgment stage the judge's function is not himself to weigh the evidence and determine the truth of the matter but to determine whether there is a genuine issue for trial."). The court should have allowed Plaintiff's claim to proceed to trial.

The district court also erred in awarding Defendants statutory damages, punitive damages, and attorneys' fees under the Federal Wiretap Act (the "Act"), 18 U.S.C. § 2510 et seq. The Act prohibits the intentional interception of wire, oral, or electronic communications. 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(a). However, the Act provides an exception for situations where one party to the communication has given prior consent to the interception. 18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d). 

In this case, Plaintiff recorded the executive session, which was a private conversation among the PLB members. However, it is arguable that Plaintiff was a party to the communication, as he was the subject of the discussion. See United States v. Campagnuolo, 592 F.2d 852, 861-62 (5th Cir. 1979) (holding that a party to a conversation is not considered to be intercepting the conversation). Moreover, the executive session was not a private conversation in the classical sense, as it was related to Plaintiff's grievance. The district court should have considered whether Plaintiff's actions fell within the exception to the Act.

Furthermore, the district court's award of $250,000 in statutory damages was excessive. The Act provides for statutory damages in an amount not less than $1,000 or more than $10,000. 18 U.S.C. § 2520(c)(2)(B). The court should have considered the circumstances of the case and the relative culpability of the parties when determining the amount of damages.

In conclusion, the district court erred in granting summary judgment to Defendants on Plaintiff's RLA claim and in awarding Defendants damages and attorneys' fees under the Act. This Court should reverse the district court's orders and remand the case for further proceedings.
