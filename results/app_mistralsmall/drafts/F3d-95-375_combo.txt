**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ABUSED ITS DISCRETION BY STRIKING THE TESTIMONY OF APPELLANTS’ EXPERTS AND GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT.

A. The district court’s sanction of striking expert testimony was an abuse of discretion because Appellants’ noncompliance with discovery deadlines was neither willful nor in bad faith.

The Fifth Circuit has long held that "[a]lthough a district court has broad discretion in imposing sanctions for discovery violations, such discretion must be exercised in a manner consistent with due process." *Nat’l Hockey League v. Metropolitan Hockey Club*, 427 U.S. 639, 642 (1976) (per curiam) (citing *Societe Internationale v. Rogers*, 357 U.S. 197, 212 (1958)). In *Hudson v. Hardy*, 412 F.2d 1091 (D.C. Cir. 1969), the court emphasized that sanctions must be "proportionate to the fault and the prejudice." *Id.* at 1094. Here, the district court struck Appellants’ experts without making a finding of willful or bad faith noncompliance, despite Appellants’ diligent efforts to comply with the court’s orders. The record reflects that Appellants timely identified their experts and sought protective orders when their experts required additional time to formulate opinions. The district court’s sanction was therefore an abuse of discretion.

B. The district court erred in excluding the testimony of Dr. Schroeder and Dr. Watson under *Daubert* because their methodologies were reliable and their opinions were based on sufficient facts and data.

The Fifth Circuit has consistently held that the admissibility of expert testimony is governed by Federal Rule of Evidence 702, which requires that expert testimony be both reliable and relevant. *United States v. 14.38 Acres of Land*, 80 F.3d 1074, 1077 (5th Cir. 1996) (citing *Daubert*, 509 U.S. at 589). In *United States v. 1,584.66 Acres of Land*, 515 F.2d 624 (5th Cir. 1975), the court held that expert testimony is admissible if it "rests on a reliable foundation and is relevant to the task at hand." *Id.* at 630. Here, Dr. Schroeder and Dr. Watson’s methodologies were based on established scientific principles and their opinions were grounded in the record. The district court’s exclusion of their testimony was therefore erroneous.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT BECAUSE GENUINE ISSUES OF MATERIAL FACT REMAINED REGARDING APPELLANTS’ EXPOSURE TO CHEMICALS AND THEIR RESULTING INJURIES.

A. Summary judgment is inappropriate where, as here, there are genuine disputes of material fact regarding causation and damages.

The Fifth Circuit has repeatedly held that summary judgment is improper where "the evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmoving party." *Vick v. Texas Employment Comm’n*, 514 F.2d 734, 736 (5th Cir. 1975) (quoting *First Nat’l Bank of Arizona v. Cities Serv. Co.*, 391 U.S. 253, 289 (1968)). In *Boudreaux v. Baton Rouge Marine Contractors, Inc.*, 437 F.2d 1011 (5th Cir. 1971), the court held that summary judgment was inappropriate where the plaintiff’s medical evidence, though disputed, raised a jury question on causation. Here, Appellants presented evidence of exposure to hazardous chemicals and resulting injuries, creating a genuine issue of material fact. The district court’s grant of summary judgment was therefore erroneous.

B. The district court’s failure to consider Appellants’ evidence of exposure and injury in the light most favorable to them further warrants reversal.

The Fifth Circuit has long held that summary judgment must be reviewed in the light most favorable to the nonmoving party. *Vick*, 514 F.2d at 736. In *Davis v. Hill Engineering, Inc.*, 549 F.2d 314 (5th Cir. 1977), the court emphasized that "[t]he evidence of the nonmovant is to be believed, and all justifiable inferences are to be drawn in his favor." *Id.* at 317. Here, the district court failed to draw all reasonable inferences in Appellants’ favor, instead resolving factual disputes against them. This was reversible error.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Appellants respectfully request that this Court reverse the district court’s order striking their experts’ testimony and granting summary judgment, and remand for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
