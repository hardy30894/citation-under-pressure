**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE ROBINSON-PATMAN AWARD MUST BE VACATED BECAUSE STELWAGON FAILED TO PROVE ACTUAL ANTITRUST INJURY AND DAMAGES WITH THE REQUISITE CERTAINTY.**

Section 4 of the Clayton Act authorizes recovery only for a plaintiff “injured in his business or property” by an antitrust violation. 15 U.S.C. § 15(a). Although the Robinson-Patman Act may be violated by discriminatory pricing, a private plaintiff still must prove actual injury and damages caused by the violation. J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp., 451 U.S. 557, 562-65 (1981); Zenith Radio Corp. v. Hazeltine Research, Inc., 395 U.S. 100, 114 n.9 (1969). The Supreme Court has repeatedly held that a finding of liability does not itself establish damages: “something more than injury to a competitor must be shown.” J. Truett Payne Co., 451 U.S. at 562.

Here, the Third Circuit has already recognized that Stelwagon established, at most, a prima facie Robinson-Patman violation, but the jury’s damages award cannot stand because the record did not support a non-speculative measure of antitrust loss. See Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc., 327 U.S. 251, 264-66 (1946) (while antitrust damages need not be proven with “mathematical precision,” they must rest on a “just and reasonable estimate” grounded in the evidence); Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co., 282 U.S. 555, 562-66 (1931). Bigelow and Story Parchment relax precision only where the plaintiff has shown injury and the uncertainty concerns the amount, not the existence, of damage. J. Truett Payne Co., 451 U.S. at 566-67. Stelwagon’s proof failed at that threshold.

Stelwagon’s theory was that it lost sales because Tarmac charged competitors lower prices. But the evidence did not establish that any lost sales, reduced margins, or diminution in business were caused by the discriminatory prices rather than by ordinary market forces, customer choice, or Stelwagon’s own business decisions. Under J. Truett Payne, a Robinson-Patman plaintiff cannot recover simply by showing that it paid more than rivals; it must prove that the discrimination caused cognizable injury. 451 U.S. at 562-66. The Court rejected the notion that “automatic damages” follow from price discrimination, explaining that the statute “does not provide a cause of action for every violation” without proof of injury. Id. at 562.

The evidence here was even weaker than that rejected in J. Truett Payne. There, the plaintiff offered some proof of adverse effects; here, Stelwagon’s damages case rested on broad assertions and an expert model untethered to proof that the alleged discriminatory prices actually diverted sales or profits from Stelwagon. Damages based on speculation cannot support a Clayton Act award. See Missouri v. Jenkins, 491 U.S. 274, 286-87 (1989); Bigelow, 327 U.S. at 264-66. The district court’s remittitur underscores that the jury’s verdict was not supported by the proof. A remittitur may correct excess, but it cannot supply missing proof of antitrust injury. Cf. Donovan v. Penn Shipping Co., 429 U.S. 648, 649-50 (1977) (per curiam).

Nor can Stelwagon rely on the fact that it was one of Tarmac’s distributors in an allegedly exclusive arrangement. A Robinson-Patman claim requires proof that price discrimination caused an injury in the plaintiff’s business or property. 15 U.S.C. § 15(a); J. Truett Payne Co., 451 U.S. at 562-65. The existence of an oral distributorship agreement does not relax that requirement. To the extent Stelwagon suffered losses from the breach of contract, those losses are not antitrust damages unless they were caused by the discriminatory pricing itself. See Cargill, Inc. v. Monfort of Colorado, Inc., 479 U.S. 104, 109-13 (1986); Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat, Inc., 429 U.S. 477, 489 (1977) (antitrust damages require injury “of the type the antitrust laws were intended to prevent and that flows from that which makes defendants’ acts unlawful”).

Because Stelwagon failed to prove actual antitrust injury and a non-speculative measure of damages, the Robinson-Patman treble-damages award must be vacated. J. Truett Payne Co., 451 U.S. at 566-67; Brunswick Corp., 429 U.S. at 489; Bigelow, 327 U.S. at 264-66.

**II. AT A MINIMUM, THE CASE MUST BE REMANDED FOR ENTRY OF JUDGMENT ON CONTRACT DAMAGES ONLY, OR FOR A NEW TRIAL ON THE ANTITRUST CLAIM.**

Where an antitrust damages award lacks evidentiary support, the proper remedy is vacatur of that award. J. Truett Payne Co., 451 U.S. at 566-67; Zenith Radio Corp., 395 U.S. at 123-24. Because the jury separately found breach of contract, any recoverable amount must be confined to the contract theory as reduced by the district court’s remittitur. See Gasoline Prods. Co. v. Champlin Refining Co., 283 U.S. 494, 500-01 (1931) (new trial may be limited only where issues are distinct and separable). The antitrust verdict here was not separable from the jury’s global damages determination; once the Robinson-Patman award is removed, the remaining judgment must be recalculated accordingly.

For these reasons, the Court should vacate the treble-damages judgment under the Clayton Act and remand for entry of judgment limited to any properly supported contract damages, or alternatively for a new trial confined to antitrust damages if the Court concludes further proceedings are necessary.
