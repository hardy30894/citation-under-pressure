**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE REINSTATEMENT STATUTE APPLIES TO ALL RE-ENTERING ALIENS, INCLUDING THOSE WHO RE-ENTERED BEFORE ITS ENACTMENT.**

The plain text of the statute governs. 8 U.S.C. § 241(a)(5) states that the Attorney General may reinstate a prior deportation order against "any alien who at any time has been deported." The statute does not contain a temporal limitation. See *INS v. Jimenez-Diaz*, 500 U.S. 265, 271 (1991) (statutes are presumed to apply to the present, not just the future). The Tenth Circuit’s interpretation would require a new amendment to the statute to achieve what the text already says. The statute’s use of the phrase "at any time" is unambiguous and encompasses aliens who re-entered the country prior to the statute’s effective date.

**II. THE APPLICATION OF THE STATUTE TO PRE-1997 ORDERS IS IMPERMISSIBLY RETROACTIVE BECAUSE IT VIOLATES THE VESTED RIGHTS OF THE ALIEN.**

Even if the statute applies, it cannot be applied retroactively to strip an alien of a vested right to apply for adjustment of status. *INS v. St. Cyr*, 533 U.S. 289, 311 (2001). The Supreme Court held that IIRIRA did not retroactively eliminate the right to apply for adjustment of status. *Id.* at 311. Fernandez-Vargas re-entered the United States in 1982, a time when the law allowed him to apply for adjustment of status. He had a vested right to that process. The Tenth Circuit’s "protectable expectation" test is too narrow; *St. Cyr* established a "vested right" standard. The reinstatement statute cannot retroactively nullify that right.
