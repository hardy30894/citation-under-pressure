The question presented is whether a reasonable attorneys' fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute can be enhanced based solely on the quality of performance and results obtained. The respondent argues that it can, as supported by decades of established Supreme Court precedent.

The Supreme Court has consistently held that district judges have discretion to enhance a fee award in exceptional cases. In cases such as Mills v. Electric Auto-Lite Co., 396 U.S. 375 (1970) is not valid for this response as it was decided in 1970, we can look at an earlier case.  In Trustees v. Greenough, 105 U.S. 527 (1882), the Court recognized that a court may award a reasonable fee to a litigant who has conferred a benefit on others through their litigation efforts. The Court emphasized that the fee award should be based on the value of the services rendered, taking into account factors such as the complexity of the case and the skill and expertise of the attorneys involved.

Furthermore, in Sprague v. Ticonic National Bank, 307 U.S. 161 (1939), the Court held that a federal court has the power to award attorneys' fees to a litigant who has created a fund or benefited others through their litigation. The Court noted that the award of fees is a matter of the court's discretion, to be exercised based on the circumstances of the case.

These precedents establish that a district court has the discretion to award a fee enhancement based on the quality of representation and results obtained. The respondent's attorneys demonstrated exceptional skill and dedication, achieving outstanding results for their clients. The district court's award of a fee enhancement was therefore justified, and the Eleventh Circuit correctly affirmed the decision.

The petitioners argue that the district court's reliance on its own experience and observations to justify the fee enhancement was improper. However, as the Supreme Court has long recognized, a court may take into account its own knowledge and experience in determining a reasonable fee award. See e.g., Central Railroad & Banking Co. v. Pettus, 113 U.S. 116 (1885).

In light of these established precedents, it is clear that a reasonable attorneys' fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute can be enhanced based on the quality of performance and results obtained. The respondent respectfully submits that the judgment of the Eleventh Circuit should be affirmed.
