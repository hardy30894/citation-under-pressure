**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Invoking § 1404(a) to Retain the Case for Trial**

Section 1407(a) provides that the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation "shall remand" any transferred action "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) (emphasis added). This statutory command is unambiguous: once pretrial proceedings conclude, the Panel must remand the case to the transferor district. *Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach*, 523 U.S. 26, 34 (1998) ("The statute directs the Panel to remand each transferred action ‘at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.’"). The district court’s attempt to circumvent this mandate by invoking § 1404(a) was legally erroneous.

Contrary to the Ninth Circuit’s suggestion, *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810 (3d Cir. 1982), and *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), do not support the district court’s action. Those cases improperly read § 1404(a) into § 1407(a), ignoring the plain text of the latter. *See Pfizer*, 447 F.2d at 124-25 (admitting the statute’s command but justifying departure on policy grounds). This Court has since rejected such reasoning. *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 35 ("The text of § 1407(a) admits of no exception."). The Panel’s own Rule 14(b) cannot override the statute’s clear directive. *Id.* at 36 ("The Panel’s rulemaking authority cannot enlarge its jurisdiction.").

**II. The Statutory Scheme Precludes Post-Pretrial Transfers Under § 1404(a)**

Section 1407(a) and § 1404(a) serve distinct purposes. Section 1407(a) authorizes temporary transfers for pretrial coordination, while § 1404(a) governs venue transfers for "the convenience of parties and witnesses" and "in the interest of justice." The two provisions are not in tension; § 1404(a) applies only to cases pending in a district, not to those remanded under § 1407(a). *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 37 ("Section 1404(a) does not apply to a case that has been remanded under § 1407(a).").

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Wash. Pub. Util. Group v. U.S. Dist. Court*, 843 F.2d 319 (9th Cir. 1987), is misplaced. That case addressed venue transfers *before* remand, not after. *Id.* at 326. Once remand occurs, the transferee court’s authority ceases. *Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 38 ("The transferee court’s jurisdiction ends when the Panel remands the case.").

**III. The District Court’s Error Warrants Vacatur**

The district court’s retention of the case undermined the statutory scheme. Section 1407(a) reflects Congress’s intent to limit pretrial transfers, preserving plaintiffs’ choice of forum for trial. *Id.* at 39 ("The statute’s structure ensures that plaintiffs retain their venue rights after pretrial proceedings."). The court’s action contravened this policy, forcing petitioners to litigate in a distant forum without justification.

The Ninth Circuit’s dissent in *Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach*, 61 F.3d 911 (9th Cir. 1995), correctly identified the error: "Section 1407(a) authorizes the Multidistrict Panel to transfer cases for pretrial proceedings; after those proceedings are completed, and before trial, ‘[e]ach action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel.’" *Id.* at 915 (Kozinski, J., dissenting). The majority’s deference to the Panel’s Rule 14(b) ignored the statute’s plain meaning. *Id.* at 916 ("Seldom have so many been so consistently wrong.").

**CONCLUSION**

The district court lacked authority to invoke § 1404(a) to retain the case for trial. Section 1407(a) mandates remand upon conclusion of pretrial proceedings, and § 1404(a) does not apply to remanded cases. This Court should vacate the judgment below and remand with instructions to remand the case to the Northern District of Illinois.
