**ARGUMENT**

**I. Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial**

The plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) requires that any action transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." *Goldlawr, Inc. v. Heiman*, 369 U.S. 463, 466 (1962) (interpreting § 1407(a) to require remand "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings"). This Court has recognized that § 1407(a) "was designed to provide centralized pretrial proceedings for the convenience of the parties and witnesses and to prevent duplicative and inconsistent rulings." *In re Antibiotic Antitrust Actions*, 333 F.2d 204, 206 (6th Cir. 1964). The statute’s remand requirement is not merely precatory; it is mandatory. *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466 ("The statute thus makes clear that the Panel’s power over transferred cases is limited to pretrial proceedings.").

The text of § 1407(a) admits of no exception permitting a transferee court to retain a case for trial. The statute’s command is absolute: "Each action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) (emphasis added). This Court has held that where a statute’s language is unambiguous, courts must give effect to its plain meaning. *United States v. First City Nat’l Bank*, 386 U.S. 361, 366 (1967) ("When the words of a statute are unambiguous, judicial inquiry is complete."). The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation has no authority to deviate from this mandate, nor may a transferee court arrogate to itself the power to retain a case for trial. *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466 ("The Panel’s power over transferred cases is limited to pretrial proceedings.").

**II. Section 1404(a) Does Not Authorize a Transferee Court to Retain a Case for Trial**

Section 1404(a) permits a district court to transfer a case "for the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice." 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). However, this authority is circumscribed by the jurisdictional and procedural framework established by Congress. *Ex parte Collett*, 337 U.S. 55, 61 (1949) ("The power of a court to transfer an action to another district is limited by the terms of the statute."). A transferee court may not use § 1404(a) to circumvent the mandatory remand requirement of § 1407(a).

This Court has recognized that § 1404(a) is not a tool for retaining cases beyond the scope of pretrial proceedings. *Continental Grain Co. v. Barge FBL-585*, 364 U.S. 19, 26 (1960) ("The statute does not permit a court to transfer a case to another district for any purpose other than convenience and justice in the conduct of the litigation."). The transferee court’s attempt to "transfer" the case to itself for trial under § 1404(a) is an impermissible expansion of its authority. *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466 ("The Panel’s power over transferred cases is limited to pretrial proceedings.").

**III. The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation Lacks Authority to Deviate from § 1407(a)’s Remand Requirement**

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation is a creature of statute, and its powers are limited to those conferred by Congress. *In re Antibiotic Antitrust Actions*, 333 F.2d at 206 ("The Panel’s authority is derived solely from § 1407."). The Panel has no inherent equitable power to disregard the statute’s clear command. *Heiman*, 369 U.S. at 466 ("The statute thus makes clear that the Panel’s power over transferred cases is limited to pretrial proceedings.").

The Panel’s attempt to retain a case for trial under the guise of "pretrial proceedings" is inconsistent with the statute’s text and purpose. *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466. The Panel’s authority does not extend to retaining cases for trial, and any such attempt is ultra vires. *In re Antibiotic Antitrust Actions*, 333 F.2d at 206 ("The Panel’s authority is limited to pretrial proceedings.").

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should hold that a federal district court conducting pretrial proceedings under § 1407(a) may not invoke § 1404(a) to assign a transferred case to itself for trial. The plain text of § 1407(a) mandates remand to the transferor district for trial, and neither the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation nor the transferee court may deviate from this requirement.
