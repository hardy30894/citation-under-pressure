A. Constitutionality of 18 U.S.C. § 1951

Congress lacked authority under the Commerce Clause to enact 18 U.S.C. § 1951. That Clause confers power only over activities that directly affect interstate commerce. United States v. E.C. Knight Co., 156 U.S. 1, 12 (1895). Activities of production or local crime remain beyond federal reach when their connection to commerce is merely indirect. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States, 295 U.S. 495, 548 (1935); Carter v. Carter Coal Co., 298 U.S. 238, 304 (1936).

The robbery of a single Kansas restaurant, even one that later purchases goods from other states, constitutes an intrastate transaction whose supposed effect on commerce is at most secondary. Houston, East & West Texas Railway Co. v. United States, 234 U.S. 342, 351 (1914). Such remote consequences do not supply the requisite direct link. A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp., 295 U.S. at 548. The statute therefore exceeds the enumerated power and cannot sustain the convictions.
