**ARGUMENT**

Section 1407(a) provides that actions transferred for pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded by the Panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). This command is “clear.” *Van Dusen v. Barrack*, 376 U.S. 612, 619 (1964). Once pretrial proceedings end, the statute leaves the transferee court no authority to retain the case for trial.

Section 1404(a) supplies no exception. It authorizes transfer only “to any other district or division where it might have been brought.” 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). The statute addresses venue among districts of equal authority; it does not empower a court whose jurisdiction is limited by another statute to enlarge that jurisdiction. *Hoffman v. Blaski*, 363 U.S. 335, 342-43 (1960) (transfer power “is not unbridled” and exists only where the statute permits). A transferee court under § 1407 therefore cannot invoke § 1404(a) to assign the action to itself.

The legislative design confirms the limitation. Section 1407 was enacted to centralize pretrial matters while preserving plaintiffs’ choice of forum for trial. *See Continental Grain Co. v. Barge FBL-585*, 364 U.S. 19, 26 (1960) (venue statutes reflect “ancient right” to select forum). Allowing self-transfer would nullify the mandatory remand and convert a temporary assignment into permanent control, contrary to the statute’s text and purpose. *Ferens v. John Deere Co.*, 494 U.S. 516, 523 (1990) (transfer under § 1404(a) “does not change the law applicable” or expand substantive authority).

The court below therefore erred in retaining the action for trial. The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
