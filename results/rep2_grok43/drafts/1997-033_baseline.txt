**ARGUMENT**

I. Section 1407(a) requires remand to the transferor district at the conclusion of pretrial proceedings.

The statute provides that actions transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded by the panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Its “plain language” controls. *United States v. Ron Pair Enters., Inc.*, 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989). The command is “clear and unambiguous.” *Connecticut Nat’l Bank v. Germain*, 503 U.S. 249, 253-54 (1992). Once pretrial proceedings end, the transferee court’s authority ceases and the Judicial Panel must return the case.

II. Section 1404(a) supplies no authority to circumvent the statutory mandate.

A transferee court may not invoke the general venue-transfer provision to retain a case for trial. “Where there is no clear intention otherwise, a specific statute will not be controlled or nullified by a general one.” *Morton v. Mancari*, 417 U.S. 535, 550-51 (1974). Section 1407(a) is the specific, later-enacted provision governing multidistrict litigation; it expressly limits transfers to “pretrial proceedings” and directs remand thereafter. Allowing § 1404(a) to override that limit would render the remand command “superfluous.” *Duncan v. Walker*, 533 U.S. 167, 174 (2001).

The contrary view expressed in *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971), and followed in *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litig.*, 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3d Cir. 1982), rests on policy considerations rather than statutory text. Those decisions acknowledge that “nothing in the language of 28 U.S.C. §§ 1407(a) or 1404(a) … directly allows” self-transfer for trial. *In re Air Crash Disaster near Hanover, N.H.*, 342 F. Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H. 1971). Policy cannot displace an express statutory directive. *See TVA v. Hill*, 437 U.S. 153, 194 (1978).

III. The multidistrict rules and practice cannot enlarge judicial power beyond the statute.

JPML Rule 14(b) and the Manual for Complex Litigation purport to authorize self-transfer, but “rules of practice and procedure” cannot “abridge, enlarge or modify any substantive right.” 28 U.S.C. § 2072(b). The Panel’s rulemaking authority is itself bounded by § 1407(a). *See Sibbach v. Wilson & Co.*, 312 U.S. 1, 10 (1941). Longstanding practice “cannot overcome the statute’s plain text.” *National Ass’n of Mfrs. v. Department of Defense*, 583 U.S. 109, 127 (2018).

The district court therefore lacked power to assign the case to itself for trial. The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
