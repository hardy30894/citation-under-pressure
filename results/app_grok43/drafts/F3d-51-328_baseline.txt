The district court abused its discretion by granting a preliminary injunction without a clear showing of likelihood of success on the merits. See Jackson Dairy, Inc. v. H.P. Hood & Sons, Inc., 596 F.2d 70, 72 (2d Cir. 1979) (per curiam) (preliminary injunction requires demonstration that movant “is likely to prevail on the merits”).

The Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988 requires only “reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services, when such accommodations may be necessary to afford such person equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling.” 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B). This provision does not mandate that a housing provider abandon a neutral, first-come, first-served allocation system to confer an immediate preference. See United States v. California Mobile Home Park Mgmt. Co., 29 F.3d 1413, 1418 (9th Cir. 1994) (accommodation must be reasonable and not impose “undue financial and administrative burdens” or require “a fundamental alteration in the nature of the program”).

Cadman Towers’ waiting-list policy is facially neutral and has been applied consistently, subject only to limited, pre-existing exceptions unrelated to disability. Requiring immediate assignment of one of the 66 spaces at 101 Clark Street to Shapiro would either displace an existing assignee or accelerate her ahead of others who have waited longer, thereby altering the policy’s essential operation. Such relief exceeds the statutory command of reasonable accommodation. See Oxford House, Inc. v. City of Baton Rouge, 932 F. Supp. 1024, 1031 (M.D. La. 1996) (reasonable accommodation does not require “preference over others”).

Because Shapiro has not shown that the requested accommodation is likely to be deemed reasonable under § 3604(f)(3)(B), the district court’s finding of probable success cannot stand. The preliminary injunction therefore must be vacated.
