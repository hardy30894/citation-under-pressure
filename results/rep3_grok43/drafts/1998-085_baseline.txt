**ARGUMENT**

Title VII authorizes punitive damages only upon a showing that the employer engaged in a discriminatory practice “with malice or with reckless indifference to the federally protected rights of an aggrieved individual.” 42 U.S.C. § 1981a(b)(1). That statutory phrase incorporates the common-law standard governing punitive awards, which requires conduct that is “egregious” or “outrageous” independent of the defendant’s state of mind. See Smith v. Wade, 461 U.S. 30, 46-47 (1983) (punitive damages available only when conduct is “of the sort that calls for deterrence and punishment over and above that provided by compensatory awards”); Restatement (Second) of Torts § 908 cmt. b (1979), cited with approval in Haslip, 499 U.S. at 19.

This Court has repeatedly held that “a showing of actual malice or its equivalent” is insufficient; the defendant’s conduct must also “evince a ‘conscious disregard of the rights of others’” rising to the level of “outrageous” behavior. See BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, 517 U.S. 559, 576-77 (1996); TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources Corp., 509 U.S. 443, 453-54 (1993). The same requirement applies under § 1981a. See Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244, 252-55 (1994) (1991 Act “significantly expands the monetary relief potentially available,” but does so by importing established common-law limits).

The court of appeals correctly recognized that Kolstad’s evidence, while sufficient for a finding of intentional discrimination, did not demonstrate the independent outrageousness necessary for punitive damages. See Kolstad v. American Dental Ass’n, 108 F.3d 1431, 1440-41 (D.C. Cir. 1997) (en banc). Preselection of a candidate and after-the-fact rationalizations, without more, do not satisfy the common-law threshold. See McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792, 804-05 (1973) (pretext evidence supports liability but does not automatically establish the higher culpability required for exemplary relief).

Because the district court properly refused to instruct the jury on punitive damages, the judgment should be affirmed.
