The OCSLA compensation provision extends LHWCA coverage solely to injuries occurring on the outer continental shelf. Section 1333(b) applies only where disability or death results from injury "occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf." 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The statute's text thus imposes a geographic limitation on the injury itself. Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207 (1986), confirms that "Congress determined that the general scope of OCSLA's coverage . . . would be determined principally by locale, not by the status of the individual injured or killed." Id. at 219. The Court further noted that "Only one provision of OCSLA superimposes a status requirement on the otherwise determinative OCSLA situs requirement; § 1333(b)." Id. at 219 n.2.

Mills v. Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs, 877 F.2d 356 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc), correctly held that an OCSLA claimant must show the injury occurred on an outer continental shelf platform or the waters above it, in addition to satisfying a but-for test. Id. at 362. The Fifth Circuit has consistently applied this rule. See Becker v. Tidewater, Inc., 586 F.3d 358, 366-67 (5th Cir. 2009); Pickett v. Petroleum Helicopters, Inc., 266 F.3d 366, 368 (5th Cir. 2001); Sisson v. Davis & Sons, Inc., 131 F.3d 555, 558 (5th Cir. 1998). Herb's Welding, Inc. v. Gray, 470 U.S. 414 (1985), reinforces the point by observing that OCSLA "draws a clear geographic boundary that will be crossed by some workers." Id. at 427. An injury sustained onshore therefore falls outside the statute. Herb's Welding v. Gray, 766 F.2d 898, 900 (5th Cir. 1985).

The Benefits Review Board properly applied these authorities when it denied benefits under the situs-of-injury test. Pedroza v. BRB, 583 F.3d 1139, 1143 (9th Cir. 2009). The Board's construction is reasonable and reflects the policy underlying the statute. Christensen v. Stevedoring Servs. of Am., 557 F.3d 1049, 1052 (9th Cir. 2009) (quoting McDonald v. Dir., Office of Workers' Comp. Programs, 897 F.2d 1510, 1512 (9th Cir. 1990)). By contrast, Curtis v. Schlumberger Offshore Service, Inc., 849 F.2d 805 (3d Cir. 1988), erroneously dispensed with any situs requirement. Id. at 809-11.

The same geographic principle governs the LHWCA claim. An employee must suffer injury "upon the navigable waters of the United States" or an adjoining area customarily used for loading or unloading vessels. 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). La Conchita is separated from the water by a highway and railroad tracks, lies three miles from the nearest pier used by Pacific Operations, and adjoins no maritime facilities. Motoviloff v. Dir., Office of Workers' Comp. Programs, 692 F.2d 87, 89 (9th Cir. 1982). Its primary function is non-maritime processing of crude oil slurry. Herb's Welding, 470 U.S. at 422-24. The Board correctly concluded that the facility is not a maritime situs. Williams v. Dir., Office of Workers' Comp. Programs, 825 F.2d 246, 247 (9th Cir. 1987).

The judgment of the Benefits Review Board should be affirmed.
