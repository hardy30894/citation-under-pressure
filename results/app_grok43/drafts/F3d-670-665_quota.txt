ARGUMENT

This Court has held that a credible showing of actual innocence equitably tolls AEDPA’s limitations period. Souter v. Jones, 395 F.3d 577, 602 (6th Cir. 2005). The district court erred by grafting onto that rule an additional reasonable-diligence requirement drawn from Pace v. DiGuglielmo, 544 U.S. 408 (2005).

The actual-innocence gateway rests on the fundamental miscarriage-of-justice exception. In Schlup v. Delo, 513 U.S. 298, 316 (1995), the Court explained that the exception permits review when a petitioner shows that “a constitutional violation has probably resulted in the conviction of one who is actually innocent.” The same standard governs successive-petition and procedural-default cases alike. Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436, 454 (1986); Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478, 496 (1986). Nothing in those decisions conditions the gateway on the petitioner’s diligence after discovering the evidence.

House v. Bell, 547 U.S. 518, 536-37 (2006), reaffirmed that the Schlup standard is “demanding” yet contains no diligence component. Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722, 750 (1991), likewise treats the miscarriage exception as an independent equitable override of procedural barriers. Holland v. Florida, 560 U.S. 631, 649 (2010), confirmed that equitable principles govern AEDPA’s limitations period and that “extraordinary circumstances” may justify tolling; a credible innocence claim satisfies that standard without separate proof of diligence.

Pace addressed only the ordinary equitable-tolling inquiry and did not purport to alter the miscarriage-of-justice line of cases. Because Perkins has presented new evidence that, if credible, would satisfy Schlup, the limitations period is equitably tolled and the petition must be addressed on the merits.
