ARGUMENT

A criminal defendant lacks standing to challenge a federal statute on Tenth Amendment grounds. The Tenth Amendment “reserved to the States respectively, or to the people” the powers not delegated to the United States. U.S. Const. amend. X. This reservation protects the states as sovereign entities. Tennessee Elec. Power Co. v. Tennessee Valley Auth., 306 U.S. 118, 144 (1939). Private parties therefore may not invoke it. Id. (“[T]he [companies], absent the states or their officers, have no standing in this suit to raise any question under the [Tenth] Amendment.”).

Standing requires that the plaintiff assert an injury to a legally protected interest of his own. Frothingham v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447, 488 (1923); Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447, 485 (1923). A litigant “cannot rest his claim to relief on the legal rights or interests of third parties.” Barrows v. Jackson, 346 U.S. 249, 255 (1953). The Tenth Amendment confers no personal right on individuals. Alabama Power Co. v. Ickes, 302 U.S. 464, 479 (1938); Ashwander v. Tennessee Valley Auth., 297 U.S. 288, 347-48 (1936) (Brandeis, J., concurring).

This Court has repeatedly refused to permit private assertion of structural constitutional claims that belong to the states. Perkins v. Lukens Steel Co., 310 U.S. 113, 125 (1940); Tileston v. Ullman, 318 U.S. 44, 46 (1943). Only the states themselves, or their officers, may litigate the division of power between the federal government and the states. United States v. Raines, 362 U.S. 17, 22 (1960); Screws v. United States, 325 U.S. 91, 109 (1945).

Bond’s conviction rests on a statute enacted to implement a treaty. Missouri v. Holland, 252 U.S. 416, 432 (1920). The validity of that enactment under the Necessary and Proper Clause does not create a personal right that Bond may assert. See Tennessee Elec. Power Co., 306 U.S. at 143-44. Because no state has objected and Bond possesses no independent interest protected by the Tenth Amendment, she lacks standing to raise the claim.
