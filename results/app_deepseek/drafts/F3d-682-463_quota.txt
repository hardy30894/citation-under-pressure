**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment Because Wasek Presented Sufficient Evidence of Same-Sex Sexual Harassment Under Title VII and the ELCRA.**

Title VII prohibits discrimination “because of . . . sex,” 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(a)(1), which includes same-sex sexual harassment. *Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc.*, 523 U.S. 75, 79 (1998). To prevail, a plaintiff must show that the harassment occurred “because of sex.” *Id.* at 80. The Supreme Court recognized three evidentiary routes for establishing this element: (1) credible evidence that the harasser was homosexual and motivated by sexual desire; (2) evidence of general hostility to one gender; or (3) “direct comparative evidence” that the harasser treated members of one sex less favorably. *Id.* at 80-81.

**A. Wasek Presented Credible Evidence That Ottobre’s Harassment Was Motivated by Sexual Desire.**

Wasek testified that Ottobre touched him in a sexual manner—grabbing his buttocks, poking his rear with a hammer handle, and jabbing him with a sucker rod. *Wasek Dep.* at 45-46, 51-52. Ottobre made sexually explicit comments, including “you’ve got a pretty mouth,” “you know you like it sweetheart,” and directed sexually explicit jokes and stories at Wasek. *Id.* at 53-54. Wasek believed Ottobre acted this way “because he was bisexual.” *Id.* at 57. This evidence directly supports an inference of sexual desire sufficient to survive summary judgment. *See Oncale*, 523 U.S. at 80 (harasser’s “explicit or implicit proposals of sexual activity” support inference of sex-based animus); *Williams v. Waste Mgmt. of Ill., Inc.*, 361 F.3d 1021, 1024 (7th Cir. 2004) (same-sex harassment where coworker made sexual remarks and grabbed plaintiff’s groin). The fact that Ottobre may have harassed others does not defeat the inference. *See EEOC v. Harbert-Yeargin, Inc.*, 266 F.3d 498, 521 (6th Cir. 2001) (harassment of multiple victims of same sex does not preclude liability).

**B. Wasek Also Satisfies the “General Hostility” Route, as the Harassment Was Based on Gender Stereotypes.**

Even absent direct evidence of sexual desire, Wasek can show harassment “because of sex” through evidence of gender-stereotype animus. In *Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins*, 490 U.S. 228, 250-51 (1989), the Supreme Court held that discrimination based on failure to conform to gender stereotypes is discrimination “because of sex.” This principle applies to same-sex harassment. *See Smith v. City of Salem*, 378 F.3d 566, 575 (6th Cir. 2004) (holding that “discrimination based on a failure to conform to stereotypical gender norms” is actionable). Here, Ottobre mocked Wasek for failing to adhere to masculine norms of aggression—calling him a “whiner,” telling him to “man up,” and suggesting he “duke it out.” *Wasek Dep.* at 58-59. Such epithets are “directed at a target’s perceived failure to conform to gender stereotypes.” *Roh v. D.O.C. Optics Corp.*, 102 F. App’x 99, 101 (8th Cir. 2004). Wasek’s “starvation schedule” threat by Tripp also suggests hostility to perceived feminine weakness. *See Nichols v. Azteca Rest. Enters., Inc.*, 256 F.3d 864, 874-75 (9th Cir. 2001) (male employees called “faggot” and “girl” for not acting masculine). This evidence creates a triable issue of fact.

**C. Arrow Energy Is Liable Because It Failed to Take Prompt and Appropriate Remedial Action.**

An employer is liable for coworker harassment if it “knew or should have known” of the conduct and failed to act. *Williams*, 361 F.3d at 1027 (quoting *Hafford v. Seidner*, 183 F.3d 506, 513 (6th Cir. 1999)). Wasek reported the harassment to Tripp, who laughed and threatened retaliation. *Wasek Dep.* at 70-71. When Wasek escalated to Smillie, Smillie told him to “kick [Ottobre’s] ass.” *Id.* at 56. When Wasek called Strong, Strong told him to stop “whining” and suggested “duking it out.” *Id.* at 57. None took any remedial action. *See Faragher v. City of Boca Raton*, 524 U.S. 775, 806 (1998) (employer must take “prompt and appropriate corrective action”). Arrow Energy’s response was “clearly unreasonable” and insufficient as a matter of law. *Blankenship v. Parke Care Ctrs., Inc.*, 123 F.3d 868, 873 (6th Cir. 1997).

**II. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Wasek’s ELCRA Claim on Identical Grounds.**

Michigan’s ELCRA is interpreted coextensively with Title VII. *Radtke v. Everett*, 442 Mich. 368, 381 (1993). Because genuine issues of fact preclude summary judgment on Wasek’s Title VII claim, the same analysis applies to his ELCRA claim. *See Brocklehurst v. PPG Indus., Inc.*, 123 F.3d 890, 895 (6th Cir. 1997) (ELCRA parallels Title VII). The district court thus erred in dismissing both claims.

**Conclusion**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the grant of summary judgment and remand for trial.
