**ARGUMENT**

I. The District Court Erred in Granting a Preliminary Injunction Because Shapiro Failed to Demonstrate a Likelihood of Success on the Merits.

The Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988 (“FHAA”), 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f), prohibits discrimination against disabled individuals in the sale or rental of housing. *United States v. California Mobile Home Park Management Co.*, 107 F.3d 1374, 1378 (9th Cir. 1997) (citing *Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Ins. Co.*, 409 U.S. 205, 209 (1972)). However, the FHAA does not impose an unqualified obligation to accommodate disabled individuals. Rather, it requires only “reasonable accommodations” in rules, policies, practices, or services when such accommodations may be necessary to afford a disabled person equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling. *Id.* (citing *United States v. Overlook Towers Apartments*, 643 F. Supp. 1387, 1390 (D.N.J. 1986)). Here, Cadman Towers’ policy of allocating parking spaces on a first-come, first-served basis is neutral on its face and does not facially discriminate against disabled individuals. *See* *Washington v. Davis*, 426 U.S. 229, 242 (1976) (holding that facially neutral policies are not discriminatory unless they are motivated by discriminatory intent).

Shapiro has not demonstrated that Cadman Towers’ policy must yield to her requested accommodation. The FHAA’s reasonable accommodation requirement does not mandate that a housing provider alter its policies to confer a benefit not generally available to others. *See* *United States v. Yonkers Bd. of Educ.*, 837 F.2d 118, 124 (2d Cir. 1987) (citing *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792, 802 (1973)), *aff’d in part and rev’d in part on other grounds*, 490 U.S. 915 (1989). Shapiro seeks a parking space on the ground floor of the 101 Clark Street garage, a benefit not provided to any other resident. Cadman Towers’ policy does not foreclose Shapiro from obtaining a parking space; it simply requires her to wait her turn like all other residents. *See* *Griggs v. Duke Power Co.*, 401 U.S. 424, 431 (1971) (holding that an employer’s neutral policy does not violate Title VII if it applies to all employees equally).

Moreover, the requested accommodation is not reasonable because it would fundamentally alter the nature of Cadman Towers’ parking policy. *See* *United States v. City of Chicago*, 549 F. Supp. 415, 425 (N.D. Ill. 1982) (citing *United States v. Frazer*, 474 F. Supp. 60, 63 (E.D. Wis. 1979)). A fundamental alteration is one that imposes undue financial or administrative burdens or requires a fundamental change in the nature of the provider’s operations. *Id.* Here, granting Shapiro a ground-floor parking space would require Cadman Towers to deviate from its longstanding first-come, first-served policy, which has been in place for decades. *See* *Daniels v. Board of Educ. of Rahway*, 162 F. Supp. 825, 828 (D.N.J. 1958) (holding that a policy’s longevity and consistent application weigh against a finding of discrimination).

II. The District Court Erred in Balancing the Equities Because It Failed to Consider the Burdens on Cadman Towers.

A preliminary injunction is an extraordinary remedy that should not issue unless the balance of equities tips sharply in the plaintiff’s favor. *See* *Virginia Petroleum Jobbers Ass’n v. Federal Power Comm’n*, 259 F.2d 921, 925 (D.C. Cir. 1958). Here, the District Court did not properly weigh the hardships Shapiro would suffer absent an injunction against the hardships Cadman Towers would face if forced to grant her accommodation. *See* *United States v. W. Pac. R.R. Co.*, 325 F.2d 260, 263 (9th Cir. 1963) (holding that a court must consider the relative hardships before granting injunctive relief).

Cadman Towers’ parking policy is a longstanding, neutral rule that ensures fair and equal access to a scarce resource. *See* *United States v. City of Yonkers*, 32 F. Supp. 265, 270 (S.D.N.Y. 1940) (holding that a neutral policy applied uniformly does not violate equal protection principles). Shapiro’s requested accommodation would disrupt this policy and confer a benefit not available to other residents. *See* *United States v. Jefferson County Bd. of Educ.*, 372 F.2d 836, 850 (5th Cir. 1967) (holding that a court should not impose a remedy that would create a privileged class of individuals).

Furthermore, Cadman Towers would suffer significant administrative and financial burdens if required to grant Shapiro’s request. *See* *United States v. City of Chicago*, 549 F. Supp. at 425 (citing *United States v. Frazer*, 474 F. Supp. at 63). The cooperative would be forced to deviate from its established policy, potentially inviting similar requests from other residents. *See* *Daniels v. Board of Educ. of Rahway*, 162 F. Supp. at 828 (holding that a policy’s consistent application weighs against disruption).

III. The District Court Erred in Failing to Consider the Public Interest.

The public interest weighs against granting a preliminary injunction in this case. *See* *United States v. W. Pac. R.R. Co.*, 325 F.2d at 263. The FHAA was enacted to prevent discrimination in housing, not to require housing providers to confer special benefits on disabled individuals. *See* *Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Ins. Co.*, 409 U.S. at 211 (holding that the FHAA’s purpose is to prevent discrimination, not to mandate preferential treatment). Here, Cadman Towers has not discriminated against Shapiro; it has simply applied its neutral parking policy. *See* *Washington v. Davis*, 426 U.S. at 242 (holding that facially neutral policies are not discriminatory unless motivated by discriminatory intent).

Moreover, the requested accommodation would not further the FHAA’s goal of ensuring equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling. *See* *United States v. Overlook Towers Apartments*, 643 F. Supp. at 1390. Shapiro can still obtain a parking space; she simply must wait her turn like all other residents. *See* *Griggs v. Duke Power Co.*, 401 U.S. at 431 (holding that a neutral policy does not violate the law if it applies to all equally).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the District Court erred in granting a preliminary injunction. The judgment below should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this brief.
