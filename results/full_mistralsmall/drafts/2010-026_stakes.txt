**ARGUMENT**

I. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE EXEMPT FROM DISCLOSURE UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 2.

A. Exemption 2’s Text and Supreme Court Precedent Limit Its Scope to “Internal Personnel Rules and Practices.”

FOIA Exemption 2 exempts from disclosure “matters that are related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Supreme Court has emphasized that FOIA exemptions are “explicitly exclusive” and must be “narrowly construed.” *Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 360-61 (1976); *U.S. Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989). The Ninth Circuit’s “predominantly internal” standard contravenes this principle by expanding Exemption 2 far beyond its statutory text.

The Supreme Court in *Rose* expressly rejected a broad reading of Exemption 2, noting that it applies only to “routine matters” such as “parking facilities, lunch hours, and sick leave.” *Id.* at 363. The Court distinguished such mundane matters from more sensitive information, which Congress addressed in other exemptions. *Id.* at 369. The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test—under which ESQD arc maps are deemed exempt—ignores *Rose*’s limiting construction and improperly conflates Exemption 2 with other, more specific exemptions. *See id.* at 361-62 (requiring “clearly delineated statutory language” for exemptions).

B. The Ninth Circuit’s “Predominantly Internal” Standard Is Inconsistent with FOIA’s Purpose and Precedent.

The Ninth Circuit’s holding that ESQD arc maps are “predominantly internal” because they guide Navy safety operations misapplies Exemption 2. *See Milner v. Dep’t of Navy*, 563 F.3d 1111, 1118-20 (9th Cir. 2009). This standard was explicitly rejected by the Supreme Court in *Rose*, which confined Exemption 2 to “trivial matters” of agency housekeeping. *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 363. The Ninth Circuit’s expansion of Exemption 2 to encompass operational safety data—information of clear public concern—undermines FOIA’s “general philosophy of full agency disclosure.” *Id.* at 360-61.

Moreover, the Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), is misplaced. *Hardy* involved a law enforcement manual governing search warrants, a quintessential “internal personnel practice” under Exemption 2. *Id.* at 656. The ESQD arc maps at issue here, by contrast, are not personnel rules but operational safety data with direct public safety implications. The Ninth Circuit’s conflation of these categories is erroneous. *See Maricopa Audubon Soc’y v. U.S. Forest Serv.*, 108 F.3d 1082, 1085 (9th Cir. 1997) (limiting Exemption 2 to “routine matters”).

C. The Navy Has Failed to Demonstrate That Disclosure of ESQD Arc Maps Would Circumvent Agency Regulation.

Exemption 2’s “High 2” variant requires a showing that disclosure “may risk circumvention of agency regulation.” *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 369. The Navy has not met this burden. Commander Whitbred’s affidavit asserts that disclosure could enable “reverse engineering” of ammunition storage details, but this speculative claim does not establish a risk of circumvention. *See id.* (requiring a “realistic” risk, not mere speculation). The Navy’s failure to classify the ESQD arc maps under Exemption 1—designed specifically for national security concerns—further undermines its circumvention argument. *See 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(1)*; *Milner*, 563 F.3d at 1124 (Kozinski, J., dissenting) (noting the Navy’s inconsistent classification practices).

II. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN FAILING TO ADDRESS FOIA EXEMPTION 7(F).

A. ESQD Arc Maps Are Not “Compiled for Law Enforcement Purposes.”

FOIA Exemption 7(F) exempts records “compiled for law enforcement purposes” if disclosure “could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of any individual.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F). The Ninth Circuit did not address this exemption, despite the Navy’s alternative argument. The ESQD arc maps are operational safety documents, not law enforcement materials. *See Church of Scientology v. U.S. Dep’t of Defense*, 611 F.2d 738, 748 (D.C. Cir. 1979) (distinguishing safety regulations from law enforcement purposes). The Navy’s use of these maps to design storage facilities and mitigate risks does not transform them into law enforcement records.

B. Disclosure of ESQD Arc Maps Would Not Endanger Life or Safety.

Even if the maps were “compiled for law enforcement purposes,” the Navy has not shown that disclosure would endanger life or safety. The Navy’s speculative claims of “reverse engineering” risks are insufficient under *Milner*, 563 F.3d at 1124 (Kozinski, J., dissenting). The Supreme Court has cautioned that FOIA exemptions must be narrowly construed, and the Ninth Circuit’s failure to apply Exemption 7(F) properly reflects this principle. *See Rose*, 425 U.S. at 361.

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit’s expansion of Exemption 2 to encompass operational safety data—without a showing of circumvention—departs from Supreme Court precedent and FOIA’s purpose. The judgment below should be reversed.
