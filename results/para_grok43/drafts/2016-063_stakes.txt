**ARGUMENT**

The Civil Service Reform Act channels judicial review of MSPB decisions in “mixed cases” to district court. 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2). A mixed case exists when an employee “has been affected by an action which [she] may appeal to the Merit Systems Protection Board” and “alleges that a basis for the action was discrimination prohibited by” enumerated statutes. 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1). The Board’s dismissal of such a case on jurisdictional grounds does not alter the prescribed forum.

In *Kloeckner v. Solis*, 568 U.S. 41 (2012), the Court held that an MSPB decision disposing of a mixed case on procedural grounds is reviewable in district court. The statute, the Court explained, “does not mention” the ground on which the Board acts; it “refers only, and categorically, to ‘cases of discrimination.’” *Id.* at 50 (quoting 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2)). The same categorical language governs jurisdictional dismissals. Nothing in § 7702(a)(1) or § 7703(b)(2) distinguishes between procedural and jurisdictional rulings. Both turn on whether the employee has alleged an appealable action and discrimination; both therefore require district-court review.

*Powell v. Department of Defense*, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), reached the opposite conclusion for jurisdictional dismissals. That holding cannot survive *Kloeckner*. The Court in *Kloeckner* rejected any “extra-textual” limitation that would send some mixed cases to the Federal Circuit while sending others to district court. 568 U.S. at 52. The statutory definition of a mixed case depends on the employee’s allegations, not on the Board’s eventual determination of its own jurisdiction. *See id.* at 50 (“No one here contests that Kloeckner brought a mixed case—that she was affected by an action … appealable to the MSPB and that she alleged discrimination.”). The same allegations are present here.

The Board’s conclusion that Perry’s retirement and suspension were voluntary does not remove the case from § 7702(a)(1). That conclusion addresses the merits of the voluntariness issue, which overlaps with the discrimination claim. The statute assigns resolution of that overlap to district court once the employee has made the requisite allegations. *Kloeckner* confirms that the forum for review does not turn on how the Board resolves the case.

Accordingly, the petition should be transferred to the district court.
