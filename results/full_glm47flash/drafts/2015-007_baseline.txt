**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE SIXTH AMENDMENT DOES NOT REQUIRE A JURY TO FIND AGGRAVATING FACTORS IN FLORIDA’S GUIDED DISCRETION SCHEME.**

The Supreme Court’s decision in *Ring v. Arizona* did not mandate that every capital sentencing scheme require a jury to find the existence of aggravating factors. *Ring* held that a capital sentencing scheme that makes an aggravating factor an “element” of the offense violates the Sixth Amendment. 536 U.S. 584, 609 (2002). Florida, however, employs a “guided discretion” model in which the trial judge, not the jury, determines the existence of statutory aggravating factors. *Steele v. State*, 921 So. 2d 540, 548 (Fla. 2006). Under this model, the judge acts as the trier of fact regarding the existence of aggravating circumstances, while the jury serves an advisory role in recommending the sentence. *Id.* at 549. Because the judge, not the jury, makes the factual findings required to impose the death penalty, the Sixth Amendment is satisfied.

**II. THE EIGHTH AMENDMENT DOES NOT REQUIRE A UNANIMOUS JURY VERDICT FOR THE DEATH SENTENCE.**

The Eighth Amendment’s prohibition against cruel and unusual punishment does not require a unanimous jury verdict to impose the death penalty. *Steele*, 921 So. 2d at 548. In *Steele*, the Florida Supreme Court explained that the judge, not the jury, determines the existence of aggravating factors. *Id.* at 549. Consequently, the requirement of unanimity applies to the judge’s finding of these factors, not to the jury’s advisory recommendation. *Id.* at 548. The Court has consistently held that the judge’s determination of aggravating factors is the critical fact-finding step in the capital sentencing process. *Coday v. State*, 946 So. 2d 1019, 1024 (Fla. 2006). Therefore, the non-unanimous recommendation of the jury does not violate the Eighth Amendment.

**III. *ATKINS* DOES NOT REQUIRE A JURY TO DETERMINE MENTAL RETARDATION.**

The Supreme Court’s decision in *Atkins v. Virginia* held that the execution of mentally retarded persons violates the Eighth Amendment. 536 U.S. 304, 321 (2002). However, *Atkins* left the determination of the method for enforcing this restriction to the states. *Id.* at 317. Florida has established a statutory scheme to determine whether a defendant is mentally retarded, requiring proof of significantly subaverage general intellectual functioning, concurrent deficits in adaptive behavior, and manifestation before age eighteen. *Nixon v. State*, 2 So. 3d 137, 142 (Fla. 2009). The trial judge, not the jury, applies this statutory test. *Id.* at 145. The jury may consider evidence of mental retardation as mitigation, but the judge makes the final determination of whether the defendant is mentally retarded and exempt from execution. *Id.* at 142. This statutory scheme is fully consistent with the *Atkins* decision.
