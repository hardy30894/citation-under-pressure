**ARGUMENT**

I. **WASEK STATED A CLAIM UNDER TITLE VII BECAUSE HE ALLEGED SEXUAL HARASSMENT THAT WAS SEVERE OR PERVASIVE ENOUGH TO ALTER THE TERMS OF HIS EMPLOYMENT.**

Title VII prohibits discrimination “with respect to [an employee’s] compensation, terms, conditions, or privileges of employment” because of sex. 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(a)(1). To prevail on a hostile work environment claim, a plaintiff must show that the workplace was “permeated with discriminatory intimidation, ridicule, and insult” that was “sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of the victim’s employment.” *Harris v. Forklift Sys., Inc.*, 510 U.S. 17, 21 (1993) (quoting *Meritor Sav. Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, 67 (1986)). The conduct must be “objectively offensive” and “subjectively offensive.” *Id.* at 21–22.

Here, Wasek alleged repeated instances of physical and verbal sexual harassment, including unwanted touching, sexually explicit comments, and derogatory remarks. These allegations, if proven, satisfy the severe-or-pervasive standard. *See, e.g., Baskerville v. Culligan Int’l Co.*, 50 F.3d 428, 430–31 (7th Cir. 1995) (holding that repeated unwanted touching and sexual comments created a hostile work environment). The district court erred in dismissing Wasek’s Title VII claim.

II. **WASEK’S HARASSMENT CLAIM IS NOT BARRED BY THE FACT THAT HE COULD NOT SHOW THE HARASSMENT WAS BASED ON HIS GENDER.**

Title VII’s prohibition on sexual harassment does not require proof that the harasser was motivated by the victim’s gender. *Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc.*, 523 U.S. 75, 80 (1998) (“[T]he prohibition of discrimination on the basis of sex . . . prevents treating individuals differently for characteristics found among both sexes.”). Same-sex harassment is actionable under Title VII if it is “because of . . . sex.” *Id.* at 79–80. Wasek’s allegations—that Ottobre targeted him with sexualized conduct and comments—are sufficient to state a claim. *See, e.g., EEOC v. Boh Bros. Constr. Co.*, 731 F.3d 444, 453–54 (5th Cir. 2013) (en banc) (holding that same-sex harassment can violate Title VII if motivated by sex).

III. **WASEK’S COMPLAINTS TO SUPERVISORS WERE SUFFICIENT TO PUT ARROW ENERGY ON NOTICE OF THE HARASSMENT.**

An employer is liable for a hostile work environment if it “knew or should have known of the harassment and failed to take prompt remedial action.” *Faragher v. City of Boca Raton*, 524 U.S. 775, 777 (1998). Wasek complained to both Tripp and Smillie, who were supervisors. Their failure to act—indeed, their encouragement of further confrontation—demonstrates that Arrow Energy had notice but failed to remedy the situation. *See, e.g., Burlington Indus., Inc. v. Ellerth*, 524 U.S. 742, 765 (1998) (holding that an employer’s failure to act on notice can establish liability).

IV. **WASEK’S CLAIM UNDER THE ELLIOTT-LARSEN CIVIL RIGHTS ACT (“EL-CRA”) WAS WRONGLY DISMISSED.**

The EL-CRA prohibits discrimination “because of . . . sex,” Mich. Comp. Laws § 37.2202(1)(a), and Michigan courts interpret it similarly to Title VII. *See, e.g., DeFazio v. Elkins Constr. Co.*, 273 Mich. App. 682, 693 (2007). Wasek’s allegations of sexual harassment fall within the statute’s protections. The district court erred in dismissing this claim.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court’s judgment should be reversed, and this matter remanded for further proceedings consistent with this brief.
