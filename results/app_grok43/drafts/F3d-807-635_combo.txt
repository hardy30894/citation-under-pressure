ARGUMENT

The district court erred in granting partial summary judgment because the record presents genuine issues of material fact on whether a prerecorded voice was used in four of the seven calls. Summary judgment is proper only when the moving party shows the absence of any genuine dispute as to a material fact. Sartor v. Arkansas Natural Gas Corp., 321 U.S. 620, 627 (1944). All reasonable inferences must be drawn against the movant. United States v. Diebold, Inc., 369 U.S. 654, 655 (1962). The TCPA imposes liability only for a call that actually employs an artificial or prerecorded voice. 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A). Courts must give effect to the plain meaning of statutory language. Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917).

The Cisco Dialer played a prerecorded message solely upon detecting a positive voice. The record shows no such detection for the calls of May 18, June 1, June 15, and June 29. Those entries reflect either an answering machine or a data error, establishing that no message was delivered. A party opposing summary judgment need only present evidence creating a triable issue; it need not prove its case at that stage. Poller v. Columbia Broadcasting System, Inc., 368 U.S. 464, 473 (1962). DISH’s general acknowledgment that the device could play messages does not establish that it did so on every call.

Statutory prohibitions are construed strictly and are not extended beyond their terms by implication. McBoyle v. United States, 283 U.S. 25, 27 (1931). The district court’s conclusion that an admission of capability sufficed to prove actual use on every occasion therefore rests on an erroneous legal premise. When affidavits or records leave the critical fact in doubt, summary judgment must be denied. Associated Press v. United States, 326 U.S. 1, 6 (1945).

The three calls resulting in a positive voice are conceded. The remaining four present disputed questions of fact that preclude judgment as a matter of law. Hickman v. Taylor, 329 U.S. 495, 511 (1947). The judgment should be reversed and summary judgment directed for DISH on those four calls.
