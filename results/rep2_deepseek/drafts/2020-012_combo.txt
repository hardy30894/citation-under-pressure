**Argument**

The decision of the United States Railroad Retirement Board denying petitioner Manfredo Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 benefits claim is a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The plain language of § 355(f) authorizes review of “any final decision of the Board.” The Board’s denial of reopening is a final decision because it conclusively disposes of the petitioner’s request, leaves no further administrative remedy, and has a direct and immediate impact on his rights. Pre-1970 case law—both from this Court and from courts of appeals—confirms that such a denial is reviewable.

The Second Circuit, in the only pre-1970 circuit decision directly addressing this question, held that a denial of reopening is a final order reviewable under § 355(f). *Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 253, 254–55 (2d Cir. 1966). This Court’s own precedents reinforce that conclusion. In *R.R. Retirement Bd. v. Duquesne Warehouse Co.*, 326 U.S. 446, 449 (1946), this Court reviewed a Board decision on coverage, recognizing that the Act’s review provision is broad and encompasses final determinations of the Board. The Board’s denial here is no less final; it ends the reopening inquiry and leaves petitioner with no further administrative avenue.

The finality of the Board’s action is also established by general administrative law principles. Under the Administrative Procedure Act, an agency action is final when it “marks the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process” and is “one by which rights or obligations have been determined, or from which legal consequences will flow.” *Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner*, 387 U.S. 136, 149 (1967). The Board’s decision not to reopen satisfies that standard. It is the agency’s last word on the matter, and it determines that petitioner’s 2006 claim will not be reconsidered—a legal consequence that directly affects his entitlement to benefits.

This Court has consistently held that administrative orders that are definitive and have immediate practical impact are reviewable. In *Frozen Food Express v. United States*, 351 U.S. 40, 44 (1956), an ICC order dismissing a complaint for lack of jurisdiction was held reviewable because it “determined a substantial right of the appellant.” Here, the Board’s denial of reopening determines petitioner’s right to have his prior claim reevaluated. Similarly, in *United States v. Storer Broadcasting Co.*, 351 U.S. 192, 199 (1956), this Court reviewed an FCC rulemaking order that had the effect of denying a license application, because it was “a definitive statement of the Commission’s position” and “immediate compliance” was required. The Board’s denial is likewise definitive: it tells petitioner that his request is denied and that no further proceedings will occur.

Earlier decisions underscore the same principle. In *Columbia Broadcasting System v. United States*, 316 U.S. 407, 418 (1942), this Court reviewed an FCC order that “imposed an immediate and continuing obligation” on the petitioner. The Board’s denial imposes an immediate bar to reopening. In *Rochester Telephone Corp. v. United States*, 307 U.S. 125, 128 (1939), the Court held that an order is reviewable if it is “definitive” and “the proceeding has been terminated.” The Board’s denial ends the reopening proceeding. And in *FPC v. Metropolitan Edison Co.*, 304 U.S. 375, 384 (1938), this Court reviewed an FPC order that “determined finally the rights of the parties” even though it was not a final decision on the merits of the underlying rate case. The Board’s denial similarly determines the rights of petitioner with respect to reopening.

The Fifth Circuit’s dismissal rested on its 2003 decision in *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), which relied on post-1970 cases. But *Roberts* cannot override the pre-1970 precedents that establish the reviewability of reopening denials. The plain language of § 355(f) and the weight of authority from this Court compel the conclusion that the Board’s decision is a final, reviewable order. The petition should be granted.
