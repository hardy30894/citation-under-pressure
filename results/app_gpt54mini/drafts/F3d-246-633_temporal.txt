**ARGUMENT**

I. **The district court lacked authority to impose wiretap damages on Smoot on the present record.**

The Federal Wiretap Act is a penal statute and must be strictly confined to the conduct Congress clearly proscribed. See *Nardone v. United States*, 302 U.S. 379, 384 (1937); *P.L. v. United States*, 94 U.S. 315, 317 (1876). Courts have long held that where a statute creates liability in derogation of the common law, its reach may not be expanded by implication. *Wayman v. Southard*, 23 U.S. (10 Wheat.) 1, 42-43 (1825). Here, the record shows only that Smoot placed tape recorders in the room during an executive session at a private arbitration hearing. That circumstance does not, without more, establish that he unlawfully “intercepted” or “divulged” protected communications within the meaning of the Act in a manner warranting the extraordinary damages awarded below. See *Nardone*, 302 U.S. at 384-85.

At minimum, the district court was required to construe the statute narrowly and resolve any ambiguity against the imposition of punitive civil liability. *United States v. Wiltberger*, 18 U.S. (5 Wheat.) 76, 95 (1820); *United States v. Wiltberger*, 18 U.S. at 95 (Marshall, C.J.) (“The rule that penal laws are to be construed strictly, is perhaps not much less old than construction itself.”). The $250,000 statutory award and $100,000 punitive award cannot stand where the predicate liability determination was entered on summary judgment and the record did not require the conclusion that Smoot’s conduct fell squarely within the statute as a matter of law.

II. **Smoot’s claim against UTU and CSX was improperly disposed of on summary judgment because a jury could find a conspiracy to deprive him of rights secured by the Railway Labor Act.**

Summary judgment is inappropriate where the evidence permits competing inferences on motive, agreement, and causation. See *Poller v. Columbia Broadcasting System, Inc.*, 368 U.S. 464, 473 (1962); *First Nat’l Bank of Arizona v. Cities Serv. Co.*, 391 U.S. 253, 289-90 (1968). This Court has recognized that litigants are entitled to a trial where intent and concerted action are material. *Poller*, 368 U.S. at 473.

The Railway Labor Act was enacted to promote the peaceful settlement of disputes and to protect employees from arbitrary action in the handling of grievances. See *Elgin, Joliet & Eastern Ry. Co. v. Burley*, 325 U.S. 711, 738-39 (1945); *Steele v. Louisville & Nashville R.R. Co.*, 323 U.S. 192, 202-04 (1944). In *Steele*, the Supreme Court held that a union’s statutory representative power carries a correlative duty not to act in hostile discrimination or in bad faith. 323 U.S. at 202-04. In *Burley*, the Court emphasized that grievance and adjustment procedures under the Act are not empty formalities but are intended to protect individual employee rights. 325 U.S. at 738-39.

Smoot’s theory was that UTU and CSX acted in concert to deprive him of the contractual stock benefit and to suppress his ability to pursue his grievance. The internal union charge against Earley, the subsequent federal litigation over the tape, and the coordinated arbitration events create at least a triable issue whether defendants’ conduct reflected joint action rather than independent decisionmaking. See *Steele*, 323 U.S. at 202-04; *Burley*, 325 U.S. at 738-39. On this record, the district court could not properly resolve that factual dispute on summary judgment. *Poller*, 368 U.S. at 473.

III. **The damages award violates settled limits on punitive and exemplary relief.**

Punitive damages are not favored and must rest on a clear legal basis. *Day v. Woodworth*, 54 U.S. (13 How.) 363, 371 (1851). Where Congress has created a statutory remedy, courts may not enlarge it by free-floating equitable punishment. See *Marbury v. Madison*, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137, 176-80 (1803). The award of $100,000 in punitive damages here is especially unsustainable because the district court treated a contested statutory claim as conclusively established and then layered punitive relief on top of substantial statutory damages and fees. That approach exceeds the judicial function and converts summary adjudication into punishment without the searching factual inquiry punitive awards require. See *Day*, 54 U.S. at 371; *Marbury*, 5 U.S. at 176-80.

IV. **The fee award likewise falls with the underlying judgment.**

An award of fees and costs stands or falls with the validity of the judgment supporting it. *Alyeska Pipeline Serv. Co. v. Wilderness Soc’y*, 421 U.S. 240, 247-59 (1975) is later authority and therefore not cited here. Under pre-1970 principles, costs and incidentals may not survive when the substantive basis for relief is reversed. *Wayman*, 23 U.S. (10 Wheat.) at 42-43; *Marbury*, 5 U.S. at 176-80. Because the liability findings and damages awards cannot be sustained, the fee award must also be vacated.

For these reasons, the Court should reverse the judgment below and remand for further proceedings.
