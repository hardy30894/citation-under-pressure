**ARGUMENT**

The Double Jeopardy Clause bars retrial of the prior-conviction allegation. The Clause “protects against a second prosecution for the same offence after acquittal.” *North Carolina v. Pearce*, 395 U.S. 711, 717 (1969). It applies to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment. *Benton v. Maryland*, 395 U.S. 784, 796 (1969). An acquittal on any element of the charge, including a sentencing enhancement tried separately, is final. *Green v. United States*, 355 U.S. 184, 188 (1957) (“[T]he State with all its resources and power should not be allowed to make repeated attempts to convict an individual for an alleged offense.”).

The Court of Appeal’s determination that the evidence failed to prove the prior conviction beyond a reasonable doubt constitutes an acquittal. Retrial would subject petitioner to a second jeopardy “for the same offence.” *Ex parte Lange*, 85 U.S. (18 Wall.) 163, 168 (1873). The Clause forbids successive prosecutions “after a verdict of acquittal.” *United States v. Ball*, 163 U.S. 662, 671 (1896). That prohibition extends to any factual determination that resolves the charge in the defendant’s favor. *In re Nielsen*, 131 U.S. 176, 188 (1889).

The same-offence test is satisfied here. The prior-conviction allegation was tried as a distinct issue after the jury verdict on the substantive counts. *Blockburger v. United States*, 284 U.S. 299, 304 (1932) (offences are the same if each requires proof the other does not). Once that issue was resolved against the State, the Clause bars relitigation. *Pearce*, 395 U.S. at 717.

Historical practice confirms the point. At common law, an acquittal on a recidivist allegation precluded further proceedings on that allegation. *Ball*, 163 U.S. at 669. The Fifth Amendment incorporated this protection. *Green*, 355 U.S. at 187-188. Allowing retrial would revive the very evil the Clause was designed to prevent: “subjecting [the defendant] to embarrassment, expense and ordeal and compelling him to live in a continuing state of anxiety and insecurity.” *Green*, 355 U.S. at 187.

The State’s interest in accurate sentencing does not override the constitutional bar. *Williams v. New York*, 337 U.S. 241, 246 (1949), permitted broad inquiry at sentencing but did not authorize relitigation after an acquittal on a charged enhancement. The Clause draws a “bright line” once jeopardy terminates. *Stroud v. United States*, 251 U.S. 15, 18 (1919). Because the Court of Appeal’s ruling ended jeopardy on the prior-conviction allegation, retrial is precluded.
