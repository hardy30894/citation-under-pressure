**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Clearly Erred by Ignoring the Statutory Mandate of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a)**

The district court’s order transferring the case to itself for trial is clearly erroneous as a matter of law because it ignored the explicit statutory command of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). The statute authorizes the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation to transfer civil actions with common issues of fact "to any district for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings," but it provides that the Panel "shall" remand any such action to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). The "pretrial proceedings" authorized by § 1407 are limited to discovery and coordination, not trial. See *In re General Motors Corp. Pickup Truck Fuel Tank Products Liability Litigation*, 554 F.2d 548, 551 (6th Cir. 1977) (explaining that § 1407 is designed to centralize pretrial proceedings, not to determine the merits of the case). By transferring the case to itself for trial under 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a), the court effectively nullified the Panel’s remand mandate. The district court’s decision is not supported by the text of the statute, which reflects a policy choice to allow plaintiffs to select the forum for trial, not to allow the transferee court to retain the case. See *Lexecon, Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach*, 61 F.3d 911, 918 (9th Cir. 1995) (Kozinski, J., dissenting) (noting that "Congress would have drafted the statute much differently" if it intended to empower the Panel to reassign cases for trial).

**II. The District Court’s Reliance on 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) Is Inconsistent with the Statute’s Text and Purpose**

The district court’s reliance on 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to retain the case is inconsistent with the statute’s text and purpose. While § 1404(a) allows transfer "to any district," it does not authorize a transferee court to retain a case transferred under § 1407 for the purpose of trial. See *In re Air Crash Disaster Near Hanover, New Hampshire*, 342 F.Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H. 1971) (recognizing that "there is nothing in the language of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) or 1404(a) which directly allows... that the transferee judge has the power to transfer cases to his district... for purposes of trial"). The Panel’s authority is carefully limited to accomplish the statute’s purpose, which is to facilitate pretrial coordination, not to reassign cases for resolution of dispositive matters. 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Allowing a district judge to use § 1404(a) to subvert the Panel’s remand power undermines the statutory scheme and the plaintiff’s right to select the forum for trial. The lower court’s decision to ignore this statutory command is an oft-repeated error that requires correction. See *Lexecon*, 61 F.3d at 918 (Kozinski, J., dissenting) (describing the practice as "illicit" and noting that "the time has come to put a stop to this illicit practice").
