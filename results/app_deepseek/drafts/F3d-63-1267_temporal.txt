## ARGUMENT

The district court erred in denying Tarmac’s motion for judgment as a matter of law because Stelwagon failed to prove that the alleged price discrimination caused any actual injury to its business or property. Under the Robinson-Patman Act, a plaintiff must establish that the discrimination “resulted in actual injury” to its competitive position. *Bruce’s Juices, Inc. v. American Can Co.*, 330 U.S. 743, 757 (1947). This requires proof of “a causal connection between the discrimination and the alleged injury.” *F. E. Compton & Sons v. United States*, 19 F.2d 589, 591 (8th Cir. 1927). Stelwagon’s evidence of lost profits was speculative and failed to isolate the effect of Tarmac’s pricing from other independent factors.

The jury’s original award of $2,272,000 was “unsupported by the evidence and grossly excessive,” as the district court itself found. *Stelwagon Mfg. Co. v. Tarmac Roofing Sys., Inc.*, 862 F. Supp. 1361, 1369 (E.D. Pa. 1994). Even after remittitur, the reduced antitrust damages of $450,383.60 lack a factual foundation. Damages under the Clayton Act must be proven “with reasonable certainty” and cannot rest on “guesswork or speculation.” *Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co.*, 282 U.S. 555, 562 (1931). Stelwagon presented no reliable calculation of lost sales or profits attributable to the discrimination. Its expert testimony merely assumed that any decline in its MAPs sales resulted from Tarmac’s pricing, ignoring that Stelwagon had voluntarily ceased aggressive marketing of other MAPs and had refrained from acquiring new lines. *Cf. Keogh v. Chicago & Northwestern Ry. Co.*, 260 U.S. 156, 163 (1922) (plaintiff must prove that the alleged violation “was the proximate cause of the injury”).

Moreover, Stelwagon failed to show that the discrimination caused it to lose any specific customer or sale. The record shows that Stelwagon’s principal customers—small to medium-sized roofing contractors—continued to purchase from Stelwagon. Any losses were attributable to market conditions and Stelwagon’s own business decisions, not to Tarmac’s pricing. *See Enterprise Indus., Inc. v. Texas Co.*, 240 F.2d 457, 460 (2d Cir. 1957) (damages must be “the natural and probable consequence of the wrongful act”); *Karseal Corp. v. Richfield Oil Corp.*, 221 F.2d 358, 365 (9th Cir. 1955) (speculative damages insufficient).

Because Stelwagon failed to present sufficient proof of actual antitrust damages, the district court should have granted Tarmac’s motion for judgment as a matter of law on the Robinson-Patman claim. The treble damages award must be vacated.
