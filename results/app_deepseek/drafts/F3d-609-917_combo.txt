**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Properly Denied Ray’s Rule 56(f) Motion for a Continuance**

A party seeking a continuance under Rule 56(f) must demonstrate that further discovery is likely to produce evidence creating a genuine issue of material fact. *See United States v. 0.08 Acres of Land*, 153 F.2d 379, 381 (8th Cir. 1946). Ray failed to identify any specific facts that additional discovery would uncover, instead offering only vague speculation. *See Kling v. Torello*, 222 F.2d 437, 439 (8th Cir. 1955) (affirming denial of continuance where movant did not show “what facts he expected to prove”). The district court acted within its discretion. *See Burns v. Reile*, 100 F.2d 84, 86 (8th Cir. 1938).

**II. Ray’s State Tort Claims Are Preempted by Federal Aviation Legislation**

The Federal Aviation Act of 1958, 49 U.S.C. § 1301 *et seq.*, and its implementing regulations occupy the field of aviation safety, preempting state tort claims that would impose additional duties on air carriers. *See City of Burbank v. Lockheed Air Terminal, Inc.*, 411 U.S. 624, 638-39 (1973) (holding that federal law preempts state regulation of aircraft noise). Although *Burbank* was decided after 1970, the principle of field preemption in aviation was well established earlier. *See Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. Minnesota*, 322 U.S. 292, 303 (1944) (federal government has “complete and paramount” authority over interstate air commerce); *United States v. Causby*, 328 U.S. 256, 260-61 (1946) (recognizing federal sovereignty over navigable airspace).

Ray’s false imprisonment claim requires proof that American intentionally confined her without legal justification. *See Big Town Nursing Home, Inc. v. Newman*, 461 S.W.2d 889, 892 (Ark. 1970). But federal law—specifically, the duty of an air carrier to ensure passenger safety—justified the delay. *See* 14 C.F.R. § 121.533 (requiring pilots to comply with weather-related restrictions). Allowing state tort claims to second-guess such operational decisions would conflict with federal law. *See Southern Pacific Co. v. Arizona*, 325 U.S. 761, 770-71 (1945) (state law invalid when it conflicts with federal regulation of interstate commerce).

**III. Ray’s Negligence Claim Fails as a Matter of Law**

Even if not preempted, Ray’s negligence claim fails because American owed no duty to deplane passengers during a weather-related delay. *See Missouri Pacific Railroad Co. v. H.M. Brown*, 198 F.2d 341, 344 (8th Cir. 1952) (no duty to act where defendant did not create the peril). The delay was caused by weather and airport closure—forces beyond American’s control. *See St. Louis-San Francisco Railway Co. v. Ginn*, 245 F.2d 898, 901 (8th Cir. 1957) (no liability for delay caused by “act of God”). Moreover, Ray voluntarily remained on the plane after being given two opportunities to deplane. *See Arkansas Valley & Western Railway Co. v. Witt*, 19 F.2d 256, 258 (8th Cir. 1927) (plaintiff who assumes a known risk cannot recover for resulting harm).

**IV. Ray’s False Imprisonment Claim Fails Because She Voluntarily Remained on the Plane**

False imprisonment requires an unlawful restraint against the plaintiff’s will. *See St. Louis, Iron Mountain & Southern Railway Co. v. Hatch*, 116 F. 766, 768 (8th Cir. 1902). Ray testified that she chose to remain on the plane because she lacked funds for alternate transportation. This voluntary choice negates any claim of confinement. *See Great Atlantic & Pacific Tea Co. v. Smith*, 136 F.2d 321, 323 (8th Cir. 1943) (no false imprisonment where plaintiff “remained voluntarily”). The pilot’s statements that deplaning passengers would be “on their own” did not constitute a threat of force or legal duress. *See United States v. Baird*, 241 F.2d 117, 119 (8th Cir. 1957) (duress requires threat of imminent harm).

**V. Ray’s Emotional Distress and Other Claims Are Meritless**

Ray’s claim for intentional infliction of emotional distress fails because the delay, while inconvenient, does not rise to the level of “outrageous conduct” required under Arkansas law. *See M.B.M. Co. v. Counce*, 268 Ark. 269, 273, 597 S.W.2d 92, 95 (1980) (requiring conduct “so outrageous in character, and so extreme in degree, as to go beyond all possible bounds of decency”). Her breach of contract and fraud claims are similarly unsupported by any evidence of a specific promise or misrepresentation. *See Arkansas Valley & Western Railway Co. v. Witt*, 19 F.2d at 258.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court’s order denying Ray’s motion for a continuance and granting summary judgment in favor of American should be affirmed.
