I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on Burnett’s FMLA Claim.

The FMLA entitles eligible employees to leave for a serious health condition that makes the employee unable to perform the functions of the position. 29 U.S.C. § 2612(a)(1)(D). An employee must furnish notice sufficient to make the employer aware that the employee needs FMLA-qualifying leave and the anticipated timing and duration of the leave. Collins v. NTN-Bower Corp., 272 F.3d 1006, 1008 (7th Cir. 2001). The notice need not expressly invoke the statute or use any magic words. Byrne v. Avon Prods., Inc., 328 F.3d 379, 382 (7th Cir. 2003).

Burnett’s communications met this standard. In October 2003 he told Polo of his “weak bladder” and impending medical evaluation. In December he presented a physician’s order for blood testing, disclosed elevated PSA and cholesterol levels, and identified upcoming urologist appointments. On December 16 he stated he had been “sick,” felt he was “getting sick or was sick,” and likened his condition to his brother-in-law’s prostate cancer. On January 7, 2004, he supplied a document describing prostate ultrasound and biopsy procedures indicated by “an elevation of prostate cancer screening blood test (PSA).” Polo thereafter scheduled leave for the January appointments. These disclosures apprised Habitat of both the need for leave and its medical basis. See Price v. City of Fort Wayne, 117 F.3d 1022, 1026 (7th Cir. 1997) (oral notice of medical condition and treatment sufficient).

II. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on Burnett’s ADA Claim.

The ADA requires an employer to provide reasonable accommodation to a qualified individual with a disability. 42 U.S.C. § 12112(b)(5)(A). This duty is triggered when the employee provides notice of both the disability and the desire for accommodation. EEOC v. Sears, Roebuck & Co., 417 F.3d 789, 797 (7th Cir. 2005). Once on notice, the employer must engage in an interactive process. Beck v. Univ. of Wis. Bd. of Regents, 75 F.3d 1130, 1135 (7th Cir. 1996).

Burnett’s repeated statements concerning his bladder condition, PSA elevation, biopsy, and need for medical appointments and a shift change placed Habitat on notice of a potential disability. Habitat’s issuance of reprimands and denial of the requested transfer without further inquiry or accommodation violated the interactive-process obligation. See Sears, 417 F.3d at 797.

The record therefore presents genuine issues of material fact precluding summary judgment on both claims.
