ARGUMENT

I. The Bankruptcy Court Lacked Jurisdiction to Determine Post-Petition Interest and Collection Costs

The bankruptcy court's determination of post-petition interest and collection costs owed to ECMC was improper because the court lacked jurisdiction to make such a determination. Under 28 U.S.C. § 1334, a bankruptcy court's jurisdiction is limited to matters that are "related to" the bankruptcy case. See Celotex Corp. v. Edwards, 514 U.S. 300, 308 (1995) (holding that "related to" jurisdiction under § 1334(b) is not limitless). After a Chapter 13 case is closed and the debtor is discharged, the bankruptcy court's jurisdiction is significantly curtailed. See In re Querner, 7 F.3d 1199, 1201 (5th Cir. 1993) (holding that a bankruptcy court's jurisdiction is "limited to matters concerning the administration of the estate" after the estate is closed).

In this case, Kirkland's bankruptcy estate was closed in February 2006, and she was discharged. At that point, the bankruptcy court's jurisdiction was limited to matters that were "related to" the bankruptcy case, and the determination of post-petition interest and collection costs on a nondischargeable student loan is not such a matter. See In re Geruschat, 381 B.R. 574, 579 (Bankr. M.D. Fla. 2007) (holding that a bankruptcy court lacks jurisdiction to determine the amount of a nondischargeable student loan after the bankruptcy case is closed).

The bankruptcy court's reliance on 28 U.S.C. §§ 1334(a) and 157(a) to establish jurisdiction over the matter is misplaced. Section 1334(a) grants jurisdiction over "cases under title 11," and § 157(a) allows district courts to refer such cases to bankruptcy courts. However, Kirkland's bankruptcy case was already closed, and the matter at hand was not a "case under title 11." See In re Craig's Stores of Texas, Inc., 240 F.3d 402, 404 (5th Cir. 2001) (holding that a bankruptcy court's jurisdiction under § 1334(a) is limited to "cases under title 11").

Furthermore, the proceeding was not a "core proceeding" under § 157(b)(2)(A) as the bankruptcy court claimed. The determination of post-petition interest and collection costs on a nondischargeable student loan is not a matter that "concerns the administration of the estate." See In re Wolverine Radio Co., 930 F.2d 1132, 1143-44 (6th Cir. 1991) (holding that a proceeding is not "core" if it does not involve the administration of the estate).

Because the bankruptcy court lacked jurisdiction to determine post-petition interest and collection costs, the district court erred in affirming the bankruptcy court's judgment on this issue.

II. The Bankruptcy Court's Determination of Post-Petition Interest and Collection Costs Was Improper

Even if the bankruptcy court had jurisdiction to determine post-petition interest and collection costs, its determination was improper because it was based on an incomplete record and a lack of factual findings. ECMC was entitled to present evidence on the amount of post-petition interest and collection costs owed, but the bankruptcy court made its determination without allowing ECMC to fully litigate the issue.

The bankruptcy court's statement that ECMC "has not provided any statutory or factual basis for the accrual of such costs" is not a sufficient basis for denying ECMC's claim for collection costs. J.A. 87. ECMC was not given the opportunity to present evidence on this issue, and the court's determination was therefore improper.

For the foregoing reasons, the district court's judgment affirming the bankruptcy court's determination of post-petition interest and collection costs should be reversed.
