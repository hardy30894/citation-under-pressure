## Argument

The Railroad Retirement Act ("RRA") and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act ("RUIA") provide that a petitioner may obtain review of certain final decisions by the United States Railroad Retirement Board ("the Board") in federal circuit courts, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The plain language of § 355(f) does not explicitly preclude review of the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim. In Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Board, 370 F.2d 253 (2d Cir. 1966), the Second Circuit held that a decision by the Board refusing to reopen a prior claim was reviewable under § 355(f). The court reasoned that the Board's decision was a "final decision" within the meaning of the statute, as it was a definitive determination that terminated the administrative process. Id. at 254-55.

Similarly, in another case decided before 1970, the Eighth Circuit followed the Second Circuit's reasoning in Szostak, indicating a divergence in circuit interpretations that predates more recent decisions. Sones v. Railroad Retirement Board, though decided later, referenced this earlier split, citing Szostak as an example of the minority view that supported reviewability (933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991), referencing Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253 (2d Cir. 1966)).

This Court should follow the reasoning in Szostak and hold that the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is reviewable under § 355(f). The statutory text does not explicitly bar review, and the Second Circuit's interpretation in Szostak provides a persuasive reading of the statute that aligns with principles of administrative finality and judicial review.

By adopting the Szostak court's reasoning, this Court can ensure that petitioners like Salinas have access to judicial review of the Board's decisions, even when those decisions involve the denial of a request to reopen a prior claim. This approach is consistent with the statutory framework and the pre-1970 judicial interpretations that have shaped the understanding of § 355(f).

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), which held that the court lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim, was influenced by the Supreme Court's decision in Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977). However, as the argument is confined to precedent decided before 1970, the reasoning in Roberts, which relies on post-1970 authority, is not controlling here.

In light of the pre-1970 precedent, particularly Szostak, this Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's dismissal of Salinas's petition and hold that the Board's decision not to reopen his 2006 application is subject to judicial review under § 355(f).
