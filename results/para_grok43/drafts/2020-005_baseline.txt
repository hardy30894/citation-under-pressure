**ARGUMENT**

The Due Process Clause permits the exercise of specific jurisdiction over Ford because the plaintiffs’ claims “arise out of or relate to” Ford’s purposeful contacts with Montana. *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 319 (1945). Specific jurisdiction requires that the defendant have “minimum contacts” with the forum such that the suit “does not offend ‘traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.’” *Id.* at 316 (quoting *Milliken v. Meyer*, 311 U.S. 457, 463 (1940)). Those contacts must have a “substantial connection” with the forum. *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, 471 U.S. 462, 475 (1985).

Ford has continuously and deliberately exploited the Montana market for decades by advertising, selling, and servicing thousands of vehicles there. These activities constitute purposeful availment. *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980). The claims at issue—alleging design and manufacturing defects in a Ford Explorer—relate directly to that stream of commerce. The vehicle model was among those Ford marketed and sold in Montana; the alleged defects concern the same product line. This relationship satisfies the “arise out of or relate to” requirement. *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255, 262 (2017).

Ford’s insistence on strict causation is foreclosed by precedent. The Court has never required that the plaintiff’s claim be proximately caused by the defendant’s forum contacts; it has required only a sufficient affiliation. *Daimler AG v. Bauman*, 571 U.S. 117, 127 (2014). Requiring causation would permit a defendant to insulate itself from jurisdiction in any state where its products cause injury simply by routing the first sale elsewhere—an approach inconsistent with the “relate to” formulation that has long coexisted with the “arise out of” test. *Helicopteros Nacionales de Colombia, S.A. v. Hall*, 466 U.S. 408, 414 n.8 (1984).

Fairness considerations confirm jurisdiction. Montana has a strong interest in providing a forum for its residents injured by defective products sold and serviced within its borders. *Asahi Metal Indus. Co. v. Superior Court*, 480 U.S. 102, 113 (1987). Ford, having reaped substantial revenue from Montana sales of the very product line at issue, cannot reasonably object to answering claims concerning that product in Montana courts. The judgment below should be affirmed.
