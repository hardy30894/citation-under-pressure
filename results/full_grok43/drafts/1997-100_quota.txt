**ARGUMENT**

The Double Jeopardy Clause bars retrial of a prior-conviction allegation once a reviewing court has found the evidence insufficient to sustain the enhancement. The Clause “protects against a second prosecution for the same offense after acquittal.” *North Carolina v. Pearce*, 395 U.S. 711, 717 (1969). It applies to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment. *Benton v. Maryland*, 395 U.S. 784, 796 (1969).

A proceeding to determine the truth of a prior-conviction allegation under California’s Three Strikes law carries the “hallmarks of the trial on guilt or innocence.” *Bullington v. Missouri*, 451 U.S. 430, 439 (1981). The prosecution must prove the allegation “beyond a reasonable doubt,” *In re Yurko*, 10 Cal. 3d 857, 862 (1974), the rules of evidence govern, *People v. Reed*, 13 Cal. 4th 217, 224 (1996), and the defendant enjoys the rights to confrontation and cross-examination, *Specht v. Patterson*, 386 U.S. 605, 610 (1967). These protections distinguish the proceeding from ordinary sentencing and render it “comparable to a trial on guilt or innocence.” *Arizona v. Rumsey*, 467 U.S. 203, 209 (1984).

When the evidence is found insufficient, the Double Jeopardy Clause therefore precludes retrial. *Burks v. United States*, 437 U.S. 1, 11 (1978). The Clause’s prohibition is not limited to capital cases; its text and purpose extend to any proceeding that “resemble[s] a trial for purposes of the Double Jeopardy Clause.” *Rumsey*, 467 U.S. at 209-10. *Caspari v. Bohlen*, 510 U.S. 383 (1994), and *Pennsylvania v. Goldhammer*, 474 U.S. 28 (1985) (per curiam), contain no holding to the contrary.

The State’s reliance on *Williams v. New York*, 337 U.S. 241 (1949), is misplaced. *Williams* addressed informal sentencing procedures lacking the statutory requirement of proof beyond a reasonable doubt. *United States v. Watts*, 519 U.S. 148, 156 (1997), and *Witte v. United States*, 515 U.S. 389, 398 (1995), reaffirmed *Williams* only in that limited context. Once a legislature elects to impose trial-like procedures, the constitutional protections that accompany those procedures—including double-jeopardy immunity—attach. *Bullington*, 451 U.S. at 446.

Because the Court of Appeal held the evidence insufficient to prove the prior-conviction allegation, the Double Jeopardy Clause bars retrial. The judgment of the California Supreme Court should be reversed.
