ARGUMENT

The Qualifications Clauses of the Constitution fix the exclusive criteria for membership in Congress. U.S. Const. art. I, § 2, cl. 2; art. I, § 3, cl. 3. Those criteria may not be altered by the States. Powell v. McCormack, 395 U.S. 486, 522 (1969) (qualifications “are fixed in the Constitution and are exclusive”).

This Court has long recognized that congressional elections are national in character and demand uniform rules. Ex parte Yarbrough, 110 U.S. 651, 662 (1884) (“the right to vote for members of Congress” is “a right secured by the Constitution”); United States v. Classic, 313 U.S. 299, 315 (1941) (same). State-imposed term limits, whether framed as disqualifications or ballot restrictions, add an age, citizenship, or residency requirement not found in the text. Wesberry v. Sanders, 376 U.S. 1, 7-8 (1964) (each State’s power over elections “is subject to the limitation that it may not be exercised in a way that violates other express provisions of the Constitution”).

The principle of uniformity precludes patchwork state qualifications. The Slaughter-House Cases, 83 U.S. (16 Wall.) 36, 79 (1873) (privileges of national citizenship “depend upon the Constitution” and are not subject to state variation); Minor v. Happersett, 88 U.S. (21 Wall.) 162, 170 (1875) (state power over suffrage “is limited by the Constitution”). Allowing Arkansas to disqualify long-serving members would invite “a hodge-podge of qualifications” inconsistent with the Framers’ design. See also Smith v. Allwright, 321 U.S. 649, 657 (1944) (state regulation of federal elections must conform to federal constitutional standards).

Ballot-access measures that achieve the same end are equally invalid. Colegrove v. Green, 328 U.S. 549, 554 (1946) (state election laws cannot “contravene the Constitution”). The Arkansas amendment’s prohibition on placing names on the ballot for members who have served the specified terms operates as an additional qualification. Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186, 209 (1962) (judicial enforcement of constitutional limits on state election practices is required when federal rights are at stake).

Because Amendment 73 adds qualifications the Constitution does not permit, it is void.
