The Freedom of Information Act requires disclosure of agency records unless the material falls squarely within a statutory exemption. 5 U.S.C. § 552. Exemption 2 covers only matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” Id. § 552(b)(2). This language receives its ordinary meaning. Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917) (“Where the language is plain and admits of no more than one meaning the duty of interpretation does not arise.”).

The withheld ESQD materials describe blast effects, storage layouts, and safety distances for ordnance. They bear no relation to rules governing agency personnel. The statute supplies no warrant for expanding the exemption to any document that is “predominantly” internal or whose release might aid circumvention of agency policy. United States v. Hartwell, 73 U.S. (6 Wall.) 385, 396 (1868) (courts enforce the letter of the law as written).

Because the documents fall outside the exemption’s plain terms, the judgment of the court of appeals must be reversed.
