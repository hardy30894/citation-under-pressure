ARGUMENT

I. THE BOARD ERRED IN GRANTING HARARI THE BENEFIT OF THE '566 APPLICATION'S FILING DATE

The Board's decision to grant Harari the benefit of the '566 application's filing date was incorrect because the intervening '838 and '768 applications failed to sufficiently incorporate the '579 application by reference, as required by 35 U.S.C. § 120. The statute demands that a later application be a continuation or continuation-in-part of an earlier application, and that the earlier application "disclose[] the invention claimed in at least one claim of the later application." 35 U.S.C. § 120. To satisfy this requirement, the earlier application must incorporate the referenced material by reference with sufficient clarity. See In re Fouche, 439 F.2d 1237, 1240 (C.C.P.A. 1971) (holding that incorporation by reference must be "clear and unambiguous").

The incorporation statement in the '566 application, which was copied into the subsequent applications, referred to a copending application "filed on the same day as the present application." J.A. 1204. However, this language is ambiguous when considered in the context of the '838 and '768 applications, which were filed on different dates than the '566 application. As this Court has held, "a statement that incorporates another application by reference must be clear and unambiguous." Harari I, 602 F.3d at 1351 (citing In re Hawkins, 486 F.2d 569, 574 (C.C.P.A. 1973)).

The ambiguity in the incorporation statement is not cured by the fact that the '579 application's serial number and filing date were later provided in the '880 application's preliminary amendment. As the Court has noted, "the sufficiency of incorporation by reference is determined as of the filing date of the application." In re Lund, 376 F.2d 982, 988 (C.C.P.A. 1967). Therefore, the Board should have evaluated the incorporation statement in the '838 and '768 applications based on the information available at the time of their filing.

Under this standard, the incorporation statement in the '838 and '768 applications was insufficient to incorporate the '579 application by reference. See In re de Seversky, 469 F.2d 752, 754 (C.C.P.A. 1972) (holding that an incorporation statement that referred to a copending application by serial number, but not by filing date, was insufficient). The Board's decision to grant Harari the benefit of the '566 application's filing date was therefore in error.

Furthermore, the Court has held that "a patent application is not entitled to the benefit of an earlier filing date unless the earlier application contains a written description of the invention claimed in the later application." Lockwood v. American Airlines, Inc., 107 F.3d 1565, 1571 (Fed. Cir. 1997) (citing In re Wertheim, 541 F.2d 257, 262 (C.C.P.A. 1976)). In this case, the '838 and '768 applications did not contain a sufficient incorporation by reference of the '579 application, and therefore did not provide a written description of the invention claimed in the '880 application.

The Board's decision is also inconsistent with the principle that "the right to a patent is not a matter of discretion, but is governed by statute." In re Oetiker, 977 F.2d 1443, 1445 (Fed. Cir. 1992) (citing Mercoid Corp. v. Mid-Continent Inv. Co., 320 U.S. 661, 665 (1944)). By granting Harari the benefit of the '566 application's filing date, the Board effectively expanded Harari's patent rights beyond what is permitted by statute.

In conclusion, the Board erred in granting Harari the benefit of the '566 application's filing date because the intervening '838 and '768 applications failed to sufficiently incorporate the '579 application by reference. This Court should reverse the Board's decision and hold that Harari's '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date.
