## Argument

The Board’s dismissal of Perry’s mixed case for lack of jurisdiction is reviewable in district court, not the Federal Circuit. The governing statute sends “cases of discrimination” to district court, while reserving Federal Circuit review for ordinary MSPB appeals. 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(1)(A), (b)(2). In **Kloeckner v. Solis**, the Supreme Court held that when the MSPB dismisses a mixed case on a non-merits ground, “judicial review of such dismissals belongs in district court, not in the Federal Circuit.” 568 U.S. 41, 50, 133 S. Ct. 596, 603 (2012). That rule controls here.

A case is a “mixed case” when an employee (1) “has been affected by an action which [she] may appeal to the Merit Systems Protection Board” and (2) alleges discrimination prohibited by federal law. 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1); **Kloeckner v. Solis**, 568 U.S. 41, 44, 133 S. Ct. 596, 600 (2012). Perry’s case fits that definition. He alleged discrimination based on race, age, and disability, and he challenged a removal/suspension/retirement package that he contends was involuntary. The MSPB’s dismissal on jurisdictional grounds did not erase the case’s mixed-case character; it simply resolved the threshold issue against him.

The statute confirms that result. Section 7703(b)(2) directs that “cases of discrimination” be filed under the district-court review provisions of Title VII, the ADEA, and the Rehabilitation Act. 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2). In **Kloeckner**, the Court rejected the government’s attempt to carve out a separate forum rule for MSPB dismissals on threshold grounds. The Court explained that the statute “says that all appeals go to district court” when the case is one of discrimination, regardless of whether the MSPB “dismisses the case on procedural grounds or decides the merits.” 568 U.S. at 44, 57, 133 S. Ct. at 600, 607.

The D.C. Circuit relied on **Powell v. Department of Defense**, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), but **Kloeckner** abrogated the rule on which Powell depended. Powell treated jurisdictional dismissals differently from other mixed-case dismissals and sent them to the Federal Circuit. 158 F.3d at 599-600. But **Kloeckner** rejected a forum rule that turns on the MSPB’s threshold disposition rather than on the employee’s claim. 568 U.S. at 49-50, 133 S. Ct. at 603. The Supreme Court made clear that the phrase “case of discrimination” does not mean only a case in which the MSPB reaches the merits; it includes a case in which the employee alleges discrimination and the Board disposes of the case without merits review. **Kloeckner v. Solis**, 568 U.S. 41, 50-53, 133 S. Ct. 596, 603-05 (2012).

That rule is reinforced by **Perry v. Merit Systems Protection Board**, 137 S. Ct. 1975 (2017), which addressed this very case. The Court held that “when the MSPB dismisses a mixed case on jurisdictional grounds, the appropriate forum for judicial review is the federal district court, not the Federal Circuit.” **Perry v. Merit Sys. Prot. Bd.**, 137 S. Ct. 1975, 1979 (2017). The Court reasoned that jurisdictional dismissals do not remove a case from § 7702(a)(1)’s mixed-case framework simply because the Board concludes it lacks authority to proceed. Id. at 1978-80. That is dispositive here.

The government may argue that a jurisdictional dismissal means the case was never “appealable to the MSPB” within § 7702(a)(1)(A). But **Perry** squarely rejected that contention, holding that a mixed case may be one in which the employee asserts that the challenged action was appealable, even if the Board ultimately decides otherwise. 137 S. Ct. at 1979-80. The statute asks whether the employee “has been affected by an action which [she] may appeal” and alleges discrimination; it does not ask whether the Board later agrees with the employee’s jurisdictional theory. 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1); **Perry v. Merit Sys. Prot. Bd.**, 137 S. Ct. 1975, 1979-80 (2017).

Because Perry’s appeal is a mixed case, review belongs in district court under 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2). The Federal Circuit’s contrary rule would revive the very forum distinction **Kloeckner** and **Perry** rejected. See **Kloeckner v. Solis**, 568 U.S. 41, 50-57, 133 S. Ct. 596, 603-07 (2012); **Perry v. Merit Sys. Prot. Bd.**, 137 S. Ct. 1975, 1979-80 (2017).

For those reasons, the Court should hold that judicial review of the MSPB’s jurisdictional dismissal lies in district court and reverse the transfer to the Federal Circuit.
