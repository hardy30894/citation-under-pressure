## ARGUMENT

The district court erred in granting partial summary judgment to Ybarra on the seven calls from DISH's 8047 number. The Telephone Consumer Protection Act ("TCPA") prohibits making any call using an artificial or prerecorded voice without the prior express consent of the called party, unless an exception applies. 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A). However, the TCPA does not prohibit the use of a prerecorded voice per se; rather, it prohibits the use of a prerecorded voice without prior express consent.

The district court's reasoning that DISH's admission to using a prerecorded voice in calls from the 8047 number was sufficient to establish liability under the TCPA is flawed. As the Supreme Court held in _United States v. Petrillo_, 332 U.S. 1, 5-6 (1947), a statute should be interpreted in a way that gives effect to all its provisions. Here, the TCPA requires that the prerecorded voice be used without prior express consent. The district court failed to consider whether Ybarra had triggered the prerecorded voice with a "positive voice," which is a crucial factor in determining liability.

Furthermore, the district court's interpretation of the TCPA would lead to an absurd result, as it would impose liability on DISH even if the prerecorded voice was not actually played to Ybarra. As the Court held in _Church of the Holy Trinity v. United States_, 143 U.S. 457, 460 (1892), a statute should not be interpreted in a way that leads to an absurd or unjust result. In this case, the district court's interpretation would impose liability on DISH for calls that may not have actually resulted in the playing of a prerecorded voice to Ybarra.

Additionally, the district court erred in granting summary judgment on four of the seven calls. The record shows that calls 2, 3, and 4 were answered by an answering machine, and call 5 resulted in a "Data Error-No Value." As the Court held in _Caminetti v. United States_, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917), a court must consider all the evidence in the record when making a determination on summary judgment. In this case, the district court failed to consider the evidence regarding the outcome of each call, which raises a genuine issue of material fact as to whether the prerecorded voice was actually played to Ybarra.

DISH is entitled to summary judgment on these four calls because there is no evidence that the prerecorded voice was played to Ybarra. The district court's grant of partial summary judgment to Ybarra should be reversed, and summary judgment should be granted to DISH on calls 2, 3, 4, and 5. As to the remaining three calls, DISH has conceded liability, and the judgment should be affirmed as to those calls.
