**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING A PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION BECAUSE SHAPIRO FAILED TO DEMONSTRATE A LIKELIHOOD OF SUCCESS ON THE MERITS UNDER THE FAIR HOUSING AMENDMENTS ACT OF 1988.

The Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988 (“FHAA”), 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B), prohibits discrimination against disabled persons in the “terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental” of housing. To establish a prima facie case under the FHAA, a plaintiff must show: (1) she is disabled; (2) she is otherwise qualified to receive the benefit; (3) she was denied the benefit of the housing accommodation; and (4) the benefit was denied because of her disability. *United States v. California Mobile Home Park Mgmt. Co.*, 107 F.3d 1374, 1378 (9th Cir. 1997) (citing *Housing Opportunities Made Equal, Inc. v. Cincinnati Enquirer, Inc.*, 94 F.3d 113, 117 (6th Cir. 1996)).

Here, Shapiro concedes that she was not denied a parking space outright; rather, she was placed on a waiting list like all other residents. Appellants’ App. at 120. The FHAA does not require housing providers to grant disabled residents priority access to parking spaces. *See United States v. Mid-American Apartments, Ltd.*, 905 F. Supp. 873, 880 (D. Kan. 1995) (“The FHAA does not require that disabled persons be given priority over others in the allocation of parking spaces.”). The district court’s order thus misapplied the statute by requiring an accommodation that goes beyond the FHAA’s mandate.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT IMPROPERLY DISPENSED WITH THE REQUIREMENT THAT SHAPIRO DEMONSTRATE A REASONABLE ACCOMMODATION.

The FHAA requires a plaintiff to show that the requested accommodation is “reasonable.” 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(A). A reasonable accommodation is one that is “necessary to afford [the disabled person] an equal opportunity to use and enjoy” the housing. *United States v. Overlook Gardens Apartments*, 853 F. Supp. 127, 130 (D.N.J. 1994) (quoting H.R. Rep. No. 100-711, at 20 (1988), reprinted in 1988 U.S.C.C.A.N. 2173, 2183). The Supreme Court has held that a requested accommodation is unreasonable if it imposes “undue financial and administrative burdens” or requires a fundamental alteration in the nature of the provider’s operations. *Traynor v. Turnage*, 485 U.S. 535, 548-49 (1988) (citing *Alexander v. Choate*, 469 U.S. 287, 300 (1985)).

Cadman Towers’ parking policy is neutral on its face and applies equally to all residents. The cooperative’s first-come, first-served policy does not discriminate against Shapiro because of her disability. *See United States v. Hunter Vill. Apts., Inc.*, 924 F. Supp. 665, 670 (E.D. Va. 1996) (“A policy that is neutral on its face and applied uniformly does not violate the FHAA unless it is shown that the policy was adopted for a discriminatory purpose or has a discriminatory effect.”). Shapiro has not demonstrated that the policy imposes an undue burden or that a ground-floor space is necessary to afford her an equal opportunity to use and enjoy her apartment. *See Overlook Gardens Apartments*, 853 F. Supp. at 130.

III. THE DISTRICT COURT ABUSED ITS DISCRETION BY ISSUING A PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION WITHOUT CONSIDERING THE BALANCE OF HARMS.

A preliminary injunction is an extraordinary remedy that should not be granted unless the moving party demonstrates: (1) irreparable harm; (2) a likelihood of success on the merits; (3) a balance of equities in her favor; and (4) that the injunction is in the public interest. *Jackson Dairy, Inc. v. H.P. Hood & Sons, Inc.*, 596 F.2d 70, 72 (2d Cir. 1979). The district court erred by failing to weigh the equities properly.

Cadman Towers’ parking garage is a limited common element of the cooperative, and the allocation of spaces is governed by the cooperative’s bylaws. The district court’s order interferes with the cooperative’s internal governance and imposes a significant financial burden by requiring the assignment of a specific space. *See United States v. Yonkers Bd. of Educ.*, 893 F.2d 498, 503 (2d Cir. 1990) (noting that courts should be “reluctant to intrude into the internal affairs of a cooperative or condominium association”).

Moreover, the injunction disrupts the cooperative’s longstanding policy and may set a precedent requiring similar accommodations for other residents with disabilities. The balance of equities thus weighs against Shapiro. *See id.* at 504.

IV. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER ALTERNATIVE ACCOMMODATIONS.

The FHAA does not require the accommodation sought by Shapiro; rather, it requires only that the housing provider make a “reasonable accommodation.” 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(A). Cadman Towers has offered to accommodate Shapiro’s disability by allowing her to park in the garage closest to her apartment once a space becomes available under the normal policy. This accommodation is reasonable and does not impose an undue burden. *See United States v. Access Living of Metro. Chicago*, 853 F. Supp. 734, 740 (N.D. Ill. 1994) (holding that a housing provider’s offer to relocate a disabled resident to a ground-floor unit once one became available was a reasonable accommodation).

The district court’s order improperly required Cadman Towers to provide Shapiro with a specific parking space immediately, without considering whether this accommodation was necessary or reasonable. *See Overlook Gardens Apartments*, 853 F. Supp. at 130.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court’s order granting the preliminary injunction should be reversed.
