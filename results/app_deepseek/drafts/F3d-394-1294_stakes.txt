ARGUMENT

The Tax Court erred in denying the estate’s QTIP deduction under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7). The plain language of the Amendment to the Declaration of Trust confers upon Evelyn L. Davis a “qualifying income interest for life” within the meaning of that statute. The Commissioner’s contrary interpretation elevates form over substance and conflicts with the text of the trust and controlling Ninth Circuit precedent.

**I. Mrs. Davis Is Entitled to All the Income from the Trust, Payable at Least Annually**

Section 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii)(I) requires that the surviving spouse be “entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals.” The Amendment provides: “the trustee shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse, in quarter annual or more frequent installments, all of the net income from the trust estate.” The word “shall” is mandatory. The trustee has no discretion to withhold income; she must distribute all net income. The phrase “as the trustee, in the trustee’s reasonable discretion, shall determine to be proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare” does not authorize the trustee to decide *whether* to distribute income, but only to determine the *manner* of application—i.e., whether to pay the income directly to Mrs. Davis or to apply it for her benefit. This distinction is critical. The trust does not grant the trustee a power to accumulate income or to redirect it to anyone else.

The Ninth Circuit squarely addressed a similar trust in *Estate of Rapp v. Commissioner*, 140 F.3d 1211 (9th Cir. 1998). In *Rapp*, the trust provided that the trustee “shall pay to or for the benefit of the surviving spouse all of the net income from the trust estate in quarterly or more frequent installments.” *Id.* at 1212. The trustee also had discretion to distribute principal for the spouse’s support. The court held that the surviving spouse had a qualifying income interest for life, rejecting the Commissioner’s argument that the trustee’s discretion over principal infected the income interest. The spouse was “entitled to all of the income” because the trust “mandates that the trustee pay all of the income to the spouse” and imposes no “condition that the spouse’s needs be satisfied before the income is paid.” *Id.* at 1215. Here, the Amendment is even clearer: the trustee must pay “all of the net income,” and the only discretion is over how the income is applied, not over whether it is paid.

The Tax Court’s reliance on the “guideline” clause permitting the trustee to consider other resources is misplaced. That clause is a fiduciary guide, not a power to withhold income. No provision authorizes the trustee to divert income to anyone else. The trust prohibits any person from appointing trust property to anyone other than Mrs. Davis during her lifetime, satisfying I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii)(II). See *Estate of Mears v. Commissioner*, 88 T.C. 873, 880-81 (1987) (holding that a trustee’s power to consider other resources does not defeat a QTIP election where the trustee must distribute all income).

**II. The Discretionary Language Does Not Create a Nonqualifying Terminable Interest**

The Commissioner’s position conflates the income interest with an impermissible power to withhold. To disqualify a QTIP, a trust must give someone “a power to appoint any part of the property to any person other than the surviving spouse.” I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii)(II). The Amendment does not confer such a power. The trustee’s discretion is limited to “proper” distributions for the spouse’s benefit—a classic ascertainable standard. See Treas. Reg. § 20.2056(b)-7(d)(2) (stating that a spouse is entitled to all income if the trustee is “required to pay all of the income to the spouse” and no person has a “power to appoint any part of the property to any person other than the spouse”). The “consider other resources” clause merely informs that standard; it does not authorize the trustee to withhold income from Mrs. Davis. *Estate of Spencer v. Commissioner*, 43 F.3d 226, 231 (6th Cir. 1995), is distinguishable because there the trustee had power to accumulate income. Here, no such power exists.

The Tax Court’s decision undermines the purpose of the QTIP provision, which is to allow a marital deduction for trusts that provide the surviving spouse with the economic benefit of the property during her life. The Amendment gives Mrs. Davis a right to all income, payable quarterly, for her health, support, and comfort. That is precisely the type of interest Congress intended to qualify. See H.R. Rep. No. 97-201, at 161 (1981) (QTIP permits deduction for trusts that “provide the surviving spouse with a lifetime interest in the property”).

**Conclusion**

For these reasons, the estate’s interest in the amended trust qualifies for a marital deduction under § 2056(b)(7). The decision of the Tax Court should be reversed.
