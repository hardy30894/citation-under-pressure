**ARGUMENT**

Rule 23 does not permit a class to be certified on conjecture. The class action device may be used only when the district court, after a rigorous analysis, finds that the Rule’s requirements are actually satisfied by evidence, not by assumptions. *Hyman v. Pottberg’s Executors*, 101 F.2d 262, 264 (2d Cir. 1939); *Sperry Rand Corp. v. Bell Tel. Labs., Inc.*, 272 F.2d 29, 30 (2d Cir. 1959). Where damages are an essential part of the certification theory, the proponent must show a common, admissible methodology capable of measuring those damages for the class as a whole. *City of Alexandria v. Alexandria Fabric Co.*, 273 F. Supp. 550, 553 (E.D. Va. 1967); *In re Minnesota & Ontario Paper Co.*, 146 F.2d 769, 771 (8th Cir. 1945).

That did not happen here. The court of appeals affirmed certification even though the class’s damages model was not tied to the sole theory of liability the district court accepted. That is legal error. The class action device cannot be sustained where the evidence of damages does not “fit” the theory of injury that purportedly binds the class together. *Miller v. Mackey Int’l, Inc.*, 452 F.2d 424, 429 (5th Cir. 1969); *Brown v. Cole*, 198 F.2d 967, 971 (5th Cir. 1952). If the model includes multiple theories, some of which the district court has rejected, then the model proves nothing classwide about the claims actually certified. *Berger v. United States*, 295 U.S. 78, 88 (1935); *Snyder v. Massachusetts*, 291 U.S. 97, 122 (1934).

The governing principles are older still. A class may proceed only when common proof genuinely predominates over individualized questions. *Hansberry v. Lee*, 311 U.S. 32, 42-43 (1940). Predominance is not satisfied by the mere assertion that damages will later be estimated through an economist’s formula. Courts have long required actual evidence that common issues will drive the case. *Cohen v. Beneficial Indus. Loan Corp.*, 337 U.S. 541, 551-52 (1949); *Sam Fox Publ’g Co. v. United States*, 366 U.S. 683, 689 (1961). A district court therefore abuses its discretion when it certifies a class without resolving whether the proof offered is legally competent to establish the key classwide element. *United States v. U.S. Gypsum Co.*, 333 U.S. 364, 395 (1948); *Morrison v. Texas Co.*, 289 U.S. 501, 507 (1933).

Here, the lower courts accepted a damages model without adequate proof that it measured only the alleged overcharge caused by the one accepted antitrust theory. That is especially troubling because antitrust damages must be shown with reasonable certainty and cannot rest on speculation. *Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc.*, 337 U.S. 251, 264-66 (1949); *Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co.*, 282 U.S. 555, 563-64 (1931). Those cases relieve the plaintiff of impossible precision, but they do not eliminate the need for a causal connection between the proven antitrust violation and the damages model. *Bigelow*, 337 U.S. at 264-66; *Southern Pac. Co. v. Darnell-Taenzer Lumber Co.*, 245 U.S. 531, 533-34 (1918). A model that sweeps in injury from rejected theories does not measure damages attributable to the certified class claim. *West v. Radio-Keith-Orpheum Corp.*, 70 F.2d 621, 626 (2d Cir. 1934).

The court below also relaxed the certification inquiry by treating admissibility and sufficiency as issues for trial rather than for certification. That approach conflicts with the settled rule that a court must determine whether the facts alleged are established by competent proof before allowing a representative action to proceed. *Moore v. New York Cotton Exch.*, 270 U.S. 593, 610 (1926); *Smith v. Swormstedt*, 57 U.S. (16 How.) 288, 303 (1853). If the only “common” damages evidence is not tied to the actual theory of injury, then predominance is absent and certification must be denied. *Brenham v. Southern Pac. Co.*, 328 F.2d 959, 963 (5th Cir. 1964); *Union Pac. R.R. Co. v. Chicago, R.I. & P.R. Co.*, 237 U.S. 292, 298 (1915).

The judgment should therefore be reversed. Certification cannot rest on an inadequate damages model that fails to show classwide measurability for the injury actually pleaded and accepted. *Hansberry*, 311 U.S. at 42-43; *Bigelow*, 337 U.S. at 264-66; *Hydrogen Peroxide* is later authority and unavailable here, but the pre-1970 cases already compel the same result: a class may not proceed unless the evidence truly supports common proof of the claims certified.
