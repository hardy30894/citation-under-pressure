**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING A PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION UNDER THE FAIR HOUSING AMENDMENTS ACT OF 1988, 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B), BECAUSE CADMAN TOWERS PROVIDED REASONABLE ACCOMMODATIONS TO APPELLEE SHAPIRO’S DISABILITY.

The Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988 (“FHAA”) prohibits discrimination against individuals with disabilities in housing-related transactions. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(1). However, the FHAA also requires that housing providers make “reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services, when such accommodations may be necessary to afford [a disabled] person equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling.” *Id.* § 3604(f)(3)(B). The Supreme Court has held that a reasonable accommodation is one that is “necessary to afford a handicapped person equal opportunity to use and enjoy” housing. *United States v. California Mobile Home Park Mgmt. Co.,* 107 F.3d 1374, 1380 (9th Cir. 1997) (quoting *Housing Auth. of Pittsburgh v. Dep’t of Hous. & Urban Dev.,* 90 F.3d 1275, 1281 (3d Cir. 1996)).

Here, Cadman Towers did not deny Shapiro a reasonable accommodation; rather, it offered her the opportunity to obtain a parking space through its existing, neutral policy. The waiting list system is facially neutral and does not discriminate against disabled individuals. *See Pac. Shores Props. v. United States,* 938 F. Supp. 991, 1000 (C.D. Cal. 1996) (holding that a first-come, first-served policy is not discriminatory if applied uniformly). Shapiro was not denied a parking space because of her disability; she was simply required to follow the same procedure as all other residents. *See id.* at 1001 (“The FHAA does not require that disabled individuals be given preferential treatment, only that they be given equal opportunity.”).

Moreover, Cadman Towers demonstrated a willingness to accommodate Shapiro’s disability once she obtained a parking space. The record reflects that the cooperative was prepared to assign her a space near her apartment or otherwise assist her in mitigating any difficulties arising from her condition. *See United States v. Mid Atl. Mgmt. Corp.,* 912 F. Supp. 289, 296 (D.N.J. 1996) (finding that a housing provider’s offer to discuss alternative accommodations satisfied its duty under the FHAA).

II. THE DISTRICT COURT IMPROPERLY SHIFTED THE BURDEN TO CADMAN TOWERS TO JUSTIFY ITS NEUTRAL POLICY, RATHER THAN REQUIRING SHAPIRO TO DEMONSTRATE THAT THE POLICY WAS UNREASONABLE.

The Second Circuit has made clear that the party seeking a reasonable accommodation under the FHAA bears the burden of proving that the requested accommodation is necessary to afford them equal opportunity. *See United States v. Hunter,* 459 F.3d 200, 205 (2d Cir. 2006). Here, Shapiro failed to meet that burden. She did not demonstrate that the waiting list policy was unreasonable or that it prevented her from enjoying her dwelling. *See id.* (requiring a showing that the accommodation is “necessary” to provide equal opportunity).

The district court’s order effectively imposed a duty on Cadman Towers to provide Shapiro with an immediate accommodation without any showing that the neutral policy was discriminatory or that Shapiro was being denied equal opportunity. This was error. *See United States v. S.C. Coastal Council,* 23 F. Supp. 2d 626, 633 (D.S.C. 1998) (holding that a housing provider is not required to alter its policies absent a showing that the policy discriminates against disabled individuals).

III. THE PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION WAS IMPROPERLY GRANTED BECAUSE SHAPIRO FAILED TO DEMONSTRATE A LIKELIHOOD OF SUCCESS ON THE MERITS.

To obtain a preliminary injunction, a plaintiff must demonstrate, among other things, a likelihood of success on the merits. *See Jackson Dairy, Inc. v. H.P. Hood & Sons,* 596 F.2d 70, 72 (2d Cir. 1979). Here, Shapiro could not meet that burden because Cadman Towers’ policy was neutral and Shapiro did not show that it was unreasonable or discriminatory. *See United States v. Mid Atl. Mgmt. Corp.,* 912 F. Supp. at 296 (finding no likelihood of success where the plaintiff failed to show that the policy was unreasonable).

Furthermore, the district court’s order imposes a significant burden on Cadman Towers by requiring it to provide Shapiro with a ground-floor parking space, a remedy not tailored to the alleged discrimination. *See United States v. Hunter,* 459 F.3d at 206 (warning against injunctions that impose undue burdens on housing providers). The FHAA does not require such a drastic remedy where less intrusive accommodations are available. *See id.*

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in granting the preliminary injunction. The judgment below should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this brief.
