Section 1407(a) says what it means and means what it says: actions transferred for multidistrict pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded” to the originating court “at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). A transferee court may not use § 1404(a) to nullify that command and keep the case for trial. The governing pre-1970 authorities point in one direction only: venue-transfer statutes must be applied according to their terms, and courts may not enlarge them by judicial convenience.

This Court has repeatedly treated venue and removal statutes as confined by their text. In Olberding v. Illinois Central R. Co., 346 U.S. 338 (1953), the Court rejected an expansive reading of a venue theory that would have “stretch[ed]” statutory consent beyond its terms. Id. at 340-41. Likewise, in Polizzi v. Cowles Magazines, Inc., 345 U.S. 663 (1953), the Court held that venue questions are governed by the specific statute Congress enacted, not by judicial gloss. Id. at 665-66. And in Lee v. Chesapeake & Ohio Ry. Co., 260 U.S. 653 (1923), the Court refused to permit a court to depart from the statute’s prescribed venue framework. Id. at 656. Those decisions establish the basic rule: when Congress has spoken specifically, courts may not use general procedural discretion to override the specific command.

That rule is decisive here. Section 1407(a) is not a permissive allocation of power; it is a mandatory remand provision. Congress used “shall,” not “may.” When statutory language is mandatory, courts are not free to substitute a more convenient practice. See United States v. Thoman, 156 U.S. 353, 359-60 (1895) (courts must give effect to the statute’s language where Congress has spoken plainly); Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917) (where statutory text is plain, “the sole function of the courts is to enforce it according to its terms”). A transferee court’s self-transfer under § 1404(a) does not harmonize the statutes; it defeats the remand command by making the transferee court the trial court. That is the very result § 1407(a) forbids.

Nor does § 1404(a) supply authority to evade § 1407(a). Section 1404(a) authorizes transfer “[f]or the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice.” But this Court has warned that a general procedural statute cannot displace a specific statutory arrangement Congress chose for a particular class of cases. In FTC v. Bunte Brothers, Inc., 312 U.S. 349 (1941), the Court declined to read a broad procedural provision in a way that would frustrate the more specific statutory scheme. Id. at 351-52. And in United States v. Chase, 135 U.S. 255 (1890), the Court held that courts may not use general judicial power to achieve a result inconsistent with the statute’s allocation of authority. Id. at 260-61. The same principle applies here: § 1404(a) cannot be used to do what § 1407(a) expressly requires the Panel to do—remand.

The Ninth Circuit’s conclusion is also irreconcilable with this Court’s venue precedents respecting party choice of forum. In Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501 (1947), the Court recognized that a plaintiff’s forum choice deserves respect and that transfer doctrines are limited equitable exceptions, not free-floating grants of power. Id. at 508. Section 1407(a) embodies that same restraint by preserving remand to the original district after coordinated pretrial proceedings end. A transferee court’s self-assignment for trial destroys the plaintiff’s chosen forum and converts a limited pretrial assignment into a permanent judicial takeover.

The better reading is the one the statute’s words compel. Congress knew how to authorize trial retention if it wished to do so, but it did not. Instead, it commanded remand. Courts must enforce that command, not replace it with a judge-made efficiency rule. See Caminetti, 242 U.S. at 485; Olberding, 346 U.S. at 340-41.

Because the District of Arizona lacked authority to assign the case to itself for trial, the judgment below should be reversed and the case remanded with instructions to return it to the Northern District of Illinois under § 1407(a).
