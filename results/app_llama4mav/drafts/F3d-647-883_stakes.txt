## Argument

The district court erred in dismissing Buckles's 28 U.S.C. § 2255 motion as untimely. The timeliness of Buckles's § 2255 motion hinges on whether his petition for certiorari was timely filed. If our order recalling the mandate restarted the clock for the 90-day period to petition for certiorari, then Buckles's petition was timely, and so was his subsequent § 2255 motion.

### I. The Recall of the Mandate Restarted the Clock for Filing a Petition for Certiorari

The Supreme Court's rules dictate that a petition for certiorari must be filed within 90 days of the denial of rehearing, unless the lower court entertains an untimely petition for rehearing or recalls its mandate. Sup.Ct. R. 13(1), (3). In this case, our court's order recalling the mandate effectively restarted the clock for filing a petition for certiorari. Although our order did not explicitly state that it was recalling the mandate for the purpose of considering Buckles's motion for appointment of certiorari counsel, it nonetheless had the effect of recalling the mandate.

As a result, the 90-day period for filing a petition for certiorari began to run anew from the date the mandate reissued, June 20, 2007. See United States v. Ibarra, 502 U.S. 1, 4 n.2 (1991) (noting that a recall of the mandate can restart the clock for filing a petition for certiorari). Therefore, Buckles's petition for certiorari, filed on September 5, 2007, was timely.

### II. Alternatively, Equitable Tolling Applies Due to Misconduct by Counsel and Misinformation from the Ninth Circuit Clerk's Office

Even if this court determines that Buckles's petition for certiorari was untimely, he is entitled to equitable tolling due to extraordinary circumstances. See Holland v. Florida, 560 U.S. 631, 649 (2010) (holding that § 2254 petitioners are entitled to equitable tolling in appropriate cases). The same principle applies to § 2255 motions. See United States v. Battles, 362 F.3d 1195, 1197 (9th Cir. 2004) (applying equitable tolling to a § 2255 motion).

Buckles's counsel failed to assist him in filing a motion for appointment of counsel, despite Buckles's repeated requests. This failure constitutes misconduct that prevented Buckles from filing a timely petition for certiorari. See Spitsyn v. Moore, 345 F.3d 796, 801 (9th Cir. 2003) (holding that an attorney's failure to file a timely petition can constitute extraordinary circumstances).

Furthermore, Buckles's sister was misinformed by someone in the Ninth Circuit Clerk's office that the timeline for filing the petition for certiorari would be stayed until a ruling was made on the motion for appointment of counsel. This misinformation, which Buckles reasonably relied upon, further supports equitable tolling. See Ramirez v. Yates, 571 F.3d 993, 997-98 (9th Cir. 2009) (holding that a petitioner's reasonable reliance on a court's statement can justify equitable tolling).

In light of these extraordinary circumstances, Buckles is entitled to equitable tolling, and his § 2255 motion should be considered timely.

The district court's dismissal of Buckles's § 2255 motion as untimely should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
