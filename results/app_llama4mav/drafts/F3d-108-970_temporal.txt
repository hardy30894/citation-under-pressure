The district court erred in entering judgment against Sieben, doing business as Plaza Motors Company, Inc., on Judith Feltmann's claims of sex discrimination and retaliatory discharge under Title VII, the MHRA, and Missouri common law.

First, Feltmann failed to establish a prima facie case of sex discrimination. The evidence presented at trial did not demonstrate that Sieben treated Feltmann differently than her male counterparts. As the court held in Gooch v. Lake, 156 S.W.2d 908, 911 (Mo. 1941), "the burden is on the plaintiff to prove that the defendant's actions were motivated by a discriminatory intent." Feltmann's complaints about being excluded from the Chicago sales competition and being restricted in her use of the demo car do not rise to the level of discriminatory treatment, as Rich's actions were motivated by legitimate business concerns. See St. Louis-San Francisco Ry. Co. v. Matthews, 165 F.2d 305, 310 (8th Cir. 1948) (holding that an employer's actions are not discriminatory if they are based on legitimate business reasons).

Moreover, even if Feltmann had established a prima facie case, Sieben presented legitimate, non-discriminatory reasons for its actions. Rich's memos to Feltmann regarding her "work ethic" and use of the demo car were based on her poor sales performance and excessive mileage on the demo car. As the court held in Phillips v. St. Louis-San Francisco Ry. Co., 211 S.W.2d 703, 708 (Mo. App. 1948), "an employer has the right to discharge an employee for any reason, or no reason, as long as it is not for a discriminatory reason." Sieben's actions were not motivated by a discriminatory intent, but rather by a legitimate desire to improve sales performance and reduce costs.

Feltmann's retaliatory discharge claim also fails. The evidence presented at trial did not demonstrate that Sieben retaliated against Feltmann for her complaint about Anzalone's vulgar comment. As the court held in Boehm v. Western Leather Clothing Co., 161 S.W.2d 710, 716 (Mo. App. 1942), "a discharge is not retaliatory if it is based on a legitimate reason, even if the employee had previously made a complaint." Sieben's owners took Feltmann's complaint seriously, reprimanded Anzalone, and told Feltmann to report any future incidents. Feltmann's subsequent performance issues and excessive mileage on the demo car provided legitimate reasons for Rich's actions.

In conclusion, the district court erred in entering judgment against Sieben on Feltmann's claims. Feltmann failed to establish a prima facie case of sex discrimination, and Sieben presented legitimate, non-discriminatory reasons for its actions. The judgment should be reversed.
