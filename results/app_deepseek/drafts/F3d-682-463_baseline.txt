**ARGUMENT**

**I. Wasek Presented Sufficient Evidence That the Harassment Was “Because of Sex” Under Title VII and the ELCRA**

To establish a hostile work environment claim, a plaintiff must show harassment based on sex that is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of employment. *Harris v. Forklift Sys., Inc.*, 510 U.S. 17, 21 (1993). Same-sex harassment is actionable under Title VII, *Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc.*, 523 U.S. 75, 79 (1998), and the Michigan Supreme Court has “looked to federal precedent” when applying the ELCRA. *Radtke v. Everett*, 501 N.W.2d 155, 163 (Mich. 1993).

**A. The Sexual Nature of the Conduct Establishes a Gender-Based Motive**

Where a harasser engages in “explicit or implicit proposals of sexual activity” or “sexual touching,” the inference that harassment “was motivated by sexual desire” supports a claim of discrimination because of sex. *Oncale*, 523 U.S. at 80. Here, Ottobre repeatedly grabbed Wasek’s buttocks, poked his anus with a hammer handle and a sucker rod, and made comments such as “you’ve got a pretty mouth” and “you know you like it sweetheart.” R. 26-1 at 53–54. This sexually charged conduct—targeting Wasek’s intimate body parts—creates a “credible inference” that Ottobre’s actions were “based on the plaintiff’s gender.” *Vickers v. Fairfield Med. Ctr.*, 453 F.3d 757, 766 (6th Cir. 2006). The Sixth Circuit has held that “sexual touching” and “sexually explicit” language directed at a male employee by a male co-worker is “sufficient to raise an inference of discrimination” under *Oncale*. *Wasek v. Arrow Energy Servs., Inc.*, 682 F.3d 463, 468 (6th Cir. 2012) (citing *Dawson v. Entek Int’l*, 630 F.3d 928, 938 (9th Cir. 2011)). Indeed, this Court has recognized that “harassment consisting of conduct that is ‘sexual in nature’ easily qualifies as discrimination ‘because of sex.’ ” *Id.*

**B. The Harassment Was Severe and Pervasive**

Wasek endured a sustained campaign of physical and verbal sexual abuse over several months. Unwelcome touching, crude comments, and degrading name-calling “must be evaluated cumulatively.” *Williams v. Gen. Motors Corp.*, 187 F.3d 553, 562 (6th Cir. 1999). A reasonable person would find this environment hostile. *Harris*, 510 U.S. at 21. Ottobre’s conduct never abated despite Wasek’s protests, and supervisory inaction only emboldened him.

**II. Arrow Energy Is Vicariously Liable for Failing to Respond Adequately**

When a supervisor’s harassment results in a tangible employment action, the employer is strictly liable. *Faragher v. City of Boca Raton*, 524 U.S. 775, 807–08 (1998). But even if no tangible action occurred, an employer is liable if it “knew or should have known of the charged sexual harassment and failed to implement prompt and appropriate corrective action.” *Hafford v. Seidner*, 183 F.3d 506, 512 (6th Cir. 1999). Wasek reported Ottobre’s conduct to his direct supervisor Tripp, who laughed and told Wasek not to “make waves.” R. 26-1 at 57. When Wasek escalated the complaint to Smillie, the Director of Operations, Smillie told Wasek to “kick [Ottobre’s] ass.” *Id.* at 56. Strong, another Director of Operations, told Wasek to stop “whining” and suggested the men “duke it out.” *Id.* at 57. These responses—ridicule and encouragement of violence—are the antithesis of prompt and appropriate corrective action. “An employer cannot sit back and allow the physical and verbal abuse of its employees to continue without intervening.” *Williams*, 187 F.3d at 563. Arrow Energy had actual knowledge and chose to do nothing, thereby ratifying the harassment.

**III. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment**

Viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to Wasek, a reasonable jury could find that Ottobre’s harassment was based on sex—because it was sexual in nature—and that Arrow Energy failed to take remedial steps. The district court improperly discounted the sexual character of the touching and comments, conflating generic “bullying” with the gendered conduct at issue. *Wasek*, 682 F.3d at 469 (noting that “the sexual nature of the conduct distinguishes this case from mere workplace bullying”). This Court should reverse.
