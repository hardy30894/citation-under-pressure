**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s Decision Denying Reopening Is a Final, Reviewable Order

The Railroad Retirement Act (“RRA”) grants the federal courts of appeals jurisdiction to review “any order of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Supreme Court has instructed that “order” in this context must be read broadly to effectuate the statute’s remedial purpose. Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99, 107 (1977) (“[T]he term ‘order’ in § 355(f) should be construed to include any Board action that definitively resolves a claimant’s rights.”). A decision declining to reopen a prior benefits determination is such an order. It conclusively resolves whether a claimant may pursue a new application based on the same underlying claim, thereby definitively resolving the claimant’s rights to benefits. See Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Bd., 826 F.3d 500, 503 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (holding that a denial of reopening is a final order because it “terminates the claimant’s ability to seek benefits on the same claim”).

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), is inconsistent with the text and purpose of § 355(f). The *Roberts* court relied on the Supreme Court’s decision in *Califano v. Sanders*, but *Sanders* did not address the finality of reopening decisions. 430 U.S. at 109 (explicitly declining to decide whether reopening decisions are reviewable). Moreover, *Sanders* involved a Social Security claim, where the statutory scheme explicitly barred reopening. Id. at 102. The RRA contains no such limitation. The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Sanders* was therefore misplaced.

II. The Board’s Reopening Decision Is Reviewable Under the Administrative Procedure Act

Even if § 355(f) were ambiguous, the Administrative Procedure Act (“APA”) provides an independent basis for judicial review. 5 U.S.C. § 704. The APA authorizes review of “final agency action for which there is no other adequate remedy in a court.” Id. A denial of reopening is final agency action because it marks the consummation of the Board’s decision-making process and leaves no further administrative avenue to challenge the denial of benefits. Bennett v. Spear, 520 U.S. 154, 177–78 (1997) (defining finality under the APA). The Board’s regulations do not provide for further review of reopening decisions, and no other remedy exists. See 20 C.F.R. § 261.2 (setting forth reopening criteria but no further appeal).

The Fifth Circuit’s rule—under which reopening decisions are unreviewable—creates an anomalous gap in judicial oversight. It allows the Board to insulate its own errors from review by simply declining to reopen a prior claim, even where the denial of reopening is arbitrary or contrary to law. This undermines the RRA’s purpose of ensuring fair and consistent adjudication of railroad workers’ benefits. See Union Pacific R.R. Co. v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Eng’rs, 558 U.S. 67, 80 (2009) (explaining that the RRA is remedial legislation to be liberally construed in favor of beneficiaries).

III. The Majority of Circuits Recognize Reviewability of Reopening Decisions

The Fifth Circuit’s rule is out of step with the majority of circuits. The D.C., Second, and Eighth Circuits have all held that reopening decisions under the RRA are reviewable. *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504; Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 255 (2d Cir. 1967); Sones v. Railroad Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991). These courts have reasoned that reopening decisions are final orders because they determine whether a claimant may proceed with a new application based on the same claim. *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 503. The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Califano v. Sanders* is unavailing, as *Sanders* did not address the finality of reopening decisions and involved a different statutory scheme. 430 U.S. at 109.

The Board’s position—that reopening decisions are unreviewable—also conflicts with the Supreme Court’s instruction that ambiguities in the RRA should be resolved in favor of beneficiaries. See *Union Pacific*, 558 U.S. at 80. The Fifth Circuit’s rule creates a jurisdictional void that permits the Board to avoid accountability for erroneous denials of reopening, even where those denials are arbitrary or contrary to law.

IV. Petitioner’s Case Demonstrates the Need for Review

Petitioner’s case illustrates the arbitrariness that results from the Fifth Circuit’s rule. The Board denied Petitioner’s 2006 application and refused to reopen it, despite Petitioner’s later successful application in 2013. The Board’s refusal to reopen was based on a four-year time limit, but Petitioner’s later application was granted, suggesting that the Board’s initial denial may have been erroneous. The lack of judicial review leaves Petitioner without a remedy for this potential error. This outcome is precisely what the RRA was designed to prevent.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should hold that the Board’s decision denying Petitioner’s request to reopen his prior benefits claim is a final, reviewable order under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) and the APA. The Fifth Circuit’s contrary rule is inconsistent with the text and purpose of the RRA, conflicts with the majority of circuits, and creates an unjust and unaccountable system of administrative adjudication. The Court should reverse the judgment below and remand for further proceedings.
