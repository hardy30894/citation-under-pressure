**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY AND PRACTICE CREATED A LIMITED PUBLIC FORUM OPEN TO ALL SPEECH, INCLUDING RELIGIOUS SPEECH, AND THE DENIAL OF ACCESS TO LAMB’S CHAPEL VIOLATED THE FIRST AMENDMENT.**

The Second Circuit erred in concluding that the school facilities were not a limited public forum open to religious speech. When a government entity opens its property for public use, it creates a limited public forum subject to heightened First Amendment scrutiny. *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educ. Fund, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 788, 802 (1985). The District’s policy and practice of allowing community groups to use school facilities after hours established such a forum. *See Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983) (holding that when a government entity opens property for public use, it creates a forum subject to First Amendment protections).

The District’s policy did not exclude religious speech on its face, but its application did. The District permitted secular groups to use school facilities while denying access to Lamb’s Chapel solely because of the religious nature of its proposed speech. This content-based exclusion is impermissible in a limited public forum. *Rosenberger v. Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995) (holding that viewpoint discrimination in a limited public forum violates the First Amendment). The District’s policy, as applied, discriminated against religious speech, violating the Free Speech Clause. *See Lamb’s Chapel v. Ctr. Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist.*, 508 U.S. 384, 393 (1993) (reversing the Second Circuit and holding that the District’s policy violated the First Amendment).

**II. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY VIOLATED THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE BY DISCRIMINATING AGAINST RELIGIOUS SPEECH.**

The District’s policy facially discriminated against religious speech by prohibiting its use of school facilities while permitting secular speech. This discrimination violated the Equal Protection Clause. *See City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Ctr.*, 473 U.S. 432, 448 (1985) (holding that laws discriminating against religious speech are subject to strict scrutiny). The District’s policy was not neutral but targeted religious speech, violating the principle of government neutrality toward religion. *See Epperson v. Arkansas*, 393 U.S. 97, 104 (1968) (holding that laws targeting religious speech are unconstitutional).

**III. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY VIOLATED THE FREE EXERCISE CLAUSE BY BURDENING LAMB’S CHAPEL’S RELIGIOUS EXERCISE.**

The District’s policy burdened Lamb’s Chapel’s free exercise of religion by denying it access to school facilities solely because of its religious nature. The Free Exercise Clause prohibits government policies that target religious exercise. *See Church of Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah*, 508 U.S. 520, 532 (1993) (holding that laws targeting religious exercise are subject to strict scrutiny). The District’s policy, as applied, violated the Free Exercise Clause by singling out religious speech for disfavored treatment. *See Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 269 (1981) (holding that a university’s policy excluding religious speech violated the Free Exercise Clause).

**IV. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY VIOLATED THE ESTABLISHMENT CLAUSE BY IMPERMISSIBLY ADVANCING SECULARISM.**

The District’s policy violated the Establishment Clause by discriminating against religious speech while permitting secular speech. The Establishment Clause prohibits government policies that prefer secularism over religion. *See Texas Monthly, Inc. v. Bullock*, 489 U.S. 1, 14 (1989) (holding that a tax exemption for secular publications violated the Establishment Clause). The District’s policy, as applied, impermissibly advanced secularism by excluding religious speech while permitting secular speech. *See Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 841 (holding that a university’s policy excluding religious speech violated the Establishment Clause).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Second Circuit should be reversed. The District’s policy and practice violated the First Amendment’s Free Speech Clause, the Equal Protection Clause, the Free Exercise Clause, and the Establishment Clause. The Court should grant the petition for a writ of certiorari and reverse the judgment below.
