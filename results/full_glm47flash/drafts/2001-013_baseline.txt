**Argument**

**I. The Court of Appeals erred in holding that the Coast Guard has exclusive jurisdiction over the working conditions of employees on uninspected vessels.**

The Court of Appeals relied on *Clary v. Ocean Drilling and Exploration Co.*, 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and *Donovan v. Texaco, Inc.*, 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), to conclude that the Coast Guard has exclusive authority over the working conditions of seamen. However, these precedents are distinguishable because they involved vessels subject to comprehensive Coast Guard regulation, whereas the MR. BELDON is an uninspected vessel subject only to minimal Coast Guard oversight. The preemption clause of the OSH Act, 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1), precludes OSHA only when another federal agency 'exercise[s] statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health.' The Court of Appeals conflated the Coast Guard's authority to 'promote safety of life and property' with authority over 'occupational safety and health.' See 14 U.S.C. § 2. While the Court of Appeals noted the Coast Guard regulates uninspected vessels, it limited those regulations to specific equipment like fire extinguishers and life preservers under 46 U.S.C. § 4102. This is not the 'comprehensive regulation' of seamen's working conditions required to trigger preemption. See *Donovan*, 720 F.2d at 827. The Court of Appeals' reliance on *Clary* is misplaced because *Clary* did not address the distinction between inspected and uninspected vessels. The Court of Appeals' holding that the Coast Guard's authority is 'exclusive' is not supported by the statutory text or the scope of the Coast Guard's regulations. Therefore, OSHA has jurisdiction.

**II. The MR. BELDON is a 'workplace' within the meaning of the OSH Act.**

The Court of Appeals improperly dismissed Mallard's argument that the barge was not a 'workplace.' Under 29 U.S.C. § 653(a), the OSH Act applies 'with respect to employment performed in a workplace in a State.' The MR. BELDON is a place where employment is performed. The Court of Appeals' finding that the barge was a workplace was supported by the record and is binding on this issue.
