ARGUMENT

I. The Text of 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4) Supplies the Admission Required by § 1255(a).

Section 1254a(f)(4) directs that a TPS recipient “shall be considered as being in, and maintaining, lawful status as a nonimmigrant” for purposes of § 1255. That command equates TPS with the lawful nonimmigrant status that, by definition, follows inspection and authorization. 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(13)(A). The Ninth Circuit therefore held that “by the very nature of obtaining lawful nonimmigrant status, the alien goes through inspection and is deemed admitted.” Ramirez v. Brown, 852 F.3d 954, 960 (9th Cir. 2017). The Sixth Circuit reached the same conclusion. Flores v. USCIS, 718 F.3d 548, 553–54 (6th Cir. 2013).

The Third Circuit’s contrary reading—that “lawful status” and “admission” remain distinct—overlooks the statutory linkage. Hanif v. Att’y Gen., 694 F.3d 479 (3d Cir. 2012), and Taveras v. Att’y Gen., 731 F.3d 281 (3d Cir. 2013), addressed adjustment under different provisions and did not construe § 1254a(f)(4). Where Congress has used “admission” and “status” in the same sentence, it has treated them as separate only when it so specifies. See 8 U.S.C. § 1255(k)(2)(A); I.N.S. v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421, 432 (1987) (“where Congress includes particular language in one section . . . but omits it in another . . . it is generally presumed that Congress acts intentionally”).

II. Structural and Purposive Considerations Confirm Eligibility.

Congress created explicit exceptions to the admission requirement for certain classes but omitted any such exception for TPS recipients. 8 U.S.C. § 1255(h), (i). That omission does not negate the affirmative command of § 1254a(f)(4); it simply leaves the statutory equivalence in place. Reading the two provisions together avoids rendering § 1254a(f)(4) superfluous. Hibbs v. Winn, 542 U.S. 88, 101 (2004).

The TPS statute’s purpose—to permit qualified aliens to remain lawfully without removal—would be frustrated if the very status Congress created could never lead to permanent residence for those who entered without inspection. The Ninth Circuit correctly rejected the anomaly that would result. Ramirez, 852 F.3d at 962. Petitioners therefore satisfy § 1255(a)’s threshold requirement.
