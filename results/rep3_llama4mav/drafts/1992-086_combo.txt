The Supreme Court has established that the government may create a public forum by dedicating property to expressive activity, and once it does so, it must respect the First Amendment rights of those who seek to use the forum (Hague v. CIO, 307 U.S. 496, 515 (1939)). In Perry Education Ass'n v. New York, 334 U.S. 558, 562 (1948)).

The District's denial of Lamb's Chapel's request to use the facilities for a religious-oriented film series is not viewpoint-neutral, as it is based on the religious content of the speech (see Cantwell v. Massachusetts, 321 U.S. 158, 166 (1944)).
