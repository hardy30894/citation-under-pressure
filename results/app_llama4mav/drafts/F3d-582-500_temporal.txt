## Argument

The District Court erred in granting summary judgment to Nationwide on Erdman's FMLA claim. To establish a prima facie case under the FMLA, Erdman must show that she was an eligible employee, entitled to FMLA leave, and that Nationwide interfered with or retaliated against her for exercising her FMLA rights. See 29 U.S.C. § 2611(2)(A)(ii) (requiring 1,250 hours of service in the preceding 12 months). The principal issue on appeal is whether Erdman accumulated sufficient hours to qualify for FMLA leave.

The FMLA defines "hours of service" to include "hours actually worked" and "hours compensated." 29 C.F.R. § 825.110(a). The record shows that Erdman regularly worked extra hours outside the office, which were either paid or used as "comp" time. Although Nationwide later prohibited Erdman from working extra hours, it had previously allowed and compensated her for doing so. Under the principles of estoppel, Nationwide should be precluded from denying that these hours were "hours of service" for FMLA purposes. See Dickerson v. United States, 62 F. Supp. 189, 192 (Ct. Cl. 1945) (estoppel applies when a party's words or actions induce another to rely on them to their detriment); see also Helvering v. Schine Chain Theatres, 121 F.2d 948, 950 (2d Cir. 1941) (same).

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has long recognized that an employer's past practices can be relevant in determining the terms of an employment relationship. See NLRB v. Niles Fire Brick Co., 124 F.2d 366, 370 (6th Cir. 1941) (employer's past practice can establish a term or condition of employment); see also NLRB v. J.H. Allison & Co., 165 F.2d 766, 768 (6th Cir. 1948) (same). Here, Nationwide's past practice of allowing and compensating Erdman for extra hours should be considered in determining whether she accumulated sufficient hours to qualify for FMLA leave.

The District Court's grant of summary judgment was improper because it failed to consider the disputed issue of whether Erdman's extra hours should be counted towards her FMLA eligibility. See Chambers v. Leiser, 43 F. Supp. 724, 726 (D. Mass. 1942) (summary judgment is inappropriate when there are genuine issues of material fact). Viewing the facts in the light most favorable to Erdman, as we must, it is clear that there are genuine issues of material fact regarding her FMLA eligibility.

In light of the disputed issues and the principles of estoppel, the District Court's grant of summary judgment on Erdman's FMLA claim should be reversed. The case should be remanded for further proceedings to determine whether Erdman accumulated sufficient hours to qualify for FMLA leave.

## Conclusion

The District Court erred in granting summary judgment to Nationwide on Erdman's FMLA claim. The disputed issue of whether Erdman's extra hours should be counted towards her FMLA eligibility precludes summary judgment. This Court should reverse the District Court's grant of summary judgment and remand the case for further proceedings.
