**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT CORRECTLY GRANTED SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON RAY’S FALSE IMPRISONMENT CLAIM BECAUSE AMERICAN DID NOT RESTRAIN HER WITHOUT LEGAL AUTHORITY.**

False imprisonment requires proof that the defendant “intentionally confined” the plaintiff “within fixed boundaries” and that the plaintiff was “conscious of and harmed by the confinement.” *Wheeler v. John Deere Co.*, 935 S.W.2d 141, 146 (Ark. 1996) (quoting *Restatement (Second) of Torts* § 35). Ray’s claim fails because she cannot show that American acted with the requisite intent to confine. The undisputed facts establish that American twice offered passengers the opportunity to deplane—once when the first bus arrived and again when the second bus arrived—and that Ray affirmatively chose to remain on the aircraft. *See id.* at 147 (no false imprisonment where plaintiff had “ample opportunity” to leave but chose to stay). Moreover, Ray’s subjective belief that she could not afford alternate transportation does not transform American’s lawful offer to deplane into an unlawful restraint. *Cf. Restatement (Second) of Torts* § 35 cmt. d (confinement does not occur where plaintiff “consents to the restraint or submits to it passively”).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT CORRECTLY GRANTED SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON RAY’S NEGLIGENCE CLAIM BECAUSE AMERICAN’S CONDUCT WAS NOT THE PROXIMATE CAUSE OF HER ALLEGED INJURIES.**

To prevail on a negligence claim, a plaintiff must prove duty, breach, causation, and damages. *See McCutchen v. Huckabee*, 337 Ark. 325, 330 (1999). Ray’s negligence claim fails because she cannot establish proximate cause. The only injury she alleges—a 24-hour stomach irritation—was not caused by American’s actions but by her own failure to seek medical attention or wash her hands after using the lavatory. *See Restatement (Second) of Torts* § 431 (liability requires a “substantial factor” in bringing about the harm). Even assuming American breached a duty by failing to provide adequate food, water, or restroom facilities, Ray’s injury was not a foreseeable consequence of that breach. *See Palsgraf v. Long Island R.R. Co.*, 248 N.Y. 339, 344 (1928) (Cardozo, J.) (“The risk reasonably to be perceived defines the duty to be obeyed.”). The district court therefore properly granted summary judgment on this claim.

**III. RAY’S STATE TORT CLAIMS ARE PREEMPTED BY FEDERAL AVIATION LAW.**

Ray’s false imprisonment and negligence claims are expressly preempted by the Airline Deregulation Act of 1978 (ADA), 49 U.S.C. § 41713(b)(1), which provides that “a State . . . may not enact or enforce a law, regulation, or other provision having the force and effect of law related to a price, route, or service of an air carrier.” The Supreme Court has held that the ADA’s preemption clause is “broad” and “sweeping,” *Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc.*, 504 U.S. 374, 378-79 (1992), and that it preempts state-law claims that “have the forbidden effect of enforcing state laws or regulations related to airline services,” *American Airlines, Inc. v. Wolens*, 513 U.S. 219, 224 (1995). Ray’s claims challenge American’s decision to keep passengers on the tarmac during a weather-related delay—a quintessential “service” decision. *See, e.g., In re Air Crash Disaster at Little Rock, Ark., Aug. 1, 1999*, 291 F.3d 509, 516 (8th Cir. 2002) (ADA preempts state-law claims arising from airline’s operational decisions during a diversion). The district court therefore correctly concluded that Ray’s state tort claims are preempted.

**IV. THE DISTRICT COURT DID NOT ABUSE ITS DISCRETION IN DENYING RAY’S MOTION FOR A CONTINUANCE UNDER FEDERAL RULE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE 56(f).**

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56(f) permits a district court to deny summary judgment if the nonmoving party demonstrates, by affidavit, that it cannot present facts essential to justify its opposition. *See Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986). The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying Ray’s motion because her affidavit failed to identify with specificity what additional evidence she needed or how it would create a genuine issue of material fact. *See Beyer v. County of Milwaukie*, 374 F.3d 989, 995 (9th Cir. 2004) (district court may deny Rule 56(f) motion where affidavit is “vague and conclusory”). Ray’s mere assertion that she needed more time to conduct discovery was insufficient to warrant a continuance. *See id.*

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the district court should be affirmed in all respects.
