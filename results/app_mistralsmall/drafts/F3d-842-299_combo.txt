**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN CONCLUDING THAT IT HAD SUBJECT-MATTER JURISDICTION UNDER 28 U.S.C. § 1331 OR 16 U.S.C. § 825p.

A. The Pressls’ complaint does not arise under federal law.

The well-pleaded complaint rule bars removal based on a federal defense, even if that defense is anticipated. *Franchise Tax Bd. of Cal. v. Constr. Laborers Vacation Trust*, 463 U.S. 1, 9–10 (1983). Here, the Pressls’ complaint alleges only state-law rights under the flowage easement and does not plead any federal cause of action. APCO’s asserted federal defenses—namely, that its FERC license preempts state-law rights—do not confer jurisdiction. *Louisville & Nashville R.R. v. Mottley*, 211 U.S. 149, 152 (1908) (“A suit arises under the Constitution and laws of the United States only when the plaintiff’s statement of his own cause of action shows that it is based upon those laws or that Constitution.”).

B. The district court lacked jurisdiction under 16 U.S.C. § 825p.

Section 825p authorizes federal jurisdiction only over “proceedings to enforce the provisions of [the Federal Power Act] or any valid order or regulation made thereunder.” 16 U.S.C. § 825p (1964). The Pressls’ suit does not seek to enforce the Federal Power Act or any FERC order; it seeks only a declaration of rights under a state-created easement. *First Iowa Hydro-Electric Coop. v. Fed. Power Comm’n*, 325 F.2d 10, 12 (D.C. Cir. 1963) (“[S]ection 313(b) [of the Federal Power Act] does not confer jurisdiction on the federal courts to adjudicate controversies between private parties over rights created by state law.”), aff’d, 378 U.S. 160 (1964).

II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DISMISSING THE COMPLAINT ON THE MERITS.

A. The flowage easement does not authorize APCO to prohibit the Pressls from building a dock.

Under Virginia law, easements are construed in light of the parties’ intent as expressed in the instrument. *Va. Elec. & Power Co. v. Westmoreland Cty.*, 186 Va. 136, 145 (1947). The easement here grants APCO the right to overflow the land and to remove obstructions, but it does not grant APCO regulatory authority over the use of the land. The easement expressly reserves to the landowners the right to “possess and use said premises in any manner not inconsistent with” APCO’s rights. This reservation preserves the Pressls’ right to build a dock, so long as it does not interfere with APCO’s flowage rights. *Smith v. United States*, 287 F.2d 585, 588 (4th Cir. 1961) (“An easement does not confer upon the easement holder the right to control the use of the servient estate beyond the scope of the easement.”).

B. APCO’s demand for an Occupancy and Use Permit is ultra vires.

APCO’s attempt to condition the Pressls’ use of their property on the execution of a permit is an assertion of regulatory authority not granted by the easement. *United States v. 1,585.61 Acres of Land*, 283 F.2d 678, 681 (4th Cir. 1960) (“A party holding an easement may not, by unilateral action, expand the scope of its rights beyond those granted by the instrument.”).

C. The district court erred in requiring the Pressls to exhaust administrative remedies before the FERC.

Exhaustion of administrative remedies is not required where the plaintiff seeks only a declaration of state-law rights. *Public Serv. Comm’n of Utah v. Wycoff Co.*, 344 U.S. 237, 246 (1952) (“[E]xhaustion of administrative remedies is not required where the controversy is not one that the agency is authorized to resolve.”). The FERC has no authority to adjudicate disputes between private parties over the interpretation of a state-created easement. *First Iowa Hydro-Elec. Coop.*, 325 F.2d at 12.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Pressls respectfully request that this Court vacate the district court’s judgment and remand the case to the state court from which it was improvidently removed.
