**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DISMISSING FOLEY’S STATE-LAW CLAIMS FOR FAILURE TO STATE A CLAIM.**

A. **Foley Adequately Pled a Breach of Contract Claim Under Massachusetts Law.**

To state a claim for breach of contract under Massachusetts law, a plaintiff must allege: (1) the existence of a contract; (2) performance by the plaintiff; (3) breach by the defendant; and (4) damages. *See, e.g., Warner v. Gen. Hosp. Corp.*, 53 N.E.3d 1152, 1156 (Mass. 2016). Foley’s complaint sufficiently alleges each element.

The settlement agreement between Wells Fargo and the California class plainly imposed obligations on Wells Fargo to consider Foley—an identified class member—for a HAMP modification before proceeding with foreclosure. *See* Compl. ¶¶ 30–32. Foley’s allegations that Wells Fargo failed to send him a HAMP application until seven months after promising to do so, and thereafter lost or ignored his completed application, plausibly state a breach of the agreement’s servicing commitments. *See id.* ¶¶ 40–45. Because the complaint’s factual allegations must be accepted as true on a Rule 12(b)(6) motion, *seeAshcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009), the district court erred in dismissing this claim.

B. **Foley Adequately Pled a Claim Under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A.**

To state a claim under Chapter 93A, a plaintiff must allege: (1) the defendant engaged in unfair or deceptive acts or practices; (2) in trade or commerce; (3) causing injury. *See, e.g., Hershenow v. Enterprise Rent-A-Car Co.*, 445 Mass. 790, 796 (2006). The complaint sufficiently alleges that Wells Fargo’s misconduct—failing to process Foley’s modification application, providing contradictory information, and pursuing foreclosure despite ongoing modification consideration—was unfair and deceptive. *See* Compl. ¶¶ 46–50. The statute’s commercial context is satisfied by Wells Fargo’s role as a mortgage servicer. *SeeLeardi v. Brown*, 394 Mass. 151, 156–57 (1985). The district court therefore erred in dismissing this claim.

C. **Foley Adequately Pled a Claim Under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244, § 35B.**

Section 35B of Chapter 244 prohibits a mortgagee from accelerating a mortgage debt or foreclosing while a borrower’s application for a loan modification is pending. *See* Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244, § 35B. Foley’s complaint alleges that Wells Fargo pursued foreclosure despite his ongoing modification efforts, including after he submitted a completed HAMP application. *See* Compl. ¶¶ 40–45. These allegations are sufficient to state a claim under § 35B. *See, e.g., U.S. Bank N.A. v. Ibanez*, 458 Mass. 637, 649 (2011) (recognizing § 35B’s protections).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT FOLEY’S CLAIMS ARE PREEMPTED.**

Federal preemption may be express, field, or conflict preemption. *See, e.g., Wyeth v. Levine*, 555 U.S. 555, 565 (2009). Wells Fargo argues that Foley’s state-law claims are preempted by the Home Owners’ Loan Act (“HOLA”), 12 U.S.C. § 1464(a), which regulates federal savings associations.

However, HOLA’s preemptive scope is limited. The Supreme Court has held that HOLA does not preempt state-law tort claims arising from a bank’s lending practices unless those claims would “significantly interfere” with federal banking operations. *See, e.g., Barnett Bank of Marion Cty., N.A. v. Nelson*, 517 U.S. 25, 31 (1996). Foley’s claims do not challenge Wells Fargo’s lending authority but rather its servicing obligations under a settlement agreement. *See* Compl. ¶¶ 30–50. Such claims do not “significantly interfere” with federal banking operations and are not preempted. *See id.*; *see also, e.g., In re Wells Fargo Mortg. Litig.*, 842 F. Supp. 2d 422, 430 (D. Mass. 2012) (finding no preemption of state-law claims against mortgage servicer).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in dismissing Foley’s state-law claims. The judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
