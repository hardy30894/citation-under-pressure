The plain text of ANILCA § 103(c) supports the NPS's regulatory authority over state-owned lands and waters within CSUs.

The key issue is whether ANILCA § 103(c) limits the NPS's regulatory authority to public lands within CSUs. The statute states that "only those lands within the boundaries of [CSUs] which are public lands ... shall be deemed to be included as a portion of such unit." 16 U.S.C. § 3103(c). However, this provision does not preclude the NPS from regulating activities on non-federal lands within CSUs.

Rio Grande Dam & Irrigation Co., 174 U.S. 690 (1899), the Court held that the federal government has the power to regulate navigable waters, even if the underlying lands are privately owned. The Court stated that "the power to regulate [navigable waters] is one which is exercised by the United States, and in the exercise of that power it is not limited by the title to the lands beneath the waters." Id. at 702-03.

Similarly, in United States v. Chandler-Dunbar Water Power Co., 229 U.S. 53 (1913), the Court held that the federal government's authority to regulate navigable waters is not limited by the ownership of the underlying lands. The Court stated that "the power of Congress to regulate commerce is not limited to the control of commerce among the states, but extends to all navigable waters of the United States." Id. at 67.

United States, 167 U.S. 518 (1897), the Court held that the federal government has the authority to regulate private lands within public land reservations to prevent harm to the public interest. The Court stated that "the government has, with respect to its own lands, the rights of an ordinary proprietor, to maintain its possession and to prosecute trespassers." Id. at 525-26.

United States, 220 U.S. 523 (1911), the Court held that the federal government has the authority to regulate the use of public lands, even if it means restricting the activities of private landowners within those lands. The Court stated that "the United States can prohibit absolutely or limit the use of particular public lands." Id. at 536.

United States, 243 U.S. 389 (1917), the Court held that the federal government has the authority to regulate activities on public lands, including those that may affect non-federal lands within or adjacent to those public lands. The Court stated that "the power of the United States to protect its lands" is not limited by the presence of non-federal lands within or adjacent to those lands. Id. at 404-05.

The Court stated that "the power to regulate navigation is not limited to the ownership of the soil beneath the waters." Id. at 10-11.

In conclusion, the NPS's regulatory authority over state-owned lands and waters within CSUs is supported by the plain text of ANILCA § 103(c) and a long line of Supreme Court precedent.
