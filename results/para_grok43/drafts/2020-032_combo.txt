The revision of the challenged policies during litigation, together with the graduation of one plaintiff, ended any live controversy. When an event occurs that renders it impossible for a court to grant effectual relief, the proper course is dismissal. Mills v. Green, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895). The same rule applies when the passage of time or change in circumstances removes the possibility of meaningful judicial action. California v. San Pablo & Tulare R.R. Co., 149 U.S. 308, 314 (1893); United States v. Alaska S.S. Co., 253 U.S. 113, 116 (1920). A federal court may not proceed to judgment once the underlying dispute has been extinguished. Ex parte McCardle, 74 U.S. (7 Wall.) 506, 514 (1868).

A request for nominal damages alone does not preserve jurisdiction. The Constitution limits the judicial power to “Cases” and “Controversies,” a limitation that requires a definite and concrete dispute touching the legal relations of parties having adverse legal interests. Aetna Life Ins. Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227, 240-241 (1937). Where the only remaining claim seeks a symbolic declaration without any allegation of actual injury, no such controversy exists. Maryland Casualty Co. v. Pacific Coal & Oil Co., 312 U.S. 270, 273 (1941). Nominal damages, untethered to proven loss, amount to an abstract pronouncement rather than redress for a concrete harm. See St. Pierre v. United States, 319 U.S. 41, 42-43 (1943).

The capable-of-repetition exception does not apply once the challenged conduct has permanently ceased. Southern Pacific Terminal Co. v. ICC, 219 U.S. 498, 515 (1911). Absent a showing that the same controversy will recur between the same parties, the exception supplies no basis for continued adjudication. United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36, 39 (1950). Nor does the mere possibility of future injury suffice when the policies at issue have been superseded and the plaintiffs no longer attend the institution. See Bell v. Hood, 327 U.S. 678, 682 (1946) (recognizing that jurisdiction depends on a present, justiciable claim for relief).

Because the record contains no allegation of compensable injury and the policies have been abandoned, the action is moot in its entirety. Dismissal is required.
