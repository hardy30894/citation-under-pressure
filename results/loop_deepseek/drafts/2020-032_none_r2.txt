**ARGUMENT**

The case is moot. Article III restricts federal courts to live “cases” and “controversies.” *Mills v. Green*, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895). That requirement endures through every stage of litigation. *Aetna Life Ins. Co. v. Haworth*, 300 U.S. 227, 240–41 (1937). When the challenged government action is withdrawn and no plaintiff remains subject to it, the controversy vanishes. *Hall v. Beals*, 396 U.S. 45, 48 (1969) (per curiam); *Golden v. Zwickler*, 394 U.S. 103, 108 (1969). Here, Georgia Gwinnett College permanently repealed both the “Freedom of Expression Policy” and the offending “Student Code of Conduct” provision. Uzuegbunam graduated and has no continuing connection to the campus. The only remaining demand is for nominal damages. That demand cannot revive a dead case.

**I. The Policy Change Eliminated Any Ongoing Controversy**

A plaintiff must demonstrate a “substantial controversy, between parties having adverse legal interests, of sufficient immediacy and reality to warrant the issuance of a declaratory judgment.” *Aetna Life Ins. Co.*, 300 U.S. at 240–41. Once the policy that allegedly caused injury is repealed, no such controversy can exist. *California v. San Pablo & Tulare R.R. Co.*, 149 U.S. 308, 314 (1893) (moot when “the act complained of” has been “abandoned”). The college’s new policy permits expressive activity anywhere on campus without a permit, except in narrow, unchallenged circumstances. The revised “Student Code of Conduct” no longer contains the provision that prohibited “open-air speaking.” The college has credibly disavowed any intention to return to the prior restrictions. *United States v. W.T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. 629, 632–33 (1953) (moot where “there is no reasonable expectation that the wrong will be repeated”). Thus, both the facial and as-applied challenges to the prior policies are moot. *Liner v. Jafco, Inc.*, 375 U.S. 301, 305 (1964).

**II. A Prayer for Nominal Damages Cannot Revive a Moot Case**

A plaintiff cannot maintain a suit solely to obtain nominal damages after the underlying controversy has dissolved. *Mills*, 159 U.S. at 653. Nominal damages are a “technical” remedy that presupposes a live dispute; they do not create one. *St. Pierre v. United States*, 319 U.S. 41, 42 (1943) (per curiam) (mootness where no collateral consequences remain). This Court has repeatedly dismissed cases as moot even when nominal damages were requested. *Atherton Mills v. Johnston*, 259 U.S. 13, 16 (1922) (moot when challenged statute expired); *Brownlow v. Schwartz*, 261 U.S. 216, 218 (1923) (moot when building demolished). The only recognized exception is when the plaintiff suffers continuing concrete, collateral consequences. *Sibron v. New York*, 392 U.S. 40, 55–56 (1968). Here, the complaint alleged no actual injury—no monetary loss, no physical or emotional harm, no reputational damage. The alleged harm was the abstract “value” of the constitutional right itself. That is insufficient. Without a concrete injury, there is no “substantial controversy” to support jurisdiction. *Golden*, 394 U.S. at 108–09.

**III. The Voluntary Cessation Doctrine Does Not Salvage the Case**

The voluntary cessation doctrine prevents mootness only when the defendant is “free to return to its old ways.” *W.T. Grant*, 345 U.S. at 632. But the college’s policy change was formal, permanent, and comprehensive—it did not simply suspend enforcement. *Southern Pac. Terminal Co. v. ICC*, 219 U.S. 498, 514–15 (1911) (moot when “no probability” of recurrence). There is no evidence that the college intends to reinstate the challenged restrictions. To the contrary, the college acted promptly to cure constitutional concerns. The burden is on the plaintiffs to show a reasonable expectation of recurrence, and they have not done so. *Liner*, 375 U.S. at 305.

**Conclusion**

Because the challenged policies were permanently repealed and no plaintiff remains subject to them, the case is moot. A request for nominal damages, unaccompanied by any allegation of actual injury, cannot supply the ongoing controversy that Article III requires. The district court properly dismissed the complaint. This Court should affirm.
