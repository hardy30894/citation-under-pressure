ARGUMENT

The Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act extends Longshore Act coverage only where the injury occurs on the outer Continental Shelf. Section 1333(b) provides benefits for “disability or death of an employee resulting from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The phrase “occurring as the result of operations conducted on” the Shelf limits coverage to injuries sustained on the Shelf itself.

This Court has long recognized that workers’ compensation statutes of this character impose a strict geographic limitation. In Nacirema Operating Co. v. Johnson, 396 U.S. 347 (1969), the Court held that the Longshore Act “covers only injuries occurring on the actual navigable waters,” and that Congress had “specifically” declined to extend coverage to land-based injuries. Id. at 354. The same textual limitation appears in § 1333(b). Just as the Longshore Act’s “upon the navigable waters” requirement was treated as a situs restriction, the parallel language in the Shelf Act confines coverage to injuries occurring on the Shelf.

The same principle appears in Crowell v. Benson, 285 U.S. 22 (1932). There the Court emphasized that the Longshore Act’s coverage turned on “the place of the injury” being within the defined maritime area, and that the statute could not be read to reach injuries occurring elsewhere. Id. at 38-39. Section 1333(b) likewise ties the compensable event to operations “conducted on” a specific location—the Shelf.

Congress enacted the Shelf Act against this settled background. When it chose to incorporate the Longshore Act’s remedy, it did so only for injuries arising from Shelf operations and occurring within the geographic bounds of those operations. The statute supplies no warrant for extending coverage to injuries sustained hundreds of feet inland.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
