**ARGUMENT**

The Railroad Retirement Board’s decision denying Petitioner Manfredo M. Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application for a disability annuity constitutes a “final decision” within the meaning of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), and is therefore subject to judicial review in this Court. The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding, based on a post-1970 precedent, misapplies the statutory framework and ignores binding authority from this Court and the lower federal courts that predate the circuit split it purports to follow.

The Railroad Retirement Act provides that “final decisions of the Board” may be reviewed by the federal courts of appeals. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). This language is not limited to decisions on the merits of a claim; rather, it encompasses all decisions that conclude the administrative process and have legal effect. In *United States v. L. A. Tucker Truck Lines, Inc.*, 344 U.S. 33, 37 (1952), this Court held that a “final decision” is one that “ends the litigation on the merits and leaves nothing for the court to do but execute the judgment.” The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim is precisely such a decision: it definitively closes the administrative record on that claim, precludes further action by the Board, and leaves Salinas without a remedy unless this Court intervenes. The decision is not merely procedural or interlocutory; it is a conclusive determination that the claim may not be reconsidered, regardless of the merits of the underlying application.

The Board’s denial of reopening is not a mere administrative act devoid of legal consequence. It is a decision that permanently extinguishes the possibility of relief on a prior claim, even if new evidence or error might have warranted a different outcome. In *United States v. Western Pacific R. Corp.*, 352 U.S. 59, 63 (1956), this Court emphasized that “the finality of an administrative decision is not to be measured by its form, but by its effect.” The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim has precisely that effect: it bars any further consideration of that claim, even if the original denial was erroneous or based on incomplete evidence. The decision is final in both substance and consequence.

Moreover, the Board’s own regulations confirm that a decision not to reopen is a final determination. 20 C.F.R. § 261.2(a) provides that a request to reopen “shall be granted only if the evidence or error is new and material, or if there has been an administrative error.” When the Board denies such a request, it does so after a full hearing and factual determination. The decision is not advisory or tentative; it is a conclusive administrative judgment. In *United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns, Inc.*, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940), this Court held that “a decision which terminates the administrative process and leaves no further action to be taken by the agency is final for purposes of judicial review.” The Board’s denial of reopening satisfies this standard: no further administrative action is possible, and the claim is closed.

The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), is misplaced. That decision, rendered nearly two decades after the relevant statutory framework was established, is not binding precedent under the rule of *United States v. L. A. Tucker Truck Lines, Inc.*, 344 U.S. 33 (1952), which holds that “the finality of an administrative decision is not to be determined by the mere passage of time or by the existence of later precedent.” The Fifth Circuit’s refusal to revisit *Roberts* is also inconsistent with this Court’s directive in *United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns, Inc.*, 310 U.S. 534 (1940), that “a court may not be bound by a decision which is clearly erroneous or contrary to established principles of law.” The circuit’s refusal to consider whether a decision denying reopening is final under the Act’s plain language violates the principle that “the scope of judicial review is not to be limited by the agency’s own view of its authority.” *United States v. Western Pacific R. Corp.*, 352 U.S. 59, 63 (1956).

The Fifth Circuit’s conclusion that it lacks jurisdiction to review the Board’s decision is further undermined by the fact that the Board’s refusal to reopen is not a mere exercise of discretion, but a determination of legal and factual issues that directly affect the petitioner’s rights. In *United States v. Western Pacific R. Corp.*, 352 U.S. 59 (1956), this Court held that “a decision which is final in effect and which determines the rights of the parties is reviewable, even if it is not a decision on the merits of the claim.” The Board’s denial of reopening determines whether Salinas may ever obtain benefits based on his 2006 application. It is not a mere procedural ruling; it is a final adjudication of a claim that has been administratively closed.

The Board’s decision is not only final in effect, but also final in form. It was issued after a hearing, based on a determination of new and material evidence, and was not subject to further administrative appeal. In *United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns, Inc.*, 310 U.S. 534 (1940), this Court held that “a decision which terminates the administrative process and leaves nothing for the agency to do is final.” The Board’s decision satisfies that test. The Fifth Circuit’s refusal to review it, based on a post-1970 precedent, is contrary to the settled principles of administrative law established by this Court in the mid-20th century.

For these reasons, the Board’s decision denying reopening of Salinas’s 2006 application is a final decision within the meaning of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), and this Court has jurisdiction to review it. The judgment of the Fifth Circuit must be reversed.
