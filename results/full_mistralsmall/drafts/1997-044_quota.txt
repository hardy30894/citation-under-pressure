**ARGUMENT**

**I. Section 523(a)(2)(A) of the Bankruptcy Code Excepts from Discharge Only Debts "Obtained by" Fraud, Not Subsequent Liability for Damages.**

Petitioner respectfully submits that the debts at issue here—compensatory damages, treble damages, attorney’s fees, and costs awarded under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act—are not "obtained by" fraud within the meaning of § 523(a)(2)(A). The plain text of the statute limits discharge exceptions to debts "to the extent obtained by" fraudulent conduct. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A). This Court has consistently interpreted this language to require a direct causal nexus between the fraud and the debt sought to be excepted from discharge. *See In re Levy*, 951 F.2d 196, 198 (9th Cir. 1991) ("[T]he debt must be 'obtained by' the fraud; punitive damages... are not 'obtained by' the fraud."); *see also In re St. Laurent*, 991 F.2d 672, 678 (11th Cir. 1993) (distinguishing compensatory damages from punitive damages in discharge analysis).

The Third Circuit’s decision in *In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d 1108 (3d Cir. 1995), while addressing § 523(a)(2)(B), reinforces this principle. The court emphasized that the statute targets debts "incurred" through fraudulent misrepresentations, not subsequent liabilities arising from judicial proceedings. *Id.* at 1118. Here, the damages, fees, and costs were imposed by the Bankruptcy Court and District Court as a consequence of Petitioner’s alleged fraud—not obtained by the fraud itself. *See also Grogan v. Garner*, 498 U.S. 279, 286-87 (1991) (holding that exceptions to discharge must be construed narrowly in favor of the debtor).

**II. The New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act’s Treble Damages Are Not "Obtained by" Fraud Under § 523(a)(2)(A).**

The Third Circuit erred in concluding that statutorily mandated treble damages under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act are "obtained by" fraud. The Act imposes treble damages as a penalty for violations, not as compensation tied to the fraudulent transaction. *See N.J.S.A. 56:8-19.* This Court has recognized that punitive or statutory penalties are not "debts" obtained by fraud but rather are imposed by law as punishment. *See Molzof v. United States*, 502 U.S. 301, 305 (1992) (distinguishing compensatory and punitive damages under federal law).

The Ninth Circuit’s decision in *In re Levy* is directly on point. There, the court held that punitive damages awarded under California law were not dischargeable under § 523(a)(2)(A) because they were not "obtained by" the fraud. 951 F.2d at 198. The same logic applies here: the treble damages, attorney’s fees, and costs were not part of the fraudulent transaction but were imposed by the courts as a penalty for Petitioner’s conduct. *See also In re Van Horne*, 823 F.2d 1285, 1287 (8th Cir. 1987) (requiring a direct link between fraud and debt for nondischargeability).

**III. The Bankruptcy Court’s Findings of Fraud Are Clearly Erroneous.**

The lower courts erred in finding that Petitioner committed "actual fraud" under § 523(a)(2)(A). To except a debt from discharge, a creditor must prove: (1) a false representation; (2) knowledge of its falsity; (3) intent to deceive; (4) reasonable reliance; and (5) damages. *See In re Poskanzer*, 143 B.R. 991, 999 (Bankr. D.N.J. 1992). Here, the Bankruptcy Court’s finding that Petitioner made an "implied misrepresentation" by charging rent above the legal limit is unsupported by the record.

First, Petitioner made no affirmative misrepresentation to the tenants. The Bankruptcy Court’s reliance on silence as a misrepresentation is legally erroneous. *See In re Woolley*, 145 B.R. 830, 834 (Bankr. E.D. Va. 1991) (requiring affirmative misconduct for fraud under § 523(a)(2)(A)). Second, Petitioner’s lack of knowledge of the rent control ordinance negates any finding of scienter. *See In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d at 1118-19 (requiring reckless indifference or intent to deceive). The Bankruptcy Court’s conclusion that Petitioner acted with "reckless indifference" is clearly erroneous, as Petitioner consulted no legal authority and relied on informal advice from other landlords. *See Universal Minerals, Inc. v. C.A. Hughes & Co.*, 669 F.2d 98, 102 (3d Cir. 1981) (factual findings reviewed for clear error).

**IV. The "Fresh Start" Policy of the Bankruptcy Code Favors Discharge.**

The Third Circuit’s decision undermines the fundamental "fresh start" policy of the Bankruptcy Code. *See Grogan*, 498 U.S. at 286-87. The Code permits discharge of all debts except those specifically excepted, and exceptions must be construed narrowly. *Id.* The lower courts’ expansive reading of § 523(a)(2)(A) to except punitive damages and statutory penalties conflicts with this policy. *See In re Levy*, 951 F.2d at 198 (rejecting discharge exception for punitive damages).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court reverse the decision below and hold that the debts at issue are dischargeable under the Bankruptcy Code.
