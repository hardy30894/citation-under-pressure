## Argument

The jury’s invalidity verdict cannot stand because Facebook did not carry its burden to prove by clear and convincing evidence that Leader’s own precritical-date commercial activity disclosed every limitation of the asserted claims in a single anticipatory reference or product. Anticipation is “a question of fact,” but it requires that “each and every element as set forth in the claim is found, either expressly or inherently described, in a single prior art reference.” *In re Omeprazole Patent Litig.*, 483 F.3d 1364, 1379 (Fed. Cir. 2007); *Finnigan Corp. v. Int’l Trade Comm’n*, 180 F.3d 1354, 1365 (Fed. Cir. 1999). Because “the burden of establishing invalidity of a patent or any claim thereof shall rest on the party asserting such invalidity,” 35 U.S.C. § 282, Facebook had to prove anticipation with evidence that was clear and convincing. *Microsoft Corp. v. i4i Ltd. P’ship*, 564 U.S. 91, 95 (2011); *PowerOasis, Inc. v. T-Mobile USA, Inc.*, 522 F.3d 1299, 1304 (Fed. Cir. 2008).

### I. The record does not establish anticipation as a matter of law.

To prove anticipation under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b), Facebook had to show that Leader’s pre-December 10, 2002 product embodied every limitation of the asserted claims. *Net MoneyIN, Inc. v. VeriSign, Inc.*, 545 F.3d 1359, 1369 (Fed. Cir. 2008); *Verdegaal Bros., Inc. v. Union Oil Co. of Cal.*, 814 F.2d 628, 631 (Fed. Cir. 1987). A product or printed description that leaves gaps, requires selection from alternatives, or only generally resembles the claim does not anticipate. *In re Gleave*, 560 F.3d 1331, 1334-35 (Fed. Cir. 2009); *Celeritas Techs., Ltd. v. Rockwell Int’l Corp.*, 150 F.3d 1354, 1361 (Fed. Cir. 1998).

The asserted claims require, among other things, dynamically associating metadata with data, tracking movement of a user from one user environment to another, and dynamically updating stored metadata so the data and application follow the user into the second environment. The question was therefore not whether Leader2Leader® was a collaborative software platform, but whether Facebook proved the specific claim limitations. *Structural Rubber Prods. Co. v. Park Rubber Co.*, 749 F.2d 707, 715 (Fed. Cir. 1984). The evidence cited by Facebook—white papers, demonstrations, and testimony describing an “underlying engine”—at most showed a sophisticated collaboration product. It did not compel the conclusion that the precritical-date product necessarily included each claimed step and relation in the asserted claims. *Schumer v. Lab. Computer Sys., Inc.*, 308 F.3d 1304, 1315-16 (Fed. Cir. 2002).

### II. The district court erred by allowing anticipation to rest on inferential reconstruction.

Anticipation cannot be established by picking and choosing among disclosures, or by reconstructing the claim from multiple teachings. *Net MoneyIN*, 545 F.3d at 1371; *Novo Indus., L.P. v. Micro Molds Corp.*, 350 F.3d 1348, 1355 (Fed. Cir. 2003). Nor may a tribunal fill missing claim limitations with probabilities or after-the-fact inference. *Atofina, S.A. v. Great Lakes Chem. Corp.*, 441 F.3d 991, 999-1000 (Fed. Cir. 2006). Yet the jury was invited to infer that because Leader2Leader® used browsers, “boards,” and collaboration tools, it must have practiced the later-claimed dynamic metadata architecture. That is exactly the sort of hindsight reasoning this Court rejects. *In re Arkley*, 455 F.2d 586, 587 (C.C.P.A. 1972); *MEHL/Biophile Int’l Corp. v. Milgraum*, 192 F.3d 1362, 1365 (Fed. Cir. 1999).

The district court also misapplied the law governing public use and on-sale activity. A prior sale or use invalidates only if the invention was “ready for patenting” and the sale or use embodied the claimed invention. *Pfaff v. Wells Elecs., Inc.*, 525 U.S. 55, 67-68 (1998); *Invitrogen Corp. v. Biocrest Mfg., L.P.*, 424 F.3d 1374, 1379-80 (Fed. Cir. 2005). Leader’s commercialization efforts and demonstrations showed, at most, an evolving product. They did not establish that by January 2002 Leader had a completed embodiment of the later-claimed system. *Allen Eng’g Corp. v. Bartell Indus., Inc.*, 299 F.3d 1336, 1353-54 (Fed. Cir. 2002).

### III. Substantial evidence does not support the verdict.

Even under deferential review, a verdict must be reversed when no reasonable jury could find invalidity on the record presented. *Ecolab, Inc. v. FMC Corp.*, 569 F.3d 1335, 1364 (Fed. Cir. 2009); *WBIP, LLC v. Kohler Co.*, 829 F.3d 1317, 1328 (Fed. Cir. 2016). Here, the district court’s denial of judgment as a matter of law was error because Facebook’s proof depended on broad descriptions and inferences rather than evidence that the precritical-date Leader2Leader® product contained every element of the asserted claims. *Nazomi Commc’ns, Inc. v. ARM Holdings, PLC*, 403 F.3d 1364, 1371-72 (Fed. Cir. 2005). Leader was entitled to judgment as a matter of law, or at minimum a new trial, because the verdict rests on speculation rather than clear and convincing proof. *Muniauction, Inc. v. Thomson Corp.*, 532 F.3d 1318, 1328 (Fed. Cir. 2008).

For these reasons, the judgment of invalidity should be reversed.
