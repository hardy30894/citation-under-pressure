ARGUMENT

I. Standard of Review

This Court reviews de novo a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal. Medina-Velázquez v. Hernández-Gregorat, 767 F.3d 103, 108 (1st Cir. 2014). Allegations are accepted as true and all reasonable inferences drawn in the plaintiff’s favor. Id.; Watterson v. Page, 987 F.2d 1, 3 (1st Cir. 1993). A complaint states a claim when its facts render relief plausible. Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 570 (2007); Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009); Ocasio-Hernández v. Fortuño-Burset, 640 F.3d 1, 12 (1st Cir. 2011).

II. The Settlement Agreement Creates Enforceable Duties

The class-action settlement is a binding contract whose terms Wells Fargo must perform. United States v. ITT Continental Baking Co., 420 U.S. 223, 236-37 (1975). Foley, a Settlement Class B member, alleges the bank never applied the required HAMP-then-MAP2R waterfall before foreclosure. These servicing commitments are not precatory; they impose sequential obligations whose breach states a claim for breach of contract. See, e.g., Rivera-Flores v. Bristol-Myers Squibb Caribbean Corp., 112 F.3d 9, 12 (1st Cir. 1997).

III. State-Law Claims Are Not Preempted

HOLA preemption does not reach every state cause of action arising from a national bank’s servicing commitments. Barnett Bank of Marion County, N.A. v. Nelson, 517 U.S. 25, 31 (1996). Foley’s claims rest on independent state duties of good faith and fair dealing, not on conflicting disclosure or lending standards. See Sovereign Bank v. Sturgis, 863 F. Supp. 2d 75, 92 (D. Mass. 2012); Dixon v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A., 798 F. Supp. 2d 336, 352 (D. Mass. 2011).

IV. The Complaint Plausibly Alleges Each Count

Foley’s repeated, unreturned inquiries and the bank’s continued foreclosure notices while modification review remained pending support claims for breach of the implied covenant, Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A violations, and negligent misrepresentation. See Foisy v. Royal Maccabees Life Ins. Co., 356 F.3d 130, 140 (1st Cir. 2004); Rodi v. S. New Eng. Sch. of Law, 389 F.3d 5, 13 (1st Cir. 2004). The district court’s contrary reading impermissibly resolved factual disputes at the pleading stage.

Conclusion

The judgment should be reversed.
