## ARGUMENT

The district court erred in denying Wood's motion for a new trial because it improperly instructed the jury to disregard testimony concerning subsequent remedial measures that was properly admitted for impeachment purposes.

### I. The District Court's Initial Ruling Under Rule 407 Was Properly Limited

Although the district court initially granted Morbark's motion in limine to exclude evidence of post-accident design changes under Rule 407, this ruling was not absolute. As the court later acknowledged, Morbark's counsel "opened the door" to rebuttal evidence when he suggested to the jury that the wood chipper's design had not been changed and that it was still in use by various government agencies. See Shepard v. United States, 290 U.S. 96, 103 (1933) (Cardozo, J.) (noting that a party may not "furnish [a] predicate for [a] misleading inference" and then complain when the opposing party seeks to rebut it).

### II. Evidence of Subsequent Remedial Measures Was Properly Admitted for Impeachment

When Morbark's counsel implied that the seventeen-inch chute was safe and still in use, Wood's counsel was entitled to rebut this implication by introducing evidence of the subsequent design changes. The testimony of John Infinger, which revealed that the infeed chute had been lengthened, was properly admitted to impeach Morbark's suggestion that the original design was safe and unchanged. See Chicago & N. W. Ry. Co. v. Kelly, 84 F.2d 569, 572-73 (8th Cir. 1936) (holding that evidence of subsequent repairs was admissible to impeach a defendant's claim that its original design was safe).

### III. The District Court's Admonition to the Jury Was Erroneous

After allowing the testimony concerning the subsequent design changes, the district court erred in admonishing the jury to disregard it. This instruction was improper because the evidence was relevant for impeachment purposes, and the jury should have been allowed to consider it for that limited purpose. See United States v. Leggett, 312 F.2d 566, 572 (6th Cir. 1962) (noting that a jury instruction must be tailored to the specific purpose for which evidence is admitted).

The district court's error in instructing the jury to disregard the evidence of subsequent remedial measures was prejudicial, as it undermined Wood's ability to rebut Morbark's misleading implications. Therefore, the denial of Wood's motion for a new trial was erroneous, and this Court should reverse and remand for a new trial.
