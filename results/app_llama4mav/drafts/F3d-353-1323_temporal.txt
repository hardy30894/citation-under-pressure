## ARGUMENT

The district court erred in dismissing Beg's complaint for lack of subject matter jurisdiction under the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA). The commercial activity exception to foreign sovereign immunity, codified at 28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2), applies to Beg's claims.

The FSIA's commercial activity exception is rooted in the principle that a foreign sovereign's commercial activities should be treated similarly to those of private entities. As the Supreme Court noted in Bank of the United States v. Planters' Bank of Georgia, 22 U.S. 904, 907 (1824), "it is not the less a bank because it is the bank of the State." This principle is relevant to the instant case, where the Government of Pakistan expropriated Beg's land and subsequently engaged in activities related to the property, including offering Beg an alternative parcel of land through a representative sent to the United States.

The expropriation of property and subsequent actions taken by the Government of Pakistan constitute commercial activity. In Berizzi Bros. Co. v. S.S. Pesaro, 271 U.S. 562, 574 (1926), Justice Van Devanter (dissenting) noted that a foreign government's operation of a merchant vessel was a commercial activity. Similarly, the Government of Pakistan's expropriation of Beg's land and its subsequent use for military housing or transfer to military personnel can be considered a commercial activity, as it involves the government's engagement in activities akin to those of private parties.

Furthermore, the Government of Punjab's actions in sending a representative to the United States to offer Beg an alternative parcel of land had a direct effect in the United States. In The Schooner Exchange v. McFaddon, 11 U.S. 116, 145 (1812), Chief Justice Marshall observed that a foreign sovereign's actions within the United States are subject to the jurisdiction of U.S. courts. The Government of Punjab's representative's actions in the United States demonstrate a sufficient nexus to the United States, thereby satisfying the "direct effect" requirement under 28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2).

Therefore, the district court erred in dismissing Beg's complaint for lack of subject matter jurisdiction. The commercial activity exception to foreign sovereign immunity applies, and the court should have exercised jurisdiction over Beg's claims. This Court should reverse the district court's order and remand the case for further proceedings.
