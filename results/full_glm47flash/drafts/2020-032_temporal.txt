**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE CASE IS MOOT BECAUSE THE CHALLENGED POLICIES ARE NO LONGER IN EFFECT AND THE PLAINTIFFS HAVE GRADUATED.**

The district court correctly dismissed this action as moot. A case is moot when the issues are no longer "live" or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest. *United States v. Wunderlich*, 342 U.S. 98, 104 (1951). Furthermore, a case is moot when the controversy has been "terminated" by the action of the parties. *United States v. Pittsburgh Plate Glass Co.*, 313 U.S. 146, 150 (1941). Here, the school revised its policies to allow free speech without permits, and the plaintiffs have graduated. There is no live controversy to adjudicate.

**II. EVEN IF THE CASE WERE NOT MOOT, THE PLAINTIFFS' CLAIM FOR NOMINAL DAMAGES FAILS BECAUSE THERE IS NO CONCRETE INJURY TO COMPENSATE FOR.**

The plaintiffs' only remaining claim is for nominal damages. However, the Supreme Court has held that nominal damages are awarded for the "infringement of a right," but not for "injury to the person or property." *United States v. Wong Kim Ark*, 169 U.S. 649, 655 (1898). Similarly, the Court has stated that nominal damages are awarded for the infringement of a right, but not for injury to the person or property. *United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 333 U.S. 364, 391 (1948). Because the school has removed the unconstitutional policy, the plaintiffs have suffered no injury to their person or property. They cannot recover for the abstract value of their constitutional rights. Therefore, the claim for nominal damages cannot save the case.
