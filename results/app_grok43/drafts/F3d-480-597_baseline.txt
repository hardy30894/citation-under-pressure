II. A. Mens Rea That the Weapon Was a Machine Gun

A conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 922(o) requires proof that the defendant “knew of the features of his or her firearm that made it a machinegun.” Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600, 619 (1994). The government therefore must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the weapon “was capable of firing automatically.” Id. at 602. When reviewing a preserved sufficiency challenge, the court asks “whether, after viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to the prosecution, any rational trier of fact could have found the essential elements of the crime beyond a reasonable doubt.” Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307, 319 (1979).

The record contains no such proof. Nieves-Castaño told agents she had opened the golf bag, seen “a rifle in it,” and recognized it as “an AK-47.” Nothing in her statement or in the physical evidence indicated she knew the rifle could fire more than one shot with a single pull of the trigger. The prosecution introduced no testimony that she had ever operated the weapon, observed it being fired automatically, or received any information about its automatic capability. An AK-47 rifle may be manufactured in either semiautomatic or automatic configurations; knowledge that the object is an “AK-47” therefore does not, without more, establish knowledge that it is a machinegun. See Staples, 511 U.S. at 615 (rejecting the notion that “all guns . . . are primarily used for criminal purposes”).

Because the evidence is insufficient to prove the mens rea element required by Staples, the conviction under § 922(o) must be reversed.
