**ARGUMENT**

I. **Meruelo’s Share of Merco’s Net Operating Loss Is Deductible Because He Established Bona Fide Indebtedness Running Directly to Him Under § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i).**

A shareholder’s basis in an S corporation includes not only stock basis but also “indebtedness of the S corporation to the shareholder.” 26 U.S.C. § 1366(d)(1)(B); Treas. Reg. § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i). To qualify, the indebtedness must be “bona fide indebtedness” that “runs directly” to the shareholder. *Gould v. Comm’r*, 177 F.2d 777, 778 (2d Cir. 1949) (holding that a shareholder’s guarantee of a corporate loan did not create basis unless the shareholder was the actual obligee); *see also Old Colony R.R. v. Comm’r*, 284 U.S. 552, 560 (1932) (noting that a shareholder’s basis is increased only by “actual investment” in the corporation, including loans made directly to it).

Here, Meruelo’s transfers through affiliates were recharacterized as direct loans from him to Merco. The Tax Court erred in requiring that the debt run *only* from Merco to Merco’s affiliates. *Cf. Helvering v. Midland Mut. Life Ins. Co.*, 300 U.S. 216, 223 (1937) (explaining that tax consequences depend on substance, not form). The record shows that Meruelo’s funds were used to cover Merco’s obligations, and the annual netting and allocation by Carreras reflected a direct debtor-creditor relationship between Meruelo and Merco. *See United States v. Snyder*, 149 U.S. 210, 214 (1893) (holding that a shareholder’s advance to a corporation, even if routed through intermediaries, may constitute a loan if the substance is a direct obligation).

II. **Meruelo Made an “Actual Economic Outlay” Sufficient to Support Basis Under § 1366(d)(1).**

A shareholder must make an “actual economic outlay” to increase basis. *Perlman v. Comm’r*, 135 F.2d 58, 59 (3d Cir. 1943) (holding that a shareholder’s guarantee of a corporate debt did not create basis unless the shareholder made an actual payment). Here, Meruelo personally borrowed funds and directed their use to cover Merco’s deposit and operating expenses. *See United States v. Chicago, B. & Q. R. Co.*, 41 F.2d 966, 968 (8th Cir. 1930) (holding that a shareholder’s personal funds advanced to a corporation constitute an economic outlay even if routed through intermediaries).

The Commissioner’s argument that Meruelo’s basis is limited to his $5 million capital contribution ignores the substance of the transactions. *Cf. Burnet v. Logan*, 283 U.S. 404, 410 (1931) (holding that tax treatment turns on the economic reality of the transaction). The funds Meruelo advanced through affiliates were ultimately used for Merco’s benefit, and the annual netting reflected a direct obligation running to him.

III. **The Tax Court Erred in Rejecting Meruelo’s Basis Calculation.**

The Tax Court’s conclusion that Meruelo failed to establish bona fide indebtedness is unsupported by the record. *See Helvering v. Lazarus & Co.*, 308 U.S. 252, 255 (1939) (holding that the Commissioner’s determination is presumptively correct but may be rebutted by evidence showing the transaction’s substance). Here, Meruelo presented evidence—including the promissory note, annual netting, and allocation—that established a direct debtor-creditor relationship. *Cf. Burnet v. Houston*, 283 U.S. 223, 227 (1931) (holding that a taxpayer’s records and accounting treatment are relevant to determining the transaction’s substance).

The Tax Court’s reliance on the form of the transactions was erroneous. *See Old Colony R.R.,* 284 U.S. at 560 (holding that tax consequences depend on substance, not form). The funds Meruelo advanced were used for Merco’s benefit, and the annual netting reflected a direct obligation running to him. The Tax Court’s conclusion to the contrary is unsupported by the record and contrary to law.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Meruelo’s share of Merco’s net operating loss is deductible because he established bona fide indebtedness running directly to him and made an actual economic outlay. The Tax Court’s decision should be reversed.
