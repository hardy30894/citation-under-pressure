# ARGUMENT

## I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING SIEBEN'S MOTION FOR JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW BECAUSE FELTMANN FAILED TO ESTABLISH A PRIMA FACIE CASE OF SEX DISCRIMINATION

To establish a prima facie case of sex discrimination under the burden-shifting framework of *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792 (1973), Feltmann was required to show that she suffered an adverse employment action under circumstances giving rise to an inference of discrimination. *Id.* at 802. Even assuming Feltmann cleared that threshold, Sieben articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for each challenged action, and Feltmann failed to demonstrate that those reasons were pretextual. *See Texas Dep't of Cmty. Affairs v. Burdine*, 450 U.S. 248, 253 (1981) (once defendant articulates a legitimate nondiscriminatory reason, plaintiff must show "that the proffered reason was not the true reason for the employment decision").

The record does not support an inference of discriminatory intent. Rich's January 4, 1993 memorandum documented legitimate performance concerns—Feltmann's premature use of unaccompanied "guest drives" and friction with colleagues—concerns wholly unrelated to her sex. The March 1993 probation, which applied equally to Feltmann and her male colleague Beutel, further undermines any inference of disparate treatment. As this Court has recognized, "the mere fact that an employee suffers an adverse employment action does not raise an inference of discrimination absent some showing that similarly situated employees outside the protected class were treated differently." *Harvey v. Anheuser-Busch, Inc.*, 38 F.3d 968, 972 (8th Cir. 1994). Beutel's identical treatment forecloses that showing here.

Nor does exclusion from the Chicago sales trip support liability. Rich offered the opportunity only to the two top-performing consultants; when they declined, he extended it to no one else—male or female. Feltmann cannot manufacture a discrimination claim from an employer's decision to withhold a benefit from all employees equally. *See Krenik v. County of Le Sueur*, 47 F.3d 953, 960 (8th Cir. 1995) (Title VII "does not require an employer to treat all employees identically, only to refrain from discrimination on the basis of a protected characteristic").

The demo-car restriction likewise fails to support an inference of discrimination. Rich imposed the geographic restriction on all consultants following the decline in dealership profitability after the incentive program ended. That Feltmann alone received a personal memorandum reflects only that she alone continued to violate the restriction by driving 300 miles round-trip to Illinois on a weekly basis—conduct that plainly warranted individualized correction regardless of the offending employee's sex.

## II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN SUBMITTING THE RETALIATION CLAIM TO THE JURY BECAUSE FELTMANN FAILED TO ESTABLISH A CAUSAL CONNECTION BETWEEN HER PROTECTED ACTIVITY AND ANY ADVERSE ACTION

A retaliation plaintiff must prove a causal connection between the protected activity and the adverse employment action. *Kim v. Nash Finch Co.*, 123 F.3d 1046, 1060 (8th Cir. 1997). "Generalized knowledge" of protected activity, without more, is insufficient; the plaintiff must show that the decisionmaker was motivated by that activity. *Id.*

Sieben's response to Feltmann's November 1992 complaint about Anzalone was immediate and effective: the Capps brothers personally met with Feltmann, offered to terminate Anzalone, reprimanded him upon her request that he not be fired, and warned him that any recurrence would result in termination. No further incidents occurred. This is precisely the type of prompt remedial action that forecloses—rather than establishes—retaliatory animus. *See Stacks v. Southwestern Bell Yellow Pages, Inc.*, 27 F.3d 1316, 1326 (8th Cir. 1994) (employer's prompt remedial action defeats inference of retaliatory motive).

The temporal gap between Feltmann's complaint and the challenged employment actions further undermines causation. Rich's performance memorandum issued nearly two months later, and the probationary period followed in March—four months after the complaint. This Court has held that "a lengthy period between the protected activity and the adverse action militates against a finding of causation." *Kipp v. Missouri Highway & Transp. Comm'n*, 280 F.3d 893, 897 (8th Cir. 2002). The intervening documented performance deficiencies—Feltmann's admittedly low gross-profit margins for five consecutive months—provide an independent, nonretaliatory explanation that Feltmann failed to rebut.

The stray remark by co-worker Barnstead that Feltmann had "polarized herself" cannot establish causation, as Barnstead was not the decisionmaker responsible for any adverse action. *See Simmons v. Oce-USA, Inc.*, 174 F.3d 913, 916 (8th Cir. 1999) (remarks by non-decisionmakers insufficient to establish retaliatory causation).

## III. THE JURY'S VERDICT WAS AGAINST THE WEIGHT OF THE EVIDENCE AND SIEBEN IS ENTITLED TO A NEW TRIAL

Even if the evidence were sufficient to survive judgment as a matter of law, the verdict was against the clear weight of the evidence, warranting a new trial under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 59. *See White v. Pence*, 961 F.2d 776, 780 (8th Cir. 1992) (new trial warranted where verdict is "against the great weight of the evidence"). Feltmann's own performance record—including the lowest gross-profit averages among all consultants for five consecutive months—provided overwhelming, unrebutted justification for Sieben's actions, rendering the jury's finding of discriminatory or retaliatory intent unsupportable.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the judgment below and direct entry of judgment for Sieben, or, in the alternative, remand for a new trial.
