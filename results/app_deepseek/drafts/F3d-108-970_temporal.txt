ARGUMENT

**I. The District Court Erred in Finding That Sieben Discriminated Against Feltmann on the Basis of Sex.**

To establish a prima facie case of sex discrimination, a plaintiff must show that she was treated differently from similarly situated male employees. *See United States v. Local 189, United Papermakers*, 416 F.2d 980, 984 (5th Cir. 1969) (holding that disparate treatment requires proof that the employer applied different standards to employees of different sexes). Feltmann failed to meet this burden. The record shows that Sieben placed Feltmann and male consultant Rick Beutel on probation simultaneously for the same reason—low sales performance in January and February 1993. No male employee received preferential treatment. Moreover, the personal memo regarding Feltmann’s “work ethic” was based on her specific selling methods, not her sex. *See Jenkins v. United Gas Corp.*, 400 F.2d 28, 33 (5th Cir. 1968) (finding no discrimination where employer’s criticisms were job-related and applied evenhandedly). Feltmann’s claim that male consultants occasionally took demo cars outside the St. Louis area without receiving memos is unsubstantiated by the record; the only evidence of such conduct was Feltmann’s own testimony, which the district court was not required to credit. *See NLRB v. Gissel Packing Co.*, 395 U.S. 575, 617 (1969) (trier of fact may reject self-serving testimony).

Even if Feltmann could establish a prima facie case, Sieben articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions. Feltmann’s sales declined after the incentive program ended, and her average gross profit per car was the lowest of all consultants from December 1992 through April 1993. Her supervisor, Bob Rich, testified that she failed to thoroughly explain car features, a sales technique that yields higher prices. Such performance-based criticisms are valid business judgments. *See Texas & Pacific Ry. Co. v. Rigsby*, 241 U.S. 33, 41 (1916) (employer may make subjective assessments of employee performance). Feltmann also violated the demo car policy by repeatedly driving 300 miles to Illinois despite a written memo restricting use to the St. Louis area. *Quarles v. Philip Morris, Inc.*, 279 F. Supp. 505, 512 (E.D. Va. 1968) (enforcing company rules against all employees is not discriminatory). Feltmann failed to show that these reasons were pretextual. She offered no evidence that Sieben’s stated reasons were false or that sex motivated any decision. *See United States v. H. K. Porter Co.*, 296 F. Supp. 40, 49 (N.D. Ala. 1968) (plaintiff must show employer’s reasons are a cover for discrimination). The district court therefore erred in concluding that Sieben discriminated against Feltmann.

**II. The District Court Erred in Finding That Sieben Retaliated Against Feltmann for Her Complaint.**

To prove retaliation, a plaintiff must show that she engaged in protected opposition activity and that a causal connection exists between that activity and an adverse employment action. *Pettway v. American Cast Iron Pipe Co.*, 411 F.2d 998, 1004 (5th Cir. 1969). Feltmann’s complaint about a vulgar comment from a co-worker at a different franchise does not constitute opposition to an unlawful employment practice under Title VII. The comment was not made by a supervisor and did not affect the terms or conditions of her employment. *See Silver v. K.C. Ry. Co.*, 270 F. Supp. 317, 321 (W.D. Mo. 1967) (isolated offensive remarks do not rise to level of discrimination). Moreover, Sieben promptly investigated, reprimanded the offending employee, and offered to terminate him—a response Feltmann declined. No further incidents occurred. Feltmann’s subsequent assumption that a co-worker’s comment about a “thick personnel file” referred to her complaint is speculative and insufficient to establish causation. *See NLRB v. Gissel Packing Co.*, 395 U.S. at 617 (speculation does not prove causation).

Even assuming protected activity, Feltmann cannot show a causal link. The adverse actions—the January memo, March probation, and eventual termination—were based on legitimate performance and policy violations, not her complaint. The memo predated the probation by two months, and the probation was imposed on a male consultant as well. *Pettway*, 411 F.2d at 1005 (temporal proximity alone is insufficient to show retaliation). The district court’s contrary finding is unsupported by the record and must be reversed.
