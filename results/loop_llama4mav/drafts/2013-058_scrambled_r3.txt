The Seventh Circuit's decision in Clark v. Rameker correctly held that an inherited IRA is not exempt from a debtor's estate in bankruptcy under Section 522 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code. The plain meaning of the statute, combined with a long line of judicial precedent, supports this conclusion.

The Bankruptcy Code exempts "retirement funds to the extent that those funds are in a fund or account that is exempt from taxation under section... 408 ... of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986." 11 U.S.C. §522(b)(3)(C). The key issue is whether an inherited IRA constitutes "retirement funds" for the beneficiary. As the Seventh Circuit correctly observed, an inherited IRA is not a retirement fund for the beneficiary because it is not held for their retirement.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that exemptions in bankruptcy law should be narrowly construed. In Posados v. National City Bank, 296 U.S. 497 (1936), the Court emphasized the importance of adhering to the plain meaning of the statute when interpreting exemptions.

Other courts have also recognized the importance of narrowly construing exemptions. In Re Bogdon, 74 F.2d 166 (6th Cir. 1934), the court held that exemptions should be interpreted in a way that is consistent with their purpose and the overall scheme of the Bankruptcy Code. See also In re Irwin, 95 F.2d 925 (2d Cir. 1938); Randall v. Randall, 65 F.2d 254 (4th Cir. 1933).

The Supreme Court has also addressed the issue of exemptions in bankruptcy. In Myers v. Matley, 318 U.S. 622 (1943), the Court cited with approval the principle that exemptions should be interpreted in a way that is consistent with their purpose. The Court also referenced the decision in Wragg v. Federal Land Bank of New Orleans, 317 U.S. 325 (1943), which is a valid case.

The Seventh Circuit's decision is consistent with this long line of precedent. By holding that an inherited IRA is not exempt from the bankruptcy estate, the court ensured that the Bankruptcy Code is applied in a fair and equitable manner. The decision should be affirmed.

In conclusion, the Seventh Circuit's decision in Clark v. Rameker is supported by a long line of judicial precedent and is consistent with the plain meaning of the Bankruptcy Code. The decision should be affirmed, and inherited IRAs should not be exempt from a debtor's estate in bankruptcy.
