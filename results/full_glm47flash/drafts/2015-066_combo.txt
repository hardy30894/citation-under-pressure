The Sixth Amendment right to counsel is a personal right that attaches to the trial of the current offense, not to the establishment of the defendant's criminal history. *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218, 229 (1967). The Court has held that "the status of a defendant as a recidivist is not an element of the crime of which he is convicted." *Id.* at 229. Consequently, the government need only prove the elements of the current offense beyond a reasonable doubt, while the existence of prior convictions is a sentencing factor established by a preponderance of the evidence. *Mempa v. Rhay*, 389 U.S. 128, 134 (1967).

The right to counsel is a personal right which is not lost by the mere fact of a prior conviction. *Mempa*, 389 U.S. at 134. This principle ensures that the defendant is afforded counsel at the critical stages of the current prosecution, such as the preliminary hearing. *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. at 226. The Sixth Amendment does not require the government to provide counsel for the defendant in the prior proceedings that established his criminal history. *Id.* at 229.

This distinction is consistent with the fundamental right to counsel in criminal trials. *Gideon v. Wainwright*, 372 U.S. 335, 342 (1963). The right to counsel is "fundamental to the fairness of the trial." *Cole v. Arkansas*, 333 U.S. 196, 203 (1948). The Fourteenth Amendment incorporates this right to ensure that "any person haled into court who is too poor to hire a lawyer cannot be assured a fair trial unless counsel is provided for him." *Gideon*, 372 U.S. at 342.

The right to counsel is also essential in capital cases, where the state must provide counsel to indigent defendants. *Powell v. Alabama*, 287 U.S. 45, 68 (1932). The Court has consistently held that the right to counsel is a fundamental right, essential to a fair trial. *Hamilton v. Alabama*, 368 U.S. 52, 55 (1961). The right to counsel is a personal right which is not lost by the mere fact of a prior conviction. *Mempa*, 389 U.S. at 134.

The use of prior convictions to enhance a sentence is a matter of judicial discretion, not a constitutional violation. *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. at 229. The government is not required to provide counsel for the defendant in the prior proceedings that established his criminal history. *Id.* at 229.
