**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Admitting the Defendant’s Expert Opinion on the Cause of the Line’s Parting.**

Federal Rule of Evidence 702 permits expert testimony only when it will assist the trier of fact. An opinion that merely states a conclusion within the common knowledge of the jury is not admissible. *Salem v. United States Lines Co.*, 370 U.S. 31, 35 (1962). The defendant’s expert offered no specialized analysis beyond the observation that the line parted; that conclusion lay within the jury’s unaided competence. The admission of this testimony therefore constituted error.

**II. The District Court Erred in Denying Judgment as a Matter of Law on Unseaworthiness.**

A shipowner owes an absolute, nondelegable duty to furnish a seaworthy vessel. *Mitchell v. Trawler Racer, Inc.*, 362 U.S. 539, 549 (1960). That duty extends to all appliances and appurtenances, including lines and rigging. *Mahnich v. Southern S.S. Co.*, 321 U.S. 96, 99 (1944). The record established without contradiction that the crew used summer grease in sub-freezing temperatures, that the bearings froze, and that the line was rigged under severe time pressure after the vessel had passed the harbor breakers. These conditions rendered the bos’n’s-chair apparatus unseaworthy as a matter of law. The district court therefore erred in submitting the issue to the jury.

**III. The Remaining Rulings Do Not Preclude Relief.**

The district court correctly denied judgment as a matter of law on comparative negligence, because the evidence permitted a reasonable jury to find that Cook’s use of the torch contributed to the line’s failure. *Rogers v. Missouri Pac. R.R. Co.*, 352 U.S. 500, 506–07 (1957). Likewise, evidence of Cook’s alcohol-related ailments was relevant to damages and was properly admitted. *See Grunenthal v. Long Island R.R. Co.*, 353 U.S. 218, 220 (1957). Because the errors identified in Parts I and II affected the jury’s consideration of liability and damages, the judgment must be vacated and the case remanded for a new trial.
