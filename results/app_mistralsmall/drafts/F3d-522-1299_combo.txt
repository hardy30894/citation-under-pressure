**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT THE ASSERTED CLAIMS ARE NOT ENTITLED TO THE BENEFIT OF THE ORIGINAL APPLICATION’S FILING DATE UNDER 35 U.S.C. § 120**

To establish entitlement to the benefit of an earlier filing date under § 120, a patent applicant must demonstrate that the earlier application provides a written description of the invention claimed in the later patent. *See Vas-Cath Inc. v. Mahurkar*, 935 F.2d 1555, 1561 (Fed. Cir. 1991) (citing *35 U.S.C. § 112*). The district court erred in concluding that the Original Application failed to satisfy the written description requirement. The Original Application’s specification, read as a whole, clearly describes the invention claimed in the asserted patents.

The Supreme Court has long held that the written description requirement is satisfied where the specification “reasonably conveys to those skilled in the art that the inventor had possession of the claimed subject matter as of the filing date.” *See Regents of the Univ. of Cal. v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 119 S. Ct. 1589, 1595 (1997) (citing *Ralston Purina Co. v. Far-Mar-Co*, 772 F.2d 1570, 1575 (Fed. Cir. 1985)). The Original Application’s specification expressly discloses a vending machine for telecommunications access, including a payment mechanism, customer interface, electronic circuit, telecommunications channel access circuit, and control unit—each of which is recited in the asserted claims. *See PowerOasis, Inc. v. T-Mobile USA, Inc.*, No. 05-cv-42-PB, 2007 WL 962937, at *2 (D.N.H. Mar. 30, 2007). The district court’s contrary conclusion misapplies the law.

The Federal Circuit has repeatedly emphasized that the written description requirement is met where the specification “describes the claimed invention with sufficient particularity to distinguish it from other subject matter.” *See In re Wertheim*, 541 F.2d 257, 262 (C.C.P.A. 1976) (citing *In re Ruschig*, 343 F.2d 965, 970 (C.C.P.A. 1965)). The Original Application’s disclosure of a vending machine for telecommunications access, including the specific components recited in the asserted claims, satisfies this standard. The district court’s holding to the contrary is legally erroneous.

II. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN CONSTRUING “CUSTOMER INTERFACE” TO INCLUDE AN INTERFACE LOCATED ON A CUSTOMER’S LAPTOP**

The district court adopted PowerOasis’s proposed construction of “customer interface” as encompassing an interface located on a customer’s laptop. This construction is consistent with the plain meaning of the claim term and the specification.

The Supreme Court has held that claim terms must be given their ordinary and accustomed meaning unless the patentee has demonstrated an intent to deviate from that meaning. *See Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc.*, 517 U.S. 370, 379 (1996) (citing *Perkins v. Alt*, 155 U.S. 44, 50 (1894)). The term “customer interface” is not defined in the asserted patents, and its ordinary meaning includes any interface that enables information to be passed between a human user and hardware or software components of a system. *See PowerOasis*, 2007 WL 962937, at *2. The district court’s construction is consistent with this ordinary meaning.

The Federal Circuit has repeatedly emphasized that claim construction must be consistent with the specification. *See Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.*, 90 F.3d 1576, 1582 (Fed. Cir. 1996) (citing *SRI Int’l v. Matsushita Elec. Corp.*, 775 F.2d 1107, 1121 (Fed. Cir. 1985)). The asserted patents’ specification describes a “customer interface” as an interface that enables information to be passed between a human user and hardware or software components of a system, without limiting the location of the interface. *See PowerOasis*, 2007 WL 962937, at *2. The district court’s construction is consistent with the specification and the plain meaning of the claim term.

III. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT THE ASSERTED CLAIMS ARE ANTICIPATED BY THE MOBILESTAR NETWORK**

The district court held that the asserted claims are anticipated by the MobileStar Network under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b). This holding is legally erroneous because the asserted claims are entitled to the benefit of the Original Application’s filing date, which antedates the MobileStar Network.

The Supreme Court has held that a patent is not invalid for anticipation unless the prior art discloses every limitation of the claimed invention, either expressly or inherently. *See In re Schreiber*, 128 U.S. 21, 26 (1888). The MobileStar Network does not disclose every limitation of the asserted claims, including the specific components recited in the claims. The district court’s holding to the contrary is legally erroneous.

The Federal Circuit has repeatedly emphasized that a patent is not invalid for anticipation unless the prior art discloses every limitation of the claimed invention. *See In re Donohue*, 766 F.2d 531, 533 (Fed. Cir. 1985) (citing *In re Paulsen*, 30 F.2d 1017, 1018 (C.C.P.A. 1929)). The MobileStar Network does not disclose every limitation of the asserted claims. The district court’s holding to the contrary is legally erroneous.
