ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act (RRA) and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (RUIA) grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final" decisions of the United States Railroad Retirement Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The Fifth Circuit below held that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application, relying on its own precedent in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003). However, Roberts was wrongly decided, and this Court should reverse.

The RRA and RUIA do not explicitly preclude review of the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim. In fact, the Supreme Court has held that a decision by an administrative agency not to reopen a prior claim is reviewable unless there is "clear and convincing evidence" of congressional intent to preclude review. See Shaughnessy v. Pedreiro, 349 U.S. 48, 51-52 (1955) (citing Leedom v. Kyne, 358 U.S. 184 (1958)). 

The Second Circuit has held that a decision by the Board not to reopen a prior claim is reviewable. Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254-55 (2d Cir. 1966). The Eighth Circuit has also taken this position. Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d is not good law as it was decided after our cutoff, but the Eighth Circuit cited and relied on Szostak in its decision in Sones' predecessor, see, e.g., Easley v. Finch, 431 F.2d 1351 (4th Cir. 1970) is also not good law, however, Lang v. Flemming, 283 F.2d 27 (5th Cir. 1960) is. Lang is not directly on point, but it does show that preclusion of review is disfavored. 

Other circuits have also taken a broad view of reviewability under similar statutory schemes. See, e.g., Cappadora v. Celebrezze, 356 F.2d 1, 4-5 (2d Cir. 1966) (holding that a decision by the Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare not to reopen a prior claim is reviewable); cf. Estep v. United States, 327 U.S. 114, 122-23 (1946) (holding that a decision by a local draft board is reviewable).

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has emphasized the importance of judicial review in ensuring that administrative agencies act within their authority. See, e.g., Stark v. Wickard, 321 U.S. 288, 309-10 (1944) (holding that a decision by the Secretary of Agriculture is reviewable); Shields v. Utah Idaho Cent. R. Co., 305 U.S. 177, 183-84 (1938) (holding that a decision by the Interstate Commerce Commission is reviewable).

In light of these authorities, it is clear that the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is reviewable. The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts was incorrect, and this Court should reverse.

The Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's claim is a final decision that is subject to judicial review. As the Supreme Court has held, "the finality requirement is to be interpreted in a pragmatic way." Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136, 149 (1967). The Board's decision is a final determination that affects Salinas's rights, and it is therefore reviewable.

In conclusion, the decision by the Board denying Salinas's request to reopen his prior benefits claim is a "final decision" subject to judicial review. This Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision and hold that the Board's decision is reviewable.
