The Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment secures to all persons "the equal protection of the laws." U.S. Const. amend. XIV. It "is essentially a direction that all persons similarly situated should be treated alike." It does not create a cause of action for an individual who alleges only that government singled out that person for different treatment without reference to any class or group. 

This Court has long held that the Clause addresses legislative classifications that burden or benefit identifiable groups. In Barbier v. Connolly, 113 U.S. 27, 31 (1885), the Court explained that the Clause "is not a prohibition of all classification" but instead forbids "class legislation" that discriminates against "persons in like situation." See also Soon Hing v. Crowley, 113 U.S. 703, 710 (1885) (same). The Clause therefore presupposes membership in a class or group whose members are treated differently from others similarly situated. Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356, 373-74 (1886); Plessy v. Ferguson, 163 U.S. 537, 551-52 (1896).

A plaintiff who fails to allege any such class membership states no claim. The Clause "does not require things which are different in fact or opinion to be treated in law as though they were the same." Tigner v. Texas, 310 U.S. 141, 147 (1940). It reaches only "purposeful discrimination between persons or classes of persons." Snowden v. Hughes, 321 U.S. 1, 8 (1944). Absent an allegation that the plaintiff belongs to a class singled out for adverse treatment, no equal-protection violation can be shown. See also Kotch v. Board of River Port Pilot Comm'rs, 330 U.S. 552, 556 (1947) (requiring demonstration that the law "discriminates against a class").

Rational-basis review likewise presupposes a classification. A classification survives unless it rests on grounds "wholly irrelevant to the achievement of the State's objective." McGowan v. Maryland, 366 U.S. 420, 425 (1961). That inquiry cannot proceed when no classification affecting a class has been alleged. Railway Express Agency, Inc. v. New York, 336 U.S. 106, 110 (1949); Williamson v. Lee Optical of Oklahoma, Inc., 348 U.S. 483, 489 (1955). 

The complaint here alleges only that the Village demanded a larger easement from the Olechs than from other property owners. No class or group is identified. Under the authorities above, such an allegation falls outside the Clause's protection. The district court therefore correctly dismissed the action.
