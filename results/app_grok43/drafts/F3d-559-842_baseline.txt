The district court erred in granting summary judgment. Genuine issues of material fact exist as to whether Rand’s violations of TILA and HOEPA extended the Mouas’ rescission period to three years under 15 U.S.C. § 1635(f).

Rand failed to furnish the three-day advance HOEPA disclosure required by 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b)(1). The Mouas received no such statement before the April 22, 2005 closing. This omission constitutes a material disclosure failure that triggers the extended rescission right. See Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, 523 U.S. 410, 411 (1998) (TILA permits rescission “until midnight of the third business day following the consummation of the transaction or the delivery of the information and rescission forms required under this section together with a statement containing the material disclosures required under this subchapter, whichever is later,” and the right expires after three years if disclosures are never made).

The purported waiver of that advance period is ineffective. The handwritten statement Manisy Moua copied at closing does not establish a bona fide personal financial emergency under 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(e). Rand’s asserted urgency arose solely from its own delay in obtaining payoff figures, not from any sudden change in the Mouas’ circumstances. See id. (waiver permitted only when “the consumer determines that the extension of credit is needed to meet a bona fide personal financial emergency”).

Rand also failed to provide clear and conspicuous notice of the right to cancel. The Mouas signed both the receipt and confirmation sections of the Notice of Right to Cancel simultaneously on April 22, 2005—before the three-day period had run. This simultaneous execution rendered the notice defective. See 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a); see also Palmer v. Champion Mortg., 465 F.3d 24, 28 (1st Cir. 2006) (notice must “clearly and conspicuously” disclose the rescission deadline so that the consumer understands the period has not yet expired).

Finally, the loan contained a prepayment penalty prohibited by HOEPA. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c)(1). That violation independently extends the rescission period. See 15 U.S.C. § 1635(f).

Because these violations are supported by the record and create triable issues, summary judgment was improper. The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for further proceedings.
