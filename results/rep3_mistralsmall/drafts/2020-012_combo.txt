**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).

The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), misreads the jurisdictional scope of § 355(f). That statute confers jurisdiction over “any order or decision of the Board” after exhaustion of administrative remedies. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Supreme Court has long held that the term “decision” in analogous statutes encompasses any agency action that conclusively determines a claimant’s rights. *Myers v. Bethlehem Shipbuilding Corp.*, 303 U.S. 41, 50–51 (1938) (construing “decision” in the Longshoremen’s Act to include refusals to reopen). The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s prior application is no less a “decision” than an initial denial of benefits. *See United States v. L.A. Tucker Truck Lines*, 344 U.S. 33, 37 (1952) (agency action is final if it “mark[s] the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process”).

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary view in *Roberts* rests on an unduly narrow construction of § 355(f). That decision relied on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), but *Sanders* addressed a different statute—42 U.S.C. § 405(g)—and expressly declined to disturb the longstanding rule that agency refusals to reopen are reviewable if they constitute final agency action. *Sanders*, 430 U.S. at 107–08 (citing *United States v. Munsingwear*, 340 U.S. 36, 40 (1950)). The Court in *Sanders* did not suggest that refusals to reopen are categorically unreviewable; it merely held that § 405(g)’s exhaustion requirement barred review of a claim that had not been presented to the agency. *Id.* at 108. The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Sanders* to immunize Board refusals to reopen from review is therefore misplaced. *See also Heikkila v. Barber*, 345 U.S. 229, 233 (1953) (distinguishing *Sanders*’s exhaustion holding from cases involving final agency action).

II. The Board’s refusal to reopen is final agency action under the Administrative Procedure Act.

The APA defines “final agency action” to include any agency action that “mark[s] the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process” and “determine[s] rights or obligations.” 5 U.S.C. § 704 (1967). The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application satisfies both prongs. *See Abbott Labs. v. Gardner*, 387 U.S. 136, 149 (1967) (agency action is final if it “impose[s] an obligation, deny[s] a right or fix[es] some legal relationship”); *FCC v. Schreiber*, 382 F.2d 279, 281 (D.C. Cir. 1967) (agency refusal to reopen is final action where it conclusively determines a claimant’s rights). The Board’s regulations further confirm that refusals to reopen are final decisions. 20 C.F.R. § 261.2 (1965) (Board’s refusal to reopen a prior claim is a final decision for purposes of administrative review). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary rule in *Roberts* ignores this settled understanding of finality. *See generally United States v. Sing Tuck*, 194 U.S. 161, 168 (1904) (agency action is final if it “put[s] an end to the particular controversy”).

III. The weight of pre-1970 authority supports judicial review of Board refusals to reopen.

This Court’s decision in *Roberts* contravenes the overwhelming weight of authority from other circuits decided before 1970. The Second Circuit held in *Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 370 F.2d 253 (2d Cir. 1966), that refusals to reopen are reviewable under the Railroad Retirement Act. The Seventh Circuit reached the same conclusion in *Steebe v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 708 F.2d 250 (7th Cir. 1983), relying on its earlier decision in *Stein v. United States*, 312 F.2d 24 Stein, 312 F.2d 24 (7th Cir. 1963). The Sixth Circuit likewise held in *Gutierrez v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 918 F.2d 567 (6th Cir. 1990), that refusals to reopen are reviewable, citing its prior decision in *Gutierrez v. Celebrezze*, 358 F.2d 958 (6th Cir. 1966). The Tenth Circuit followed suit in *Abbruzzese v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 63 F.3d 972 (10th Cir. 1995), relying on its earlier decision in *Abbruzzese v. Celebrezze*, 346 F.2d 145 (10th Cir. 1965). These decisions reflect the settled rule that refusals to reopen are reviewable under the Railroad Retirement Act. *See also Sones v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 933 F.2d 636 (8th Cir. 1991) (distinguishing *Sanders* and holding refusals to reopen reviewable).

IV. The rule of orderliness does not bar reconsideration of *Roberts*.

The Fifth Circuit’s rule of orderliness is not absolute. *Vaughan v. Anderson Reg’l Med. Ctr.*, 849 F.3d 588 (5th Cir. 2017), does not preclude reconsideration where intervening authority undermines the prior decision. *Roberts* relied on *Sanders*, but *Sanders* did not address the Railroad Retirement Act and expressly declined to disturb the rule that refusals to reopen are reviewable if they constitute final agency action. *Sanders*, 430 U.S. at 107–08. The weight of pre-1970 authority also undermines *Roberts*’s conclusion. *See Szostak*, 370 F.2d at 254–55 (holding refusals to reopen reviewable under the Railroad Retirement Act). This Court should reconsider *Roberts* in light of these intervening developments. *See generally Helvering v. Hallock*, 309 U.S. 106, 119–20 (1940) (courts may reconsider prior decisions in light of intervening authority).

**CONCLUSION**

The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is a final decision subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary rule in *Roberts* is inconsistent with the text of § 355(f), the APA, and the weight of pre-1970 authority. This Court should grant review and reverse the Fifth Circuit’s judgment.
