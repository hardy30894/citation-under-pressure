**ARGUMENT**

The Civil Service Reform Act channels judicial review of Merit Systems Protection Board decisions to the Federal Circuit, 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(1)(A), except for “mixed cases” alleging both an appealable adverse action and prohibited discrimination, which “shall be filed in district court,” Kloeckner v. Solis, 568 U.S. 41, 604 (2012). A mixed case exists when an employee “has been affected by an action which [she] may appeal to the Merit Systems Protection Board” and “alleges that a basis for the action was discrimination prohibited by” enumerated statutes. 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1). Kloeckner held that this exception applies whenever the Board dismisses a mixed-case appeal on procedural grounds without reaching the merits of the discrimination claim. 568 U.S. at 600, 602–03.

The same rule governs jurisdictional dismissals. The statutory text turns on whether the employee was “affected by an action which [she] may appeal to the” Board, not on the ground the Board ultimately invokes to dispose of the appeal. Id. at 604. In both procedural and jurisdictional settings, the employee presents a claim that the action was appealable and that discrimination supplied a basis for it. The Board’s conclusion that the action was voluntary simply resolves one element of the mixed-case inquiry; it does not convert the case into a non-mixed appeal reviewable only in the Federal Circuit.

Kloeckner rejected any merits-procedure distinction for determining the proper forum. The Court observed that “two sections of the CSRA, read naturally, direct employees like Kloeckner to district court.” Id. at 604. Nothing in the text or structure of §§ 7702 and 7703 supports carving out jurisdictional dismissals. The Court was aware of decisions suggesting such a carve-out yet framed its holding solely in terms of “procedural grounds,” confirming that the statutory command is indifferent to the label the Board attaches to its dismissal. Id. at 603 & n.3.

Elgin v. Department of the Treasury, 567 U.S. 1 (2012), reinforces this conclusion. There the Court noted that the Federal Circuit’s exclusive jurisdiction under § 7703(b)(1) applies to cases “the Board has authority to adjudicate.” Id. at 12. When the Board determines it lacks jurisdiction, the case never enters that exclusive channel; instead, the employee’s right to district-court review of the discrimination allegation remains intact under § 7703(b)(2).

The contrary rule of Powell v. Department of Defense, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), cannot survive Kloeckner. Powell treated jurisdictional dismissals as categorically non-mixed, but that premise is incompatible with Kloeckner’s textual analysis. Because the Board’s jurisdictional ruling does not alter the employee’s statutory right to present a mixed case, review lies in district court.

Petitioner’s petition should therefore be transferred to the United States District Court for the District of Columbia.
