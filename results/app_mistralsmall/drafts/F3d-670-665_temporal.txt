**ARGUMENT**

I. **Equitable Tolling is Warranted Where a Petitioner Presents a Credible Claim of Actual Innocence**

This Court has long recognized that “a credible showing of actual innocence” may warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA’s statute of limitations. *See, e.g., Schlup v. Delo*, 513 U.S. 298, 324 (1995) (citing *Townsend v. Sain*, 372 U.S. 293, 317 (1963)). The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized that actual innocence is a “gateway” through which a petitioner may pass to have otherwise untimely claims heard. *See, e.g., Murray v. Carrier*, 477 U.S. 478, 496 (1986) (citing *Townsend*, 372 U.S. at 317). The Sixth Circuit has followed this principle, holding that “a credible claim of actual innocence” is sufficient to justify equitable tolling. *See, e.g., Doe v. Million*, 342 F.3d 440, 445 (6th Cir. 2003) (citing *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 324).

The Warden’s reliance on *Pace v. DiGuglielmo*, 544 U.S. 408 (2005), is misplaced. *Pace* does not alter the rule that actual innocence alone may warrant equitable tolling. Rather, *Pace* merely clarified that a petitioner must demonstrate diligence in pursuing his claims—a separate and distinct requirement from the showing of actual innocence. *Id.* at 419 (citing *Barefoot v. Estelle*, 463 U.S. 880, 893 (1983)). The Warden conflates these two requirements, erroneously suggesting that diligence is a prerequisite to establishing actual innocence. This Court has rejected that argument. *See, e.g., Doe*, 342 F.3d at 445.

II. **Perkins Has Presented a Credible Claim of Actual Innocence**

Perkins has submitted three affidavits that, if credited, establish that Jones—not Perkins—stabbed Henderson. These affidavits allege that Jones bragged about the killing, was seen with bloodstained clothing, and sought to clean bloodstained garments. These allegations directly contradict Jones’s trial testimony and implicate Jones as the true perpetrator. *See, e.g., Townsend*, 372 U.S. at 317 (holding that newly discovered evidence that “strongly suggests” a petitioner’s innocence may warrant equitable tolling).

The Warden argues that these affidavits were “substantially available” at trial and thus do not constitute newly discovered evidence. However, the affidavits were signed years before trial—Hudson’s in 1997, Louis’s in 1999, and Fleming’s in 2002—yet Perkins could not have reasonably discovered their contents earlier. *See, e.g., Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 327 (noting that the “focus” is on whether the evidence “could have been discovered through the exercise of due diligence”). The district court erred in discounting these affidavits as merely corroborative of Perkins’s trial theory. *See, e.g., Townsend*, 372 U.S. at 317 (recognizing that newly discovered evidence need not be “conclusive” to warrant relief).

III. **Reasonable Diligence is Not a Prerequisite to Establishing Actual Innocence**

The Warden’s argument that Perkins must demonstrate diligence to rely on actual innocence misreads the law. The Supreme Court has distinguished between the requirements for equitable tolling based on actual innocence and those based on other grounds. *See, e.g., Murray*, 477 U.S. at 496 (citing *Townsend*, 372 U.S. at 317). Actual innocence alone suffices to trigger equitable tolling; diligence is only required when a petitioner seeks to excuse untimeliness on other grounds. *See, e.g., Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 324.

This Court has followed this distinction. *See, e.g., Doe*, 342 F.3d at 445. The Warden’s reliance on *Pace* is unavailing because *Pace* addressed diligence in the context of a petitioner’s failure to exhaust state remedies—not in the context of an actual innocence claim. *See Pace*, 544 U.S. at 419. Perkins’s diligence (or lack thereof) is irrelevant to whether his actual innocence claim warrants equitable tolling.

**CONCLUSION**

Perkins has presented a credible claim of actual innocence, and the district court erred in denying equitable tolling on that basis. The judgment below should be reversed, and this case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
