The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) regulates the conduct of "debt collectors," not creditors. 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). The statute defines a "debt collector" as a person who regularly collects or attempts to collect debts owed or due another. Id. The Fourth Circuit correctly held that Santander Consumer USA, Inc. (Santander) is not a "debt collector" under the FDCPA because it was collecting debts on its own behalf, not on behalf of another.

The petitioners argue that Santander is a "debt collector" because it acquired their debts after they had defaulted. However, the FDCPA's definition of "debt collector" is not solely determined by the status of the debt at the time of acquisition. Rather, it is determined by whether the person is collecting debts on behalf of others or for its own account. See Heintz v. Jenkins, 514 U.S. 291, 293 (1995) (quoting 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6)).

The Fourth Circuit correctly applied this principle, holding that Santander was not a "debt collector" because it was collecting debts on its own behalf after purchasing them from CitiFinancial Auto. Henson v. Santander Consumer USA, Inc., 817 F.3d 131, 136-37 (4th Cir. 2016). The court noted that the complaint did not allege that Santander was collecting debts on behalf of CitiFinancial Auto, but rather on its own behalf after purchasing the debts. Id. at 137.

The petitioners' reliance on the exclusion from the definition of "creditor" in 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(4) is misplaced. That exclusion applies only to a person who receives a debt in default "solely for the purpose of facilitating collection of such debt for another." Id. (emphasis added). Since Santander purchased the debts for its own account, not solely for the purpose of collecting for another, it does not fall within this exclusion.

Furthermore, the petitioners' interpretation would lead to an absurd result, where a company like Santander, which engages in various financial activities, including lending and collecting debts for itself, would be subject to the FDCPA for all its collection activities simply because it also collects debts for others. See Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A., 797 F.3d 1309, 1315-16 (11th Cir. 2015).

The Fourth Circuit's decision is consistent with the FDCPA's purpose to regulate abusive debt collection practices by debt collectors. 15 U.S.C. § 1692(e). Santander, as a creditor collecting its own debts, is not subject to the FDCPA. The judgment of the Fourth Circuit should be affirmed.
