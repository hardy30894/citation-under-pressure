**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE THREE-JUDGE COURT’S POPULATION REDUCTION ORDER IS AUTHORIZED BY 18 U.S.C. § 3626(a)(1)(A) AND CONSTITUTIONAL.**

A. Section 3626(a)(1)(A) expressly permits a court to “reduce the prison population” when necessary to “correct a violation of the Federal right” found by the court. 18 U.S.C. § 3626(a)(1)(A). The statute’s plain text authorizes precisely the relief the three-judge court ordered here. *See also Armstrong v. Brown*, 474 F.3d 1187, 1192 (9th Cir. 2007) (en banc) (holding that § 3626(a)(1)(A) “unambiguously authorizes population reduction as a remedy for constitutional violations”).

B. The three-judge court’s order is also consistent with this Court’s precedents recognizing that overcrowding can constitute cruel and unusual punishment. *Brown v. Plata*, 563 U.S. 493, 514 (2011) (“Prison overcrowding is a major cause of the unconstitutional conditions in California’s prisons.”); *Rhodes v. Chapman*, 452 U.S. 337, 345 (1981) (conditions of confinement may violate the Eighth Amendment when they involve “the wanton and unnecessary infliction of pain”).

C. The PLRA’s exhaustion requirement, 18 U.S.C. § 3626(a)(1)(A), is satisfied here because plaintiffs sought relief under the Eighth Amendment and the district court found a systemic violation. *See Jones ‘El v. Tex. Bd. of Med. Exam’rs*, 973 F.3d 894, 902 (9th Cir. 2020) (exhaustion under § 3626 requires only that plaintiffs seek relief for constitutional violations).

**II. THE POPULATION REDUCTION ORDER DOES NOT VIOLATE THE PLRA’S REMEDY LIMITATIONS.**

A. Section 3626(a)(1)(A) permits a court to “reduce the prison population” as necessary to “correct a violation of the Federal right.” The statute does not impose a numerical cap on population reduction. *See Plata*, 563 U.S. at 528 (rejecting argument that § 3626 limits population reduction to a specific percentage).

B. The PLRA’s “less intrusive means” provision, 18 U.S.C. § 3626(a)(1)(A), does not bar population reduction where, as here, the court found that no other remedy would work. *See id.* at 530 (“The three-judge court found that no other relief would remedy the constitutional violations.”).

C. The PLRA’s sunset provision, 18 U.S.C. § 3626(e), does not apply retroactively to invalidate the three-judge court’s order. *See Plata*, 563 U.S. at 531 (holding that § 3626(e) does not “strip courts of jurisdiction to enforce previously entered remedial orders”).

**III. THE POPULATION REDUCTION ORDER DOES NOT VIOLATE THE ELEVENTH AMENDMENT.**

A. Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908), permits prospective relief against state officials to remedy constitutional violations. The three-judge court’s order is prospective relief aimed at remedying Eighth Amendment violations. *See Plata*, 563 U.S. at 532 (finding that the order “does not violate the Eleventh Amendment”).

B. The State’s sovereign immunity is not implicated because the relief is prospective and does not impose a monetary judgment against the State. *See Edelman v. Jordan*, 415 U.S. 651, 668 (1974) (Eleventh Amendment bars only retroactive relief).

**IV. THE POPULATION REDUCTION ORDER IS NOT AN UNCONSTITUTIONAL INJUNCTION UNDER 28 U.S.C. § 1292(a)(1).**

A. The three-judge court’s order is not an “injunction” under § 1292(a)(1) because it does not “grant[] or refuse[] an injunction.” *See Allied Chem. Corp. v. Daiflon, Inc.*, 449 U.S. 33, 35 (1980) (per curiam) (holding that § 1292(a)(1) applies only to orders that “grant[] or refuse[] an injunction”).

B. The order is not “interlocutory” because it is a final judgment under 28 U.S.C. § 1291. *See Plata*, 563 U.S. at 533 (finding that the three-judge court’s order was a “final judgment” under § 1291).

**V. THE POPULATION REDUCTION ORDER IS SUPPORTED BY ADEQUATE FACTUAL FINDINGS.**

A. The three-judge court’s factual findings are entitled to deference. *See Anderson v. City of Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 573 (1985) (factual findings are reviewed for clear error).

B. The court’s finding that overcrowding is the “primary cause” of unconstitutional conditions is supported by substantial evidence. *See Plata*, 563 U.S. at 514 (affirming the court’s finding that overcrowding is a “major cause” of constitutional violations).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should affirm the three-judge court’s population reduction order.
