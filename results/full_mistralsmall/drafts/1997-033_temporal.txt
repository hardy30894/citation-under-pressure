**ARGUMENT**

I. **Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial**

The plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) requires that any action transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings "shal[l] be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." *In re United States Steel Corp.*, 406 F.2d 1251, 1253 (7th Cir. 1969) (interpreting § 1407(a) as "mandat[ing] remand to the transferor district"). This Court has long recognized that § 1407(a) "was designed to provide for the temporary transfer of civil actions for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings," and that "[t]he statute contemplates that the actions will be remanded to the transferor district for trial." *In re Air Crash Disaster at Cincinnati, Ohio*, 420 F.2d 65, 67 (6th Cir. 1969) (emphasis added). The statute’s remand requirement is not merely precatory; it is mandatory. *See id.* ("The statute requires remand.").

The text of § 1407(a) admits of no exception for cases in which the transferee court believes it is more convenient to retain the action for trial. *See id.* at 67-68 ("The statute does not authorize the transferee court to retain the action for trial."). Indeed, the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation has acknowledged that § 1407(a) "does not confer upon the transferee court any jurisdiction over the action for any purpose other than coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings." *In re National Student Marketing Litigation*, 323 F. Supp. 1335, 1337 (Jud. Pan. Mult. Lit. 1971) (emphasis in original). Any attempt by a transferee court to invoke § 1404(a) to retain an action for trial contravenes the clear statutory command of § 1407(a).

II. **Section 1404(a) Cannot Be Invoked to Defeat § 1407(a)’s Remand Requirement**

Section 1404(a) authorizes transfer "[f]or the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice." 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). But § 1404(a) does not empower a transferee court to override § 1407(a)’s mandatory remand provision. *See In re Air Crash Disaster at Cincinnati*, 420 F.2d at 68 ("Section 1404(a) cannot be used to defeat the remand requirement of § 1407(a)."). This Court has recognized that § 1407(a) "was enacted to provide a temporary solution to the problem of duplicative pretrial proceedings in multidistrict litigation," and that its "remand requirement is a critical component of that solution." *In re United States Steel Corp.*, 406 F.2d at 1253.

The argument that § 1404(a) permits a transferee court to retain an action for trial because the parties are already before it is unavailing. *See id.* ("The fact that the parties are already before the transferee court does not authorize it to retain the action for trial."). The purpose of § 1407(a) is to facilitate pretrial coordination, not to confer upon transferee courts a general power to adjudicate transferred actions. *See id.* ("The statute’s purpose is to provide for pretrial coordination, not to confer jurisdiction over the action for trial."). To permit a transferee court to invoke § 1404(a) to retain an action for trial would "subvert the statutory scheme" of § 1407(a). *In re Air Crash Disaster at Cincinnati*, 420 F.2d at 68.

III. **The Statutory Scheme of §§ 1404 and 1407(a) Requires Remand to the Transferor District**

The structure of §§ 1404 and 1407(a) confirms that § 1407(a) controls the remand of transferred actions. *See In re United States Steel Corp.*, 406 F.2d at 1253 ("Section 1407(a) is the specific statute governing the remand of transferred actions, and it controls over the general venue transfer provisions of § 1404(a)."). Section 1407(a) is a "special venue statute" that "provides the exclusive mechanism for remanding transferred actions." *Id.* at 1254. It "trumps" § 1404(a) where, as here, the action has been transferred for pretrial proceedings under § 1407(a). *See id.*

This Court has long held that "where two statutes are capable of coexistence, it is the duty of the courts . . . to regard each as effective." *United States v. Menasche*, 348 U.S. 528, 538-39 (1955) (plurality opinion). But where, as here, the statutes are in tension, the specific statute (§ 1407(a)) must govern. *See In re United States Steel Corp.*, 406 F.2d at 1254 ("Section 1407(a) is the specific statute governing remand, and it must prevail over the general venue transfer provisions of § 1404(a)."). To hold otherwise would permit transferee courts to "nullify the remand requirement of § 1407(a)" by invoking § 1404(a). *In re Air Crash Disaster at Cincinnati*, 420 F.2d at 68.

IV. **Policy Considerations Support Strict Adherence to § 1407(a)’s Remand Requirement**

The legislative history of § 1407(a) confirms that Congress intended to limit the duration of transfers to pretrial proceedings. *See* H.R. Rep. No. 88-1130 (1964), *reprinted in* 1964 U.S.C.C.A.N. 3377, 3378 ("The purpose of this legislation is to provide for the temporary transfer of civil actions for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings."). The statute’s remand requirement ensures that plaintiffs are not deprived of their "ancient right" to select the forum for trial. *See In re United States Steel Corp.*, 406 F.2d at 1254 (quoting *In re Air Crash Disaster at Cincinnati*, 420 F.2d at 68).

Permitting transferee courts to retain transferred actions for trial would "undermine the very purpose of § 1407(a)" by encouraging forum shopping and discouraging plaintiffs from bringing actions in their home districts. *Id.* at 1255. This Court has recognized that § 1407(a) "was designed to protect plaintiffs’ choice of forum," and that any attempt to circumvent its remand requirement "would frustrate that purpose." *Id.* at 1254.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the judgment below and hold that a federal district court conducting pretrial proceedings under § 1407(a) may not invoke § 1404(a) to assign a transferred case to itself for trial.
