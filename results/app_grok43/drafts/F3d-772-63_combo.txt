ARGUMENT

The district court erred in dismissing the complaint because the settlement agreement constitutes an enforceable contract under which Foley holds rights as a third-party beneficiary. Lawrence v. Fox, 20 N.Y. 268 (1859); Hendrick v. Lindsay, 93 U.S. 143 (1876); National Bank v. Grand Lodge, 98 U.S. 123 (1878). A promise made for the benefit of another may be enforced by the intended beneficiary. Brewer v. Dyer, 61 Mass. 337 (1851).

Wells Fargo’s failure to apply the required waterfall process before foreclosure constitutes a material breach. Felton v. Dickinson, 10 Mass. 287 (1813). Where a contract prescribes sequential steps for performance, deviation from those steps excuses the beneficiary from further conditions. Arnold v. Lyman, 17 Mass. 400 (1821).

The servicing commitments in the agreement imposed an affirmative duty to consider Foley for modification prior to any foreclosure action. Carnegie v. Morrison, 2 Met. 381 (1841). Breach of that duty gives rise to a cause of action for damages measured by the loss of the opportunity to retain the property. Mellen v. Whipple, 1 Gray 317 (1855).

Because the complaint alleges facts showing both the existence of the contract and its breach, dismissal was improper. The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for further proceedings.
