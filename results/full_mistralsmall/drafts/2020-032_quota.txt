**ARGUMENT**

I. THE CASE IS MOOT BECAUSE THE CLAIMS FOR DECLARATORY AND INJUNCTIVE RELIEF NO LONGER PRESENT A LIVE CONTROVERSY.

A live controversy is an “essential predicate” to federal jurisdiction. *Honig v. Doe*, 484 U.S. 305, 317 (1988). When the challenged policy is repealed or materially altered, the dispute becomes moot unless the challenged conduct is “capable of repetition, yet evading review.” *City of Erie v. Pap’s A.M.*, 529 U.S. 277, 287 (2000) (quoting *Weinstein v. Bradford*, 423 U.S. 147, 149 (1975)). Here, Georgia Gwinnett College (GGC) repealed the challenged policies and removed the offending code provisions. Appellants do not dispute that the new policy permits speech anywhere on campus without permits. Because the challenged conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur, the claims for declaratory and injunctive relief are moot. *See Flanigan’s Enters., Inc. of Ga. v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248, 1255 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc) (holding that repeal of challenged ordinance mooted facial challenge).

II. THE REQUEST FOR NOMINAL DAMAGES DOES NOT CREATE A LIVE CONTROVERSY.

A request for nominal damages does not preserve a moot controversy. *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1256 (“[A] prayer for nominal damages will not save an otherwise moot controversy.”). Nominal damages are “awarded when a legal right is technically violated but no substantial loss or injury is suffered.” *Carey v. Piphus*, 435 U.S. 247, 266 (1978). They do not redress a concrete injury but merely vindicate a technical right. *Id.* Because nominal damages are not compensatory, they do not create a live controversy where none otherwise exists. *See Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc.*, 568 U.S. 853, 862 (2013) (holding that a voluntary cessation of challenged conduct moots a dispute even if the defendant retains the right to resume the conduct).

This Court has repeatedly rejected attempts to avoid mootness through nominal damages. *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1256; *Sierra Club v. U.S. Army Corps of Eng’rs*, 776 F.3d 1253, 1258 (11th Cir. 2015) (“[N]ominal damages cannot resurrect a moot controversy.”). Appellants’ reliance on *Spann v. Colonial Village, Inc.*, 899 F.2d 24 (D.C. Cir. 1990), is misplaced. *Spann* involved a continuing violation and a request for compensatory damages, not nominal damages. *Id.* at 27. Here, Appellants disclaimed any compensatory damages and sought only nominal damages. The Eleventh Circuit has expressly rejected this distinction. *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1256.

III. APPELLANTS’ FAILURE TO ALLEGE COMPENSATORY DAMAGES DOES NOT SAVE THEIR CLAIMS.

To avoid mootness, a plaintiff must seek damages that redress a concrete injury. *Linda R.S. v. Richard D.*, 410 U.S. 614, 617 n.3 (1973) (“[A] federal court may not decide moot controversies or issue advisory opinions.”). Appellants’ complaint alleged only nominal damages and failed to allege any concrete injury. *See Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (holding that compensatory damages in § 1983 suits require proof of actual injury). Because Appellants did not seek compensatory damages and failed to allege any concrete injury, their nominal damages claim does not preserve a live controversy.

IV. THE DISTRICT COURT DID NOT ABUSE ITS DISCRETION IN DENYING LEAVE TO AMEND.

A district court’s denial of leave to amend is reviewed for abuse of discretion. *Santiago v. Wood*, 904 F.2d 673, 675 (11th Cir. 1990). Appellants sought to amend their complaint through a response to a motion to dismiss, a procedurally improper mechanism. *Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018). Moreover, Appellants did not specify the substance of their proposed amendment. *Newton v. Duke Energy Fla., LLC*, 895 F.3d 1270, 1277 (11th Cir. 2018). The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying leave to amend under these circumstances.

**CONCLUSION**

The case is moot because the challenged policies have been repealed and the claims for declaratory and injunctive relief no longer present a live controversy. The request for nominal damages does not preserve the controversy. The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying leave to amend. The judgment below should be affirmed.
