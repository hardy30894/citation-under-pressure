ARGUMENT

Section 523(a)(2)(A) excepts from discharge only “any debt … for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by … actual fraud.” The treble damages awarded here were not “obtained by” fraud; they were imposed by statute after the fact. Neal v. Clark, 95 U.S. 704, 709 (1878), construed the parallel provision of the 1867 Act to reach only “positive fraud, or fraud in fact, involving moral turpitude or intentional wrong,” not “implied fraud, or fraud in law.” The same limitation applies here. Chapman v. Forsyth & Limerick, 43 U.S. 202, 208 (1844), likewise held that a discharge bars even provable fiduciary claims once the statutory requirements are met.

The phrase “to the extent obtained by” confines nondischargeability to the sum actually received through the misrepresentation. Moore v. Bay, 284 U.S. 4, 5 (1931), emphasized that the trustee’s rights are measured strictly by the Bankruptcy Act, not by later state-law enhancements. Bruning v. United States, 376 U.S. 358, 361 (1964), confirmed that post-petition accruals are not part of the provable debt. Treble damages, like the post-petition interest disallowed in Bruning, arise only after the fraudulent act and therefore fall outside the statutory exception.

Miller v. Irving Trust Co., 296 U.S. 256, 260 (1935), and Miller v. Guasti, 226 U.S. 170, 172 (1912), illustrate that a discharge releases all provable claims unless the Act itself creates an exception. Scott v. Ellery, 142 U.S. 381, 384 (1892), held that once a creditor participates in the bankruptcy proceeding, the discharge bars further collection. Bluthenthal v. Jones, 208 U.S. 64, 66 (1908), reached the same result: a discharge granted after proper proceedings extinguishes the debt.

Missouri State Life Insurance Co. v. Jones, 290 U.S. 199, 202 (1933), and United States v. Johnson, 319 U.S. 302, 305 (1943), underscore that statutory additions labeled “damages” remain subject to the limits of federal bankruptcy law. Because the treble award was not money “obtained by” fraud, it is dischargeable. The judgment below should be reversed.
