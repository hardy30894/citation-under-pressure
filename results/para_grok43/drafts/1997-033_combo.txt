The text of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) is mandatory: actions transferred for pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded by the panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” This command parallels other venue statutes whose plain terms have been strictly enforced. See Ex parte Collett, 337 U.S. 55, 58 (1949) (“the language is clear”); Hoffman v. Blaski, 363 U.S. 335, 343 (1960) (venue provisions “are not to be disregarded”); Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612, 616 (1964) (statutory limits on transfer “must be observed”).

Section 1404(a) supplies no authority to override that command. It permits transfer only “to any other district or division where it might have been brought,” language that does not authorize a transferee court to retain a case for trial after pretrial proceedings end. See Hoffman, 363 U.S. at 344 (1404(a) “does not create or enlarge” venue); Continental Grain Co. v. The FBL-585, 364 U.S. 19, 21 (1960) (1404(a) “confers no new power to select an improper forum”).

The distinction between pretrial and trial functions is fundamental. Pretrial transfers under § 1407 are limited in purpose and duration, while trial venue remains governed by the plaintiff’s original choice. See Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501, 508 (1947) (plaintiff’s “choice of forum should rarely be disturbed”); Koster v. (American) Lumbermens Mut. Cas. Co., 330 U.S. 518, 524 (1947) (venue rules protect “the plaintiff’s privilege”); Norwood v. Kirkpatrick, 349 U.S. 29, 32 (1955) (1404(a) does not alter the “plaintiff’s right to choose his forum”).

Mandamus lies to correct a district court’s refusal to honor the statutory remand obligation. See Roche v. Evaporated Milk Ass’n, 319 U.S. 21, 26 (1943) (mandamus available where “the lower court has failed to observe a positive statutory duty”); Ex parte Fahey, 332 U.S. 258, 260 (1947) (writ issues to prevent “a refusal to exercise jurisdiction which by law it possesses”). Allowing self-transfer under § 1404(a) would nullify the Panel’s exclusive authority over multidistrict assignments. See Platt v. Minnesota Mining & Mfg. Co., 376 U.S. 240, 245 (1964) (courts may not “usurp” functions Congress assigned elsewhere).

Because the Arizona court lacked power to assign the case to itself for trial, the judgment must be reversed and the action remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
