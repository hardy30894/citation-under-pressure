**ARGUMENT**

Section 103(c) of the Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act provides that “[n]o lands which, before, on, or after December 2, 1980, are conveyed to the State, to any Native Corporation, or to any private party shall be subject to the regulations applicable solely to public lands within such units.” 16 U.S.C. § 3103(c). The hovercraft prohibition, 36 C.F.R. § 2.17(e), applies to “the operation or use of hovercraft * * * within” all lands and waters administered by the National Park Service. It therefore is not a regulation “applicable solely to public lands.”

This Court has repeatedly instructed that statutory interpretation begins with the plain text. *Connecticut Nat’l Bank v. Germain*, 503 U.S. 249, 253-254 (1992). Where the language is unambiguous, “judicial inquiry is complete.” *Rubin v. United States*, 449 U.S. 424, 430 (1981). Section 103(c) exempts only regulations that apply exclusively to public lands. Because the hovercraft rule extends to every area within a park unit’s boundaries regardless of ownership, the exemption does not apply.

The same conclusion follows from the structure of ANILCA. Congress defined “conservation system unit” to include lands “irrespective of the ownership status of the lands.” *Sturgeon v. Frost*, 136 S. Ct. 1061, 1067 (2016) (quoting 16 U.S.C. § 3102(4)). The Service’s organic authority to promulgate “rules and regulations” for “the use of Federal areas known as national parks,” 54 U.S.C. § 100751(a), therefore operates on the unit as a whole. Section 103(c) carves out a narrow exception for rules that single out public lands; it does not withdraw the Service’s general regulatory power over the geographic area it administers.

Petitioner’s contrary reading would require the Service to maintain two separate regulatory regimes inside a single park unit—an outcome Congress did not enact. *See* 16 U.S.C. § 3207 (preserving existing federal regulatory authority). The Ninth Circuit correctly held that the hovercraft ban falls outside the limited exemption created by § 103(c). Its judgment should be affirmed.
