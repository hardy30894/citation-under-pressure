**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT’S DENIAL OF LAMB’S CHAPEL’S REQUEST VIOLATED THE FIRST AMENDMENT’S FREE SPEECH CLAUSE BECAUSE THE SCHOOL DISTRICT CREATED A LIMITED PUBLIC FORUM OPEN TO ALL SPEECH OF A SIMILAR CHARACTER.**

The Second Circuit erred in concluding that the school facilities were not a limited public forum open to religious speech. This Court has long recognized that when a government entity opens its property for public use, it creates a forum for expressive activity subject to heightened First Amendment scrutiny. *Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983). The District’s policy and practice of permitting community groups to use school facilities for a wide range of expressive activities—including lectures, films, and performances—demonstrates that it has created a limited public forum. *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Def. & Educ. Fund, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 788, 802 (1985).

The District’s refusal to allow Lamb’s Chapel to use the facilities for religious-oriented films while permitting secular groups to show films on similar topics constitutes impermissible viewpoint discrimination. *Rosenberger v. Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995). The Court has made clear that once a forum is opened to a class of speech, the government may not exclude speech based on its viewpoint. *Id.* at 829-30. The District’s policy of excluding religious speech while allowing secular speech on the same subject matter violates this principle.

**II. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY OF EXCLUDING RELIGIOUS SPEECH VIOLATES THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE.**

The District’s policy discriminates against religious speech in violation of the Equal Protection Clause. The Court has held that laws or policies that discriminate based on religious viewpoint are subject to strict scrutiny. *Church of Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah*, 508 U.S. 520, 542-43 (1993). The District’s policy fails strict scrutiny because it is not narrowly tailored to serve a compelling state interest. *Id.* at 546.

The District’s reliance on *Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist.*, 508 U.S. 384 (1993), is misplaced. In that case, the Court reversed the Second Circuit’s decision, holding that the District’s policy violated the Free Speech Clause. *Id.* at 394. The Court did not reach the Equal Protection Clause issue, but its reasoning compels the conclusion that the District’s policy is unconstitutional under the Equal Protection Clause as well.

**III. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY DOES NOT VIOLATE THE ESTABLISHMENT CLAUSE.**

The District argues that its policy is justified by the Establishment Clause. However, the Court has made clear that the Establishment Clause does not permit the suppression of religious speech in a public forum. *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 276 (1981). The District’s policy of excluding religious speech while allowing secular speech on the same subject matter is not neutral and does not advance a secular purpose. *Id.* at 272-73.

The District’s reliance on *Board of Educ. of the Westside Community Schs. v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226 (1990), is misplaced. In *Mergens*, the Court upheld the Equal Access Act, which requires schools to allow religious student groups to meet on the same terms as secular student groups. *Id.* at 247. The Court did not address the Establishment Clause issue, but its reasoning compels the conclusion that the District’s policy is unconstitutional under the Establishment Clause as well.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Second Circuit should be reversed. The District’s policy of excluding religious speech while allowing secular speech on the same subject matter violates the First Amendment’s Free Speech and Equal Protection Clauses. The District’s policy does not violate the Establishment Clause.
