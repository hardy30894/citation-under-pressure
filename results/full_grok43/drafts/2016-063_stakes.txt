**ARGUMENT**

The Civil Service Reform Act provides that judicial review of a Merit Systems Protection Board decision in a “mixed case” lies in federal district court. 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2). A mixed case exists when an employee “has been affected by an action which [she] may appeal to the Merit Systems Protection Board” and “alleges that a basis for the action was discrimination” prohibited by enumerated federal statutes. 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1). The Board’s dismissal of such a case on jurisdictional grounds does not remove it from the district-court review provision.

In *Kloeckner v. Solis*, 568 U.S. 41 (2012), the Court held that “mixed cases—those appealable to the MSPB and alleging discrimination—shall be filed in district court.” *Id.* at 50 (quoting 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2)). The Court reached this conclusion even though the Board had dismissed the appeal on a procedural ground without reaching the merits of the discrimination claim. *Id.* at 46. The statutory text, the Court explained, “does not distinguish between” dismissals on the merits and dismissals on other grounds; once the employee presents a qualifying mixed case, “judicial review lies where Congress said it lies.” *Id.* at 52, 55.

The same reasoning governs jurisdictional dismissals. The definition in § 7702(a)(1) turns on the employee’s allegations and the nature of the underlying action, not on whether the Board ultimately concludes it possesses jurisdiction. *See id.* at 50. Nothing in the text of §§ 7702 or 7703 creates an exception for jurisdictional rulings. As the Court observed in *Kloeckner*, “the Federal Circuit’s jurisdiction is the exception, not the rule, for mixed cases.” *Id.* at 52.

*Powell v. Department of Defense*, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), which held that jurisdictional dismissals belong in the Federal Circuit, is incompatible with *Kloeckner*. *Powell* rested on the premise that a jurisdictional dismissal means the case was never “appealable to the MSPB,” thereby taking it outside § 7702(a)(1). 158 F.3d at 599. *Kloeckner* rejected an analogous distinction between procedural and merits dismissals, confirming that the forum for review is determined by the character of the case presented to the Board, not by the Board’s disposition. 568 U.S. at 52-55. Because *Kloeckner* “effectively overrules” *Powell*, the latter precedent no longer controls. *See Dellums v. U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Comm’n*, 863 F.2d 968, 978 n.11 (D.C. Cir. 1988).

Uniform application of the district-court forum to all mixed-case dismissals also serves the statutory purpose of consolidating discrimination claims in a single court. *See Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 55. The decision below therefore erred in transferring the petition to the Federal Circuit.
