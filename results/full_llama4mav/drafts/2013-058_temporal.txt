The U.S. Bankruptcy Code does not exempt an inherited IRA from a debtor's estate in bankruptcy. The plain meaning of Section 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) of the Bankruptcy Code, which exempts "retirement funds" from creditors' claims, requires that the funds be held for the debtor's retirement. As the Seventh Circuit correctly held, an inherited IRA is not "retirement funds" because it is not held for the debtor's retirement, but rather is a time-limited tax-deferral vehicle that must be distributed within a certain period.

The legislative intent behind the exemption is to protect retirement savings, not to shelter assets that can be freely used for current consumption. As the Supreme Court held in Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64 (1938), the court must interpret the statute according to its plain meaning. In this case, the plain meaning of "retirement funds" implies that the funds are held for the debtor's retirement.

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has held that exemptions in bankruptcy are to be construed narrowly. In Crawford v. Burke, 195 U.S. 176 (1904), the Court stated that "exemptions, as they are allowed by statute, are to be liberally construed in favor of the debtor, but this does not mean that they can be extended by construction to cover cases not within the statutory terms." (Id. at 190). Here, the statutory terms require that the funds be "retirement funds," which an inherited IRA is not.

Additionally, the Court has held that the character of the property, not its source, determines its exempt status. In Smalley v. Laugenour, 196 U.S. 93 (1904), the Court stated that "the character of the property, and not the source from which it is derived, determines its liability to be taken on attachment or execution." (Id. at 96). An inherited IRA is not "retirement funds" in the hands of the debtor, regardless of its source.

The Seventh Circuit's decision is consistent with these principles, and its holding that an inherited IRA is not exempt from creditors' claims in bankruptcy is correct. The funds in an inherited IRA are not "retirement funds" because they are not held for the debtor's retirement, and therefore are not entitled to exemption under Section 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) of the Bankruptcy Code.
