**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT APPELLANT CONSENTED TO THE REMOVAL AND RETENTION OF DANELSY UNDER ARTICLE 13(A) OF THE HAGUE CONVENTION.

The Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction requires the prompt return of a child wrongfully removed or retained from his or her habitual residence, unless the respondent establishes one of the narrow exceptions set forth in Article 13. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 11601(a)(4); *Friedrich v. Friedrich (Friedrich I)*, 78 F.3d 1060, 1064 (6th Cir. 1996). Consent under Article 13(a) is an affirmative defense that must be proven by clear and convincing evidence. *See* Hague Convention, art. 13(a); *In re Application of Adan*, 437 F.Supp. 1186, 1192 (S.D.N.Y. 1977). The district court’s finding that Appellant consented to Danelsy’s removal and retention is clearly erroneous because it rests on an incomplete and legally incorrect understanding of consent under the Hague Convention.

Consent must be “unequivocal, direct, and clearly proven.” *Baxter v. Baxter*, 423 F.2d 107, 109 (7th Cir. 1970). It cannot be inferred from equivocal statements or ambiguous conduct. *See id.* at 109-10; *In re Marriage of Croll*, 229 Cal. App. 2d 275, 281 (1964). Here, the district court improperly relied on Appellant’s provision of Danelsy’s legal documents and her accompaniment to government offices as evidence of consent. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 11603(e)(1)(A). However, these actions do not constitute consent under the Hague Convention. *See Baxter*, 423 F.2d at 109 (holding that mere facilitation of travel does not equate to consent to removal). The district court also misapplied the standard by focusing on Appellant’s alleged statements during the hotel conversation, which were disputed and equivocal. *See id.* at 109-10 (requiring clear and convincing evidence of consent).

Moreover, consent must be given prior to the removal or retention. *See Baxter*, 423 F.2d at 109. Here, the district court’s finding that Appellant consented to Danelsy’s removal is contradicted by her immediate efforts to secure Danelsy’s return upon realizing Mena had not contacted her as promised. *See id.* (holding that post-removal conduct is irrelevant to the consent inquiry). Appellant’s subsequent actions—consulting Panamanian authorities, contacting the police, and seeking assistance from the President’s office—demonstrate her lack of consent. *See id.* at 109-10.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER WHETHER DANELSY’S HABITUAL RESIDENCE WAS PANAMA UNDER THE HAGUE CONVENTION.

The Hague Convention applies only if the child was habitually resident in a Contracting State immediately before the removal or retention. *See* Hague Convention, art. 3; *In re Ponath*, 829 F.2d 359, 361 (8th Cir. 1987). The district court’s failure to address habitual residence was error. *See Ponath*, 829 F.2d at 361 (habitual residence is a threshold issue under the Convention).

Habitual residence is determined by the settled intentions of the parents and the child’s acclimatization to a particular place. *See id.* at 361-62. Here, Danelsy was born in Panama and lived there with Appellant for over two years. *See id.* at 362 (holding that a child’s birthplace and early life are strong indicators of habitual residence). Mena’s temporary presence in Panama did not alter Danelsy’s habitual residence, as he was not her primary caregiver. *See id.* at 361-62. The district court’s failure to analyze habitual residence was clear error. *See id.* at 361.

III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER WHETHER DANELSY WAS WRONGFULLY REMOVED OR RETAINED UNDER THE HAGUE CONVENTION.

A removal or retention is wrongful if it breaches the custody rights of the petitioner under the law of the child’s habitual residence. *See* Hague Convention, art. 3; *Ponath*, 829 F.2d at 361. The district court’s failure to address wrongful removal was error. *See Ponath*, 829 F.2d at 361 (wrongful removal is a threshold issue under the Convention).

Here, Panamanian law vested custody rights in Appellant as Danelsy’s mother. *See* Panamanian Civil Code, arts. 303, 310 (1960). Mena’s removal of Danelsy without Appellant’s consent breached her custody rights. *See Ponath*, 829 F.2d at 361-62. The district court’s failure to analyze wrongful removal was clear error. *See id.* at 361.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court’s denial of Appellant’s petition for return of Danelsy should be reversed. The district court erred in finding consent under Article 13(a), in failing to address habitual residence, and in failing to address wrongful removal. Appellant’s petition should be granted, and Danelsy should be returned to Panama.
