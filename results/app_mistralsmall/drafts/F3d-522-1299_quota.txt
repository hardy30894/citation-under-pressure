**ARGUMENT**

I. The District Court Erred in Concluding That the Asserted Claims Are Not Entitled to the Benefit of the Original Application’s Filing Date Under 35 U.S.C. § 120.

To establish entitlement to an earlier filing date under § 120, a patent applicant must demonstrate that the earlier application provides a written description of the invention claimed in the later patent. *See* 35 U.S.C. § 112 ¶ 1; *Lockwood v. Am. Airlines, Inc.*, 107 F.3d 1565, 1572 (Fed. Cir. 1997). The district court erred in holding that the Original Application failed to satisfy the written description requirement because it did not explicitly disclose every limitation of the asserted claims. *See PowerOasis, Inc. v. T-Mobile USA, Inc.*, No. 05-cv-42-PB, 2007 WL 962937, at *2 (D.N.H. Mar. 30, 2007).

The Federal Circuit has repeatedly held that the written description requirement is met where the earlier application “reasonably conveys to those skilled in the art that the inventor had possession of the claimed subject matter as of the filing date.” *Ariad Pharm., Inc. v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 598 F.3d 1336, 1351 (Fed. Cir. 2010) (en banc) (quoting *Regents of the Univ. of Cal. v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 119 F.3d 1559, 1566 (Fed. Cir. 1997)). The Original Application need not recite the exact claim language so long as it describes the invention with sufficient clarity and precision to enable a skilled artisan to recognize that the inventor was in possession of the claimed subject matter. *See id.* at 1352; *Fiers v. Revel,* 984 F.2d 1164, 1169 (Fed. Cir. 1993).

Here, the Original Application’s specification discloses a vending machine for selling telecommunications access, including a payment mechanism, a customer interface, and a telecommunications channel access circuit. *See* ’643 patent col. 3 ll. 10–30. The asserted claims merely recite structural and functional details inherent in the Original Application’s disclosure. *See* *In re Wertheim*, 541 F.2d 257, 263 (C.C.P.A. 1976) (holding that claims are adequately supported where the earlier disclosure describes the invention in a manner sufficient to convey possession of the claimed subject matter). The district court’s contrary conclusion improperly elevated form over substance by requiring verbatim recitation of claim elements. *See Ariad*, 598 F.3d at 1351.

II. The District Court Erred in Construing “Customer Interface” to Include an Interface on the Customer’s Laptop.

The district court’s construction of “customer interface” as encompassing an interface located on the customer’s laptop was erroneous. *See* PowerOasis, 2007 WL 962937, at *2. Claim terms are generally given their ordinary and customary meaning as understood by a person of ordinary skill in the art at the time of invention. *Phillips v. AWH Corp.*, 415 F.3d 1303, 1312–13 (Fed. Cir. 2005) (en banc). The specification and prosecution history must be consulted to confirm or refine that meaning. *Id.* at 1315–17.

The asserted claims recite a “customer interface for indicating the status of said vending machine,” which plainly refers to a component of the vending machine itself. *See* ’658 patent col. 12 ll. 20–25; ’400 patent col. 12 ll. 20–25. The district court’s construction ignored the plain language of the claims and the specification, which consistently describe the customer interface as part of the vending machine. *See* *Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.*, 90 F.3d 1576, 1582 (Fed. Cir. 1996) (holding that claims must be read in light of the specification). The district court’s reliance on new matter added in the 2000 CIP Application was improper, as that application is not part of the Original Application’s disclosure. *See* *Transco Prods., Inc. v. Performance Contracting, Inc.*, 38 F.3d 551, 557 (Fed. Cir. 1994) (holding that later-added matter cannot supply a missing written description).

III. The Asserted Claims Are Not Anticipated by the MobileStar Network Under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b).

Because the asserted claims are entitled to the Original Application’s filing date, the MobileStar Network—disclosed and publicly used prior to June 15, 1999—does not qualify as § 102(b) prior art. *See* 35 U.S.C. § 102(b); *Am. Hoist & Derrick Co. v. Sowa & Sons, Inc.*, 725 F.2d 1350, 1359 (Fed. Cir. 1984) (holding that prior art under § 102(b) must predate the patent’s effective filing date). The district court’s contrary conclusion rested on an erroneous construction of the claims and an improper denial of priority. *See PowerOasis*, 2007 WL 962937, at *2–3.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in granting summary judgment of invalidity. The asserted claims are entitled to the Original Application’s filing date, and the MobileStar Network does not anticipate them. The judgment below should be reversed.
