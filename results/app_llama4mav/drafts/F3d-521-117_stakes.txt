## Argument

The district court erred in denying Michael Soares's motion to suppress the handgun found on his person because the police pat-frisked him in violation of the Fourth Amendment.

The Fourth Amendment permits a police officer to conduct a pat-down search of an individual if the officer reasonably believes that the individual is armed and poses a threat to the officer or others. Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1, 27 (1968). The reasonableness of the officer's belief is determined by the totality of the circumstances. United States v. Cortez, 449 U.S. 411, 417 (1981). In this case, the totality of the circumstances did not justify the pat-frisk of Soares.

The stop of the Lexus was initiated because the driver failed to turn on his headlights, a minor traffic infraction. United States v. Brown, 510 F.3d 57, 65 (1st Cir. 2007) (noting that a traffic stop is justified if the officer has probable cause to believe that a traffic violation has occurred). The officers observed furtive movements by the passengers, but this alone is insufficient to justify a pat-frisk. See United States v. McKoy, 428 F.3d 38, 40 (1st Cir. 2005) (holding that furtive movements, without more, do not justify a pat-frisk).

Soares's agitation and verbal abuse towards the officers, while concerning, do not necessarily indicate that he was armed and posed a threat. See United States v. Cardona-Vicenty, 542 F.3d 912, 918 (1st Cir. 2008) (noting that a suspect's agitation and refusal to comply with police orders do not automatically justify a pat-frisk). Reid's testimony that he "got a little uneasy" and "felt really uneasy" about Soares's behavior is not enough to establish a reasonable belief that Soares was armed. See id. (holding that an officer's subjective feeling of unease is insufficient to justify a pat-frisk).

The government cannot rely on Soares's behavior after the stop to justify the pat-frisk, as the officers' actions were not justified at their inception. See United States v. Chhien, 266 F.3d 1, 8 (1st Cir. 2001) (noting that the government's burden to justify a warrantless search is not met if the search was not justified at its inception). The fact that Soares swatted Lapolis's hand away during the attempted pat-frisk is also irrelevant, as the pat-frisk was already unjustified.

The pat-frisk of Soares was not justified under the Fourth Amendment, and the handgun found on his person should have been suppressed. The district court's denial of Soares's motion to suppress was therefore in error.

## Conclusion

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
