ARGUMENT

The rule announced in *Johnson v. United States*, 135 S. Ct. 2551 (2015), applies retroactively to cases on collateral review. Under *Teague v. Lane*, 489 U.S. 288 (1989), a new rule is retroactive if it is substantive. Substantive rules “include decisions that narrow the scope of a criminal statute by interpreting its terms” as well as “constitutional determinations that place particular conduct or persons covered by the statute beyond the State’s power to punish.” *Schriro v. Summerlin*, 542 U.S. 348, 351-52 (2004). *Johnson* is such a rule.

*Johnson* held that the ACCA residual clause, 18 U.S.C. § 924(e)(2)(B)(ii), “denies fair notice to defendants and invites arbitrary enforcement by judges” and is therefore void under the Due Process Clause. 135 S. Ct. at 2557. The decision “narrow[ed] the class of people who are eligible for” an ACCA sentence by eliminating an entire category of predicate offenses. *Schriro*, 542 U.S. at 352. After *Johnson*, conduct that “otherwise involves conduct that presents a serious potential risk of physical injury to another” no longer supports an enhanced sentence. Because the residual clause is invalid, any sentence resting on it exceeds statutory authority.

This Court has applied analogous decisions retroactively. In *Bousley v. United States*, 523 U.S. 614 (1998), the Court held that *Bailey v. United States*, 516 U.S. 137 (1995), which narrowed the meaning of “use” in 18 U.S.C. § 924(c), applies on collateral review because it “decriminalize[d] a class of conduct.” 523 U.S. at 620. *Johnson* likewise decriminalizes a class of conduct for ACCA purposes. *See also Montgomery v. Louisiana*, 136 S. Ct. 718, 729 (2016) (substantive rules “set forth categorical constitutional guarantees that place certain criminal laws and punishments altogether beyond the State’s power to impose”).

The government’s contrary position—that *Johnson* is merely procedural—fails. Procedural rules “regulate only the manner of determining the defendant’s culpability.” *Schriro*, 542 U.S. at 353. *Johnson* did not alter sentencing procedures; it removed the statutory basis for enhancement altogether. Once the residual clause is invalidated, no procedure can lawfully impose the ACCA penalty on the basis of that clause.

Because Welch’s Florida robbery conviction was counted solely under the residual clause, his ACCA sentence is invalid under *Johnson*. The rule announced in *Johnson* therefore applies retroactively to his case.
