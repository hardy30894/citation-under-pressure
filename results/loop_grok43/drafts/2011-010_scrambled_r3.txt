ARGUMENT

The text of 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) extends Longshore Act coverage only when “injury occur[s] as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” That language imposes a geographic limitation on the place of injury. The words of a statute are to be taken in their ordinary sense. The Lottawanna, 88 U.S. (21 Wall.) 558, 576 (1874). Nothing in the provision authorizes compensation for an injury sustained on land miles from the Shelf.

Congress enacted the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act against the settled rule that federal maritime remedies stop at the water’s edge. Southern Pacific Co. v. Jensen, 244 U.S. 205, 217-18 (1917). The statute therefore cannot be read to displace state compensation schemes for purely onshore accidents. “The Constitution does not permit the federal government to extend its maritime jurisdiction over injuries occurring on land.” Knickerbocker Ice Co. v. Stewart, 253 U.S. 149, 160 (1920).

The same principle of strict geographic demarcation appears throughout the Court’s pre-OCSLA decisions. The Thomas Jefferson, 23 U.S. (10 Wheat.) 428, 429 (1825) (admiralty jurisdiction does not extend to non-tidal waters). The Belfast, 74 U.S. (7 Wall.) 624, 637 (1868) likewise confined coverage to the precise situs described by Congress and reaffirmed that any extension beyond navigable waters requires explicit statutory language.

When Congress wished to enlarge remedies, it did so with unmistakable words. See, e.g., The Lottawanna, 88 U.S. (21 Wall.) 558, 576 (1874) (admiralty jurisdiction extended only after express statutory amendment). Section 1333(b) contains no comparable language removing the traditional situs requirement.

Grant Smith-Porter Ship Co. v. Rohde, 257 U.S. 469, 477 (1922) (coverage limited to injuries upon navigable waters). The Ninth Circuit’s “substantial nexus” test therefore contravenes the statute’s plain terms and the uniform line of authority limiting federal maritime remedies to the precise geographic area Congress designated. The judgment should be reversed.
