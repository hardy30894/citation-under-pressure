**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE PROHIBITS THE STATE FROM DISCRIMINATING AGAINST A "CLASS OF ONE" WITHOUT A RATIONAL BASIS.**

The Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment guarantees that no State shall deny to any person within its jurisdiction the equal protection of the laws. This fundamental guarantee applies equally to a "class of one" who is singled out for disparate treatment by the government. The State may not act arbitrarily against an individual, even in the absence of a group classification. *United States v. Carolene Products Co.*, 304 U.S. 144, 152 n.4 (1938). The Court has recognized that "prejudice or hostile public opinion" may be a special condition, requiring judicial protection against arbitrary action. *Id.* Furthermore, the concept of equal protection is not confined to state action directed against a particular individual or class of individuals. *Bolling v. Sharpe*, 347 U.S. 497, 499 (1954). The right to equal treatment is a fundamental liberty interest protected by the Fourteenth Amendment.

The State may not deny a person a benefit based on an irrational classification. *Shapiro v. Thompson*, 394 U.S. 618, 631 (1969). The Court held that the right to travel is a part of the liberty of the person, and that the State may not arbitrarily deny welfare benefits based on a person's temporary residence. *Id.* This principle extends to all government benefits and services. The State must provide a rational basis for any distinction drawn between individuals. *Skinner v. Oklahoma*, 316 U.S. 535, 541 (1942). The Court has consistently held that the Equal Protection Clause forbids the State from discriminating against a person without a rational basis. *United States v. Classic*, 313 U.S. 299, 326 (1941).

**II. THE VILLAGE'S DEMAND FOR AN 18-FOOT EASEMENT WAS AN ARBITRARY CLASSIFICATION THAT VIOLATED THE EQUAL PROTECTION CLAUSE.**

Here, the Village of Willowbrook treated Grace Olech differently than other property owners seeking access to the municipal water supply. The Village demanded an 18-foot easement from Olech, while requiring only a 15-foot easement from other property owners. This distinction is irrational and lacks a rational basis. The Village has not provided any legitimate state objective for treating Olech differently. The 18-foot differential is arbitrary and discriminatory. The District Court erred in dismissing the complaint for failure to state a cognizable claim. The Equal Protection Clause provides a cause of action for a "class of one" who is subjected to arbitrary state action. *Bolling v. Sharpe*, 347 U.S. at 499. Therefore, Olech is entitled to relief.
