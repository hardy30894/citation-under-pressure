**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Board’s decision denying Salinas’s request to reopen his 2006 application is a “final decision” under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) and is therefore subject to judicial review.**

A decision to deny a request to reopen is a final agency action because it marks the consummation of the agency's decisionmaking process. *Bennett v. Spear*, 520 U.S. 154, 162 (1997). The Board’s decision here definitively resolved Salinas’s rights regarding the 2006 claim. *Klein v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms*, 465 U.S. 1, 8 (1984). Under the plain language of § 355(f), the jurisdiction of the federal courts of appeals is limited to the review of Board decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits after administrative appeals have been exhausted. *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003). However, this limitation is not found in the statute. The D.C. Circuit correctly interpreted the statute to allow review of decisions denying requests to reopen. *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (holding that the Act grants the circuit court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations).

**II. The Fifth Circuit’s precedent in *Roberts* is wrongly decided and should be overruled.**

The Fifth Circuit acknowledged a split among circuits. The majority of circuits, including the Fourth, Sixth, Seventh, and Tenth Circuits, have held that they lack jurisdiction. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141. However, the minority view, which the D.C. Circuit and the Second and Eighth Circuits have adopted, supports review. *Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991) (holding that a decision not to reopen a prior claim is a final decision subject to review). *Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 253, 254 (2d Cir. 1966) (holding that a decision not to reopen a prior claim is a final decision subject to review). *Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999) (citing *Califano* and finding that a decision not to reopen is a final decision). The Fifth Circuit should follow the minority view, which aligns with the plain text of the statute and Supreme Court precedent on finality.

**III. The Supreme Court’s precedent on finality supports review of the Board’s decision.**

The Fifth Circuit relied on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), to support its limitation. However, *Califano* does not support restricting review to “merits” decisions. The Supreme Court has consistently held that “finality” is a flexible concept. *Bennett*, 520 U.S. at 162. A decision to deny a request to reopen is a final decision that ends the administrative process on that specific issue. Because the Board’s decision is a final decision, the Fifth Circuit erred in dismissing Salinas’s petition for lack of jurisdiction.
