**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT’S POLICY AND PRACTICE OPEN THE FACILITIES TO ALL SPEECH, INCLUDING RELIGIOUS SPEECH.**

The District’s facilities constitute a public forum, and the District’s exclusion of religious speech is a content-based restriction that violates the First Amendment. The extent of permissible governmental regulation of expressive activity on publicly owned property is dependent upon the character of the public property in question. *Perry Education Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 44 (1983). The District has opened its facilities to the general public for expressive activities, such as the library-sponsored lecture series, thereby creating a forum where all speech, including religious speech, must be permitted.

The District’s refusal to allow Lamb’s Chapel to show religious films is a direct violation of the principle established in *Widmar v. Vincent*. In *Widmar*, the Supreme Court held that a state university could not deny access to university facilities to students who wished to conduct religious meetings on campus. 454 U.S. 263, 273 (1981). The Court reasoned that "the State may not, by its own action, withhold that benefit from those who wish to create and disseminate religious ideas on the premises." *Id.* at 273. Here, the District has not merely allowed religious speech; it has created a forum open to the community. By allowing secular community groups to use the facilities for films and lectures, the District has established a policy of openness that necessarily encompasses religious speech. The District’s attempt to exclude religious speech based on its content is impermissible.

**II. THE DISTRICT’S CONTENT-BASED EXCLUSION OF RELIGIOUS SPEECH VIOLATES THE FIRST AMENDMENT.**

Even if the facilities were a limited public forum, the District’s exclusion of religious speech is a content-based restriction that must survive strict scrutiny. In a public forum, a regulation providing a content-based exclusion may be enforced only when "necessary to serve a compelling state interest" and must be "narrowly drawn" to serve that purpose. *Perry*, 460 U.S. at 45. The District has failed to demonstrate a compelling interest in excluding religious speech from its community use policy.

The District’s stated interest in the "separation of church and state" is not a compelling interest sufficient to justify a content-based exclusion of speech. The First Amendment guarantees the freedom of speech, and the government may not suppress expression merely because public officials oppose the speaker’s view. *International Soc’y For Krishna Consciousness v. Lee*, 925 F.2d 576, 580 (2d Cir. 1991). The District’s policy effectively bans religious speech while allowing secular speech, creating a viewpoint discrimination that is unconstitutional. *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 273. The District has not shown that the religious content of the films poses a greater threat to the school environment than the secular content of the library lectures or other permitted uses.

**III. THE DISTRICT’S DISCRIMINATORY EXCLUSION VIOLATES THE ESTABLISHMENT CLAUSE.**

The District’s policy of allowing secular speech while banning religious speech violates the Establishment Clause of the First Amendment. The Establishment Clause prohibits the government from favoring one religion over another or from discriminating against religion. By opening its facilities to the community for secular purposes but closing them for religious purposes, the District has engaged in viewpoint discrimination that violates the Establishment Clause.

The Supreme Court has consistently held that the government may not exclude religious speech from a forum it has opened to the public. *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 273. The District’s actions here are indistinguishable from those in *Widmar*, where the Court found that the university’s policy of accommodating student meetings created a forum generally open for use by student groups. *Id.* at 267. The District’s facilities are similarly open to the general public for community use. To allow secular groups to use the facilities while denying religious groups the same access is a clear violation of the First Amendment’s guarantee of equal access to public forums.
