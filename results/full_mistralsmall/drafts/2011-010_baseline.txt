**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE OCSLA’S WORKERS’ COMPENSATION PROVISION REQUIRES THAT AN INJURY OCCUR ON THE OUTER CONTINENTAL SHELF.**

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that § 1333(b) of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act ("OCSLA") extends Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act ("LHWCA") coverage to injuries occurring on land. Section 1333(b) provides compensation for "the disability or death of an employee resulting from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf." 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) (emphasis added). The plain text of the statute requires that the *injury* occur on the outer Continental Shelf. *See generally* *United States v. Ron Pair Enters., Inc.*, 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989) ("[W]hen the statute’s language is plain, the sole function of the courts—at least where the disposition required by the text is not absurd—is to enforce it according to its terms.").

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary interpretation—that the statute requires only that the *operations* occur on the outer Continental Shelf—ignores the statutory text. The statute does not say that injuries "resulting from operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf" are covered. It says that injuries "occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf" are covered. *Compare* 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) *with* *id.* § 1333(a) (providing that state law applies to "the subsoil and seabed of the outer Continental Shelf, and artificial islands and fixed structures erected thereon"). The distinction is critical: the former requires that the injury occur on the outer Continental Shelf, while the latter applies state law to operations on the outer Continental Shelf.

The Supreme Court has recognized that OCSLA’s coverage provisions are "determined principally by locale." *Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire*, 477 U.S. 207, 219 (1986). In *Tallentire*, the Court noted that § 1333(b) "superimposes a status requirement on the otherwise determinative OCSLA situs requirement." *Id.* at 219 n.2. The Court’s footnote, while dictum, is consistent with the plain text of the statute and the general rule that OCSLA’s coverage is determined by situs. *See id.*; *Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray (Herb’s Welding I)*, 470 U.S. 414, 422-24 (1985) (holding that a worker on a platform in state waters was not covered by OCSLA because the injury did not occur on the outer Continental Shelf).

The Fifth Circuit has likewise held that § 1333(b) requires a situs-of-injury test. *See Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs*, 877 F.2d 356, 362 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc) ("[A]n OCSLA claimant must show that the injury occurred on an outer continental shelf platform or on the waters above the outer continental shelf."). The Third Circuit’s contrary decision in *Curtis v. Schlumberger Offshore Service, Inc.*, 849 F.2d 805 (3d Cir. 1988), is unpersuasive. The *Curtis* court relied on a "but for" test that would extend coverage to any injury that would not have occurred but for operations on the outer Continental Shelf. *Id.* at 810. This interpretation reads the "occurring as the result of" language out of the statute and conflicts with the Supreme Court’s recognition that OCSLA’s coverage is determined by situs. *See Tallentire*, 477 U.S. at 219.

**II. MR. VALLADOLID’S INJURY DID NOT OCCUR ON THE OUTER CONTINENTAL SHELF.**

Even if § 1333(b) did not require a situs-of-injury test, Mr. Valladolid’s injury did not occur "as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf." 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The statute requires a direct causal connection between the injury and operations on the outer Continental Shelf. *See Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray (Herb’s Welding II)*, 766 F.2d 898, 900 (5th Cir. 1985) (holding that an injury occurring on an oil platform in state waters was not eligible for OCSLA benefits because the operations were not conducted on the outer Continental Shelf).

Here, Mr. Valladolid was killed at Pacific Operations’s onshore oil-processing facility, La Conchita, which is located on the California coast, not on the outer Continental Shelf. The facility processed crude oil slurry received from the Hogan and Houchin platforms via pipeline, but the injury occurred during Mr. Valladolid’s maintenance duties at the facility, not during the processing of the slurry. There is no evidence that the forklift accident was caused by or directly related to operations on the outer Continental Shelf. The Ninth Circuit’s "substantial nexus" test is inconsistent with the statute’s plain text and the Supreme Court’s guidance that OCSLA’s coverage is determined by situs. *See Tallentire*, 477 U.S. at 219.

**III. MR. VALLADOLID WAS NOT INJURED ON A MARITIME SITUS AND WAS NOT ENGAGED IN MARITIME EMPLOYMENT.**

Even if § 1333(b) extended coverage to injuries occurring on land, Mr. Valladolid’s injury did not occur on a maritime situs, and he was not engaged in maritime employment. The LHWCA provides compensation only for injuries occurring on the navigable waters of the United States or on an "adjoining area customarily used by an employer in loading, unloading, repairing, or building a vessel." 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). La Conchita is not an "adjoining area" because it is separated from the water by a highway and railroad tracks, has no direct access to any pier or dock, and is three miles from the nearest maritime facility. *See Brady-Hamilton Stevedore Co. v. Herron*, 568 F.2d 137, 141 (9th Cir. 1978) (setting forth the factors for determining whether a facility is an "adjoining area").

Moreover, Mr. Valladolid was not engaged in maritime employment. The LHWCA covers only employees engaged in "maritime employment," which includes longshoremen, harbor workers, and other employees engaged in loading, unloading, repairing, or building vessels. *See* 33 U.S.C. § 902(3). Mr. Valladolid’s duties as a roustabout—cleaning, maintenance, and operating a forklift at an onshore processing facility—were not maritime in nature. *See Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Perini North River Associates*, 459 U.S. 297, 300 (1983) (holding that a construction worker injured while building a pier was not engaged in maritime employment).

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that § 1333(b) of the OCSLA extends LHWCA coverage to injuries occurring on land. The statute requires that the injury occur on the outer Continental Shelf. Even if the statute did not require a situs-of-injury test, Mr. Valladolid’s injury did not occur "as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf," and he was not injured on a maritime situs or engaged in maritime employment. The judgment of the Ninth Circuit should be reversed.
