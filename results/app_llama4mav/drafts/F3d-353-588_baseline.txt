## ARGUMENT

### I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on Epps's ADA and MHRA Claims

The district court incorrectly concluded that Epps failed to establish that Pine Lawn regarded him as disabled under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), 42 U.S.C. § 12101 et seq., and the Missouri Human Rights Act (MHRA), Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.010 et seq. To prevail on his ADA and MHRA claims, Epps must show that he was regarded as having a disability that substantially limited one or more major life activities. See 42 U.S.C. § 12102(2); Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.010(4); see also Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., 527 U.S. 471, 489 (1999) (holding that to be regarded as disabled, a plaintiff must show that the employer believed the plaintiff was substantially limited in a major life activity).

The record demonstrates that Pine Lawn regarded Epps as unable to perform the essential functions of his job as a police officer due to his physical impairments. Chief Hardy’s memoranda to the Mayor and Board of Aldermen, recommending Epps's termination, explicitly referenced Dr. Mirkin's assessment that Epps had degenerative disk disease and would likely need to seek a different occupation. See App. at 123-125. Moreover, Hardy noted that the department's officers were required to bend, squat, and run as part of their full duties, and that there were no light-duty assignments available. Id. This evidence indicates that Pine Lawn perceived Epps as having a physical impairment that substantially limited his ability to perform the major life activity of working.

The Eighth Circuit has held that an employer's perception of an employee's inability to perform a specific job or a narrow range of jobs can be sufficient to establish that the employer regarded the employee as disabled. See Conant v. City of Hibbing, 271 F.3d 782, 785 (8th Cir. 2001) (holding that an employer's perception of an employee's inability to perform a specific job can be sufficient to establish a regarded-as claim). Here, Pine Lawn's actions and statements demonstrate that it regarded Epps as unable to perform not just his specific job, but the broader category of jobs that require physical activities such as bending, squatting, and running.

Therefore, the district court erred in granting summary judgment on Epps's ADA and MHRA claims, as there is a genuine issue of material fact regarding whether Pine Lawn regarded Epps as disabled.

### II. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Epps's Workers' Compensation Retaliation Claim

The district court incorrectly held that Missouri's sovereign immunity statute, Mo. Rev. Stat. § 537.600, barred Epps's workers' compensation retaliation claim under Mo. Rev. Stat. § 287.780. However, the Missouri Supreme Court has held that § 287.780 creates a statutory cause of action against employers, including municipalities, for retaliating against employees who file workers' compensation claims. See Hansome v. N. Cent. Corr. Facility, 78 S.W.3d 151, 155 (Mo. banc 2002) (holding that § 287.780 applies to public employers).

Pine Lawn, as a municipal corporation, is not entitled to sovereign immunity for claims arising under § 287.780. See id. at 156. Epps presented evidence that he filed multiple workers' compensation claims and that Chief Hardy was aware of these claims when he recommended Epps's termination. See App. at 123-125. This evidence raises a genuine issue of material fact regarding whether Pine Lawn retaliated against Epps for filing workers' compensation claims.

Therefore, the district court erred in dismissing Epps's workers' compensation retaliation claim.

### III. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Epps's Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress Claim

The district court also erred in dismissing Epps's intentional infliction of emotional distress claim under Missouri common law. The Missouri Supreme Court has held that a plaintiff must show that the defendant's conduct was extreme and outrageous, and that it caused the plaintiff severe emotional distress. See Gibson v. Brewer, 952 S.W.2d 239, 249 (Mo. banc 1997).

Epps presented evidence that Pine Lawn's actions, including terminating him after he filed multiple workers' compensation claims, were extreme and outrageous. See App. at 130-132. Moreover, Epps alleged that he suffered severe emotional distress as a result of Pine Lawn's actions. Id. at 133. This evidence raises a genuine issue of material fact regarding whether Pine Lawn's conduct was extreme and outrageous and whether it caused Epps severe emotional distress.

Therefore, the district court erred in dismissing Epps's intentional infliction of emotional distress claim.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the district court's grant of summary judgment in favor of Pine Lawn should be reversed, and the case should be remanded for further proceedings.
