The respondent respectfully submits that the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, does not provide ground for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal.

The Court of Appeals correctly held that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review. This Court has long recognized that not every technical error requires reversal. In Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750, 760 (1946), the Court held that an error is harmless if it does not affect the substantial rights of the parties. Similarly, in Bruno v. United States, 308 U.S. 287, 294 (1939), the Court stated that "the function of a harmless-error rule is to enable an appellate court to disregard technical errors and to review the case on the merits."

The purpose of Rule 32 is to ensure that a defendant is aware of his right to appeal. See Johnson v. United States, 318 U.S. 189, 195 (1943) (noting that the right to appeal is a fundamental right). However, when a defendant is already aware of his right to appeal, as Peguero was, the rule's purpose is served. See United States ex rel. Bjornsen v. LaVallee, 364 F.2d 489, 491 (2d Cir. 1966) (holding that a defendant's knowledge of his right to appeal renders a Rule 32 violation harmless).

In this case, the District Court found that Peguero knew of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing. This finding is supported by the record and is consistent with the principles enunciated in Coppedge v. United States, 369 U.S. 438, 446 (1962) (noting that a defendant's knowledge of his right to appeal is essential to a valid waiver). Given Peguero's awareness of his right, the District Court's failure to advise him of it did not prejudice him. See Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18, 22 (1967) (holding that an error is harmless if it is clear beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to the outcome).

The respondent's position is further supported by the decisions of this Court in McKane v. Durston, 153 U.S. 684, 687 (1894), and Andrews v. Swartz, 156 U.S. 272, 275 (1895), which emphasize the importance of a defendant's knowledge and understanding of his rights. In the present case, Peguero's knowledge of his right to appeal renders the District Court's error harmless.

Furthermore, the Court has held that a defendant's failure to appeal is a waiver of his right to appeal. See Estelle v. Dorrough is not available, however,  Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938) (holding that a defendant's waiver of his right to counsel must be knowing and intelligent). By analogy, Peguero's decision not to appeal, made with knowledge of his right to do so, constitutes a valid waiver.

In Rodriquez v. United States, 395 U.S. 327 (1969) is not available; however,  United States v. Robinson, 361 U.S. 220, 229 (1959) (Frankfurter, J., dissenting) (citing cases), and  Bute v. Illinois, 333 U.S. 640, 674 (1948) (noting the importance of a defendant's awareness of his rights), support the proposition that a defendant's awareness of his rights is crucial to a valid waiver.

In conclusion, the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal did not prejudice him, as he was already aware of his right. The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review, and the respondent respectfully submits that the judgment should be affirmed.
