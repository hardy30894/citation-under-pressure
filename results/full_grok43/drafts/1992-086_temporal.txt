The School District's refusal to permit Lamb's Chapel to use its facilities after hours for the presentation of films on family values and child-rearing violates the First Amendment freedoms of speech and assembly. When a state opens school property for community use, it may not thereafter exclude particular speakers or messages on the basis of their religious character. As the Court observed in Hague v. CIO, 307 U.S. 496, 515 (1939), public places "have immemorially been held in trust for the use of the public and, time out of mind, have been used for purposes of assembly, communicating thoughts between citizens, and discussing public questions." The same principle governs property that the State has affirmatively designated for expressive activity. See Saia v. New York, 334 U.S. 558, 560 (1948); Niemotko v. Maryland, 340 U.S. 268, 272 (1951).

The record establishes that the District has permitted its facilities to be used for lectures, musical performances, and other community programs. Having created such a forum, the District may impose reasonable time, place, and manner restrictions, but it may not engage in viewpoint discrimination. Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569, 574 (1941); Edwards v. South Carolina, 372 U.S. 229, 237-238 (1963). The denial here rests solely on the religious perspective of the proposed films. Such content-based exclusion is incompatible with the First Amendment. Kunz v. New York, 340 U.S. 290, 293 (1951); Cox v. Louisiana, 379 U.S. 536, 557-558 (1965).

Nor can the District justify the exclusion as necessary to avoid an establishment of religion. The films address secular subjects of family life and child-rearing; any religious element is incidental to the message. See Cantwell v. Connecticut, 310 U.S. 296, 303-304 (1940); Murdock v. Pennsylvania, 319 U.S. 105, 109 (1943). The Constitution does not require the suppression of religious speech in a forum otherwise open to comparable secular expression. Brown v. Louisiana, 383 U.S. 131, 142 (1966); Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503, 513 (1969).

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
