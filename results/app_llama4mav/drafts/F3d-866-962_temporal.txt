The district court erred in granting summary judgment to BNSF Railway Company on the appellants' retaliation claims under the Federal Railroad Safety Act, 49 U.S.C. § 20109(a)(1)(C). To establish a prima facie case of retaliation, the appellants must show that they engaged in protected activity, that BNSF took adverse action against them, and that there was a causal connection between the protected activity and the adverse action. See Texas & N.O.R. Co. v. Brotherhood of Ry. & S.S. Clerks, 281 U.S. 548, 559 (1930).

The appellants engaged in protected activity when they reported the unsafe conditions at the bridge to BNSF officials, including Trainmaster Dixon and Claims Representative Murphy. Kline and Snyder mentioned the lack of side rails and lighting on the bridge in their written statements to Dixon, and Foster noted that employees had previously reported the location as dangerous to Murphy. This activity is protected under the FRSA, as it involves reporting a hazardous condition that could affect the safety of railroad operations. See id. at 558-59.

BNSF took adverse action against the appellants when it disciplined them for alleged rule infractions following the accident. The discipline included a formal reprimand for Snyder, a thirty-day record suspension for Kline, and termination for Foster. These actions constitute adverse employment actions, as they affect the terms and conditions of the appellants' employment. See Steele v. Louisville & N.R. Co., 323 U.S. 192, 204 (1944).

The causal connection between the protected activity and the adverse action is established by the temporal proximity between the appellants' reports of the unsafe conditions and the disciplinary actions taken by BNSF. The appellants reported the hazardous conditions in early April 2012, and BNSF initiated disciplinary proceedings against them shortly thereafter. The investigating hearing was held in January 2013, and the appellants were disciplined following the hearing. This sequence of events suggests that BNSF's actions were motivated by the appellants' protected activity. See Moore v. Illinois Cent. R. Co., 180 F.2d 514, 516 (5th Cir. 1950).

Furthermore, the appellants have presented evidence that BNSF's proffered reasons for disciplining them are pretextual. The appellants have shown that they were subjected to increased operations testing, which they claim was retaliatory, and that they were disciplined for rule infractions that were not consistently enforced. This evidence raises a genuine issue of material fact as to whether BNSF's actions were motivated by a retaliatory intent. See Virginian Ry. Co. v. System Federation No. 40, 300 U.S. 515, 558 (1937).

In light of the evidence presented, the district court erred in granting summary judgment to BNSF. The appellants have established a prima facie case of retaliation, and there are genuine issues of material fact that preclude summary judgment. Therefore, the judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case should be remanded for further proceedings.
