ARGUMENT

Section 1407(a) commands that actions transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall" be remanded at their conclusion. This mandatory language admits of no exception permitting a transferee court to retain the case for trial. See Ex parte Collett, 337 U.S. 55, 58 (1949) (statutory "shall" is imperative); Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917) (plain words control). A general transfer provision cannot override this specific directive. See Fourco Glass Co. v. Transmirra Prods. Corp., 353 U.S. 222, 228-29 (1957) (specific venue statute prevails over general one); Stonite Prods. Co. v. Melvin Lloyd Co., 315 U.S. 561, 566 (1942) (same).

Section 1404(a) authorizes transfers only between districts where venue and jurisdiction independently exist. Hoffman v. Blaski, 363 U.S. 335, 342-43 (1960). It does not empower a court whose authority is limited to pretrial matters to confer trial jurisdiction upon itself. See Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612, 616 (1964) (1404(a) preserves plaintiff's venue rights); Norwood v. Kirkpatrick, 349 U.S. 29, 32 (1955) (1404(a) effects change of venue, not expansion of judicial power).

Mandamus lies to correct a district court's refusal to honor the statutory remand obligation. Roche v. Evaporated Milk Ass'n, 319 U.S. 21, 26 (1943) (mandamus available where court exceeds authority); La Buy v. Howes Leather Co., 352 U.S. 249, 256 (1957) (same for clear legal error). Petitioners lack any other adequate remedy once trial proceeds in the wrong forum. Ex parte Fahey, 332 U.S. 258, 260 (1947); Bankers Life & Cas. Co. v. Holland, 346 U.S. 379, 384-85 (1953).

The policy of 1407—to consolidate pretrial proceedings without permanently displacing plaintiffs' chosen forums—would be defeated by self-transfer. Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501, 508-09 (1947) (venue rules protect plaintiff's choice); Koster v. (American) Lumbermens Mut. Cas. Co., 330 U.S. 518, 524 (1947) (same). Allowing routine retention of cases for trial would render the remand command a nullity. See United States v. Borden Co., 308 U.S. 188, 198-99 (1939) (statutes construed to give effect to every provision).

The district court's order therefore exceeds its statutory authority and must be reversed.
