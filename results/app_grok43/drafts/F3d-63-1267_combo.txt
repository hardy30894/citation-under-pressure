ARGUMENT

Stelwagon failed to prove actual injury to its business or property from the alleged price discrimination, as required to recover damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act. The Robinson-Patman Act proscribes only those discriminations that cause competitive injury. See FTC v. Morton Salt Co., 334 U.S. 37, 46 (1948) (price differences must be shown to “substantially lessen competition”); Corn Prods. Refining Co. v. FTC, 324 U.S. 726, 738 (1945) (same). Proof of a violation alone does not establish the fact of damage. See Bruce’s Juices, Inc. v. American Can Co., 330 U.S. 743, 755 (1947) (“The statute does not make the mere existence of a discrimination a basis for liability.”).

To recover, a plaintiff must demonstrate with reasonable certainty that the discrimination caused actual pecuniary loss. Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co., 282 U.S. 555, 562 (1931) (“The plaintiff must show that the defendant’s unlawful conduct caused the injury of which he complains.”); Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc., 327 U.S. 251, 264 (1946) (damages must rest on “a just and reasonable estimate” supported by evidence, not speculation). Mere proof that competitors received lower prices is insufficient without evidence that the plaintiff lost sales or profits as a direct result. See Standard Oil Co. v. FTC, 340 U.S. 231, 249 (1951); Automatic Canteen Co. of Am. v. FTC, 346 U.S. 61, 79 (1953).

Stelwagon’s evidence consisted of price differentials and generalized assertions of lost sales. Such proof does not satisfy the requirement that damages be traced to the violation with reasonable certainty. See Continental Ore Co. v. Union Carbide & Carbon Corp., 370 U.S. 690, 697 (1962) (antitrust damages must be proved by evidence linking the violation to specific injury); United States v. Socony-Vacuum Oil Co., 310 U.S. 150, 225 (1940) (injury must be shown, not presumed). Absent competent evidence of actual loss, the district court should have granted judgment as a matter of law on the treble-damage claim.

The breach-of-contract verdict is likewise unsupported once the antitrust component is removed, because the jury’s single damages award rested on the same inadequate proof of lost profits. See Maple Flooring Mfrs. Ass’n v. United States, 268 U.S. 563, 582 (1925) (damages cannot be awarded on conjecture). The judgment awarding treble damages must therefore be vacated.
