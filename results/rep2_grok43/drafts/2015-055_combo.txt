The rule announced in Johnson v. United States applies retroactively to cases on collateral review. A conviction under a statute later held unconstitutionally vague cannot stand, for the Due Process Clauses require that penal laws give fair notice of what conduct they punish. Lanzetta v. New Jersey, 306 U.S. 451, 453 (1939); Connally v. General Construction Co., 269 U.S. 385, 391 (1926).

Retroactivity turns on whether the new rule is substantive or procedural and whether it implicates the fundamental fairness of the proceeding. Linkletter v. Walker, 381 U.S. 618, 629 (1965). When a decision “goes to the fairness of the trial—the very integrity of the fact-finding process,” full retroactive effect follows. Tehan v. United States ex rel. Shott, 382 U.S. 406, 416 (1966); see also Johnson v. New Jersey, 384 U.S. 719, 727 (1966). A holding that an entire clause of the Armed Career Criminal Act is void for vagueness is substantive; it removes the power to impose an enhanced sentence on the basis of that clause. Stovall v. Denno, 388 U.S. 293, 297 (1967).

The same principle governs when a new rule “substantially improves the accuracy of the fact-finding process.” Desist v. United States, 394 U.S. 244, 251 (1969). Because the residual clause required courts to imagine hypothetical risks posed by abstract crimes, its invalidation corrects an arbitrary and unpredictable basis for sentencing. That correction must apply to all sentences resting on the clause, including those already final. Griffin v. Illinois, 351 U.S. 12, 17 (1956).

Equal treatment under the law further compels retroactivity. Once the Court declares a statute incapable of constitutional application, continued enforcement against some defendants but not others denies equal protection. Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335, 344 (1963). The same logic that required retroactive application of the right to counsel applies here: a sentence imposed under an invalid statute is no sentence at all.

Petitioner’s 1996 robbery conviction therefore cannot serve as a predicate under the residual clause. The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
