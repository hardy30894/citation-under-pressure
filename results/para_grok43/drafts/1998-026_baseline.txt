ARGUMENT

A district court’s failure to comply with the advice requirement of Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) does not furnish a basis for relief under 28 U.S.C. § 2255 when the defendant was independently aware of his right to appeal and made a knowing decision not to pursue one. Section 2255 relief is available only for “a fundamental defect which inherently results in a complete miscarriage of justice.” Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424, 428 (1962). A technical violation of Rule 32 that causes no prejudice falls well short of that standard. See United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780, 783-84 (1979) (holding that a Rule 11 violation, absent prejudice, is not cognizable on collateral review).

The text of Rule 32 itself confirms that its purpose is informational. Once that purpose has been fulfilled by the defendant’s actual knowledge, the omission of the prescribed statement produces no injury. The District Court’s factual finding that Peguero “knew of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing” therefore disposes of any claim of harm. That finding, undisturbed on appeal, establishes that the Rule’s objective was achieved.

This Court has repeatedly declined to convert every procedural lapse into a ground for vacating a final judgment. In United States v. Addonizio, 442 U.S. 178, 185 (1979), the Court emphasized that § 2255 “was intended to afford federal prisoners a remedy . . . only when the error is of a fundamental character.” A sentencing court’s silence concerning appellate rights, where the defendant already possesses that knowledge, does not rise to that level. See also United States v. Frady, 456 U.S. 152, 166 (1982) (requiring a showing of actual prejudice on collateral attack).

The harmless-error analysis applied by the court of appeals is consistent with these principles. Because the record affirmatively demonstrates that Peguero elected not to appeal with full awareness of his rights, the Rule 32 omission was harmless. No precedent of this Court requires automatic vacatur of a sentence for a Rule 32 violation divorced from any showing of prejudice. Accordingly, the judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
