The Railroad Retirement Act provides that any party aggrieved “may obtain a review of any final decision of the Board” by filing a petition in the appropriate court of appeals. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is such a decision. It conclusively resolved his request for reopening after full administrative consideration, leaving no further agency recourse. See Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (“the Railroad Retirement Act grants the Court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations”).

This conclusion follows from the statute’s text. Section 355(f) contains no limitation to “decisions on the merits” or decisions “after a hearing.” The Fifth Circuit’s contrary reading in Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board, 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003), imported language absent from the provision. The Second and Eighth Circuits have rejected that gloss, holding that a denial of reopening satisfies the statutory requirement of finality. Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Board, 370 F.2d 253, 254 (2d Cir. 1966); Sones v. Railroad Retirement Board, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991).

Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), does not compel a different result. There the Court construed the distinct review provision of the Social Security Act, which expressly limits judicial review to “final decision[s] … made after a hearing.” Id. at 108. The Railroad Retirement Act lacks that qualifier. The Board’s regulations themselves treat a reopening denial as the termination of administrative proceedings, confirming its final character. 20 C.F.R. § 261.2.

Because the decision is final within the meaning of § 355(f), the court of appeals possessed jurisdiction to review it. The petition should be granted and the judgment reversed.
