**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE MERIT SYSTEMS PROTECTION BOARD’S DISMISSAL OF PETITIONER’S APPEAL FOR LACK OF JURISDICTION IS A “MIXED CASE” SUBJECT TO REVIEW IN DISTRICT COURT.**

The Civil Service Reform Act of 1978 (“CSRA”) provides that a federal employee who alleges discrimination in connection with an adverse employment action over which the Merit Systems Protection Board (“MSPB” or “Board”) has jurisdiction may seek judicial review in district court. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2); *Kloeckner v. Solis*, 568 U.S. 41, 47 (2012). This Court has long recognized that the term “mixed case” refers to an appeal to the MSPB that includes both a challenge to an adverse action within the Board’s jurisdiction and an allegation of discrimination. *See, e.g., id.* at 47-48; *Brown v. Secretary of Army*, 78 F.3d 645, 647 (Fed. Cir. 1996). Here, Petitioner’s appeal to the MSPB plainly alleged discrimination in connection with his suspension and retirement—both of which are appealable actions under 5 U.S.C. § 7512—and thus constituted a mixed case subject to review in district court.

The Board’s dismissal of Petitioner’s appeal for lack of jurisdiction did not alter the nature of his claim. As this Court has explained, “[t]he critical question is whether the employee’s claim, as presented to the MSPB, included a nonfrivolous allegation of discrimination.” *Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 50 (emphasis added). Here, Petitioner’s complaint to the MSPB explicitly alleged that his suspension and retirement were the result of discrimination based on race, age, and disability, as well as retaliation for prior discrimination claims. *See* J.A. 30-31. The Board’s subsequent determination that Petitioner’s retirement was voluntary did not negate the fact that his appeal included a nonfrivolous discrimination claim. *See id.* at 50-51 (holding that a mixed case remains a mixed case even if the Board ultimately dismisses it on procedural grounds).

The D.C. Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Powell v. Department of Defense*, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), is inconsistent with *Kloeckner* and must be rejected. The *Powell* court erroneously concluded that a dismissal for lack of jurisdiction removes a case from the mixed-case exception, reasoning that the employee’s claim was not “appealable to the MSPB” within the meaning of 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)(A). *Powell*, 158 F.3d at 599-600. This interpretation ignores the plain text of the statute, which focuses on whether the employee *alleged* discrimination in connection with an action appealable to the Board—not whether the Board ultimately found the action appealable. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)(A) (defining a mixed case as one in which the employee “has been affected by an action which [she] may appeal to the [MSPB]” and “alleges that a basis for the action was discrimination”).

The Supreme Court in *Kloeckner* expressly rejected the argument that a procedural dismissal removes a case from the mixed-case exception. *See* 568 U.S. at 50-51. The Court held that an employee who brings a nonfrivolous discrimination claim to the MSPB is entitled to district court review, regardless of whether the Board dismisses the appeal on procedural grounds. *Id.* at 50. The same logic applies here: Petitioner’s appeal included a nonfrivolous discrimination claim, and the Board’s dismissal for lack of jurisdiction does not divest the district court of jurisdiction.

**II. THE D.C. CIRCUIT’S ATTEMPT TO DISTINGUISH *KLOECKNER* IS UNPERSUASIVE.**

The D.C. Circuit in this case acknowledged that *Kloeckner* abrogated its prior precedent holding that procedural dismissals must be reviewed in the Federal Circuit. *See* J.A. 10-11. However, the court attempted to carve out an exception for jurisdictional dismissals, reasoning that such dismissals involve actions that were never appealable to the MSPB. *Id.* at 15-16. This distinction is unsupported by the statute and the Supreme Court’s decision in *Kloeckner*.

First, the CSRA does not differentiate between procedural and jurisdictional dismissals in defining a mixed case. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)(A). The statute requires only that the employee allege discrimination in connection with an action that *could* be appealed to the MSPB—not that the Board ultimately find the action appealable. *See Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 50 (emphasizing that the “critical question” is whether the employee’s claim included a nonfrivolous discrimination allegation).

Second, the D.C. Circuit’s attempt to distinguish *Kloeckner* based on the nature of the dismissal is inconsistent with the Supreme Court’s reasoning. The Court in *Kloeckner* explicitly rejected the argument that the type of dismissal affects the forum for review, stating that “[t]he Board’s jurisdictional determination does not change the nature of the claim.” *Id.* at 51. The Court further noted that its holding applied to all MSPB dismissals, regardless of the ground for dismissal. *Id.* at 50-51.

Finally, the D.C. Circuit’s reliance on *Powell* is misplaced. *Powell* predated *Kloeckner* and was abrogated by the Supreme Court’s decision. *See id.* at 50-51. The *Powell* court’s reasoning—that a dismissal for lack of jurisdiction removes a case from the mixed-case exception—directly conflicts with *Kloeckner* and must be rejected.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully submits that the MSPB’s dismissal of his appeal for lack of jurisdiction is a mixed case subject to review in district court. The D.C. Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Powell* is inconsistent with *Kloeckner* and must be reversed.
