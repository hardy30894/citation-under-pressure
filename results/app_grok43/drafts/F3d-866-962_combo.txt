ARGUMENT

The district court erred in granting summary judgment, as the record presents genuine issues of material fact on protected activity and retaliatory motive.

Summary judgment is not a substitute for trial where the evidence permits conflicting inferences. Poller v. Columbia Broadcasting System, Inc., 368 U.S. 464, 467 (1962). All reasonable inferences must be drawn in favor of the nonmoving party, and credibility determinations are for the trier of fact. Sartor v. Arkansas Natural Gas Corp., 321 U.S. 620, 627 (1944); United States v. Diebold, Inc., 369 U.S. 654, 655 (1962). The employees’ statements to BNSF officials describing the absence of rails and lighting on the bridge constitute reports of unsafe conditions. Such reports of hazardous working conditions by railroad employees are entitled to protection against reprisal. Texas & N.O.R. Co. v. Brotherhood of Railway & Steamship Clerks, 281 U.S. 548, 570 (1930); Virginian Railway Co. v. System Federation No. 40, 300 U.S. 515, 545 (1937).

The timing and nature of the subsequent investigation and discipline raise triable questions of causation. Increased operations testing, altered work assignments, and formal charges followed directly after the reports. Circumstantial evidence of motive, including the sequence of events and the company’s knowledge of the complaints, precludes summary disposition. NLRB v. Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp., 301 U.S. 1, 45-46 (1937). The investigative hearing itself does not eliminate these issues; the conducting officer relied on testimony from sequestered supervisors who disputed the crew-change location, and the discipline was later modified by the Public Law Board. Fair administrative process requires an opportunity to meet the evidence with knowledge of the case against the employee. Morgan v. United States, 304 U.S. 1, 18-19 (1938); Ohio Bell Telephone Co. v. Public Utilities Commission, 301 U.S. 292, 304-05 (1937).

Because the record permits a reasonable trier of fact to find that the employees engaged in protected reporting and suffered adverse action because of it, the grant of summary judgment must be reversed.
