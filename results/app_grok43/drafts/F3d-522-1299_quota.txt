The asserted claims are entitled to the February 6, 1997 filing date of the Original Application under 35 U.S.C. § 120. That statute requires only that the earlier application disclose the invention claimed in the later patent “in the manner provided by the first paragraph of section 112.” *Vas-Cath Inc. v. Mahurkar*, 935 F.2d 1555, 1560 (Fed. Cir. 1991). Compliance turns on whether the Original Application “reasonably conveys to the artisan” that the inventor possessed the claimed subject matter. *Lockwood v. American Airlines, Inc.*, 107 F.3d 1565, 1572 (Fed. Cir. 1997); *In re Gosteli*, 872 F.2d 1008, 1012 (Fed. Cir. 1989). The district court’s contrary conclusion rests on an erroneous claim construction and an unduly narrow view of written-description support.

Claim construction begins with the intrinsic record of the patent in suit. *Phillips v. AWH Corp.*, 415 F.3d 1303, 1314 (Fed. Cir. 2005) (en banc). The agreed construction—“an interface that enables information to be passed between a human user and hardware or software components of a system”—contains no locational limitation. Nothing in the asserted claims or the specifications of the ’658 and ’400 patents requires the interface to reside on the vending machine itself. *Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc.*, 517 U.S. 370, 388 (1996). The district court’s importation of such a limitation from the 2000 CIP’s new matter therefore violated the principle that later-added subject matter cannot retroactively narrow the scope of claims for priority purposes. *Tronzo v. Biomet, Inc.*, 156 F.3d 1154, 1159 (Fed. Cir. 1998).

Once the proper construction is applied, the Original Application supplies the requisite written description. It discloses a vending machine that provides telecommunications access upon customer payment and that communicates status information to the user. A person of ordinary skill would understand that the customer’s own laptop can serve as the interface for receiving that information, because the specification describes the machine as enabling connection to “an external telecommunications device of the customer.” *Gentry Gallery, Inc. v. Berkline Corp.*, 134 F.3d 1473, 1479 (Fed. Cir. 1998) (written description satisfied where specification shows possession of the claimed invention even if not every detail is explicitly stated). No new matter is needed to support this reading.

Because the asserted claims are entitled to the 1997 filing date, the MobileStar Network—first offered for sale in 1999—cannot anticipate them under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b). *Schering Corp. v. Geneva Pharms., Inc.*, 339 F.3d 1373, 1379 (Fed. Cir. 2003). Summary judgment of invalidity was therefore improper.
