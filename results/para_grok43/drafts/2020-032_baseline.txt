**ARGUMENT**

The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that the revision of Georgia Gwinnett College’s policies rendered petitioners’ claims moot. Once a defendant “unambiguously terminated” the challenged conduct and “there is no reasonable basis to expect that it will return,” a live controversy ceases to exist. *Flanigan’s Enters., Inc. of Ga. v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248, 1265 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc). Petitioners do not contest that the revised policies eliminated the speech zones and code-of-conduct provisions they attacked. Their sole remaining contention—that a request for nominal damages preserves jurisdiction—fails under settled precedent.

Article III requires an actual controversy “at all stages of review.” *Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona*, 520 U.S. 43, 67 (1997) (quoting *Preiser v. Rodriguez*, 411 U.S. 475, 491 (1973)). When injunctive and declaratory relief become unavailable, “a claim for nominal damages, which is a symbolic remedy for a past harm, does not save an otherwise moot case.” *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1263. Nominal damages “are not a substitute for a concrete, redressable injury.” *Id.* at 1264. Petitioners alleged no actual injury—monetary loss, emotional distress, or reputational harm—cognizable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. *See Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (compensatory damages must rest on “actual injury caused by the defendant rather than on the ‘abstract value’ of the constitutional rights”).

This Court has repeatedly rejected the notion that a purely symbolic award satisfies the redressability element once the underlying conduct has ended. *See Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc.*, 568 U.S. 85, 92 (2013) (voluntary cessation must leave “no reasonable expectation” of recurrence); *City of Los Angeles v. Lyons*, 461 U.S. 95, 105 (1983) (past exposure to illegal conduct does not confer standing absent continuing effect). Allowing nominal damages alone to revive moot claims would convert every constitutional challenge into a perpetual live controversy, undermining the case-or-controversy limitation.

Petitioners’ attempt to recast their complaint as seeking compensatory damages is unavailing. The prayer for relief requested only “nominal damages,” and the factual allegations identified no concrete harm. The district court properly declined to infer unpled compensatory damages from ambiguous references to “monetary damages.” *See Newton v. Duke Energy Fla., LLC*, 895 F.3d 1270, 1277 (11th Cir. 2018). Nor did the court abuse its discretion in denying an eleventh-hour request to amend embedded in an opposition brief. *Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018).

Because the policies have been superseded and petitioners seek only nominal damages for past abstract injury, no Article III case or controversy remains. The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
