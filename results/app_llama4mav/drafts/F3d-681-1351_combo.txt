ARGUMENT

The Board erred in granting Harari's motion seeking the benefit of the filing date of the '566 application for the '880 application. To determine whether the '880 application is entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date, the Board had to assess whether the intervening '838 and '768 applications sufficiently incorporated the '579 application by reference, satisfying 35 U.S.C. § 120's continuity requirements.

The law is clear that an incorporation by reference must be clear and unambiguous to be effective. As the Supreme Court held in United States v. Dubilier Condenser Corp., 289 U.S. 178, 187 (1933), "the disclosure in the application must be such as to constitute a compliance with the requirements" of the patent statute. In this case, the incorporation statement in the '566 application, which was copied into the subsequent applications, is ambiguous because it refers to an application "filed on the same day as the present application." 

The ambiguity in the incorporation statement is not cured by the fact that the '579 application was indeed filed on the same day as the '566 application. As the court held in Crown Cork & Seal Co. v. Aluminum Stopper Co., 108 F. 845, 848 (4th Cir. 1901), "the mere fact that a prior application contains a disclosure which is relevant to the subject-matter of a later application does not necessarily mean that the later application is entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the prior application." The incorporation statement must be clear and unambiguous, and in this case, it is not.

Furthermore, the court in In re Stauber, 45 F.2d 661, 663 (C.C.P.A. 1930), held that "an applicant cannot rely on an ambiguous disclosure to support his claims." Similarly, in Ex parte Hall, 61 USPQ 247, 248 (P.O. Bd. App. 1943), the Board held that "an incorporation by reference must be clear and definite." The incorporation statement in this case is not clear and definite, and therefore, it is insufficient to incorporate the '579 application by reference.

The '838 and '768 applications did not sufficiently incorporate the '579 application by reference. As a result, the '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date. See Godfrey v. Eames, 68 U.S. 317, 324-25 (1863) (holding that a patent application must comply with the statutory requirements to be entitled to the benefit of an earlier filing date); Ex parte Hall, 61 USPQ at 248.

Moreover, the court in Milburn Co. v. Davis-Bournonville Co., 270 U.S. 390, 401 (1926), held that "the disclosure in a prior application is not available to support a later application unless the prior application is sufficiently identified in the later application." In this case, the '579 application was not sufficiently identified in the '838 and '768 applications.

Additionally, the court in Chapman v. Wintroath, 252 U.S. 126, 137 (1920), held that "the statute requires that the application for the later patent shall be a continuation, continuation-in-part, or divisional of the application for the earlier patent." The '880 application does not meet this requirement because the '838 and '768 applications did not sufficiently incorporate the '579 application by reference.

The Board's decision to grant Harari's motion was incorrect, and this Court should reverse it. The '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date, and therefore, Hollmer's '601 patent takes priority.

As the court held in Alexander Milburn Co. v. Davis-Bournonville Co., 270 U.S. 390, 401 (1926), and Crown Cork & Seal Co. v. Aluminum Stopper Co., 108 F. 845, 848 (4th Cir. 1901), an ambiguous incorporation by reference is insufficient to support a later application. The same principle applies here, and the Board's decision should be reversed.

In conclusion, the Board erred in granting Harari's motion, and this Court should reverse the decision. The '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date due to the ambiguous incorporation statement in the intervening applications.
