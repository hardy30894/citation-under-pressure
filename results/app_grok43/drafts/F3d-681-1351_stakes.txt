ARGUMENT

The Board erred in granting Harari the benefit of the April 13, 1989 filing date of the '566 application. Section 120 requires that each intervening application in a priority chain “contain a specific reference to the earlier filed application” and that the subject matter relied upon be disclosed in each such application. 35 U.S.C. § 120; see also *In re de Seze*, 481 F.2d 966, 968 (C.C.P.A. 1973) (holding that continuity fails when an intervening application does not incorporate necessary subject matter). The '838 and '768 applications contain only the original incorporation statement referring to an application “filed on the same day as the present application.” J.A. 1204. That statement cannot incorporate the '579 application, which was not filed on the same day as either the '838 or '768 application.

This Court’s decision in *Harari v. Hollmer*, 602 F.3d 1348 (Fed. Cir. 2010), does not compel a contrary result. There the Court applied a “reasonable examiner” standard solely because the '880 application was at the “initial filing stage,” where the transmittal sheet and preliminary amendment were before the examiner. *Id.* at 1352-53. The Court expressly limited its holding to that procedural posture. *Id.* at 1353. The '838 and '768 applications present no comparable circumstances; they were examined years earlier without any clarifying amendment or transmittal identifying the '579 application by serial number. Under the ordinary standard, incorporation by reference is effective only when the language “clearly identify[ies]” the referenced document so that a person of ordinary skill can ascertain the subject matter intended to be incorporated. *See In re Lund*, 376 F.2d 982, 989 (C.C.P.A. 1967); *Zenon Envtl., Inc. v. U.S. Filter Corp.*, 506 F.3d 1370, 1379 (Fed. Cir. 2007).

Because the '838 and '768 applications fail to satisfy § 120, the '880 application is entitled at most to the December 20, 1996 filing date of the '708 application. That date is later than Hollmer’s date of conception. The Board’s award of priority to Harari therefore rests on an erroneous legal standard and must be reversed.
