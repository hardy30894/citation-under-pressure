ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act (RRA) and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (RUIA) grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final" decisions of the United States Railroad Retirement Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The Fifth Circuit below held that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application, relying on its prior decision in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003). However, Roberts was wrongly decided, and this Court should now correct that error.

The RRA and RUIA do not explicitly define what constitutes a "final decision." However, the statute's language and relevant case law suggest that a decision denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim is indeed a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

In Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253 (2d Cir. 1966), the Second Circuit held that a Board decision denying a request to reopen a prior claim was reviewable. The court reasoned that the Board's decision was a "final" determination that had exhausted the available administrative remedies. Id. at 254-55. Similarly, in Union Pacific R.R. Co. v. United States, 99 F. Supp. 685 (D. Neb. 1951), the district court held that a Board decision was "final" and subject to judicial review when it had exhausted the available administrative remedies. Id. at 687.

Other circuits have also recognized the importance of judicial review of administrative decisions. In Steward v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 251 F.2d 386 (7th Cir. 1958), the Seventh Circuit held that the Board's decision was subject to judicial review when it had made a "final" determination on a claimant's eligibility. Id. at 388. See also R.R. Retirement Bd. v. Duquesne Truck Service, Inc., 239 F.2d 578 (3d Cir. 1957) (holding that a Board decision was reviewable when it had made a "final" determination on an employer's liability).

The Supreme Court has also emphasized the importance of judicial review of administrative decisions. In United States v. ICC, 337 U.S. 426 (1949), the Court held that an administrative agency's decision was subject to judicial review when it had made a "final" determination. Id. at 433. See also Rochester Tel. Corp. v. United States, 307 U.S. 125 (1939) (holding that an administrative agency's decision was "final" and subject to judicial review when it had exhausted the available administrative remedies).

Furthermore, in St. Joseph Stock Yards Co. v. United States, 298 U.S. 38 (1936), the Supreme Court emphasized the importance of judicial review in ensuring that administrative agencies act within their statutory authority. Id. at 52. See also Ohio Bell Tel. Co. v. Public Utilities Comm'n, 301 U.S. 292 (1937) (holding that an administrative agency's decision must be based on evidence in the record and subject to judicial review).

In light of these authorities, it is clear that a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim is a "final decision" subject to judicial review. The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts was incorrect, and this Court should now correct that error.

The Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a "final" determination that has exhausted the available administrative remedies. Therefore, this Court has jurisdiction to review the Board's decision under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).

This Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision and hold that it has jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application.
