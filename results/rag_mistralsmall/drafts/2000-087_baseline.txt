**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Post-Removal-Period Statute Does Not Authorize Indefinite Detention Beyond the 90-Day Removal Period**

The Supreme Court has long recognized that the Government may detain aliens pending removal, but only for a period reasonably necessary to effectuate that removal. *Zadvydas v. Davis*, 533 U.S. 678, 690 (2001). The Court held that § 1231(a)(6) of the Immigration and Nationality Act, which permits detention beyond the 90-day removal period, must be read to contain an implicit “reasonable time” limitation to avoid serious constitutional concerns. *Id.* at 690–91. The Court further explained that “once removal is no longer reasonably foreseeable, continued detention is no longer authorized by the statute.” *Id.* at 701.

This Court has consistently applied this principle. In *Fong Haw Tan v. Phelan*, 333 U.S. 6, 10 (1948), the Court cautioned that deportation statutes must be construed in light of constitutional protections, and in *Wong Wing v. United States*, 163 U.S. 228, 237 (1896), it held that even administrative detention must comport with due process. The Court’s construction of § 1231(a)(6) in *Zadvydas* is consistent with these precedents, ensuring that detention does not become punitive or indefinite. *Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 690–91.

Here, Petitioner has been detained for years beyond the removal period with no realistic prospect of removal. The Government has conceded that no country will accept him. Under *Zadvydas*, such detention is no longer authorized by statute. *Id.* at 701.

**II. Petitioner’s Indefinite Detention Violates the Due Process Clause**

The Due Process Clause prohibits the Government from detaining individuals indefinitely without justification. *Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 690. The Court has repeatedly held that detention must be tied to a legitimate government purpose, such as ensuring an alien’s presence for removal. *Id.* at 691–92. When removal is not reasonably foreseeable, continued detention serves no legitimate purpose and violates due process.

In *Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei*, 345 U.S. 206, 212 (1953), the Court upheld detention of an excludable alien at the border, but emphasized that such detention was temporary and justified by the alien’s lack of a right to enter. The Court distinguished *Mezei* in *Zadvydas*, noting that Petitioner, unlike Mezei, was a lawful permanent resident with a significant connection to the United States. *Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 700. The Court further explained that indefinite detention of removable aliens would raise serious constitutional concerns. *Id.* at 690.

Petitioner’s detention, now stretching for years beyond the removal period, bears no rational relationship to the Government’s asserted interest in removal. Under *Zadvydas*, this detention violates the Due Process Clause. *Id.* at 701.

**III. The Government’s Reliance on *Mezei* Is Misplaced**

The Government cites *Mezei* for the proposition that detention may continue indefinitely if removal is difficult. However, *Mezei* involved an excludable alien at the border, not a removable alien with deep ties to the United States. *Mezei*, 345 U.S. at 208–09. The Court in *Zadvydas* expressly distinguished *Mezei*, noting that Petitioner’s status as a lawful permanent resident and his long residence in the United States weigh against indefinite detention. *Zadvydas*, 533 U.S. at 700.

Moreover, *Mezei* did not address the constitutional concerns raised by indefinite detention of removable aliens. *Id.* at 699–700. The Court in *Zadvydas* held that such detention would raise serious constitutional questions, requiring a construction of § 1231(a)(6) to avoid those concerns. *Id.* at 690–91. The Government’s reliance on *Mezei* is therefore unavailing.

**IV. The Six-Month Presumption in *Zadvydas* Confirms That Petitioner’s Detention Is Unlawful**

The Court in *Zadvydas* established a presumptively reasonable period of six months for post-removal detention, after which the Government must show a significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future. *Id.* at 705. Here, Petitioner has been detained for years beyond the six-month presumptive period, and the Government has not met its burden. The Court further explained that as the period of detention grows, the “reasonably foreseeable future” shrinks. *Id.*

Petitioner has demonstrated that removal is not reasonably foreseeable. The Government has not rebutted this showing. Under *Zadvydas*, Petitioner’s continued detention is unlawful. *Id.* at 701, 705.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court reverse the judgment below and order his release.
