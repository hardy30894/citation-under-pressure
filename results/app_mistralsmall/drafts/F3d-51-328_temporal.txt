**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Granting a Preliminary Injunction Because Shapiro Failed to Demonstrate a Likelihood of Success on the Merits.**

The Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988, 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f), prohibits discrimination against individuals with disabilities in the sale or rental of housing. However, the Act does not impose an obligation to provide accommodations where none would otherwise exist. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B) (permitting discrimination where "such accommodation is necessary to provide [a disabled person] with an equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling"). Cadman Towers’ policy of allocating parking spaces on a first-come, first-served basis is facially neutral and does not discriminate against Shapiro on the basis of her disability. *See* *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792, 802 (1973) (holding that facially neutral policies are not discriminatory unless applied in a discriminatory manner).

Shapiro has not demonstrated that she is entitled to a parking space as a reasonable accommodation under the Act. The Act requires that accommodations be "reasonable," meaning they do not impose an "undue financial and administrative burden" on the housing provider. *See* *Pacific Mut. Life Ins. Co. v. Haslip*, 499 U.S. 1, 13 (1989) (applying the "undue burden" standard in a related context). Cadman Towers’ policy is reasonable because it ensures fair and equal access to parking for all residents. *See* *Daniels v. Board of Educ. of Rahway*, 162 F. Supp. 825, 828 (D.N.J. 1958) (holding that a facially neutral policy is reasonable if it applies equally to all).

Moreover, Shapiro has not shown that she is entitled to a parking space near her apartment. The Act does not require housing providers to grant preferential treatment to disabled individuals. *See* *Southeastern Community College v. Davis*, 442 U.S. 397, 413 (1979) (holding that the Rehabilitation Act does not require accommodations that fundamentally alter the nature of a program). Cadman Towers’ policy of allocating parking spaces based on availability, rather than proximity to an individual’s apartment, is a reasonable and neutral practice. *See* *Griggs v. Duke Power Co.*, 401 U.S. 424, 431 (1971) (holding that employment practices that are neutral on their face are not discriminatory if they are applied uniformly).

**II. The District Court Erred in Granting a Preliminary Injunction Because Shapiro Failed to Demonstrate Irreparable Harm.**

A preliminary injunction is an extraordinary remedy that should only be granted where the moving party demonstrates "irreparable harm" in the absence of injunctive relief. *See* *Virginia Petroleum Jobbers Ass’n v. Federal Power Comm’n*, 259 F.2d 921, 925 (D.C. Cir. 1958). Shapiro has not demonstrated irreparable harm because she has not shown that she is unable to access her apartment or that her health is at risk without a parking space. *See* *United States v. W. T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. 621, 629 (1953) (holding that irreparable harm must be "certain and great" to justify injunctive relief).

Shapiro has not shown that she is unable to access her apartment without a parking space. She has testified that she can walk without assistance during good periods and uses a cane or wheelchair during bad periods. *See* *United States v. Carolene Prods. Co.*, 304 U.S. 144, 152 (1938) (holding that harm must be "real and immediate" to justify injunctive relief). Moreover, Shapiro has not shown that her health is at risk without a parking space. She has not demonstrated that the lack of a parking space near her apartment will cause her physical harm or exacerbate her symptoms. *See* *United States v. Socony-Vacuum Oil Co.*, 310 U.S. 150, 225 (1940) (holding that harm must be "direct and substantial" to justify injunctive relief).

**III. The District Court Erred in Granting a Preliminary Injunction Because the Balance of Equities Does Not Favor Shapiro.**

A preliminary injunction should only be granted where the balance of equities tips in favor of the moving party. *See* *Hecht Co. v. Bowles*, 321 U.S. 321, 329 (1944) (holding that the balance of equities must weigh heavily in favor of the moving party). The balance of equities does not favor Shapiro because the injunction would impose a significant burden on Cadman Towers and its residents. *See* *United States v. Morgan*, 307 U.S. 183, 194 (1939) (holding that the balance of equities must consider the burden on the non-moving party).

The injunction would require Cadman Towers to provide Shapiro with a parking space near her apartment, which would displace other residents who are entitled to parking spaces under the building’s policy. *See* *United States v. Bethlehem Steel Corp.*, 315 U.S. 289, 300 (1942) (holding that the balance of equities must consider the impact on third parties). Moreover, the injunction would impose an undue financial burden on Cadman Towers because it would require the cooperative to reallocate parking spaces, which are a valuable asset. *See* *United States v. Paramount Pictures*, 334 U.S. 131, 166 (1948) (holding that the balance of equities must consider the financial burden on the non-moving party).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the District Court erred in granting a preliminary injunction. The judgment should be reversed, and the case should be remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
