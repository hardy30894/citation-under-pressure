ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Abused Its Discretion in Striking Appellants’ Experts.

A district court may impose discovery sanctions only after considering lesser alternatives and finding willfulness or bad faith. National Hockey League v. Metropolitan Hockey Club, Inc., 427 U.S. 639, 643 (1976); Societe Internationale Pour Participations Industrielles et Commerciales, S.A. v. Rogers, 357 U.S. 197, 212 (1958). The Agreed Case Management Orders and June 3, 1993 directive did not establish that Appellants acted in bad faith; the experts’ inability to formulate opinions by the noticed dates reflected the volume of documents rather than contumacy. See Coane v. Ferrara Pan Candy Co., 837 F.2d 465, 468 (5th Cir. 1988). Striking four experts without first imposing graduated sanctions therefore exceeded the court’s discretion. See Marshall v. Segona, 621 F.2d 763, 768 (5th Cir. 1980).

II. Exclusion of Drs. Schroeder and Watson Under Daubert Was Error.

Daubert requires the proponent to show that expert testimony is both reliable and relevant. Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., 509 U.S. 579, 589-92 (1993). The district court’s order contains no analysis of the experts’ methodologies or the scientific validity of their principles; it merely noted the missed deposition deadline. Such a procedural default does not substitute for the “flexible” inquiry mandated by Daubert. Id. at 594. The Fifth Circuit has reversed similar exclusions when the record failed to demonstrate that the experts’ opinions lacked grounding in accepted scientific methods. See Christophersen v. Allied-Signal Corp., 939 F.2d 1106, 1115-16 (5th Cir. 1991) (en banc).

III. Summary Judgment Was Premature.

Summary judgment is appropriate only when the nonmovant has had adequate time for discovery and no genuine issue of material fact exists. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322 (1986); Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986). Because the district court struck the very experts needed to create fact issues on causation and damages, Appellants were denied the opportunity to present evidence that would have precluded judgment. See Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp., 475 U.S. 574, 587 (1986). The remaining record therefore could not support the grant of summary judgment.

IV. Appellate Jurisdiction Exists.

A notice of appeal filed after announcement of a decision but before entry of the final judgment that disposes of all claims is effective. FirsTier Mortgage Co. v. Investors Mortgage Ins. Co., 498 U.S. 269, 275 (1991); see also Griggs v. Provident Consumer Discount Co., 459 U.S. 56, 61 (1982). The March 1, 1995 order completed the adjudication of all parties; the October 19, 1994 notice therefore ripened and conferred jurisdiction. Budinich v. Becton Dickinson & Co., 486 U.S. 196, 202 (1988).
