The Court should answer the question presented in the negative. Under a federal fee-shifting statute, a “reasonable” attorneys’ fee may not be enhanced solely because counsel performed well and achieved a strong result. The lodestar—reasonable hours multiplied by a reasonable rate—already captures the ordinary value of counsel’s labor, and the Court has repeatedly held that the lodestar is presumptively sufficient. Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424, 433-37 (1983); Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 897-901 (1984); Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air, 478 U.S. 546, 565-68 (1986).

That rule is rooted in the function of fee shifting itself. A fee award compensates for necessary legal services; it is not a separate reward for excellence. Ex parte Peterson, 253 U.S. 300, 312-13 (1920); Sprague v. Ticonic National Bank, 307 U.S. 161, 166-67 (1939). The Court has repeatedly cautioned that a fee award must be reasonable, not a windfall. Hensley, 461 U.S. at 433-37; Blum, 465 U.S. at 897; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-66.

Blum is the starting point. There, the Court held that “quality of representation” and “results obtained” could not support an enhancement on the record before it because those considerations were already reflected in the lodestar. Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-900. The Court explained that the lodestar “normally” yields a fully compensatory fee. Id. at 897-901. Delaware Valley confirmed that understanding, stating that the lodestar “includes most, if not all, of the relevant factors constituting a ‘reasonable’ attorney’s fee.” 478 U.S. at 566. Together, those decisions foreclose the proposition that superior lawyering and favorable results, standing alone, authorize an upward adjustment. Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-900; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-68.

Hensley reinforces the same point. There, the Court held that the “degree of success obtained” is central to the reasonableness inquiry, but it discussed that factor in assessing whether the hours claimed were justified and whether a reduction was warranted for partial success. Hensley, 461 U.S. at 434-37. The opinion did not create a second, duplicative pathway to increase a fee after the lodestar has already been set. Id. at 433-37. To allow enhancement for the same “success” already reflected in the lodestar would count the same factor twice. Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-900; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-68.

Older authority points the same way. In Johnson v. Georgia Highway Express, Inc., 488 F.2d 714, 717-19 (5th Cir. 1974), the court listed “the skill requisite to perform the legal service properly” and “the amount involved and the results obtained” among the considerations relevant to a fee. But Johnson predates the lodestar framework and does not authorize a post-lodestar premium for the same considerations. Id. at 717-19; Hensley, 461 U.S. at 433-37. Likewise, King v. Greenblatt, 560 F.2d 1024, 1027-28 (1st Cir. 1977), and Fitzpatrick v. Bitzer, 427 U.S. 445, 456-57 (1976), treated fee awards as compensation for legal services rendered, not as a bonus for exceptional performance. See also Lindy Bros. Builders, Inc. v. American Radiator & Standard Sanitary Corp., 487 F.2d 161, 167-69 (3d Cir. 1973); Lindy Bros. Builders, Inc. v. American Radiator & Standard Sanitary Corp., 540 F.2d 102, 117-18 (3d Cir. 1976).

The contrary rule would undermine the lodestar’s purpose. If enhancement may rest solely on counsel’s quality and the magnitude of the result, then every successful fee case invites a discretionary premium untethered to objective indicators of market value. That approach would reintroduce the uncertainty the Court sought to eliminate by adopting the lodestar method. Blum, 465 U.S. at 897-901; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-68. It would also expand fee shifting beyond the narrow limits the Court has imposed under the American Rule. Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240, 262-71 (1975); Arcambel v. Wiseman, 3 U.S. (3 Dall.) 306 (1796).

The Court should therefore hold that quality of performance and results obtained may inform the lodestar calculation, but they cannot, standing alone, justify enhancing a fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute. Hensley, 461 U.S. at 433-37; Blum, 465 U.S. at 897-901; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-68.
