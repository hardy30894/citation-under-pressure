The Federal Circuit erred in affirming the district court's award of damages to Apple based on Samsung's total profits from the sale of its smartphones. The Patent Act requires that damages for design patent infringement be limited to the total profits attributable to the infringing design, not the total profits from the sale of the product. 35 U.S.C. § 289.

In this case, the design patents at issue relate to specific components of Samsung's smartphones, such as the screen and bezel design. The jury awarded Apple damages based on Samsung's total profits from the sale of its smartphones, rather than limiting damages to the profits attributable to the infringing design. This was error.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that the purpose of awarding profits in design patent cases is to provide a remedy for the specific infringement, not to punish the infringer for unrelated conduct. See Dobson v. Dornan, 118 U.S. 10, 16 (1886) (explaining that the award of profits is "limited to the profits derived from the manufacture, sale, or use of the infringing article"). The Federal Circuit's decision to affirm the award of damages based on total profits is inconsistent with this principle.

Furthermore, the Federal Circuit's decision is also inconsistent with the language of 35 U.S.C. § 289, which provides that the infringer shall be liable for "the total profit." The statute does not specify that the total profit is to be calculated based on the sale of the entire product, rather than the component that infringes the design patent. In fact, the statute's focus on "the article" that infringes the design patent suggests that the profits attributable to that specific article are the relevant measure of damages. See 35 U.S.C. § 289.

Other circuits have recognized that damages for design patent infringement should be limited to the profits attributable to the infringing design. For example, in Schnadig Corp. v. Gaines Manufacturing Co., 620 F.2d 1166 (6th Cir. 1980), the Sixth Circuit held that the district court erred in awarding damages based on the total sales of the infringing product, rather than limiting damages to the profits attributable to the infringing design. Id. at 1171.

The Federal Circuit's decision to affirm the award of damages based on Samsung's total profits from the sale of its smartphones is also inconsistent with the principles of apportionment recognized in other areas of patent law. See Garretson v. Clark, 111 U.S. 120, 121 (1884) (explaining that damages for patent infringement should be apportioned to reflect the value of the infringing feature).

In conclusion, the Federal Circuit erred in affirming the district court's award of damages to Apple based on Samsung's total profits from the sale of its smartphones. Damages for design patent infringement should be limited to the profits attributable to the infringing design, not the total profits from the sale of the product. This Court should reverse the Federal Circuit's decision and remand for a determination of damages based on the profits attributable to the infringing design.
