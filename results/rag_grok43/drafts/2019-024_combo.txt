The three-year limitations period under 29 U.S.C. § 1113(2) begins on the earliest date a plaintiff possesses actual knowledge of the breach. That period is measured from the transaction or disclosure itself. Baker v. Cummings, 181 U.S. 117, 125 (1901) (knowledge arises from the facts placed before the party). When plan documents revealing the investments and strategy were furnished to Sulyma more than three years before suit, the bar attached regardless of whether he opened the files. Exploration Co. v. United States, 247 U.S. 435, 446 (1918) (limitations runs from the date the operative facts occurred, even if later discussion revealed details).

This Court has repeatedly rejected the notion that subjective unreadiness or forgetfulness postpones accrual once the information is supplied. American Surety Co. v. Pauly, 170 U.S. 160, 171 (1898) (notice given in writing fixes the date of knowledge). Unexcelled Chemical Corp. v. United States, 345 U.S. 59, 65 (1953) (cause of action accrues upon the act that supplies the facts, not upon later realization of their significance). United States v. A. S. Kreider Co., 313 U.S. 443, 448 (1941) (statute commences when the operative events are complete and known to the party).

The same principle governs claims resting on alleged imprudence or failure to monitor. Curtis v. Connly, 257 U.S. 260, 263 (1921) (limitations measured from the date the challenged acts took place and were recorded). United States v. Wurts, 303 U.S. 414, 416 (1938) (period runs from the date the facts became available, not from subsequent discovery of their legal effect). United States v. Goldman, 277 U.S. 229, 236 (1928) (actual knowledge exists once the documents containing the facts are delivered). Jaben v. United States, 381 U.S. 214, 220 (1965) (limitations is not tolled by a party’s failure to examine materials already in hand).

Because the disclosures occurred before October 29, 2012, the action filed in 2015 is time-barred. The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
