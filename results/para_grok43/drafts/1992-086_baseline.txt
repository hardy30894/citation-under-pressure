ARGUMENT

The School District’s denial of access to its facilities for the exhibition of films addressing family values from a religious perspective violated the First Amendment. The facilities constitute a limited public forum. In such a forum, “the first amendment protections provided to traditional public forums only apply to entities of a character similar to those the government admits to the forum.” Calash v. City of Bridgeport, 788 F.2d 80, 82 (2d Cir. 1986); see Perry Education Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n, 460 U.S. 37, 46 (1983). Once the government opens property “for use by the public for expressive activity,” regulation of speech must satisfy the standards applicable to traditional public forums. Perry, 460 U.S. at 46.

The District permitted numerous community groups to use its facilities for lectures, performances, and discussions on child-rearing, psychology, and social issues. These uses opened the forum to speech on family and moral development. Excluding Lamb’s Chapel’s films solely because they approached the same subjects from a religious viewpoint constitutes impermissible viewpoint discrimination. See Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund, Inc., 473 U.S. 788, 806 (1985) (“[T]he government violates the First Amendment when it denies access to a speaker solely to suppress the point of view he espouses on an otherwise includible subject.”).

The District’s reliance on New York Education Law § 414 and Local Rule 7 does not alter the analysis. A state may not “enforce a content-based exclusion” from a limited public forum unless the restriction is “necessary to serve a compelling state interest and . . . narrowly drawn to achieve that end.” Perry, 460 U.S. at 45. No such interest justifies barring religious perspectives on topics already addressed by secular groups. Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263, 277 (1981) (holding that a university’s “equal access” policy for religious speech did not violate the Establishment Clause and that exclusion on religious grounds was unconstitutional).

The District’s prior allowance of events with incidental religious elements, while denying Lamb’s Chapel’s explicitly religious films, confirms that the line drawn is viewpoint-based rather than content-neutral. Because the films concern subjects the forum has been opened to discuss, the denial abridges the freedom of speech guaranteed by the First Amendment.
