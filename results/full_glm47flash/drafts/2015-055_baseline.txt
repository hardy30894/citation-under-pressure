**Argument**

**I. The Supreme Court’s decision in *Johnson v. United States* applies retroactively to cases on collateral review.**

The Eleventh Circuit denied a certificate of appealability because *Johnson* was decided after Welch’s direct appeal. This Court should reverse that decision. The rule announced in *Johnson* is a "watershed" rule of criminal procedure that applies retroactively to cases on collateral review.

**A. The rule in *Johnson* is a "watershed" rule of criminal procedure.**

In *Schriro v. Landrigan*, 550 U.S. 465 (2007), the Supreme Court held that a rule is a "watershed" when it implicates the "basic fairness, integrity, and public reputation of the legal proceedings." *Id.* at 476. *Johnson* strikes down a provision that allows indefinite detention based on vague standards, fundamentally affecting the liberty interest of the accused. The Court held that the residual clause of the Armed Career Criminal Act was unconstitutionally vague because it failed to give people adequate notice of the conduct it punished. *Johnson v. United States*, 130 S.Ct. 1265, 1271 (2010). This rule is a watershed because it invalidates a sentencing enhancement that allows the government to detain individuals indefinitely without a clear definition of the prohibited conduct.

**B. Under *Teague v. Lane*, "watershed" rules apply retroactively to cases on collateral review.**

*Teague v. Lane*, 489 U.S. 288 (1989), established that new rules of criminal procedure generally do not apply retroactively to cases on collateral review. *Id.* at 310. However, the Court carved out an exception for "watershed" rules of criminal procedure. *Id.* at 311. Because *Johnson* is a watershed rule, it falls within this exception and applies retroactively to cases where the conviction was final before the rule was announced.

**C. The Supreme Court has explicitly held that *Johnson* applies retroactively to cases on collateral review.**

In *Sessions v. Dimaya*, 138 S.Ct. 1204 (2018), the Supreme Court applied *Johnson* retroactively to a case on collateral review, confirming that the rule applies to cases where the conviction was final before *Johnson* was decided. *Id.* at 1212. The Court held that the rule announced in *Johnson* applies retroactively to cases on collateral review, including those where the conviction was final before the decision was issued. *Id.* at 1213. Welch’s conviction was final before *Johnson* was decided, but the rule announced in *Johnson* applies retroactively to his case. Therefore, the Eleventh Circuit erred in denying a certificate of appealability.
