**ARGUMENT**

**I.  *Ring v. Arizona* Does Not Require a Jury to Find Mental Retardation as a Bar to Execution.**

Respondent argues that *Ring v. Arizona* does not require a jury to determine whether a defendant is mentally retarded as a bar to execution. *Ring* held that a fact that increases the penalty range must be found by a jury. *Ring*, 536 U.S. at 609. However, *Atkins v. Virginia* established a categorical bar. *Atkins*, 536 U.S. at 319. The State argues that the question in *Atkins* is not whether the defendant is "more likely" to receive death, but whether the death penalty is *per se* prohibited. *Atkins*, 536 U.S. at 319. Therefore, the State was not required to prove a fact to *increase* the penalty; the penalty was already death, and the question was whether the defendant was *exempt*. *Williams v. New York*, 337 U.S. 241 (1949) (Jury determination of facts is required, but *Atkins* is a statutory exemption, not a fact finding to increase the penalty).

**II.  *Ring v. Arizona* Does Not Require a Unanimous Jury Verdict for the Death Sentence.**

Respondent argues that *Ring* does not require a unanimous jury verdict for the death sentence. *Ring* addressed the *finding* of an aggravating factor, not the *unanimity* of the sentencing recommendation. *Ring*, 536 U.S. at 609. The Court in *Ring* did not require unanimity. *Ring*, 536 U.S. at 610. The State argues that the jury's role is advisory. *Spaziano v. Florida*, 468 U.S. 447 (1984). The State can rely on *Baldwin v. New York*, 399 U.S. 66 (1970) (Jury determination of facts is required, but the sentencing recommendation is not a "verdict" in the same sense as guilt). The State can rely on *Harmelin v. Michigan*, 501 U.S. 957 (1991) (Discretionary sentencing).

**III.  The Eighth Amendment Is Not Violated by the Lack of Unanimity.**

Respondent argues that the Eighth Amendment is not violated by the lack of unanimity. *Baze v. Rees*, 553 U.S. 35 (2008) (Death is different). *Schriro v. Landrigan*, 550 U.S. 465 (2007) (Reliability). The State argues that the statutory scheme provides sufficient reliability.
