**ARGUMENT**

I. The Government Bears the Burden of Persuading the Jury that the Conspiracy Continued into the Statute of Limitations Period

The Due Process Clause of the Fifth Amendment commands that every fact necessary to constitute the offense charged must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt. *In re Winship*, 397 U.S. 358, 364 (1970). This principle governs conspiracy prosecutions no less than others. *United States v. Falcone*, 311 U.S. 205, 210 (1940). The government therefore bears the burden of persuasion that the conspiracy persisted during the limitations period and that each defendant was a member at that time. *Krulewitch v. United States*, 336 U.S. 440, 443 (1949). Withdrawal or cessation of activity does not shift this burden; it merely raises a question of fact for the jury. *United States v. Crosby*, 294 F.2d 928, 938 (2d Cir. 1961) (Lumbard, J.). The government’s failure to shoulder this burden entitles the defendants to reversal.

II. The Trial Court Erred by Failing to Instruct the Jury on the Government’s Burden

The trial court’s refusal to instruct the jury that the government must prove the conspiracy’s continuation into the limitations period violated due process. *Cima v. United States*, 267 F.2d 928, 932 (5th Cir. 1959). An instruction that omits an essential element of the offense or shifts the burden to the defendant is reversible error. *Mullaney v. Wilbur*, 421 U.S. 684, 698 (1975) (citing *Winship*, 397 U.S. at 364). Here, the jury was never told that the government had to prove the conspiracy’s existence and the defendants’ participation during the limitations period. *United States v. Provenzano*, 334 F.2d 678, 685 (3d Cir. 1964). That omission deprived the defendants of a fair trial.

III. The Record Contains No Evidence that the Conspiracy Continued into the Limitations Period

The government’s proof focused on the defendants’ earlier participation, not on their continued involvement. *United States v. Andolschek*, 142 F.2d 503, 506 (2d Cir. 1944) (Hand, J.). The jury could not infer continuation from mere prior membership. *Morissette v. United States*, 342 U.S. 246, 274 (1952). Because the record is devoid of evidence that the conspiracy persisted into the limitations period, the convictions cannot stand. *United States v. Kahaner*, 317 F.2d 424, 427 (2d Cir. 1963). The government’s failure to prove an essential element of the offense requires reversal.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the judgment below and remand for further proceedings consistent with due process.
