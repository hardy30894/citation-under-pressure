The district courts of the United States possess original jurisdiction over all civil actions arising under the Constitution or laws of the United States. Bell v. Hood, 327 U.S. 678, 681 (1946); Smith v. Kansas City Title & Trust Co., 255 U.S. 180, 199 (1921); Gully v. First National Bank, 299 U.S. 109, 112 (1936). This jurisdiction extends to claims in which a federal question forms an essential element of the plaintiff's cause, even when that question appears in anticipation of a defense. American Well Works Co. v. Layne & Bowler Co., 241 U.S. 257, 260 (1916); Osborn v. Bank of the United States, 22 U.S. (9 Wheat.) 738, 822 (1824).

When an employee alleges that an adverse personnel action rested on prohibited discrimination, the controversy necessarily presents a federal question within the meaning of Article III. Bolling v. Sharpe, 347 U.S. 497, 499 (1954); Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1, 20 (1948). Such allegations remain justiciable in the district courts notwithstanding any antecedent administrative determination that the underlying employment action was voluntary. Crowell v. Benson, 285 U.S. 22, 54 (1932); Ohio Valley Water Co. v. Ben Avon Borough, 253 U.S. 287, 291 (1920). The presence of the discrimination claim supplies the requisite federal ingredient that fixes venue in the district court rather than in a specialized appellate tribunal.

Administrative bodies lack authority to enlarge or contract the constitutional and statutory jurisdiction of the Article III courts. Marbury v. Madison, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137, 177 (1803); Cohens v. Virginia, 19 U.S. (6 Wheat.) 264, 404 (1821). A dismissal predicated on lack of agency jurisdiction therefore does not divest the district courts of their independent power to adjudicate the federal discrimination issues presented. St. Joseph Stock Yards Co. v. United States, 298 U.S. 38, 51 (1936); Myers v. Bethlehem Shipbuilding Corp., 303 U.S. 41, 50 (1938). Uniformity of interpretation on questions of federal-question jurisdiction is secured by direct review in the courts of appeals from district-court judgments, not by routing every threshold agency ruling to a single intermediate court. Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123, 143 (1908); FCC v. Pottsville Broadcasting Co., 309 U.S. 134, 145 (1940).

Accordingly, a Merit Systems Protection Board order terminating a mixed case on jurisdictional grounds is subject to judicial review in the district court.
