I. The Board’s Denial of Reopening Constitutes a Reviewable Final Decision Under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).

Section 355(f) authorizes judicial review of “any final decision of the Board” upon a petition filed in the appropriate court of appeals. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application satisfies this text: it is the Board’s last word on that claim and terminates all administrative proceedings concerning it. See Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003) (describing the decision as one “declining to reopen a prior claim for benefits”).

The statute contains no limitation restricting review to decisions rendered “after a hearing.” Cf. Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99, 108 (1977) (construing 42 U.S.C. § 405(g), which expressly requires a “final decision … made after a hearing”). Because Congress omitted that phrase from § 355(f), the provision encompasses the Board’s final disposition of a reopening request. See Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 826 F.3d 500, 503 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (contrasting the two statutes and concluding that “the plain language of the Railroad Retirement Act grants jurisdiction”).

This reading accords with the presumption that “judicial review of a final agency action will not be cut off unless there is persuasive reason to believe that such was the purpose of Congress.” Abbott Labs. v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136, 140 (1967). The Board’s regulations themselves treat a denial of reopening as the conclusion of the administrative process, 20 C.F.R. § 261.2, confirming that no further agency action remains available.

The contrary view adopted in Roberts and similar decisions rests on an atextual importation of the hearing requirement from the Social Security Act. That importation cannot override the plain language Congress enacted in § 355(f). Accordingly, the Fifth Circuit erred in dismissing the petition for lack of jurisdiction.
