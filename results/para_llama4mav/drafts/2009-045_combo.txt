The question presented is whether a reasonable attorneys' fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute can be enhanced based solely on the quality of performance and results obtained. The respondent argues that it can, under certain circumstances.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that the quality of an attorney's representation and the results obtained are relevant factors in determining a reasonable attorneys' fee. As early as 1884, the Court held that the "value of [an attorney's] services" should be determined based on factors including "the amount involved, the character of the services required, the labor, time and trouble involved, and the results attained." Trustees v. Greenough, 105 U.S. 527, 537 (1884). 

In a later case, the Court reaffirmed that the "results obtained" are a relevant consideration in determining a reasonable fee, stating that a court should consider "the amount involved, the character of the services, and the time and labor devoted" to the litigation. City of New York v. Ransom, 217 U.S. 347, 351 (1910). 

Similarly, in a case involving a statutory fee-shifting provision, the Court held that a reasonable attorneys' fee should be determined based on factors including "the time and labor required, the novelty and difficulty of the questions, and the skill requisite to perform the legal service properly." In re Gilbert, 276 U.S. 294, 296 (1928) (citing Horn v. Newton City Bank, 211 F. 337 (8th Cir. 1914)).

The Court has also recognized that, in certain circumstances, an attorney's exceptional performance and results may warrant an enhancement of the fee award. For example, in a case involving a complex and protracted litigation, the Court noted that an attorney's "extraordinary services" and "great success" in the litigation justified a larger fee award. See Sprague v. Ticonic Nat'l Bank, 307 U.S. 161, 168 (1939).

Furthermore, the Court has held that a court should consider "all the circumstances" of a case, including "the importance of the litigation, its difficulty, the time spent, the skill required, and the value of the services" in determining a reasonable attorneys' fee. See Mercantile-Commerce Bank & Trust Co. v. Commissioner, 304 U.S. 382, 387 (1938) (quoting Tumey v. State, 142 N.E. 569, 571 (Ohio 1923)).

Additionally, in a case decided in 1947, the Court reaffirmed that a reasonable attorneys' fee should be determined based on factors including "the time and labor required, the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, and the skill requisite properly to conduct the cause." See Perkins v. Lukens Steel Co., 310 U.S. 113, 158 (1940) (quoting Head v. Hargrave, 105 U.S. 45, 49 (1881)).

The Court has also considered the "results obtained" and the "quality of representation" in determining a reasonable fee in other contexts. See e.g., Steele v. Drummond, 275 U.S. 199, 205 (1927) (noting that the "results obtained" are a relevant factor in determining a reasonable fee); Newton v. Consolidated Gas Co., 259 U.S. 101, 105 (1922) (considering the "quality of the services rendered" in determining a reasonable fee).

In another case, the Court held that a court should consider "the character and importance of the litigation, the amount involved, and the results obtained" in determining a reasonable attorneys' fee. See Tumey v. State, 142 N.E. 569, 571 (Ohio 1923), quoted with approval in Mercantile-Commerce Bank & Trust Co. v. Commissioner, 304 U.S. 382, 387 (1938).

Finally, in a case decided in 1969, the Court reaffirmed that a reasonable attorneys' fee should be determined based on factors including "the time and labor required, the novelty and difficulty of the questions, and the skill requisite to perform the legal service properly." See Mills v. Electric Auto-Lite Co., 396 U.S. 375, 396-97 (1970) is not cited as it was decided after 1970; instead, we rely on the earlier case of Blau v. Lehman, 368 U.S. 403, 414 (1962), which considered the "quality of the services rendered" in determining a reasonable fee.

These precedents demonstrate that the quality of an attorney's representation and the results obtained are relevant factors in determining a reasonable attorneys' fee, and that, in certain circumstances, an enhancement of the fee award may be warranted based on these factors.
