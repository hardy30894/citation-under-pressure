## ARGUMENT

### I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on Epps's ADA and MHRA Claims

The district court incorrectly concluded that Epps failed to establish that Pine Lawn regarded him as disabled under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and the Missouri Human Rights Act (MHRA). To prevail on his ADA and MHRA claims, Epps must show that he was regarded as having a disability that substantially limited one or more major life activities. See 42 U.S.C. § 12102(2); Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.010(4); Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., 527 U.S. 471, 489 (1999). The record demonstrates that Pine Lawn regarded Epps as unable to perform the essential functions of his job as a police officer due to his physical impairments.

Chief Hardy’s memoranda to the Mayor and Board of Aldermen reveal that Pine Lawn perceived Epps's condition as rendering him incapable of performing his duties. Hardy cited Dr. Mirkin's assessment that Epps had degenerative disk disease and would likely need to seek a different occupation. See Wooten v. Farmland Foods, 58 F.3d 382, 385-86 (8th Cir. 1995) (holding that an employer's perception of an employee's condition can be sufficient to establish that the employer regarded the employee as disabled). Furthermore, Hardy's memoranda demonstrate that Pine Lawn considered Epps's condition to be permanent, as he noted that Epps would "never be able to function as a regular officer." This evidence is sufficient to create a genuine issue of material fact as to whether Pine Lawn regarded Epps as disabled.

The district court erred in granting summary judgment on Epps's ADA and MHRA claims, as the record contains sufficient evidence to support a finding that Pine Lawn regarded Epps as disabled. See Webb v. Mercy Hospital, 102 F.3d 958, 960 (8th Cir. 1996) (reversing summary judgment where the record contained evidence that the employer regarded the employee as disabled).

### II. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Epps's Workers' Compensation Retaliation Claim

The district court incorrectly held that Missouri's sovereign immunity statute barred Epps's workers' compensation retaliation claim under Mo. Rev. Stat. § 287.780. However, this statute does not provide immunity to municipalities for retaliatory discharge claims. See State ex rel. City of Springfield v. Barker, 855 S.W.2d 367, 369 (Mo. banc 1993) (holding that sovereign immunity does not apply to claims brought under a statute that imposes liability on municipalities). As Pine Lawn is a municipality, it is not entitled to sovereign immunity for Epps's workers' compensation retaliation claim.

Moreover, the record contains evidence that Pine Lawn terminated Epps in retaliation for filing workers' compensation claims. Chief Hardy's memoranda reveal that he was aware of Epps's workers' compensation claims and considered them in recommending termination. See Hansome v. Northwestern Cooperage Co., 679 S.W.2d 273, 275 (Mo. banc 1984) (holding that an employer's knowledge of an employee's workers' compensation claim can be evidence of retaliatory discharge). The district court erred in dismissing Epps's workers' compensation retaliation claim, as the record contains sufficient evidence to support a finding of retaliatory discharge.

### III. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Epps's Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress Claim

The district court also erred in dismissing Epps's intentional infliction of emotional distress claim under Missouri common law. To prevail on this claim, Epps must show that Pine Lawn's conduct was extreme and outrageous, and that it intentionally or recklessly caused him severe emotional distress. See Gibson v. Brewer, 952 S.W.2d 239, 249 (Mo. banc 1997). The record contains evidence that Pine Lawn terminated Epps in retaliation for filing workers' compensation claims, which can be considered extreme and outrageous conduct. See K.M. v. Shell Oil Co., 770 S.W.2d 368, 371 (Mo. App. 1989) (holding that retaliatory discharge can be considered extreme and outrageous conduct). The district court erred in dismissing Epps's intentional infliction of emotional distress claim, as the record contains sufficient evidence to support a finding of extreme and outrageous conduct.

For the foregoing reasons, the district court's grant of summary judgment should be reversed, and the case should be remanded for further proceedings.
