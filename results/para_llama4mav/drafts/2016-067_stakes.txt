The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) does not apply to Santander Consumer USA, Inc. (Santander) because it is not a "debt collector" as defined by the statute. The FDCPA distinguishes between "debt collectors" and "creditors," with the former collecting debts on behalf of others and the latter collecting debts owed to themselves. 

The definition of "debt collector" under 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6) includes three main categories: (1) a person whose principal purpose is to collect debts; (2) a person who regularly collects debts owed to another; and (3) a person who collects its own debts using a name other than its own. The petitioners argue that Santander falls under the second category because it acquired defaulted debts from CitiFinancial Auto. However, the statute's language indicates that a debt collector is one who regularly collects debts "owed or due another." The plain meaning of this phrase requires that the debt be owed to another at the time of collection, not just at its origination. 

As the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals correctly held, Santander is not a debt collector because it collected debts owed to itself, not to another. See Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc., 817 F.3d 131 (4th Cir. 2016). The complaint alleges that Santander purchased the loans from CitiFinancial Auto and then attempted to collect on them for its own account. This is consistent with the definition of a creditor under 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(4), which includes a person to whom a debt is owed.

Other circuits have reached similar conclusions. In Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A., 797 F.3d 1309, 1315-16 (11th Cir. 2015), the Eleventh Circuit held that a person must regularly collect or attempt to collect debts for others to qualify as a "debt collector" under the second definition. The Ninth Circuit in Schlegel v. Wells Fargo Bank, NA, 720 F.3d 1204, 1209 (9th Cir. 2013), also rejected the argument that "owed or due another" means "originally owed or due another."

The petitioners' argument that Santander's status as a debt collector before purchasing the loans makes it a debt collector afterward is unpersuasive. The FDCPA regulates debt collectors when they are acting as such, not for all their activities. See 15 U.S.C. § 1692(e). Santander's collection activities on its own behalf are not subject to the FDCPA.

In conclusion, Santander is not a "debt collector" under the FDCPA because it collected debts owed to itself, not to another. The Fourth Circuit's decision should be affirmed.
