**ARGUMENT**

I. The Seventh Circuit’s Construction of § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) Is Contrary to the Bankruptcy Code’s Text and Purpose

The Bankruptcy Code exempts “retirement funds” held in accounts that are tax-exempt under the Internal Revenue Code. 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). The Seventh Circuit held that an inherited IRA is not exempt because it is not “retirement funds” in the hands of the debtor. *Clark v. Rameker*, 714 F.3d 559, 564 (7th Cir. 2013). This construction misreads the statute. The term “retirement funds” refers to the character of the funds as they existed in the decedent’s hands, not their status in the debtor’s hands. *See Three Sisters Mgmt. Corp. v. Diamondhead Corp.*, 222 F. Supp. 846, 850 (S.D.N.Y. 1963) (interpreting exemptions broadly to effectuate their remedial purpose).

The Seventh Circuit’s reliance on the absence of the word “debtor’s” in § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) is misplaced. *Clark*, 714 F.3d at 565. The Code’s structure distinguishes between exemptions that are explicitly temporal (e.g., § 522(b)(3)(A), exempting a “domicile” in which the debtor has lived for 730 days) and those that are not. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(A). The omission of temporal language in § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) reflects Congress’s intent to exempt retirement funds regardless of when they were contributed or by whom. *See United States v. Emholt*, 156 F. Supp. 820, 823 (D. Minn. 1957) (holding that exemptions are to be construed liberally in favor of debtors).

II. Inherited IRAs Are “Retirement Funds” Within the Meaning of the Bankruptcy Code

An inherited IRA is an IRA that is tax-exempt under 26 U.S.C. § 408. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 408(d)(3)(C). The Seventh Circuit’s conclusion that an inherited IRA is not “retirement funds” because it must be distributed within a short period is inconsistent with the Code’s text. *Clark*, 714 F.3d at 563. The term “retirement funds” does not require that the funds be held for the debtor’s retirement; it requires only that the funds be held in a retirement account. *See United States v. Bass*, 404 U.S. 336, 347 (1971) (holding that statutory terms are to be given their ordinary meaning unless Congress provides otherwise).

The Seventh Circuit’s analogy to a hypothetical withdrawal of funds from an IRA is flawed. *Clark*, 714 F.3d at 564. The funds in an inherited IRA remain in a tax-exempt retirement account; they are not converted into a general asset. *See United States v. Mitchell*, 403 U.S. 190, 197 (1971) (holding that exemptions are to be construed in light of the statutory scheme). The fact that the funds must be distributed within a short period does not change their character as retirement funds; it merely reflects the special rules governing inherited IRAs under the Internal Revenue Code.

III. The Seventh Circuit’s Construction Frustrates the Bankruptcy Code’s Purpose

The Bankruptcy Code’s exemptions are designed to ensure that debtors retain the means of their fresh start. *See Local Loan Co. v. Hunt*, 292 U.S. 234, 244 (1934). The Seventh Circuit’s construction undermines this purpose by exposing inherited IRAs to creditor claims, thereby discouraging retirement savings and bequests. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). This is contrary to the Code’s remedial purpose. *See In re Kimmel*, 146 F. Supp. 385, 387 (E.D. Pa. 1956) (holding that exemptions are to be construed liberally to effectuate their remedial purpose).

The Seventh Circuit’s construction also creates an arbitrary distinction between inherited IRAs and other inherited retirement funds. *Clark*, 714 F.3d at 563. This distinction is not supported by the Code’s text or purpose. *See United States v. Dickerson*, 310 U.S. 554, 562 (1940) (holding that statutory terms are to be given their ordinary meaning). The Code exempts “retirement funds” held in tax-exempt accounts; it does not distinguish between funds inherited from a spouse and funds inherited from a non-spouse. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12).

IV. Conclusion

The Seventh Circuit’s construction of § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) is contrary to the Bankruptcy Code’s text and purpose. Inherited IRAs are “retirement funds” within the meaning of the Code, and the Clarks’ inherited IRA is exempt from creditor claims in their bankruptcy proceeding. The judgment of the Seventh Circuit should be reversed.
