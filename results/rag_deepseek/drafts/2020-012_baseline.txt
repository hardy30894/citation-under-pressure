**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Board’s Decision Not to Reopen a Prior Benefits Claim Is a “Final Decision” Subject to Judicial Review Under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).**

The plain text of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) grants courts of appeals jurisdiction to review “any final decision of the Board.” A decision denying a request to reopen a prior claim is a “final decision” because it conclusively resolves the claimant’s request for reconsideration of a prior denial. The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding in *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), misreads the statute and conflicts with the D.C. Circuit’s well-reasoned decision in *Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016).

**A. The Text of § 355(f) Does Not Exclude Reopening Decisions.**

Section 355(f) provides that “[a]ny party aggrieved by a final decision of the Board may obtain review of such decision in the United States Court of Appeals.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The statute contains no language limiting review to “merits” decisions or to decisions made after a hearing. The Fifth Circuit in *Roberts* imported such a limitation from the Social Security context, relying on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977). But *Sanders* interpreted a different statute—42 U.S.C. § 405(g)—which expressly conditions review on a “final decision of the Secretary made after a hearing.” 430 U.S. at 108 (emphasis added). Section 355(f) contains no such “after a hearing” requirement. The absence of that language is dispositive: Congress knew how to limit review to decisions made after a hearing, as it did in the Social Security Act, and chose not to do so in the Railroad Retirement Act. *See Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 503 (noting that “§ 355(f) does not contain the ‘after a hearing’ language that the Supreme Court found dispositive in *Sanders*”).

**B. The Board’s Reopening Decision Is Final.**

A decision is “final” when it “ends the litigation on the merits and leaves nothing for the court to do but execute the judgment.” *Henderson v. Shinseki*, 562 U.S. 428, 434 (2011). The Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim meets that standard. The Board’s hearing officer concluded that the 2006 application was “beyond the four-year timeframe for reopening based on new and material evidence or administrative error.” That decision resolved the reopening request with finality; no further administrative steps remained. *See Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504 (holding that a Board decision denying reopening is “final” because it “disposes of the request to reopen and leaves nothing for the Board to do”).

**C. The Majority Rule Relied on by the Fifth Circuit Is Incorrect.**

The Fifth Circuit in *Roberts* joined a majority of circuits in holding that reopening decisions are unreviewable. *See* 346 F.3d at 141 (citing *Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 198 F.3d 139 (4th Cir. 1999); *Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 63 F.3d 972 (10th Cir. 1995); *Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 918 F.2d 567 (6th Cir. 1990); *Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 708 F.2d 250 (7th Cir. 1983)). But those decisions, like *Roberts*, improperly imported the *Sanders* framework into the Railroad Retirement Act. As the D.C. Circuit recognized, *Sanders* “does not control the interpretation of § 355(f)” because the two statutes are materially different. *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 503. The Second and Eighth Circuits have likewise held that reopening decisions are reviewable. *See Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 253, 254-55 (2d Cir. 1966); *Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991). The D.C. Circuit’s approach is the better one: it respects the statutory text and avoids the anomaly of insulating agency decisions from any judicial scrutiny.

**D. Policy Considerations Support Reviewability.**

Denying review of reopening decisions would allow the Board to act arbitrarily without any check. As this Court has recognized, “the opportunity to reopen final decisions . . . is afforded by the Secretary’s regulations and not by the Social Security Act.” *Sanders*, 430 U.S. at 108. But the Railroad Retirement Act’s broader review provision reflects a congressional judgment that Board decisions should be subject to judicial oversight. *See Your Home Visiting Nurse Servs., Inc. v. Shalala*, 525 U.S. 449, 456 (1999) (noting that “Congress has provided for judicial review of certain agency actions”). Moreover, the Board’s regulations themselves contemplate that reopening decisions may be reviewed. *See* 20 C.F.R. § 261.2. There is no sound reason to treat those decisions as immune from review.

**II. The Fifth Circuit Erred in Dismissing Salinas’s Petition for Lack of Jurisdiction.**

Because the Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim is a “final decision” under § 355(f), the Fifth Circuit had jurisdiction to review it. The court’s reliance on *Roberts* was misplaced, as *Roberts* misinterpreted the statute. This Court should reverse and remand for consideration of the merits of Salinas’s petition.
