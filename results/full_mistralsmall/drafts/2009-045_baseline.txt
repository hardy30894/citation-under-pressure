**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Did Not Abuse Its Discretion in Enhancing the Lodestar Based on Exceptional Performance and Results.**

The Eleventh Circuit correctly affirmed the district court’s award of attorneys’ fees, including a $4.5 million enhancement, because the lodestar method does not foreclose consideration of superior performance and exceptional results. *See Kenny A. ex rel. Winn v. Perdue*, 532 F.3d 1209, 1242 (11th Cir. 2008). This Court has long recognized that fee-shifting statutes such as 42 U.S.C. § 1988 permit enhancements in “rare” and “exceptional” cases where the lodestar fails to reflect the true value of counsel’s work. *See Blum v. Stenson*, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984); *Pennsylvania v. Del. Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air (“Delaware Valley I”)*, 478 U.S. 546, 565 (1986).

The Supreme Court has repeatedly declined to categorically bar enhancements for superior performance. In *Blum*, the Court held that the lodestar is presumed reasonable but left open the possibility of an upward adjustment where “the quality of service rendered was superior to that one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged” and the results were “exceptional.” 465 U.S. at 899. The Court reiterated this principle in *Delaware Valley I*, affirming that enhancements remain permissible in extraordinary cases supported by specific evidence. 478 U.S. at 565. The Court’s refusal to overrule *Blum* in *Delaware Valley I* confirms that the lodestar is not an absolute cap. *See id.* at 564 (citing *Blum* with approval).

The Eleventh Circuit’s decision is consistent with this precedent. The district court’s detailed findings—based on its observation of counsel’s “stellar performance” and the “exceptional” nature of the relief obtained—satisfy the exacting standards set forth in *Blum* and *Delaware Valley I*. *See Kenny A.*, 532 F.3d at 1242. The court did not enhance the fee arbitrarily; it relied on specific evidence of superior advocacy and extraordinary results, as required. *See Blum*, 465 U.S. at 899.

**II. The District Court’s Findings Were Sufficiently Specific and Supported by the Record.**

The dissent’s suggestion that the district court’s findings were impermissibly vague is belied by the record. The court explicitly tied the enhancement to its “substantial experience and familiarity with prevailing rates in Atlanta” and its observation of counsel’s performance over the course of the litigation. *Kenny A. ex rel. Winn v. Perdue*, 454 F. Supp. 2d 1260, 1286, 1290 (N.D. Ga. 2006). This is precisely the type of individualized assessment that *Blum* and *Delaware Valley I* contemplate. *See Blum*, 465 U.S. at 899; *Delaware Valley I*, 478 U.S. at 565.

Moreover, the Eleventh Circuit has repeatedly upheld enhancements where the district court provided detailed, case-specific justifications. *See, e.g., NAACP v. City of Evergreen*, 812 F.2d 1332, 1336-37 (11th Cir. 1987); *Norman v. Housing Auth. of Montgomery*, 836 F.2d 1292, 1302 (11th Cir. 1988). The district court here did no less. Its findings were not the product of unchecked discretion but rather a reasoned application of the lodestar framework to the unique circumstances of this case.

**III. The Circuit Is United in Permitting Enhancements for Exceptional Performance and Results.**

Contrary to the dissent’s suggestion, there is no circuit split on this issue. Every circuit to consider the question—including the Second, Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Eighth, Ninth, Tenth, and Eleventh—has held that enhancements are permissible in rare and exceptional cases. *See, e.g., Geier v. Sundquist*, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); *Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co.*, 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000); *Quaratino v. Tiffany & Co.*, 166 F.3d 422, 425 (2d Cir. 1999). This consensus underscores the reasonableness of the Eleventh Circuit’s decision.

**Conclusion**

The district court did not abuse its discretion in enhancing the lodestar based on exceptional performance and results. The Eleventh Circuit’s decision is consistent with Supreme Court precedent and the uniform practice of its sister circuits. This Court should affirm.
