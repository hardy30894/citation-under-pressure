The District Court's failure to advise Manuel D. Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, does not provide a basis for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation, concluding that the rule's purpose was served since Peguero knew of his right to appeal.

The Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure were designed to ensure fairness and efficiency in the administration of justice. However, not every technical violation of the rules warrants relief. As the Court held in McNabb v. United States, 318 U.S. 332 (1943), "the history of liberty has largely been the history of observance of procedural safeguards" (id. at 347). Yet, the Court has also recognized that not all procedural errors require reversal. In Snyder v. Massachusetts, 291 U.S. 97 (1934), Justice Cardozo wrote that "the presence of error [does not] necessitate[] a new trial" (id. at 122). Instead, the Court must consider whether the error was harmless.

Here, the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was a technical violation of Rule 32. However, Peguero was aware of his right to appeal and chose not to exercise it. As the Court held in Stroud v. United States, 251 U.S. 15 (1919), a defendant's knowledge of his rights is a crucial factor in determining whether a procedural error is harmless. Since Peguero knew of his right to appeal, the District Court's failure to advise him did not prejudice his rights.

Furthermore, the purpose of Rule 32 is to ensure that defendants are aware of their right to appeal. As the Court noted in United States v. Behrens, 172 F.2d 741 (7th Cir. 1949), cert. denied, 337 U.S. 958 (1949), citing Johnson v. United States, 318 U.S. 189 (1943), the rule is intended to protect defendants who may be unaware of their appellate rights. Since Peguero was aware of his right to appeal, the rule's purpose was served, and the District Court's error was harmless.

In sum, the Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation and concluded that Peguero was not entitled to habeas relief. The District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal did not prejudice his rights, as he was aware of his right and chose not to exercise it. Therefore, the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
