**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE SEARCH WARRANT LACKED ADEQUATE NEXUS TO THE PLACE BEING SEARCHED AND MUST BE INVALIDATED.**

The Fourth Amendment requires that a warrant particularly describe the place to be searched and the things to be seized. *Maryland v. Garrison*, 480 U.S. 79, 84 (1987). A nexus between the items sought and the place to be searched must be established in the warrant application. *United States v. Ladd*, 711 F.3d 57, 61 (1st Cir. 2013). Here, the warrant application failed to establish a sufficient nexus between Joubert’s parents’ home and the evidence sought.

The affidavit recited general allegations of Joubert’s prior misconduct and his destruction of computer components, but it did not tie those allegations to the parents’ home. *See id.* at 62 (finding insufficient nexus where affidavit did not link defendant’s criminal activity to the place to be searched). The officer’s conclusory statement that evidence "may confirm or dispel" Joubert’s background and relationships was insufficient to establish probable cause that evidence of sexual exploitation would be found at the parents’ home. *See id.* (requiring more than speculative assertions).

Because the warrant lacked the required nexus, the evidence seized must be suppressed. *See United States v. DiMuro*, 540 F.3d 15, 20 (1st Cir. 2008) (excluding evidence obtained under a defective warrant).

---

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ABUSED ITS DISCRETION IN ADMITTING TESTIMONY OF UNCHARGED CHILD MOLESTATION.**

Federal Rule of Evidence 404(b) prohibits the admission of extrinsic acts to prove a defendant’s character. *Huddleston v. United States*, 485 U.S. 681, 685 (1988). Such evidence may be admitted only if it is (1) relevant to a material issue, (2) necessary, and (3) not overly prejudicial. *United States v. Ciampaglia*, 628 F.3d 82, 88 (1st Cir. 2010).

Here, the government introduced testimony regarding uncharged acts of child molestation under the guise of "other bad acts" evidence. However, the evidence was not probative of any material issue in the case, as Joubert was charged with specific acts involving KC, not a pattern of general misconduct. *See id.* at 89 (finding reversible error where evidence was not probative of a material issue). Moreover, the prejudicial effect of this testimony far outweighed its minimal probative value, as it invited the jury to convict Joubert based on propensity. *See id.* at 90.

The district court’s admission of this testimony was an abuse of discretion and requires reversal. *See id.*

**III. THE COMMERCE CLAUSE DOES NOT SUPPORT APPLICATION OF 18 U.S.C. §§ 2251(a) AND 2252A(a)(5)(B) TO JOUBERT.**

Joubert’s conviction under §§ 2251(a) and 2252A(a)(5)(B) rests on the theory that his conduct affected interstate commerce. However, the government must prove a substantial connection between the regulated activity and interstate commerce. *United States v. Morrison*, 529 U.S. 598, 612 (2000).

Here, the government failed to establish that Joubert’s conduct had a substantial effect on interstate commerce. The evidence showed only that Joubert possessed and produced child pornography in New Hampshire, with no nexus to interstate commerce. *See id.* at 613 (requiring more than attenuated effects on commerce). The government’s reliance on *United States v. Lopez*, 514 U.S. 549 (1995), and *Gonzales v. Raich*, 545 U.S. 1 (2005), is misplaced, as those cases involved economic activity with a clear nexus to interstate commerce. *See Morrison*, 529 U.S. at 613 (distinguishing economic from noneconomic activity).

Because the statutes as applied to Joubert exceed Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, his convictions must be vacated. *See id.* at 617.

---
**IV. JOUBERT’S 480-MONTH SENTENCE WAS SUBSTANTIVELY UNREASONABLE.**

A sentence is substantively unreasonable if it reflects an abuse of discretion, such as a failure to consider relevant sentencing factors or an unreasonable weighing of the § 3553(a) factors. *Gall v. United States*, 552 U.S. 38, 51 (2007).

Here, the district court imposed a sentence at the bottom of the Guidelines range (480 months) without adequately explaining why a lower sentence was unwarranted. The court failed to meaningfully address Joubert’s arguments for a below-Guidelines sentence, including his lack of a criminal history and the disparity between his sentence and those imposed on similarly situated defendants. *See id.* at 50 (requiring meaningful explanation for variance).

Because the sentence was substantively unreasonable, remand for resentencing is required. *See id.* at 59.
