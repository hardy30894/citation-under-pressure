## ARGUMENT

The Bankruptcy Code excepts from discharge “any debt … for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by … actual fraud.” 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A). This provision encompasses all liability arising from fraudulent conduct, including treble damages and attorney’s fees awarded under state consumer protection law.

**I. The Plain Language of § 523(a)(2)(A) Covers All Debts Arising from Fraud**

The phrase “any debt” is unqualified and broad. In *Brown v. Felsen*, 442 U.S. 127, 134 (1979), this Court instructed that the Bankruptcy Code should be construed to give effect to the “fresh start” policy only for honest debtors, and that debts arising from fraud are not dischargeable. The term “debt” means “liability on a claim.” *In re St. Laurent*, 991 F.2d 672, 679 (11th Cir. 1993). A judgment for treble damages is a liability on a claim for fraud. *Id.* at 678-79. The “to the extent obtained by” language does not limit nondischargeability to the precise amount fraudulently transferred; rather, it requires that the debt be “for” money obtained by fraud. *Id.* at 679. The debt here—including treble damages and fees—is for the money Cohen obtained by overcharging tenants through actual fraud.

**II. Treble Damages Are Part of the Debt Obtained by Fraud**

The New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act mandates treble damages for violations. This award is a direct consequence of Cohen’s fraud and is therefore nondischargeable. In *Grogan v. Garner*, 498 U.S. 279, 287 (1991), this Court held that the exceptions to discharge should be construed broadly to protect victims of fraud. The preponderance standard applies, *id.* at 286, and the bankruptcy court found fraud by that standard. The treble damages are not punitive in the traditional sense but are remedial. Even if punitive, they are nondischargeable under § 523(a)(2)(A). *St. Laurent*, 991 F.2d at 680. The contrary view in *In re Levy*, 951 F.2d 196, 198 (9th Cir. 1991), is unpersuasive because it improperly narrows the statutory text.

**III. Cohen’s Conduct Constituted Actual Fraud**

The elements of actual fraud under § 523(a)(2)(A) are: (1) a material misrepresentation; (2) knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard; (3) intent to deceive; (4) reasonable reliance; and (5) damages. *In re Poskanzer*, 143 B.R. 991, 999 (Bankr. D.N.J. 1992). Cohen impliedly represented that the rents he charged were lawful. His silence, coupled with the rental amount, constituted a misrepresentation. The bankruptcy court’s finding of misrepresentation is an ultimate fact reviewed de novo, but the underlying historical facts are reviewed for clear error. *Universal Minerals, Inc. v. C.A. Hughes & Co.*, 669 F.2d 98, 102 (3d Cir. 1981). Those facts—Cohen never read the rent control ordinance, never consulted an attorney, and ignored the Board’s notice—are undisputed.

**IV. Reckless Disregard Satisfies Knowledge and Intent**

Intent to deceive may be inferred from reckless disregard for the truth. *In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d 1108, 1118-19 (3d Cir. 1995). Reckless conduct is “unreasonable conduct in disregard of a known or obvious risk from which it is highly probable that harm would follow.” *In re Woolley*, 145 B.R. 830, 834 (Bankr. E.D. Va. 1991). Cohen’s pattern of willful ignorance—failing to learn the law, failing to appeal, and failing to reimburse—demonstrates reckless indifference. This satisfies both knowledge and intent.

**V. The “Fresh Start” Policy Does Not Protect Fraudulent Debtors**

The Bankruptcy Code’s “fresh start” is reserved for the “honest but unfortunate debtor.” *Grogan*, 498 U.S. at 286-87. Cohen, who engaged in actual fraud, is not entitled to discharge the debts arising from that fraud. The meaning of “punitive damages” in a federal statute is a federal question. *Molzof v. United States*, 502 U.S. 301, 305 (1992). Here, the treble damages are a direct consequence of the fraud and are therefore nondischargeable.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
