# ARGUMENT

## I. STELWAGON FAILED TO PRESENT SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE OF ACTUAL ANTITRUST DAMAGES, AND THE DISTRICT COURT THEREFORE ERRED IN DENYING TARMAC'S MOTION FOR JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW ON THE ROBINSON-PATMAN CLAIM.

This Court reviews the denial of a motion for judgment as a matter of law de novo, applying the same standard as the district court. *Lightning Lube, Inc. v. Witco Corp.*, 4 F.3d 1153, 1166 (3d Cir. 1993). Judgment as a matter of law is warranted where the evidence, viewed in the light most favorable to the verdict-winner, is insufficient to sustain the verdict. *Id.*

Section 4 of the Clayton Act does not automatically convert a Robinson-Patman violation into a damages award. The Supreme Court has been unequivocal that "the antitrust plaintiff must prove more than a violation of § 2(a), since it is possible to have a violation of that section without any actual injury to competition." *J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp.*, 451 U.S. 557, 562 (1981). Proof of a price differential alone establishes only a prima facie case for injunctive purposes under *FTC v. Morton Salt Co.*, 334 U.S. 37, 49 (1948); it does not relieve a private plaintiff seeking treble damages of its burden to prove that the discrimination caused it actual injury and to quantify that injury with competent evidence. *J. Truett Payne*, 451 U.S. at 562.

This burden is not satisfied by conjecture. Although antitrust plaintiffs are afforded some latitude in proving the amount of damages because "the wrongdoer" should not "profit by his own wrongdoing," *Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co.*, 282 U.S. 555, 563 (1931), that latitude extends only to the *measure* of damages once causation and the *fact* of injury have been shown. *Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc.*, 327 U.S. 251, 264 (1946) (damages may not be based on "speculation or guesswork"). The Supreme Court reaffirmed in *Zenith Radio Corp. v. Hazeltine Research, Inc.*, 395 U.S. 100, 114 (1969), that "the vagaries of the jury" cannot substitute for a plaintiff's obligation to prove damages were "in fact caused by the wrong," even where the precise dollar figure requires reasonable estimation.

Here, Stelwagon's damages case rested on undifferentiated lost-profit projections that made no attempt to isolate the effect of Tarmac's discriminatory pricing from other causes of Stelwagon's declining sales — including the admitted breach of the exclusive distributorship territory, general market softness, and Stelwagon's own business decisions. Stelwagon's expert testimony conflated harm flowing from the territorial breach with harm allegedly flowing from price discrimination, offering the jury no rational basis to apportion the $2,272,000 verdict between the two theories. This is precisely the kind of undifferentiated, speculative damages proof that *J. Truett Payne* and *Bigelow* forbid. Indeed, in *Brooke Group Ltd. v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp.*, 509 U.S. 209, 222 (1993), the Court emphasized that Robinson-Patman plaintiffs must demonstrate "antitrust injury" causally linked to the challenged conduct, not merely injury coincident with it. Because Stelwagon's proof failed to segregate antitrust injury from contract injury, and because the district court's own remittitur order — reducing the antitrust award by nearly two-thirds — implicitly acknowledged the unreliability of the jury's calculation, *see* 862 F. Supp. at 1369, judgment as a matter of law should have been granted. A remittitur cannot cure a fundamental failure of proof; it merely substitutes the court's guess for the jury's. *Story Parchment*, 282 U.S. at 563, does not license courts to manufacture a damages figure where none was competently proven.

Falls City Industries v. Vanco Beverage, Inc., 460 U.S. 428, 434–35 (1983), confirms that even where a price differential is conceded, the plaintiff retains the burden of showing that the differential — as opposed to other market forces — proximately caused quantifiable harm. Stelwagon's failure to meet that burden here is fatal to its treble-damages recovery.

## II. THE JUDGMENT ON THE BREACH OF CONTRACT CLAIM SHOULD LIKEWISE BE VACATED BECAUSE THE COMPENSATORY DAMAGES AWARD WAS NOT SUPPORTED BY EVIDENCE SEGREGATED FROM THE FLAWED ANTITRUST CALCULATION.

Because Stelwagon's lost-profits evidence conflated the two theories of recovery, the same infirmity that dooms the antitrust award undermines confidence in the $74,242 contract award. Pennsylvania law requires that contract damages be proven "with reasonable certainty" and not be the product of speculation. *See generally Delahanty v. First Pa. Bank, N.A.*, 464 A.2d 1243, 1257 (Pa. Super. 1983). Where, as here, a single, unitary damages model improperly commingled distinct legal theories, neither award can be said to rest on a reliable evidentiary foundation.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should vacate the judgment insofar as it awards treble damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act, and should vacate or remand for a new trial on damages with respect to the breach of contract claim.
