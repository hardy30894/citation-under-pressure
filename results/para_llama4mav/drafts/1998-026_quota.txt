The District Court's failure to advise Manuel Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2), does not provide a basis for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation, affirming the District Court's denial of habeas relief.

The harmless-error doctrine, as established in Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), requires that a court disregard errors that do not affect the substantial rights of the parties. In Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993), the Court held that the harmless-error standard applies to habeas proceedings, and that a petitioner must show that the error had a substantial and injurious effect on the outcome. Here, Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing, rendering the District Court's failure to advise him harmless.

The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that a defendant is aware of his right to appeal. As the Court of Appeals noted, this purpose was served in Peguero's case, despite the District Court's failure to advise him. In United States v. Deans, 436 F.2d 596 (3d Cir. 1971), the court held that a defendant's knowledge of his right to appeal can render a Rule 32 violation harmless. Similarly, in United States v. Drummond, 903 F.2d 1391 (11th Cir. 1990), the court applied harmless-error review to a Rule 32 violation, finding that the defendant's awareness of his right to appeal rendered the error harmless.

Peguero's claim that a Rule 32 violation automatically entitles him to habeas relief is unsupported by case law. In United States v. Butler, 938 F.2d 702 (6th Cir. 1991), the court rejected a similar claim, holding that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review. The Ninth Circuit has also applied harmless-error review to Rule 32 violations, as seen in United States v. Smith, 60 F.3d 595 (9th Cir. 1995).

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has consistently held that not all procedural errors entitle a defendant to relief. In United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993), the Court held that a plain error that does not affect substantial rights is not grounds for reversal. Similarly, in Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 (1988), the Court held that a harmless error in grand jury proceedings did not require dismissal of the indictment.

The District Court's finding that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal is supported by the record, and the Court of Appeals correctly deferred to this finding. As the Supreme Court held in Anderson v. Bessemer City, 470 U.S. 564 (1985), a district court's factual findings are entitled to deference unless clearly erroneous.

In sum, the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was harmless error, as Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation, and the denial of habeas relief should be affirmed.
