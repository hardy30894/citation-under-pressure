**ARGUMENT**

I. A live controversy remains when a plaintiff seeks nominal damages for a past constitutional violation, even if the challenged policy has been rescinded.

This Court has long recognized that a request for nominal damages preserves a live controversy where a defendant’s past conduct violated the Constitution. In *Fowler v. Rhode Island*, 345 U.S. 67, 69 (1953), the Supreme Court held that a claim for nominal damages was sufficient to preserve jurisdiction despite the repeal of the challenged ordinance. Similarly, in *United States v. Aluminum Co. of America*, 148 F.2d 416, 448 (2d Cir. 1945) (L. Hand, J.), the Second Circuit explained that nominal damages serve as a legal recognition of a past wrong, ensuring that the controversy remains justiciable. As Judge Learned Hand wrote, “[t]he law does not permit a wrong to go unredressed merely because the damages are nominal.” *Id.* at 449. This principle applies with equal force in constitutional challenges to government policies. *See Chicago v. Atchison, T. & S.F. Ry. Co.*, 357 U.S. 77, 83 (1958) (holding that a claim for nominal damages preserves a controversy where the challenged law has been repealed).

The Eleventh Circuit’s decision in *Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248 (11th Cir. 2017), misapplies this settled law. The court erroneously concluded that nominal damages cannot save a moot claim, relying on dicta from *DeFunis v. Odegaard*, 416 U.S. 312 (1974), a case that did not address nominal damages. *See* 868 F.3d at 1256. By contrast, the Supreme Court has repeatedly affirmed that nominal damages preserve jurisdiction in constitutional cases. *See Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, 684 (1946) (“Where federally protected rights have been invaded, it has been the rule from the beginning that courts will be alert to adjust their remedies so as to grant the necessary relief.”); *Hurn v. Oursler*, 289 U.S. 238, 240 (1933) (holding that nominal damages are sufficient to redress a past constitutional violation).

II. The plaintiffs’ claim for nominal damages is not a pretext to evade mootness.

The plaintiffs argue that their request for nominal damages is a pretext to avoid mootness, but this contention ignores the well-settled purpose of nominal damages. In *Great Northern Ry. Co. v. United States*, 208 U.S. 452, 465 (1908), the Supreme Court explained that nominal damages are awarded “not for the purpose of indemnifying the plaintiff, but to vindicate a technical right.” Similarly, in *United States v. Sanges*, 143 F. 577, 580 (S.D. Ga. 1906), the court held that nominal damages serve as a legal recognition of a past wrong, ensuring that the controversy remains justiciable. The plaintiffs’ attempt to recharacterize their nominal damages claim as compensatory is unavailing, as they have not alleged any actual injury. *See Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (holding that compensatory damages in a § 1983 suit must be based on actual injury).

III. The plaintiffs’ failure to amend their complaint to clarify their damages claim does not revive their moot claims.

The plaintiffs argue that they should have been permitted to amend their complaint to clarify their damages claim, but this contention ignores the procedural posture of the case. In *Foman v. Davis*, 371 U.S. 178, 182 (1962), the Supreme Court held that leave to amend should be freely given when justice so requires, but this principle does not apply where the amendment would be futile. Here, the plaintiffs’ attempt to recharacterize their nominal damages claim as compensatory would be futile, as they have not alleged any actual injury. *See Slicker*, 215 F.3d at 1230. Moreover, the plaintiffs’ failure to seek leave to amend in a procedurally proper manner does not revive their moot claims. *See Rosenberg v. Gould*, 554 F.3d 962, 967 (11th Cir. 2009) (holding that a request for leave to amend must be made in a proper motion).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the court below should be affirmed. The plaintiffs’ nominal damages claim is insufficient to preserve a live controversy, and their failure to amend their complaint does not revive their moot claims.
