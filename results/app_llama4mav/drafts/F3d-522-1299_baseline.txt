ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Erred in Concluding that the Asserted Claims are Not Entitled to the Benefit of the Filing Date of the Original Application

The district court’s conclusion that the asserted claims are not entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the Original Application is based on its finding that the Original Application did not provide a written description of the invention claimed in the asserted patents, as required by 35 U.S.C. § 112. However, this conclusion is not supported by the law.

To satisfy the written description requirement, the original application must "convey with reasonable clarity to those skilled in the art that, as of the filing date sought, [the inventor] was in possession of the invention." Vas-Cath Inc. v. Mahurkar, 935 F.2d 1555, 1563-64 (Fed. Cir. 1991). The district court erred in focusing solely on the new language added to the 2000 CIP Application, rather than considering whether the Original Application provided a sufficient written description of the invention.

The Original Application disclosed a vending machine for vending telecommunications channel access to a customer, which is the same invention claimed in the asserted patents. The fact that the 2000 CIP Application added new language to the specification does not necessarily mean that the Original Application lacked a written description of the invention. See, e.g., In re Wright, 866 F.2d 422, 425 (Fed. Cir. 1989) ("The fact that [the applicant] added new language to the specification in the continuation-in-part application does not necessarily mean that the original application lacked a written description of the invention.").

Furthermore, the district court’s reliance on the new language added to the 2000 CIP Application to construe the claim term “customer interface” is misplaced. The court should have considered whether the Original Application provided a sufficient written description of the invention, regardless of the later-added language. See, e.g., TurboCare Div. of Demag Delaval Turbomachinery Corp. v. General Elec. Co., 264 F.3d 1111, 1119 (Fed. Cir. 2001) ("The written description requirement is satisfied if the original application conveys to one of ordinary skill in the art that the inventor possessed the claimed invention at the time of filing.").

Therefore, the district court erred in concluding that the asserted claims are not entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the Original Application.

II. The Asserted Claims are Entitled to the Benefit of the Filing Date of the Original Application, and Therefore, are Not Anticipated by the MobileStar Network

If the asserted claims are entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the Original Application (February 6, 1997), then the MobileStar Network, which was publicly used, sold, and offered for sale after that date (prior to June 15, 1999), is not § 102(b) prior art. See, e.g., In re Wertheim, 541 F.2d 257, 262 (C.C.P.A. 1976) ("A reference is not prior art under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b) if it was published or became publicly known after the filing date of the application to which the applicant is entitled to claim the benefit of an earlier filing date.").

As a result, T-Mobile’s argument that the asserted claims are anticipated by the MobileStar Network fails. The district court’s grant of summary judgment of invalidity should be reversed.

CONCLUSION

The district court erred in concluding that the asserted claims are not entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the Original Application. The asserted claims are entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the Original Application, and therefore, are not anticipated by the MobileStar Network. The grant of summary judgment of invalidity should be reversed.
