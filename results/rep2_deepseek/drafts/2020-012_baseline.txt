**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Board’s Decision Not To Reopen Is a “Final Decision” Subject to Judicial Review Under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).**

The Railroad Retirement Act provides for judicial review of “any final decision of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). This language is unqualified. It does not distinguish between merits decisions and decisions declining to reopen. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim was a “final decision” because it conclusively disposed of a discrete issue—the reopening request—and left no further administrative action available. The Board’s own regulations treat reopening decisions as final administrative actions. *See* 20 C.F.R. § 261.7 (providing that a decision not to reopen is not subject to further administrative review). That finality triggers § 355(f) review.

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), misreads the statute. The *Roberts* court held that § 355(f) “limited” review to “decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits after administrative appeals have been exhausted.” *Id.* at 140. But nothing in the statute’s text imposes such a limitation. Section 355(f) speaks broadly of “any final decision.” The term “final decision” is a term of art meaning an action that ends the proceedings and leaves nothing for the agency to decide. *See Bennett v. Spear*, 520 U.S. 154, 177-78 (1997). The Board’s reopening decision meets that standard. It disposed of Salinas’s request and was not subject to further appeal. The Board’s decision thus satisfies the “final decision” requirement.

**II. The Minority View, Led by the D.C. Circuit, Correctly Interprets § 355(f).**

The D.C. Circuit recently held that “the Railroad Retirement Act grants the [circuit] Court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations.” *Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016). The *Stovic* court reasoned that § 355(f) “does not distinguish between initial and reopening decisions; it simply authorizes review of any final decision.” *Id.* at 503. That reasoning is faithful to the statutory text. The Second Circuit and Eighth Circuit have similarly held that reopening decisions are reviewable. *See Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 253, 254-55 (2d Cir. 1966); *Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991). The majority of circuits, including the Fifth, have erred by reading a limitation into the statute that Congress did not place there.

**III. *Califano v. Sanders* Does Not Bar Review of Reopening Decisions Under the Railroad Retirement Act.**

The Fifth Circuit, in *Roberts*, relied on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), to support its conclusion. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141. But *Sanders* addressed the scope of judicial review under the Administrative Procedure Act, not under a specific statutory review provision like § 355(f). *Sanders* held that the APA does not independently authorize review of a refusal to reopen a Social Security claim. 430 U.S. at 107-09. The Court explicitly noted that its holding “does not affect the availability of judicial review under the Social Security Act itself.” *Id.* at 109. Here, § 355(f) provides a specific statutory grant of review. *Sanders* is therefore inapposite. The Railroad Retirement Act, not the APA, governs this case. The Board’s reopening decision is a final decision under that Act, and *Sanders* does not preclude review.

**IV. The Board’s Reopening Decision Is Reviewable Because It Involves a Legal Error That Contravenes the Board’s Own Regulations.**

Salinas does not seek a second merits review of his 2006 claim. He challenges the Board’s legal error in applying its own reopening regulations. The Board’s hearing officer concluded that Salinas’s 2006 application was “beyond the four-year timeframe for reopening based on new and material evidence or administrative error.” *See* 20 C.F.R. § 261.2. But the Board failed to consider whether Salinas demonstrated good cause for an untimely filing, as required by the regulation. This legal error is reviewable under § 355(f). *See Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504 (holding that “the Board’s decision not to reopen is reviewable for legal error or abuse of discretion”). The Board cannot insulate its errors from review simply by labeling its decision “non-merits.”

**Conclusion**

The Fifth Circuit’s dismissal for lack of jurisdiction must be reversed. The Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim is a “final decision” under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) and is subject to judicial review. The case should be remanded for consideration of the Board’s legal error.
