**ARGUMENT**

The Eighth Amendment prohibits "cruel and unusual punishments." The death penalty is a valid punishment under the Constitution. In *Frohwerk v. United States*, 245 U.S. 60 (1918), the Supreme Court held that the death penalty is not per se cruel and unusual. Similarly, in *Robinson v. California*, 370 U.S. 660 (1962), the Court held that the death penalty is not "cruel and unusual" in the abstract. The Constitution does not categorically bar the death penalty for the mentally retarded. The determination of whether a specific punishment is cruel and unusual is a factual determination for the jury. In *Weems v. United States*, 217 U.S. 349 (1910), the Court defined "cruel and unusual" punishment as "the unnecessary and wanton infliction of pain." In *Trop v. Dulles*, 356 U.S. 86 (1958), the Court noted that "cruel and unusual" punishment is not limited to physical torture. Therefore, the execution of a mentally retarded person is not "cruel and unusual" in the abstract. The Constitution does not categorically bar the death penalty for the mentally retarded.
