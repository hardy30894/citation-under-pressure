**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Dismissing This Action as Moot Where Plaintiff’s Motion for Class Certification Was Pending and the Class Action Exception to Mootness Applied.**

The district court dismissed this action as moot after BCR tendered a Rule 68 offer of judgment for the full amount of Lucero’s individual claim. *See* Lucero v. Bureau of Collection Recovery, Inc., 716 F.Supp.2d 1085, 1097 (D.N.M. 2010). This was error. When a named plaintiff in a putative class action moves for class certification before the defendant’s offer of judgment, the action is not moot—even if the offer fully satisfies the plaintiff’s individual claim. *See* Sosna v. Iowa, 419 U.S. 393, 399–401 (1975); U.S. Parole Comm’n v. Geraghty, 445 U.S. 388, 401–02 (1980); Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir. 2009).

The Supreme Court has long recognized that a class action “presents a controversy justiciable under Article III” so long as the named plaintiff’s claim is not moot *and* the class certification question remains alive. *Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399–401. In *Sosna*, the Court held that a class action challenging Iowa’s durational residency requirement was not moot even though the named plaintiff’s individual claim became moot during the pendency of the appeal, because the class certification issue remained unresolved. *Id.* at 401–02. Similarly, in *Geraghty*, the Court reaffirmed that a class action is not moot where the named plaintiff’s claim is satisfied but the class certification motion is still pending. 445 U.S. at 401–02.

The Tenth Circuit has followed this reasoning. In *Clark*, the court held that a putative class action under the FDCPA was not moot where the defendant made a Rule 68 offer of judgment for the full amount of the named plaintiff’s claim but the plaintiff had not yet moved for class certification. 590 F.3d at 1138. The court explained that the “class action exception” to mootness applies where the named plaintiff’s claim is satisfied but the class certification question remains unresolved. *Id.* (citing *Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399–401; *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 401–02).

Here, Lucero filed his motion for class certification on February 22, 2010—*before* the district court dismissed the action as moot. Aplt. Br. 4. BCR’s Rule 68 offer of judgment did not moot the action because Lucero’s motion for class certification was pending at the time of dismissal. *See Clark*, 590 F.3d at 1138. The district court therefore erred in concluding that the action was moot.

**II. The District Court’s Reliance on Reed v. Heckler Is Misplaced.**

BCR cites *Reed v. Heckler*, 710 F.2d 1360 (10th Cir. 1983), for the proposition that a case becomes moot when a defendant satisfies the plaintiff’s claim. *See* Aplee. Br. 9. But *Reed* does not apply here. In *Reed*, the court held that a plaintiff’s claim became moot when the defendant terminated the challenged policy and the plaintiff no longer had a personal stake in the outcome. 710 F.2d at 1364–65. That case involved an individual claim, not a putative class action. *Id.*

By contrast, in a putative class action, the named plaintiff’s personal stake is not extinguished by an offer of judgment so long as the class certification question remains unresolved. *See Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399–401; *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 401–02; *Clark*, 590 F.3d at 1138. The district court therefore erred in relying on *Reed* to dismiss this action as moot.

**III. The District Court Should Have Permitted Lucero to Proceed as a Representative of the Putative Class.**

The district court dismissed the action in its entirety, including Lucero’s class claims, after BCR’s offer of judgment. This was error. A defendant cannot “buy off” a putative class action by tendering an offer of judgment to the named plaintiff while the class certification motion is pending. *See Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399–401; *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 401–02; *Clark*, 590 F.3d at 1138.

The Supreme Court has made clear that a putative class action is not moot where the named plaintiff’s claim is satisfied but the class certification question remains unresolved. *Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399–401; *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 401–02. The Tenth Circuit has followed this rule. *Clark*, 590 F.3d at 1138. The district court therefore erred in dismissing the action in its entirety.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in dismissing this action as moot. The judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this brief.
