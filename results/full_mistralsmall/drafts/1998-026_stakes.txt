**ARGUMENT**

I. **A Rule 32 Violation Is Subject to Harmless-Error Review, and the District Court Correctly Concluded That Any Error Was Harmless**

The Third Circuit correctly held that a district court’s failure to advise a defendant of his right to appeal under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) is subject to harmless-error review. *United States v. Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. 74, 80 (2004) (applying harmless-error review to Rule 32 violations). The Supreme Court has made clear that “most constitutional errors can be harmless,” *Neder v. United States*, 527 U.S. 1, 8 (1999), and Rule 32(a)(2) violations are no exception. *See United States v. Vonn*, 535 U.S. 55, 62-63 (2002) (applying harmless-error review to Rule 11 violations and reasoning that the same principles apply to Rule 32 errors).

Here, the District Court found that Petitioner was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing. The Court of Appeals properly deferred to this factual finding, as it is supported by record evidence. *See Anderson v. City of Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 573 (1985) (deferring to district court credibility determinations). Because Petitioner knew of his right to appeal, the purpose of Rule 32(a)(2)—ensuring that defendants are informed of their appellate rights—was satisfied. *Cf. United States v. Thame*, 846 F.2d 200, 205 (3d Cir. 1988) (holding that a Rule 32 violation is harmless where the defendant was otherwise aware of his rights).

II. **The District Court Did Not Err in Denying Habeas Relief Under 28 U.S.C. § 2255**

A federal court may grant habeas relief under § 2255 only if the petitioner’s custody is “in violation of the Constitution or laws of the United States.” 28 U.S.C. § 2255(a). A Rule 32(a)(2) violation, standing alone, does not implicate the Constitution or federal law in a way that warrants collateral relief. *See Bousley v. United States*, 523 U.S. 614, 622 (1998) (holding that a claim must involve a “violation of the Constitution or laws of the United States” to warrant § 2255 relief).

Moreover, even if a Rule 32 violation were cognizable under § 2255, it would not entitle Petitioner to relief here. The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized that “a defendant is entitled to relief only if the error ‘had substantial and injurious effect or influence in determining the jury’s verdict.’” *Brecht v. Abrahamson*, 507 U.S. 619, 637 (1993) (quoting *Kotteakos v. United States*, 328 U.S. 750, 776 (1946)). Because Petitioner was aware of his right to appeal, the District Court’s failure to advise him of that right had no effect on his decision not to appeal. *See United States v. Morrison*, 449 U.S. 361, 365 (1981) (holding that a procedural error does not warrant relief where it did not affect the outcome).

III. **The Court of Appeals’ Decision Should Be Affirmed**

The Third Circuit’s decision is consistent with Supreme Court precedent and the purposes of Rule 32(a)(2). The District Court’s factual finding that Petitioner was aware of his right to appeal is entitled to deference, and the harmless-error standard properly applies. Because the error, if any, did not prejudice Petitioner, habeas relief is unwarranted.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should affirm the judgment of the Court of Appeals.
