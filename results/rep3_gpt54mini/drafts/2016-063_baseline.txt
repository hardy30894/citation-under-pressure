**Argument**

The Board’s dismissal of Perry’s case for lack of jurisdiction should be reviewed in **district court**, not the Federal Circuit. The Civil Service Reform Act routes “cases of discrimination” to district court when an employee “has been affected by an action which [he] may appeal to the Merit Systems Protection Board” and alleges discrimination as a basis for the action. 5 U.S.C. §§ 7702(a)(1), 7703(b)(2). In **Kloeckner v. Solis**, the Supreme Court held that this district-court route applies whether the Board resolves the mixed case on the merits or dismisses it on a pre-merits ground. 568 U.S. 41, 50-56, 133 S. Ct. 596, 603-07 (2012). The Court’s reasoning controls here.

1. **The statute turns on the nature of the employee’s claim, not on the Board’s label for its dismissal.**  
Section 7702(a)(1) asks whether the employee was “affected by an action” appealable to the MSPB and alleges discrimination. 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1). In **Kloeckner**, the Court explained that a mixed case is one in which the employee challenges an adverse action appealable to the MSPB and also alleges discrimination; such cases “shall be filed in district court.” 568 U.S. at 45, 54, 133 S. Ct. at 599, 604. Nothing in the statute makes the forum depend on whether the Board dismisses the case as untimely, involuntary, or otherwise jurisdictionally defective. The Supreme Court rejected a forum rule that would make the path of review turn on the Board’s preliminary disposition rather than on the employee’s mixed-case allegations. Id. at 51-52, 55-56, 133 S. Ct. at 603-05, 606-07.

2. **Kloeckner’s rule is not limited to merits rulings or procedural dismissals.**  
The Court in **Kloeckner** expressly held that district-court review applies when the MSPB dismisses a mixed case on a “procedural ground.” 568 U.S. at 50-51, 133 S. Ct. at 603. The Court’s analysis did not rest on any distinction between procedural and jurisdictional dismissals. To the contrary, it read § 7703(b)(2) as providing a single route for mixed cases: district court. Id. at 53-56, 133 S. Ct. at 604-07. The Court rejected the Government’s attempt to send mixed cases to the Federal Circuit whenever the Board did not reach the merits. Id. at 55-56, 133 S. Ct. at 606-07. That reasoning applies with equal force where, as here, the Board dismisses after concluding that the employee’s resignation or retirement was voluntary. A voluntaryness ruling does not alter the underlying character of the case as one “alleg[ing] that a basis for the action was discrimination.” 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1); see **Kloeckner**, 568 U.S. at 54, 133 S. Ct. at 604.

3. **The Federal Circuit’s contrary rule is inconsistent with the Supreme Court’s later decision in Perry’s own case.**  
After the D.C. Circuit transferred this case, the Federal Circuit’s decision was eventually rejected by the Supreme Court in **Perry v. Merit Systems Protection Board**, 582 U.S. 420, 137 S. Ct. 1975 (2017). There, the Court held that “[c]ases of discrimination” under § 7703(b)(2) include cases the MSPB dismisses for lack of jurisdiction, not just cases the Board resolves on the merits or on procedural grounds. Id. at 427-31, 137 S. Ct. at 1980-83. The Court explained that “a mixed case should be filed in district court” whether the Board dismisses it for procedural reasons or for lack of jurisdiction. Id. at 428, 137 S. Ct. at 1981. It further held that “the jurisdictional nature of the MSPB’s dismissal does not take this case outside § 7703(b)(2).” Id. at 431, 137 S. Ct. at 1983. That is the controlling answer here.

4. **The D.C. Circuit’s reliance on Powell cannot survive Perry and Kloeckner.**  
The D.C. Circuit relied on **Powell v. Department of Defense**, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), but **Perry** abrogated that approach. See **Perry**, 582 U.S. at 430-31, 137 S. Ct. at 1982-83. Once the Board has before it a mixed case and dismisses it, the proper forum for judicial review is district court. Id.; **Kloeckner**, 568 U.S. at 56, 133 S. Ct. at 607. A contrary rule would require employees and courts to litigate the Board’s jurisdictional conclusion in the Federal Circuit even though the statute directs mixed cases to district court. That is precisely the sort of forum confusion the Supreme Court rejected. **Kloeckner**, 568 U.S. at 55-56, 133 S. Ct. at 606-07; **Perry**, 582 U.S. at 430-31, 137 S. Ct. at 1982-83.

The Court should hold that the MSPB’s jurisdictional dismissal of Perry’s mixed case is reviewable in district court.
