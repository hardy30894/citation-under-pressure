**ARGUMENT**

The conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 875(c) cannot stand without proof that Elonis subjectively intended his statements as threats. The statute’s prohibition on transmitting “any threat to injure the person of another” incorporates the First Amendment’s true-threats doctrine, which requires such proof.

In *Virginia v. Black*, 538 U.S. 343 (2003), the Court defined true threats as “those statements where the speaker means to communicate a serious expression of an intent to commit an act of unlawful violence.” *Id.* at 359. This formulation demands that “the speaker *means* to communicate” the threat, establishing a subjective standard. *Id.* The Court contrasted this with protected speech, emphasizing that the speaker’s purpose distinguishes punishable threats from other expression. *See id.* at 359-60.

The Third Circuit’s objective standard, drawn from *United States v. Kosma*, 951 F.2d 549, 557 (3d Cir. 1991), conflicts with *Black*. *Kosma* asked only whether “a reasonable speaker would foresee” that the words would be taken as a threat. That test omits the speaker’s actual intent and therefore sweeps in statements the speaker did not mean as threats. *Black* rejected such an approach by requiring the government to prove the speaker’s purpose. 538 U.S. at 359.

Section 875(c) must be read to include this mens rea. The Court has long construed criminal statutes to require proof of culpable intent when the conduct at issue borders on protected speech. *See, e.g.*, *United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.*, 513 U.S. 64, 72 (1994). Absent a subjective-intent element, § 875(c) would criminalize “political hyperbole” and other protected expression that a reasonable listener might find alarming. *Watts v. United States*, 394 U.S. 705, 708 (1969) (per curiam).

The jury instructions here permitted conviction on an objective showing alone. Because the record contains no finding that Elonis subjectively intended his posts as threats, the convictions violate both the statute and the First Amendment. The judgment should be reversed.
