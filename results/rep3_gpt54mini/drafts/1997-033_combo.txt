**Argument**

The district court lacked authority to assign Lexecon’s case to itself for trial. Section 1407(a) authorizes transfer only “for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings,” and it commands that “[e]ach action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Congress thus drew a sharp line between pretrial coordination and trial. The court below erased that line by using § 1404(a) to keep the case for trial, but § 1404(a) cannot be deployed to defeat § 1407(a)’s mandatory remand command. See *Kerr v. United States Dist. Court*, 426 U.S. 394, 402 (1976) (mandamus is appropriate to confine a court to a lawful exercise of its jurisdiction); *La Buy v. Howes Leather Co.*, 352 U.S. 249, 256-59 (1957) (mandamus proper to correct district court action that exceeded lawful authority).

The statute’s text is decisive. Section 1407(a) speaks in mandatory terms: “shall be remanded.” Congress did not say “may be remanded,” nor did it authorize the transferee court to convert a temporary pretrial assignment into a trial forum. Where Congress has spoken with that clarity, courts may not revise the statute in the name of convenience. *United States v. Missouri Pac. R.R. Co.*, 278 U.S. 269, 278 (1929) (“plain words” of a statute control); *Caminetti v. United States*, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917) (where statutory language is plain, courts must enforce it as written); *Cochran v. United States*, 157 U.S. 286, 296 (1895) (courts cannot disregard statutory language under the guise of interpretation).

This Court has long recognized that venue statutes serve important structural and fairness interests, not mere administrative convenience. In *Neirbo Co. v. Bethlehem Shipbuilding Corp.*, 308 U.S. 165, 168-69 (1939), the Court treated venue as a privilege tied to the litigant’s forum entitlement. In *Missouri ex rel. St. Louis, Brownsville & Mexico Ry. Co. v. Taylor*, 266 U.S. 200, 209-10 (1924), the Court emphasized that venue provisions reflect deliberate legislative policy. And in *Lee v. Chesapeake & Ohio Ry. Co.*, 260 U.S. 653, 656 (1923), the Court refused to dilute a venue restriction by judicial invention. Those principles apply with special force here, where Congress expressly limited multidistrict transfer to pretrial proceedings and required remand thereafter.

The district court’s reliance on § 1404(a) cannot be reconciled with § 1407(a). Section 1404(a) permits transfer “[f]or the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice,” but it presupposes a case that may be transferred in the first instance. It does not authorize a transferee court to defeat a separate statute’s mandatory remand command. When two statutes address the same subject, the specific governs the general. *Morton v. Mancari*, 417 U.S. 535, 550-51 (1974); *Radzanower v. Touche Ross & Co.*, 426 U.S. 148, 153-54 (1976). Even before those decisions, the Court applied the same canon. *Ginsberg & Sons, Inc. v. Popkin*, 285 U.S. 204, 208 (1932) (specific statutory direction controls over more general language); *United States v. Chase*, 135 U.S. 255, 261-62 (1890) (later general words do not repeal or override specific provisions absent clear intent).

Nor can the transferee court’s action be justified by any supposed inherent authority. Federal courts possess only the powers Congress confers. *United States v. Hudson*, 11 U.S. (7 Cranch) 32, 33-34 (1812). A court may not enlarge its own authority because doing so seems efficient. *Wayman v. Southard*, 23 U.S. (10 Wheat.) 1, 42-43 (1825) (judicial power must remain within statutory bounds); *Ex parte Bollman*, 8 U.S. (4 Cranch) 75, 93-94 (1807) (jurisdiction must be grounded in lawful grant). Here, Congress granted a limited pretrial assignment and expressly required remand. The district court’s self-assignment for trial was therefore ultra vires.

Finally, mandamus is the appropriate remedy. When a district court departs from a clear statutory command and no ordinary appeal can restore the lost forum right, this Court may and should issue the writ. *Bankers Life & Cas. Co. v. Holland*, 346 U.S. 379, 383-84 (1953); *Ex parte Republic of Peru*, 318 U.S. 578, 583-84 (1943); *Roche v. Evaporated Milk Ass’n*, 319 U.S. 21, 25-26 (1943). Lexecon was entitled to remand at the conclusion of pretrial proceedings. The district court’s contrary ruling should be vacated, and the case remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
