**ARGUMENT**

I. **The Government Bears the Burden of Persuasion to Prove Continuing Conspiracy Beyond a Reasonable Doubt**

The government’s burden in a conspiracy prosecution is to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant was a member of the conspiracy *during the relevant period*—that is, within the statute of limitations. *United States v. Kissel*, 218 U.S. 601, 607 (1910) ("The gist of the offense of conspiracy is the unlawful agreement, and the overt act is only evidence of the agreement."). Where a defendant presents evidence of withdrawal from the conspiracy prior to the limitations period, the government must rebut that evidence with proof that the conspiracy continued and that the defendant remained a participant. *United States v. Irvine*, 246 F. 940, 942 (D. Mass. 1917) ("If the conspiracy is shown to have terminated before the statute of limitations began to run, the prosecution must fail.").

This principle is consistent with the general rule that the government bears the burden of proving every element of the offense, including the defendant’s participation in the conspiracy during the charged period. *Davis v. United States*, 160 U.S. 469, 482 (1895) ("The burden of proof is upon the prosecution to establish every essential element of the crime charged beyond a reasonable doubt."). The government cannot shift this burden to the defendant by arguing that the defendant’s withdrawal was insufficiently proven. *Morrison v. California*, 291 U.S. 82, 90 (1934) ("The burden of proof is upon the prosecution throughout.").

II. **The Jury Must Be Instructed on the Government’s Burden to Prove Continuing Conspiracy**

The trial court must instruct the jury that the government bears the burden of proving the defendant’s participation in the conspiracy during the limitations period. *Stirone v. United States*, 361 U.S. 212, 219 (1960) ("The trial court’s instructions must fairly and adequately cover the issues presented by the evidence."). Failure to do so is reversible error. *Bollenbach v. United States*, 326 U.S. 607, 614 (1946) ("The court’s charge to the jury must correctly state the law.").

Here, the government argued that the defendants’ withdrawal from the conspiracy prior to the limitations period shifted the burden to the defendants to prove their withdrawal. This argument is legally erroneous. The government’s burden to prove the defendant’s participation in the conspiracy during the relevant period is not diminished by the defendant’s evidence of withdrawal. *United States v. Rabinowich*, 238 U.S. 78, 86 (1915) ("The burden of proof is upon the prosecution to establish the defendant’s guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.").

III. **The Government’s Burden Includes Proving the Conspiracy Continued Into the Limitations Period**

The government must prove that the conspiracy continued into the limitations period and that the defendant was a member of that continuing conspiracy. *United States v. Socony-Vacuum Oil Co.*, 310 U.S. 150, 253 (1940) ("The conspiracy must be shown to have continued into the period covered by the indictment."). If the evidence shows that the conspiracy ended prior to the limitations period, the prosecution must fail. *United States v. Irvine*, 246 F. at 942.

In this case, the defendants presented evidence that the leaders of the conspiracy, Rodney Moore and Kevin Gray, split up prior to the limitations period. The government did not rebut this evidence with proof that the conspiracy continued or that the defendants remained participants. The trial court erred in failing to instruct the jury that the government bore the burden of proving the conspiracy continued into the limitations period and that the defendants were members of that continuing conspiracy.

IV. **The Government’s Failure to Rebut Evidence of Withdrawal Requires Reversal**

Where the government fails to rebut evidence of withdrawal from the conspiracy prior to the limitations period, the prosecution must fail. *United States v. Irvine*, 246 F. at 942. The trial court’s failure to instruct the jury on the government’s burden to prove continuing conspiracy into the limitations period was reversible error. *Bollenbach v. United States*, 326 U.S. at 614.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
