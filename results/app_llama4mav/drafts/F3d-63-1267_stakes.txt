ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING TARMAC'S MOTION FOR JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW ON STELWAGON'S ROBINSON-PATMAN ACT CLAIM BECAUSE STELWAGON FAILED TO PROVE ACTUAL ANTITRUST DAMAGES.

To recover damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act, a plaintiff must prove that it suffered actual antitrust damages as a result of the defendant's anticompetitive conduct. J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp., 451 U.S. 557, 562 (1981). The Supreme Court has held that a plaintiff cannot recover damages under section 4 without showing a causal link between the antitrust violation and the injury suffered. Zenith Radio Corp. v. Hazeltine Research, Inc., 395 U.S. 100, 114 (1969). In this case, Stelwagon failed to present sufficient evidence to establish a causal link between Tarmac's alleged price discrimination and Stelwagon's claimed damages.

The evidence presented by Stelwagon at trial was insufficient to prove that it suffered actual antitrust damages. Stelwagon's damages expert testified that Stelwagon's damages were calculated based on the difference between the prices Stelwagon paid for Tarmac's products and the lower prices paid by Stelwagon's competitors. However, this testimony was speculative and lacked a sufficient foundation to establish causation. See, e.g., Amerinet, Inc. v. Xerox Corp., 972 F.2d 1483, 1493-94 (8th Cir. 1992) (holding that damages calculations based on speculation and conjecture are insufficient to support a damages award under the Clayton Act).

Furthermore, the district court's reduction of the damages award from $2,272,000 to $450,383.60 demonstrates that the jury's verdict was not supported by the evidence. Stelwagon Manufacturing Company v. Tarmac Roofing Systems, Inc., 862 F.Supp. 1361, 1369 (E.D.Pa.1994). This significant reduction underscores the lack of evidence supporting Stelwagon's damages claim.

In light of the insufficient evidence presented by Stelwagon, the district court erred in denying Tarmac's motion for judgment as a matter of law on Stelwagon's Robinson-Patman Act claim. The judgment awarding Stelwagon treble damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act should be vacated.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO GRANT TARMAC'S MOTION FOR JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW ON STELWAGON'S BREACH OF CONTRACT CLAIM.

Although the breach of contract claim is not directly related to the Robinson-Patman Act claim, the district court's error in allowing the antitrust damages to stand tainted the entire verdict. Given that the antitrust damages award should be vacated, the court should re-examine the breach of contract claim. However, as the argument is focused on the Robinson-Patman Act claim, we will not extensively brief the breach of contract claim here.

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should vacate the district court's judgment insofar as it awards Stelwagon treble damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act and remand for further proceedings consistent with this Court's opinion.
