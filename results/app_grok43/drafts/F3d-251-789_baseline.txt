ARGUMENT

The district court clearly erred in finding that Gonzalez-Caballero consented to Mena’s removal and retention of Danelsy. Under the Hague Convention, a court may deny return if the petitioner “consented to or subsequently acquiesced in the removal or retention.” Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction, art. 13(a), 19 I.L.M. 1501 (1980); see also 42 U.S.C. § 11603(e)(2)(B). Consent must be shown by a preponderance of the evidence and must be “unequivocal.” Mozes v. Mozes, 239 F.3d 1067, 1076 (9th Cir. 2001); see also Feder v. Evans-Feder, 63 F.3d 217, 226 (3d Cir. 1995).

The record establishes no such consent. Gonzalez-Caballero’s accounts uniformly describe an agreement conditioned on Mena’s assistance with her immigration and her eventual reassumption of custody. Mena’s own testimony confirms that the parties discussed Danelsy’s “better life in the United States” only in the context of Gonzalez-Caballero’s planned relocation. Conditional permission does not constitute consent to permanent retention. See, e.g., In re Kim, 404 F. Supp. 2d 495, 515 (S.D.N.Y. 2005) (consent limited to temporary stay insufficient).

Even if Gonzalez-Caballero initially agreed to Danelsy’s departure, she promptly revoked any acquiescence. Within days she contacted Panamanian authorities, the United States military liaison, and the Institute for Children. Such immediate and consistent efforts negate any inference of acquiescence. See, e.g., Friedrich v. Friedrich, 78 F.3d 1060, 1069 (6th Cir. 1996) (acquiescence requires “an act or statement with the requisite formality” and “a clear and unequivocal” manifestation of consent).

The district court’s contrary finding rests on Gonzalez-Caballero’s delivery of travel documents and her presence at the airport. These acts are consistent with a temporary arrangement and do not demonstrate the unequivocal consent required by the Convention. See Mozes, 239 F.3d at 1076. Because the finding of consent is unsupported by the record, it is clearly erroneous. The petition for return should have been granted.
