The revision of Georgia Gwinnett College’s policies during litigation rendered the constitutional challenges moot. When an event occurs that makes it impossible for a court to grant effectual relief, the case must be dismissed. Mills v. Green, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895). A live controversy must persist at every stage of review. Southern Pacific Terminal Co. v. ICC, 219 U.S. 498, 514 (1911). Declaratory and injunctive claims against superseded policies present no such controversy. United States v. Alaska Steamship Co., 253 U.S. 113, 116 (1920).

Nominal damages alone do not preserve jurisdiction once the challenged conduct has ended. St. Pierre v. United States, 319 U.S. 41, 42 (1943). A judgment awarding only a symbolic sum would not affect the legal relations of the parties. Aetna Life Ins. Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227, 240-241 (1937). Courts have long refused to entertain actions seeking merely to vindicate abstract rights without concrete consequences. Maryland Casualty Co. v. Pacific Coal & Oil Co., 312 U.S. 270, 273 (1941).

The capable-of-repetition exception does not apply where the policies have been permanently withdrawn and no reasonable expectation of recurrence exists. United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36, 39 (1950). As-applied claims likewise become moot when the specific restrictions no longer operate. A.L. Mechling Barge Lines, Inc. v. United States, 368 U.S. 324, 329-330 (1961). Plaintiffs who have graduated or ceased their challenged activities lack the requisite personal stake. Sibron v. New York, 392 U.S. 40, 57 (1968).

A request for nominal damages cannot convert an otherwise academic dispute into a justiciable case. Golden v. Zwickler, 394 U.S. 103, 108 (1969). The Constitution limits federal courts to actual controversies between adverse parties. Muskrat v. United States, 219 U.S. 346, 361 (1911). Allowing nominal damages to revive moot claims would expand judicial power beyond its historic bounds. Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447, 488 (1923).

The district court and court of appeals correctly dismissed the action.
