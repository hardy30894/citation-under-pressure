**Argument**

I. **Section 1333(b) does not extend OCSLA benefits to every land-based injury with some connection to shelf operations.**

OCSLA extends LHWCA coverage only for “disability or death of an employee resulting from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The statutory text ties coverage to injuries that result from OCS operations, but it does not erase ordinary territorial limits or convert every downstream, land-based accident into a shelf injury. The Ninth Circuit’s “substantial nexus” test rewrites the statute and conflicts with the Supreme Court’s repeated recognition that OCSLA coverage is defined by location and structure, not by free-floating causal connection. Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207, 219 (1986); Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray, 470 U.S. 414, 423-24 (1985).

Tallentire is especially important. There, the Court explained that “Congress determined that the general scope of OCSLA’s coverage . . . would be determined principally by locale, not by the status of the individual injured or killed.” 477 U.S. at 219. Although the Court’s footnote 2 was dicta, it still confirms that OCSLA operates through a situs-based framework, not an unlimited causation rule. Id. at 219 n.2. The Ninth Circuit erred by treating that footnote as a prompt to invent a new nexus standard rather than by respecting the structure Congress chose.

Herb’s Welding reinforces that point. The Court rejected an expansive reading of OCSLA-linked coverage where the worker’s activity was not part of the statutory offshore regime, emphasizing that OCSLA “draws a clear line between land and sea.” 470 U.S. at 426. The Ninth Circuit’s rule blurs that line and extends federal compensation to injuries occurring miles inland at an oil-processing facility.

II. **The better reading of Section 1333(b) is the Fifth Circuit’s situs-of-injury rule, which provides administrable limits and tracks the statute’s offshore focus.**

The Fifth Circuit correctly held that OCSLA benefits apply only when the injury occurs on the outer Continental Shelf or on waters above it. Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs, 877 F.2d 356, 361-62 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc). Mills gives effect to the statutory phrase “resulting from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf” without detaching coverage from the shelf itself. Id. at 362. That interpretation also accords with subsequent Fifth Circuit decisions applying the same geographic limitation. Becker v. Tidewater, Inc., 586 F.3d 358, 366-67 (5th Cir. 2009); Pickett v. Petroleum Helicopters, Inc., 266 F.3d 366, 368 (5th Cir. 2001); Sisson v. Davis & Sons, Inc., 131 F.3d 555, 558 (5th Cir. 1998).

By contrast, the Ninth Circuit’s substantial-nexus standard has no textual anchor. Its breadth would allow coverage for injuries occurring in warehouses, parking lots, roads, or homes so long as a claimant can identify some operational connection to offshore work. That is not what Congress enacted. See Mills, 877 F.2d at 362; Tallentire, 477 U.S. at 219.

III. **Curtis does not justify the Ninth Circuit’s rule.**

The Third Circuit’s contrary decision in Curtis v. Schlumberger Offshore Service, Inc., 849 F.2d 805, 809-11 (3d Cir. 1988), is unpersuasive because it transformed the words “as the result of” into a general but-for test untethered to situs. Curtis allowed coverage for a car accident on the way to a helicopter, id. at 806, 811, but that approach collapses the statute’s limiting language into proximate cause. The Supreme Court has not read OCSLA that way. Tallentire, 477 U.S. at 219; Herb’s Welding, 470 U.S. at 423-26.

IV. **The Ninth Circuit’s decision also creates needless overlap with the LHWCA and undermines the statutory scheme.**

The LHWCA separately covers injuries “upon the navigable waters of the United States” and certain adjoining maritime areas. 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). The Supreme Court has described the LHWCA as a statute with a defined maritime situs-and-status structure. Northeast Marine Terminal Co. v. Caputo, 432 U.S. 249, 265-67 (1977); P.C. Pfeiffer Co. v. Ford, 444 U.S. 69, 80-83 (1979). If OCSLA is read to cover any land injury with a substantial offshore nexus, its compensation scheme becomes broader than the LHWCA without the LHWCA’s situs limits, a result Congress did not create. See Herb’s Welding, 470 U.S. at 423-26; Northeast Marine Terminal, 432 U.S. at 264-67.

V. **This Court should reject the substantial-nexus test and reaffirm that OCSLA coverage remains tied to offshore situs.**

The proper rule is the one applied by the Benefits Review Board and the Fifth Circuit: OCSLA benefits do not extend to an injury that occurs on land, away from the outer Continental Shelf, merely because the worker’s duties were related to offshore operations. Mills, 877 F.2d at 361-62; Becker, 586 F.3d at 366-67; Pickett, 266 F.3d at 368; Sisson, 131 F.3d at 558. That reading best fits the text, the Supreme Court’s guidance, and the need for a workable jurisdictional boundary. Tallentire, 477 U.S. at 219; Herb’s Welding, 470 U.S. at 423-26.

For those reasons, the judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
