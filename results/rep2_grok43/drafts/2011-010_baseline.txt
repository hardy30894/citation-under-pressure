**ARGUMENT**

The text of 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) extends Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act coverage only when “disability or death of an employee result[s] from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” The phrase “injury occurring” limits coverage to injuries sustained on the Shelf itself. See Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs, 877 F.2d 356, 361 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc) (“the injury must occur on an OCS platform or on the waters above the OCS”).

In Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207 (1986), the Court observed that “Congress determined that the general scope of OCSLA’s coverage . . . would be determined principally by locale, not by the status of the individual injured or killed.” Id. at 219. Although the statement addressed § 1333(a)(2)(A), the Court expressly noted that § 1333(b) “makes compensation for the death or injury of an ‘employee’ resulting from certain operations on the Outer Continental Shelf payable under the Longshoremen’s and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act.” Id. at 219 n.2. This language confirms that both the operations and the injury must occur on the Shelf.

The Fifth Circuit’s situs-of-injury requirement follows directly from that reading. An employee injured onshore while constructing a platform destined for the Shelf receives no OCSLA benefits. Mills, 877 F.2d at 362. The same rule bars recovery here: Valladolid’s fatal injury occurred at the La Conchita facility, three miles inland from the nearest pier and separated from the ocean by a highway and railroad tracks.

The Third Circuit’s contrary “but for” test in Curtis v. Schlumberger Offshore Service, Inc., 849 F.2d 805, 809-11 (3d Cir. 1988), cannot be reconciled with the statutory text. Section 1333(b) does not say “resulting from operations that are connected to the Shelf”; it requires an injury “occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” The Ninth Circuit’s “substantial nexus” gloss adds language Congress omitted.

Legislative history reinforces the geographic limit. The 1978 amendments incorporated the LHWCA “with respect to disability or death . . . occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” Pub. L. No. 95-372, § 203, 92 Stat. 629, 635. Congress chose the same situs-based formulation it had used for fixed platforms in § 1333(a). See Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray, 470 U.S. 414, 422 (1985) (recognizing OCSLA’s “explicit geographic limitation”).

Because Valladolid’s injury did not occur on the outer Continental Shelf, the Benefits Review Board correctly denied OCSLA benefits. The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
