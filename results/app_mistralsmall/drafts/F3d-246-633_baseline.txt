**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON APPELLANT’S CONSPIRACY CLAIM UNDER THE RAILWAY LABOR ACT (RLA), 45 U.S.C. § 153.**

The RLA provides a comprehensive scheme for the resolution of labor disputes in the railroad industry, including mandatory arbitration procedures. *See* 45 U.S.C. § 153 First (i); *Union Pacific R.R. Co. v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Eng’rs & Trainmen,* 558 F.3d 979, 984 (9th Cir. 2009). Appellant’s conspiracy claim arises from the alleged collusion between UTU and CSX to deny him the shares of CSX stock to which he claimed entitlement under the labor agreement. To state a claim for conspiracy under the RLA, a plaintiff must allege facts showing an agreement between a union and an employer to deprive employees of their rights under the RLA. *See* 45 U.S.C. § 153; *see also Steele v. Louisville & N.R.R. Co.,* 323 U.S. 192, 202 (1944) (recognizing that unions and employers may not conspire to deprive employees of their statutory rights).

Here, the district court erred in granting summary judgment because there exists a genuine dispute of material fact as to whether UTU and CSX conspired to deprive Appellant of his rights under the RLA. Specifically, the record reflects that UTU’s internal appeals board ruled in Appellant’s favor, ordering that he receive the disputed shares of stock, yet UTU’s representative, Earley, argued against Appellant’s position during the arbitration hearing. This conduct, viewed in the light most favorable to Appellant, could support an inference of collusion between UTU and CSX to deny Appellant his rightful entitlement. *See Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.,* 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986) (summary judgment is inappropriate where there is a genuine dispute of material fact).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN AWARDING DAMAGES AND ATTORNEYS’ FEES ON DEFENDANTS’ COUNTERCLAIMS UNDER THE FEDERAL WIRETAP ACT, 18 U.S.C. § 2510 ET SEQ.**

The Federal Wiretap Act prohibits the intentional interception, use, or disclosure of wire, oral, or electronic communications. *See* 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(a). The Act, however, contains an exception for the recording of conversations where one party to the conversation has given prior consent. *See* 18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d). The district court erred in concluding that Appellant’s recording of the executive session violated the Act because the executive session was not a “private conversation” within the meaning of the statute.

The Sixth Circuit has held that the Wiretap Act does not apply to conversations in which the parties do not have a reasonable expectation of privacy. *See United States v. Jones,* 545 F.3d 607, 613 (6th Cir. 2008) (citing *Babcock v. Kimberly Clark Corp.,* 437 F.3d 804, 809 (6th Cir. 2006)). Here, the executive session was a formal proceeding of a private arbitration panel, and the participants were aware that the session was being held to discuss procedural matters and pending cases. Under these circumstances, the participants could not have had a reasonable expectation that their statements would remain private. *See also In re Grand Jury Proceedings (United States v. Doe),* 841 F.2d 1556, 1561 (11th Cir. 1988) (holding that conversations in a grand jury room are not private for purposes of the Wiretap Act).

Moreover, even if the executive session were deemed a private conversation, Appellant’s recording did not violate the Act because he was a party to the conversation and did not act with the intent to intercept it. *See* 18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d); *see also United States v. Amen,* 831 F.2d 373, 378 (2d Cir. 1987) (holding that a party to a conversation may record it without violating the Wiretap Act). The district court’s contrary conclusion was erroneous and warrants reversal.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in granting summary judgment on Appellant’s conspiracy claim under the RLA and in awarding damages and attorneys’ fees on Defendants’ counterclaims under the Federal Wiretap Act. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and this matter should be remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
