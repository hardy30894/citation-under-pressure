\title{Citation under Pressure: What Deployment Constraints Do to Legal Citation Integrity, and What Deterministic Verification Can Repair}

\author{\fnms{Harsh} \snm{Dubey}%
\thanks{Corresponding Author: Harsh Dubey, New York University; hd2225@nyu.edu.}}
\runningauthor{H. Dubey}
\address{New York University, New York, USA}

\begin{abstract}
Sanctions over fabricated legal citations have made language-model drafting a problem for courts, and most research on the problem has produced detection benchmarks that score errors in finished briefs. This paper measures how the errors are produced. Seven models from seven vendors, from a 30B open-weight model to frontier systems, drafted argument sections for 48 U.S. Supreme Court matters under five prompting conditions: baseline, a citation quota, a restriction to pre-1970 authority, a sanctions warning, and all three combined. Every citation in the \nDrafts{} drafts was checked deterministically, with no language-model judge in any primary measurement: existence against an 18-million-row reporter index, quotations against a verbatim checker validated on seeded faults, and pinpoint pages against official reporter pagination from public archives. The effects are specific to the model and the constraint, and the failure mode moves with capability: under combined pressure the smallest model's citation existence fell from \existQwenBase{} to \existQwenCombo{}, verbatim quotation accuracy fell for four models in the pre-registered run and for six of seven once three generations are pooled, and Sonnet~5's rate did not fall because it quoted less under pressure; its residual errors are paraphrase presented as quotation, real language from the wrong case, and wrong pages. In a second experiment the checker fed its findings back into revision. Sonnet~5 reached \loopSonnetTrueFinal{} verbatim accuracy, and a per-quotation trace shows how: of its \trFlagRZeroSonnet{} flagged quotations, \trTrueFlagRepairedSonnet{} were corrected and \trTrueFlagDeletedSonnet{} deleted. A flag that carries the closest passage of the cited opinion raises the corrections fourfold, and removal still exceeds repair in every model. False feedback produced the same deletions aimed at correct quotations, and a verifier with 50 percent precision removed \trHalfAccRemovedSonnet{} of Sonnet~5's \trAccRZeroSonnet{} correct quotations against \trTrueAccRemovedSonnet{} under a precise one. A second task on 48 federal appellate matters shows lower baseline existence for six of seven models. We release the instruments, including a page-level pinpoint verifier from public data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Since 2023, courts have repeatedly sanctioned lawyers who filed briefs with citations a language model invented, from \textit{Mata v.~Avianca}~\cite{mata2023} to a docket a public tracker now counts in the thousands~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}. Most published work frames the problem as detection: given a brief that already contains errors, how many can a model or agent find~\cite{liu2026whochecks,chen2026legalcitebench}? Detection accuracy is the right measure for reviewing a filed brief; it does not tell a deployer which prompting conditions produce the errors, this paper's question.

This paper asks two production-side questions. First, which prompting conditions cause citation failure, and for which models? Deployed prompts carry constraints of the kind Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} studied for scholarly citations, where a publication-year window lowered citation existence even in capable models while a breadth demand raised it for the strongest model and lowered it for the open-weight ones; we add a sanctions warning and know of no comparable study for law. Second, when a deterministic checker feeds its findings back into revision, does the model correct them, leave them, or change the draft so that the tests pass without the quotations being corrected?

Both questions need an instrument the field has said does not exist in public: the best published detection agent misses nearly half of incorrect pinpoint citations (52.8 percent recall), its authors concluding that ``no models can reliably detect wrong pincites'' because page-level information lives behind commercial databases~\cite{liu2026whochecks}. We built it from public sources: existence against a local index derived from CourtListener bulk data, verbatim quotation checking validated on seeded faults and audited on real drafts, and page-level pinpoint verification for U.S. Reports authority against the official pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's volume archives~\cite{cap2024}.

Our primary setting is the closed-book use of general-purpose models, the setting of most sanctioned filings documented to date~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}; a grounded arm with ten retrieved, verified authorities in the prompt measures what retrieval changes. Drafts under the four non-baseline conditions are the pressured drafts (\nPressured{} in the Supreme Court task, three refusals), and the models with the highest baseline existence are the top of the range (Section~\ref{sec:method}). The paper contributes three things: a constraint-sensitive evaluation, in which reliability at a neutral prompt does not predict reliability under drafting constraints and the failure mode moves with capability; deterministic verification of existence, verbatim quotation, and pinpoint page with no language-model judge, released with the drafts; and the finding that verification is not repair, since flags fed back to the model raise measured accuracy by deletion and an imprecise verifier damages correct content.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Legal citation reliability.} Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} build a detection benchmark from real appellate briefs with injected errors and a taxonomy grounded in sanctioned filings; we adopt their error categories and measure the naturally occurring errors their limitations section asks for. LegalCiteBench~\cite{chen2026legalcitebench} shows that models asked closed-book for a named case's citation almost never produce it or abstain; our task asks the easier question, whether the cases a model chooses to cite exist. Dahl et al.~\cite{dahl2024fictions} and Magesh et al.~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree} measure hallucinated authority in chatbots and research tools.

\textbf{Constraints and fabrication.} Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} is the closest design: five prompting regimes over scholarly claims, verified by fuzzy bibliographic matching that left 36 to 61 percent of citations unresolved. We transplant their factorial to law with deterministic verification (\unverifiablePct{} percent unresolved) and a formal statistical model.

\textbf{Revision with execution feedback.} The loop protocol follows execution-feedback work in code generation~\cite{gehring2025rlef}, with its no-feedback control separating feedback content from extra revision; we add controls in which a quarter to all of the feedback is false.

\textbf{AI and law.} Blair-Stanek and Van Durme~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable} found at ICAIL that frontier models flip the winning party across identical zero-temperature runs, which Section~\ref{sec:limits} bounds with repeated generations. Evaluation in this community pairs automated metrics with the checks lawyers perform~\cite{gultekin2025twodim,sanchi2024hybrid}; citation integrity is one with deterministic ground truth. Garneau et al.~\cite{garneau2024pincites} found retrieving the paragraph a proposition rests on far harder than retrieving the case; our verifier meets the same gap from the other side, checking a generated pincite against the page text. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025overruled} show models miss overruled precedent; ConsRAG~\cite{nguyen2024consrag} constrains retrieval, complementary to our loop's feedback; Trozze et al.~\cite{trozze2025crypto} and Harasta et al.~\cite{harasta2026cannotberight} study how jurors and lawyers perceive model-written documents, a perception machine verification does not depend on. Evaluations of generated legal argument~\cite{gray2025magnitudes,pires2025legalwriting} do not check the authority it cites, the layer this paper adds; citation-worthiness and citation-network work~\cite{khatri2023citecaselaw,sadeghian2018edgelabeling,dadgostari2021lawsearch,mumford2025citationnetworks} studies the network our checker queries.

\section{Tasks, Conditions, and Instruments}\label{sec:method}

\textbf{Terms.} In 347 U.S. 483, 495, the volume, reporter, and first page identify the case and the second page is the \textit{pincite}, for a quotation the page where the quoted language appears. \textit{Citation existence} is the share of adjudicable citations that resolve to a real case; \textit{strict quotation accuracy} the share of scored quotations, those the draft attributes to a case whose text the checker holds, that reproduce the opinion verbatim; a quotation the draft does not attribute is outside the rate.

\textbf{Matters.} The matters are forty-eight Supreme Court cases from 1990 to 2020 drawn from the Supreme Court Database~\cite{spaeth2025scdb}. Each packet holds the question presented, party names, a factual summary, and a lower-court opinion excerpt, the Supreme Court's decision excluded and outcome leakage screened. The model argues an assigned side (petitioner for even-numbered matters, respondent for odd) in an argument section of under 900 words; nothing predicts outcomes, so the memorization confound documented for Supreme Court case classification~\cite{ortega2025scotusmem} is out of scope. A second task uses forty-eight published F.3d decisions from the Caselaw Access Project archive (1995 to 2019, four from each of the First through Eleventh and Federal Circuits, no cross-appeals). The packet is the opinion's own statement of the case and facts, cut where the analysis begins and scrubbed of outcome sentences, and the model argues for the named appellant under all five conditions.

\textbf{Conditions.} The five conditions are baseline; quota (``cite at least eight distinct case authorities''); temporal (``every authority you cite MUST have been decided before 1970''), a clause no user writes that stands in for the binding-authority and jurisdiction limits practitioners do impose, and which, unlike Zhao et al.'s window on recent work, moves citations toward well-memorized opinions; stakes (``this section will be filed with the court; citing authority that does not exist, or misquoting authority, risks sanctions under Rule~11''); and combined, which concatenates the three clauses. Each condition has one fixed template in the primary run, at temperature 0. The stakes clause names Rule~11, the rule invoked in the sanctioned-filing cases~\cite{mata2023}, which governs district-court civil actions~\cite{frcp2024}, so for a Supreme Court filing the clause is a stakes signal only. Each clause is a single sentence so that its effect can be isolated; a second template rephrasing every condition was run on all 48 matters and seven models. A \textit{grounded} arm reran baseline and combined with ten retrieved authorities in the prompt: the U.S. Reports opinions most similar to the question and facts (TF-IDF over the first 2,500 words of the \retrievalCorpus{} cached opinions of 2,000 characters or more with a name and date in the index, decided before argument and before 1970 for the combined condition, neither party named), each with citation, year, and its best-matching 250-word passage, stated to be real, quotation restricted to the excerpts.

{\sloppy\textbf{Models.} The models are Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mistral Small, Llama-4 Maverick, DeepSeek V4 Flash, Grok~4.3, GPT-5.4-mini, and Claude Sonnet~5, one per vendor, spanning open-weight 30B to frontier; tables are ordered by baseline strict quotation accuracy, and Sonnet~5, GPT-5.4-mini, and Grok~4.3, with the highest baseline existence, are called the top of the range. The seven were run through OpenRouter in August 2026 with completion limits of 1,400 to 12,000 tokens; the identifiers are \texttt{qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507}, \texttt{mistral-small-2603}, \texttt{llama-4-maverick}, \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash}, \texttt{grok-4.3}, \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini}, and \texttt{claude-sonnet-5}. An eighth model, GLM-4.7-flash, produced no output on \glmMissing{} of 240 drafts (\glmMissingCombo{} combined), exhausting a 30,000-token budget in reasoning, a third failure mode beside fabrication and refusal; it is excluded from the tables.\par}

\textbf{Adjudication.} Citations are extracted with eyecite~\cite{cushman2021eyecite} and given one of three labels: exists, not found, or unverifiable (vendor citations and coverage gaps), so that index coverage is never counted as fabrication; \unverifiablePct{} percent of the \nCitationRecords{} citation records in the full run are unverifiable. Citations resolve against an 18-million-row table; quotations are located by balanced quotation marks and attributed, in order, to the citation whose parenthetical holds them, the case named in their own sentence, the citation that follows them in that sentence (not one introduced by \textit{see} or \textit{cf.}), the citation an \textit{Id.} points to, or the last citation in the paragraph; a quotation introduced by a statute, rule, the record, or a lower court, or with no citation in its paragraph, is left unpaired, since the checker has nothing to test it against: \unpairedPctAll{} percent of the \quotesAll{} quotations in the full run, \unpairedNoncasePct{} percent introduced by non-case material and \unpairedNoCitePct{} percent standing in a paragraph that cites nothing, a share that runs from \unpairedPctMin{} to \unpairedPctMax{} percent across models. Quotations are checked verbatim against the cited opinion's text from public sources. \textit{Exists} means the volume, reporter, and page resolve to an indexed opinion; the case name is compared separately, and \nmMismatch{} of \nmChecked{} resolving citations (\nmPct{} percent) carry a name the index does not match, a ceiling that includes abbreviated names (Sonnet~5: \nmMismatchSonnet{} of \nmCheckedSonnet). A quotation is \textit{near miss} when every fragment reaches token coverage of at least 0.85 without matching exactly, and \textit{inaccurate} otherwise. The strict rate for a draft $d$ is $S_d = n_{\text{acc}} / (n_{\text{acc}} + n_{\text{near}} + n_{\text{inacc}})$, and a lenient rate counting near misses as correct is reported alongside it. Matching is case and punctuation insensitive, splits on ellipses and on bracketed alterations (so that a lawful substitution is read as an omission and the words around it must match), and drops alteration parentheticals; the lenient rate is a diagnostic, since 0.85 coverage admits one wrong word in seven. The quotation checker scores 240 of 240 on twenty synthetic drafts planting twelve item kinds, six of them attribution traps (a rival citation after a signal phrase, a statute, an \textit{Id.}, a string-cite parenthetical, paragraphs with and without a citation), and the existence and pinpoint checks \valPinsPass{} of \valPinsTotal{} on \valPinsCases{} seeded opinions; Section~\ref{sec:residual} audits the checker on real drafts. Pinpoint pages are verified against the official reporter pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's HTML volumes, which mark every page break; a quotation counts as on the cited page when its longest fragment lies within that page's text.

\textbf{Human reference.} The same instrument scored \humanExcerpts{} pre-ChatGPT appellate brief excerpts from Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} (no injected error; OCR-converted, so a floor); human lawyers reach \humanStrict{} strict and \humanLenient{} lenient quotation accuracy (\humanScored{} scored quotations) and \humanExist{} citation existence (\humanExistUsPubFed{} on U.S. Reports and published federal reporters; Federal Appendix spellings, once mismatched against the index, now resolve for models and briefs alike), a reference for the severity of the verbatim standard only, since human briefs differ in citation mix. The literal matcher the strict standard replaced, which read a bracketed alteration as its contents, puts the human rate at \humanAltStrict{} and moves model cells by at most \altMaxCellDiff{} points, with the same \altSurv{} contrasts surviving correction (Section~\ref{sec:exp1}); of the \humanFailTotal{} human strict failures that remain, \humanFailNearUnaltered{} are near misses without any alteration, the signature of a corrupted character.

\textbf{Inference.} The primary analysis is a citation-level logistic generalized estimating equation per model, $\text{logit}\,P(y_{imk}=1)=\beta_0+\beta_k$ for citation $i$ in matter $m$ under condition $k$, with exchangeable working correlation within matters~\cite{liang1986gee}, so that the 48 matters are the independent units. Each model has \nHolmTests{} contrasts (four conditions, two outcomes); we report Holm-corrected $p$ values within model, each model being the family the paper draws conclusions about, and call an effect significant only when it survives correction; counts of models (``three of seven'') are descriptive, and with 48 matters as the unit the effective sample is 48, not the thousands of citations. Odds ratios, intervals, and $p$ values for all 56 contrasts are released, and a matter-level cluster bootstrap agreed in sign with every corrected result. Three robustness fits follow the same model: one pools two further generations of the baseline and combined drafts (Section~\ref{sec:limits}) with a run effect (Holm over 14 tests), the others pool both templates or both tasks with a template or task effect, corrected within model. For the revision experiment, pre-registered descriptively, we add matter-paired comparisons of final strict accuracy between arms (Wilcoxon, bootstrap interval). Hypotheses and decision rules were frozen before the full run; every later decision, including two withdrawn findings, is in a dated ledger.

\textbf{Citation existence.} In the single run, only the 30B model's existence moves significantly under pressure: from \existQwenBase{} at baseline to \existQwenCombo{} under the combined condition, with the quota, temporal, and combined contrasts all significant after correction (combined OR \orCiteQwenCombo, 95 percent CI \ciCiteQwenCombo, corrected $p\,\holmCiteQwenCombo$). Mistral Small falls under the temporal and combined conditions without reaching significance, and the five other models stay between 0.93 and 1.00 in every condition. Each surviving existence contrast rests on at least \nfQwenBase{} citations not found per arm; a record is one occurrence of a full citation (short forms are not counted), and refitting on distinct authorities per draft leaves the same \distinctSurv{} contrasts.

\textbf{Quotation accuracy.} The temporal restriction damages quoting most; after correction, strict quotation accuracy falls under temporal and combined pressure for Llama-4 (\strictLlamaBase{} to \strictLlamaCombo), under the combined condition for Qwen3-30B (\strictQwenBase{} to \strictQwenCombo) and DeepSeek (\strictDeepseekBase{} to \strictDeepseekCombo), and under the temporal clause for Mistral Small; GPT-5.4-mini and Grok~4.3 fall in the same direction without surviving correction in the single run, and Sonnet~5 does not fall (below). Llama-4 shows the largest drop. At baseline its strict rate is \strictLlamaBase{} (the human floor is \humanStrict), and under the temporal restriction it drops to \strictLlamaTemporal{} (OR \orQuoteLlamaTemporal, CI \ciQuoteLlamaTemporal, corrected $p\,\holmQuoteLlamaTemporal$); refitting every contrast on the lenient outcome, \lenSurv{} survive correction, the combined fall among them for Qwen3-30B, DeepSeek, and Mistral Small. Cell rates differ between templates by up to \phrMaxStrictDiff{} points of quotation accuracy and \phrMaxExistDiff{} of existence.

Baseline citation existence on the 48 appellate matters runs from \aExistBaseMin{} for Qwen3-30B to \aExistSonnetBase{} for Sonnet~5, lower than on the Supreme Court matters for \aLowerThanScotus{} of the seven models, Sonnet~5 alone unchanged. The rise is selection, fewer and safer quotations, and the temporal restriction also shifts the cited cases toward older, better-memorized opinions (median decision year \sonnetYearCombo{} against \sonnetYearBase). With three generations pooled Sonnet~5's existence rise survives the logistic fit (corrected $p$=\runsHolmCiteSonnet), but it rests on twelve citations not found (\sonnetRunsNfBase{} of \sonnetRunsAdjBase{} at baseline, \sonnetRunsNfCombo{} of \sonnetRunsAdjCombo{} under combined), on which an exact test gives $p$=\sonnetRunsFisherP; the quotation rise reaches corrected $p$=\runsHolmQuoteSonnet{} there. \section{Experiment 2: The Checker inside the Revision Loop}\label{sec:exp2}

Combined-condition drafts entered a revision loop: each round the checker returned one line per failure (a citation that does not resolve; a quotation not verbatim in the cited opinion), the model revised, and the loop ended on a clean draft or after three revisions. A \textit{false feedback} control gave the same number of identically formatted lines aimed at correct items, \textit{mixed} controls replaced each line with a false one at random for a precision of about 0.25, 0.5, or 0.75, and a \textit{no-feedback} control gave the same rounds with a generic instruction to revise. The loop used the first 24 matters in pre-registered order; loop rates are means over drafts with at least one scored quotation (a draft left with none drops out; \lcEmptyTrueTotal{} of the 168 true-arm episodes ended so, \lcEmptyTrueGrok{} of Grok~4.3's 24, so its final rate rests on \lcGrokTrueDrafts{} drafts), so round-0 figures differ from the pooled rates in Table~\ref{tab:pressure}. The loop was rerun after the attribution rule was corrected; the original run, whose true-arm flags had realized precision \rpTrueAll{} under the corrected rule (\rpTrueSonnet{} for Sonnet~5), is released beside it. Every scored round-0 quotation was traced to the final draft as corrected, dequoted, deleted, or left in place (Table~\ref{tab:true}).

\section{Residual Errors at the Top of the Range}\label{sec:residual}

\textbf{Paraphrase inside quotation marks.} Decomposing every inaccurate quotation against the cited opinion's text, \decompParaPctSonnet{} percent of Sonnet~5's (\decompParaSonnet{} of \inaccTotalSonnet) are paraphrases of the correct case presented as verbatim quotation, \decompFabSonnet{} match nothing substantial in the cited opinion, and \decompOutSonnet{} cite authority outside the U.S. Reports, beyond the decomposition's corpus.

\textbf{Real language, wrong case.} Searching every inaccurate quotation against the \provenanceCorpus{} U.S. Reports opinions in the Caselaw Access Project cache locates \misattrTotal{} quotations across the seven models (\misattrSonnet{} for Sonnet~5, \misattrQwen{} for Qwen3-30B) whose language appears verbatim in a different opinion than the one cited (in \misattrSameDraft{} the true source is cited in the same draft, so the count is a ceiling); the rest, found nowhere or matching only stock phrases (\notInCorpusSonnet{} and \genericMatchSonnet{} for Sonnet~5), are the paraphrases above and quotations from other courts. Two audits bound the checker's error: the same search finds the language present in the attributed case for \falseAlarmSonnet{} of Sonnet~5's \inaccTotalSonnet{}, and a hand reading of \auditSampleN{} inaccurate verdicts drawn at random across models found \auditSampleCheckerSide{} on the checker's side (\auditSampleCheckerSidePct{} percent, 95 percent interval \auditSampleCiLow{} to \auditSampleCiHigh: non-case material in quotation marks, or a quotation bound to the wrong case), \auditSampleModel{} the model's, \auditSampleAlteration{} failing only on a bracketed alteration, and \auditSampleOpen{} open. The share was lowest under the combined condition (\auditCondLowN) and highest under the quota (\auditCondHighN), so residual error works against the falls reported, and \auditAccN{} accurate verdicts read the same way all held. The rule's first version put about half of a forty-item sample on the checker's side.

\textbf{Right case, wrong page.} Of the \pinAllPins{} pinpoints in the Supreme Court drafts, \pinSpanPct{} percent were checked against the cited case's page span (the rest cite other reporters or cases past volume \capLastVolume, the archive's last) and \pinWithQuote{} carry a quotation on the same citation; for those the quotation lies on the cited page \pinAtSonnet{} percent of the time for Sonnet~5, \pinAtMistral{} to \pinAtLlama{} percent for the other six, and \pinAtHuman{} percent for the human briefs; \pinNearPct{} percent lie on an adjacent page and the rest elsewhere in the cited case.


\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Where the checker belongs.} The revision experiment favors the checker at the gate, returning failures to a person, over a loop in which the model resolves bare flags on its own, and says what a loop should carry if built, the passage; removing an unverifiable quotation is a defensible response to a flag, but the quotation is deleted or survives as paraphrase the checker cannot test, false flags get the same response (Figure~\ref{fig:repair}), and a loop-pattern tool reports a clean draft.

\textbf{Verifier precision is a deployment requirement.} Our checker's verifiable error among inaccurate verdicts is \falseAlarmPctSonnet{} percent of Sonnet~5's by provenance and \auditSampleCheckerSidePct{} percent across models by the hand reading (Section~\ref{sec:residual}), and published detectors are less precise~\cite{liu2026whochecks}. Figure~\ref{fig:repair} shows what false flags do: at precision one half Sonnet~5 lost \trHalfAccRemovedSonnet{} of its \trAccRZeroSonnet{} correct quotations, against \trTrueAccRemovedSonnet{} under a precise verifier and \trNoneAccRemovedSonnet{} with no feedback at all. A pipeline that feeds a probabilistic detector's flags to a model should report the detector's precision beside its recall, and a deployer should enforce the strict standard as defined here, which admits bracketed alterations, with the 0.85-coverage rate as a diagnostic.

\textbf{What evaluations should include.} A single fabrication rate at a neutral prompt misses existence under constraints~\cite{zhao2026deployment} and on other courts, quotation accuracy, and the residual classes at the top of the range; the three checks also run on any filing a court receives, covering the failures of the sanctions cases~\cite{mata2023,charlotin2025tracker}.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

Both tasks are United States federal case law, one under two prompt templates and one under one; the stakes clause names a sanctions rule that does not govern the forum; and the checks stop at existence, wording, and page; three model panels for relevance, calibrated on expert labels~\cite{liu2026whochecks}, fell short of a \juryBar{} bar (\juryBinary{} to \juryThreeway), so none was used~\cite{norman2026reliability,zheng2023judging}. The controls, the grounded arm and loop, the second task, and the per-quotation trace were added after the pre-registered analysis, their tests chosen after the data were seen, and are reported separately from the pre-registered fit; the attribution rule was corrected after that analysis too (Section~\ref{sec:residual}), and every contrast was recomputed under it. Three generations of the baseline and combined cells (\repDrafts{} drafts per run) bound run-to-run variation~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable}: cell standard deviations were at most \repMaxExistSd{} (existence) and \repMaxStrictSd{} (strict), and the combined-versus-baseline quotation contrast kept its sign in every run for all \repSignStable{} models; we rely on the contrasts that survive correction across runs and templates, since the between-template spread of cells (\phrMaxStrictDiff{} points of quotation accuracy) exceeds many single-run condition effects. The seven models are one per vendor, confounding capability, family, and recipe.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Under a quota, a date restriction, and a sanctions warning, the failure mode of citation moves with capability: the 30B model loses existence, the middle of the range loses verbatim quotation, and the strongest model's rate holds because it quotes less, its residue paraphrase in quotation marks, misattribution, and wrong pages. Returned to the model, the checker's findings raise existence chiefly by removal, flagged quotations are removed far more often than corrected even with the passage in the flag, and false flags remove correct ones; verification belongs at the gate, and verifier precision in the evaluation of any system that acts on it. Instruments, drafts, records, and ledger are released at \url{https://github.com/hardy30894/citation-under-pressure}.
