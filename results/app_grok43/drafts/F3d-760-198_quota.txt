I. The District Court Erred by Failing to Consider the Totality of Circumstances Supporting Moll’s Hostile Work Environment Claim.

Title VII prohibits discrimination because of sex that creates a hostile work environment. Meritor Sav. Bank, FSB v. Vinson, 477 U.S. 57, 66 (1986). Whether conduct is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of employment is determined by looking at all the circumstances. Harris v. Forklift Sys., Inc., 510 U.S. 17, 23 (1993). The inquiry includes both sexually offensive and facially sex-neutral incidents when the latter may be linked to gender-based animus. Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc., 523 U.S. 75, 80 (1998); Alfano v. Costello, 294 F.3d 365, 375 (2d Cir. 2002).

The Second Circuit has repeatedly held that sex-neutral conduct must be evaluated in context with other evidence of hostility. Howley v. Town of Stratford, 217 F.3d 141, 148 (2d Cir. 2000); Cruz v. Coach Stores, Inc., 202 F.3d 560, 570 (2d Cir. 2000); Leibovitz v. N.Y.C. Transit Auth., 252 F.3d 179, 189-90 (2d Cir. 2001). The district court’s refusal to consider Moll’s allegations of disparate treatment, exclusion from professional events, denial of promotion opportunities, and work restrictions alongside the earlier sexually offensive notes therefore misapplied controlling precedent.

A hostile work environment claim may rest on a continuing violation when at least one act contributing to the claim occurs within the filing period. Nat’l R.R. Passenger Corp. v. Morgan, 536 U.S. 101, 117 (2002). The district court’s segmentation of the record into isolated “sexually offensive” acts ignored this doctrine and the requirement that all evidence be viewed in the light most favorable to the non-moving party. Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 255 (1986); Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 323 (1986). Because the record contains evidence from which a reasonable jury could find a sex-based hostile environment under the totality standard, summary judgment was improper.
