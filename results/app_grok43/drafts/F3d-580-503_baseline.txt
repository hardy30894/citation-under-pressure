The district court abused its discretion by denying Winbush’s motion for a fingerprint expert and accompanying continuance. See United States v. Carter, 410 F.3d 942, 949 (7th Cir. 2005) (reviewing denial of expert assistance for abuse of discretion). A defendant must be afforded “a fair opportunity to present his defense” when expert testimony is necessary to rebut the government’s evidence on a central factual issue. Ake v. Oklahoma, 470 U.S. 68, 76 (1985).

The government’s October 3 notice disclosed that its own fingerprint examiner would report no latent prints of value and that Deputy Commander Reilly would explain the difficulties of recovering prints from various surfaces. Winbush’s November 8 motion sought a defense expert solely to address the interpretive significance of that absence—the precise subject on which the government planned affirmative expert testimony. The magistrate judge’s sole ground for denial was untimeliness, yet the record shows the government’s expert disclosures occurred only five weeks before trial and the parties’ stipulation was reached only eleven days before trial. Under these circumstances, the denial left Winbush without any means to challenge the government’s fingerprint narrative. See United States v. Fazzini, 871 F.2d 635, 641 (7th Cir. 1989) (error to deny defense expert when government evidence on same subject is presented).

The simultaneous denial of a continuance compounded the prejudice. See United States v. Robbins, 197 F.3d 829, 846 (7th Cir. 1999) (listing factors governing continuance requests, including diligence, surprise, and prejudice). Winbush had no prior indication that the government would rely on fingerprint testimony until the October notice; once that notice arrived, he acted within weeks. The five-day continuance sought would not have disrupted the court’s calendar or the government’s preparation, yet its refusal left the defense unable to meet the government’s expert proof on an issue the jury was invited to consider.

Because the fingerprint evidence was offered to link Winbush to the recovered narcotics and firearm, the erroneous denial was not harmless. The judgment of conviction must therefore be vacated.
