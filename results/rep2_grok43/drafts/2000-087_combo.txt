The post-removal-period statute confers no authority for indefinite detention. Detention beyond the removal period must be limited to what is reasonably necessary to effect removal, for the statute cannot be read to permit prolonged restraint once removal becomes impossible. This construction follows from the principle that ambiguous grants of detention power receive the narrower reading that avoids serious constitutional doubt. See United States v. Witkovich, 353 U.S. 194, 199 (1957); Carlson v. Landon, 342 U.S. 524, 543 (1952).

The Fifth Amendment protects resident aliens from arbitrary restraint. Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356, 369 (1886). Detention that continues after removal has become infeasible ceases to serve any legitimate immigration purpose and therefore constitutes punishment without trial. Wong Wing v. United States, 163 U.S. 228, 237 (1896). The same limitation appears in the immigration statutes themselves, which have long been construed to require periodic judicial inquiry into the necessity of continued custody. Ng Fung Ho v. White, 259 U.S. 276, 284 (1922); Yamataya v. Fisher, 189 U.S. 86, 100 (1903).

Where removal is no longer reasonably foreseeable, the government may not rely on the mere possibility of future diplomatic success to justify ongoing imprisonment. Fong Yue Ting v. United States, 149 U.S. 698, 713 (1893). Supervision under release conditions remains available and suffices to protect the government's interest in eventual removal. Bridges v. Wixon, 326 U.S. 135, 154 (1945). Indefinite detention, by contrast, exceeds the bounds of any power the statute can be understood to grant. Kwong Hai Chew v. Colding, 344 U.S. 590, 602 (1953).

The writ of habeas corpus supplies the mechanism for testing whether detention has exceeded its lawful duration. Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei, 345 U.S. 206, 213 (1953). Once an alien demonstrates that removal is not reasonably foreseeable, continued custody is no longer authorized. The statute therefore does not permit indefinite post-removal detention.
