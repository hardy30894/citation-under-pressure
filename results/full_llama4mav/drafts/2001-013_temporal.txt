ARGUMENT

I. The Occupational Safety and Health Administration Has Jurisdiction to Issue Citations to Uninspected Vessels

The Occupational Safety and Health Act (Act) of 1970 grants the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) jurisdiction to regulate workplace safety and health. Section 4(a) of the Act applies to "employment performed in a workplace in a State." 29 U.S.C. § 653(a). The Act defines a workplace as any location where employees perform work. The MR. BELDON, a drilling barge, is a workplace within the meaning of the Act.

The Court of Appeals erred in holding that the Coast Guard's exclusive jurisdiction over the regulation of seamen's working conditions aboard vessels precluded OSHA's regulation under section 4(b)(1) of the Act. Section 4(b)(1) provides that the Act does not apply to "working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health." 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1).

However, the Coast Guard's authority to regulate vessels is not exclusive with respect to uninspected vessels. While the Coast Guard has authority to issue safety regulations for uninspected vessels under 46 U.S.C. § 4102, this authority is limited to specific areas such as fire extinguishers, life preservers, and ventilation of engine and fuel tank compartments.

The Court of Appeals' reliance on Clary v. Ocean Drilling and Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), is misplaced. These cases were decided after the enactment of the Occupational Safety and Health Act and therefore do not reflect the pre-1970 understanding of the relationship between the Coast Guard's authority and OSHA's jurisdiction.

Pre-1970 case law suggests that the Coast Guard's authority to regulate vessels is not necessarily exclusive. In United States v. Haskins, 206 F. Supp. 589 (D. Conn. 1962), the court held that the Coast Guard's authority to regulate vessels under 46 U.S.C. § 375 (now repealed) did not preclude the application of other federal statutes to vessels. Similarly, in United States v. Schiffer, 351 F.2d 91 (6th Cir. 1965), the court held that the Coast Guard's authority to regulate vessels did not exempt them from compliance with other federal regulations.

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has long recognized that the Coast Guard's authority is not unlimited. In The Lottawanna, 88 U.S. 558 (1874), the Court held that the Coast Guard's authority to regulate vessels is subject to the limits imposed by Congress. The Court stated that "the power to regulate commerce, so far as it extends to the subject of ... the safety of those employed in navigating the vessels engaged in it, is vested in Congress." Id. at 577.

In light of these pre-1970 authorities, it is clear that the Coast Guard's authority to regulate uninspected vessels is not exclusive, and OSHA has jurisdiction to issue citations to such vessels.

II. The MR. BELDON is a Workplace Within the Meaning of Section 4(a) of the Act

As discussed above, the MR. BELDON is a workplace within the meaning of section 4(a) of the Act. The Act applies to employment performed in a workplace in a State, and the MR. BELDON meets this definition.

The Court of Appeals did not address this issue, but the Administrative Law Judge correctly found that the MR. BELDON was a workplace within the meaning of the Act.

CONCLUSION

For the reasons stated above, OSHA has jurisdiction to issue citations to uninspected vessels, and the MR. BELDON is a workplace within the meaning of section 4(a) of the Act. The Court of Appeals' decision should be reversed, and the citation issued by OSHA should be upheld.
