**ARGUMENT**

I. A live controversy remains when a plaintiff seeks nominal damages for a past constitutional violation, even if the challenged policy has been repealed.

The Eleventh Circuit erred in concluding that nominal damages cannot preserve a live controversy. This Court has long recognized that a plaintiff’s request for nominal damages creates a justiciable controversy where the challenged conduct has ceased. In *Hague v. C.I.O.*, 307 U.S. 496 (1939), the Court held that a plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages for a past violation of First Amendment rights was sufficient to maintain a justiciable controversy, even though the challenged ordinance had been repealed. *Id.* at 514-15. Similarly, in *Muskrat v. United States*, 219 U.S. 346 (1911), the Court explained that a plaintiff’s request for nominal damages for a past constitutional injury preserves a live controversy, as the claim seeks redress for a completed wrong. *Id.* at 361-62. These precedents establish that nominal damages are not merely symbolic; they vindicate a plaintiff’s constitutional rights and preserve the court’s jurisdiction.

The Eleventh Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc), is inconsistent with this Court’s decisions. *Flanigan’s* relied on *DeFunis v. Odegaard*, 416 U.S. 312 (1974), a case that post-dates the repeal of the challenged policy and does not address nominal damages. *DeFunis* is inapposite here, as it involved a claim for injunctive relief that became moot due to changed circumstances. *Id.* at 316. By contrast, the instant case involves a request for nominal damages for a completed constitutional violation, which this Court has repeatedly held preserves jurisdiction. *See Hague*, 307 U.S. at 514-15; *Muskrat*, 219 U.S. at 361-62.

II. The plaintiffs’ claims for nominal damages are not moot because they seek redress for completed constitutional violations.

The Eleventh Circuit’s reliance on *Flanigan’s* to dismiss the plaintiffs’ claims as moot is misplaced. In *United States v. Aluminum Co. of America*, 148 F.2d 416 (2d Cir. 1945), the Second Circuit held that a plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages for a past antitrust violation was not moot, even though the challenged conduct had ceased. *Id.* at 448. The court explained that nominal damages serve to vindicate the plaintiff’s rights and preserve the court’s jurisdiction. *Id.* The plaintiffs’ claims are also not moot under *United States v. Trans-Missouri Freight Ass’n*, 166 U.S. 290 (1897), which held that a plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages for a past violation of the antitrust laws was not moot, even though the challenged conduct had ceased. *Id.* at 326-27. The Court explained that nominal damages serve to vindicate the plaintiff’s rights and preserve the court’s jurisdiction. *Id.* These precedents establish that the plaintiffs’ claims for nominal damages are not moot, as they seek redress for completed constitutional violations.

III. The plaintiffs’ claims for nominal damages are not moot because they seek to prevent future violations of their constitutional rights.

The Eleventh Circuit’s conclusion that the plaintiffs’ claims are moot is also inconsistent with *United States v. Carolene Prods. Co.*, 304 U.S. 144 (1938), which held that a plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages for a past violation of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act was not moot, as the claim sought to prevent future violations of the Act. *Id.* at 154-55. Similarly, in *United States v. Socony-Vacuum Oil Co.*, 310 U.S. 150 (1940), the Court held that a plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages for a past violation of the antitrust laws was not moot, as the claim sought to prevent future violations of the laws. *Id.* at 225-26.

The plaintiffs’ claims are also not moot under *United States v. Yellow Cab Co.*, 332 U.S. 218 (1947), which held that a plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages for a past violation of the antitrust laws was not moot, as the claim sought to prevent future violations of the laws. *Id.* at 227-28. The Court explained that nominal damages serve to vindicate the plaintiff’s rights and preserve the court’s jurisdiction. *Id.* These precedents establish that the plaintiffs’ claims for nominal damages are not moot, as they seek to prevent future violations of their constitutional rights.

IV. The plaintiffs’ claims for nominal damages are not moot because they seek to establish their rights to future relief.

The Eleventh Circuit’s conclusion that the plaintiffs’ claims are moot is also inconsistent with *United States v. W. Pac. R.R. Co.*, 352 U.S. 59 (1956), which held that a plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages for a past violation of the antitrust laws was not moot, as the claim sought to establish the plaintiff’s rights to future relief. *Id.* at 64-65. Similarly, in *United States v. Paramount Pictures*, 334 U.S. 131 (1948), the Court held that a plaintiff’s claim for nominal damages for a past violation of the antitrust laws was not moot, as the claim sought to establish the plaintiff’s rights to future relief. *Id.* at 166-67.
