ARGUMENT

Section 1366(d)(1) limits a shareholder’s loss deduction to the sum of his basis in S corporation stock and “the adjusted basis of any indebtedness of the S corporation to the shareholder.” 26 U.S.C. § 1366(d)(1). The regulation requires that any such indebtedness be “bona fide” and “run[] directly” to the shareholder. Treas. Reg. § 1.1366-2(a)(2)(i). Subchapter S attributes corporate losses to shareholders “in a manner akin to the tax treatment of partnerships.” Bufferd v. Comm’r, 506 U.S. 523, 525 (1993).

The Tax Court correctly recognized that Meruelo’s $4,985,035 personal loan proceeds transferred to Merco created qualifying basis. The same principles establish basis for the remaining indebtedness. Each year Carreras netted Merco’s accounts payable to its affiliates against its receivables, recorded the net as a “shareholder loan,” and charged that amount to the $10 million unsecured line of credit Meruelo had extended to Merco. The resulting promissory note created a direct debtor-creditor relationship between Merco and Meruelo. Because Meruelo controlled the affiliates that advanced the funds and bore the economic risk through his personal borrowing and ownership interests, the advances constituted an “actual economic outlay” by him. The reclassification therefore satisfied both the statutory and regulatory requirements for basis.

The Commissioner’s contrary position—that the debt ran only to the affiliates—ignores the annual netting, the promissory note, and Meruelo’s personal guarantee of repayment. Those steps converted the intercompany balances into direct indebtedness of Merco to Meruelo. Accordingly, the Tax Court’s disallowance of $9 million in basis should be reversed.
