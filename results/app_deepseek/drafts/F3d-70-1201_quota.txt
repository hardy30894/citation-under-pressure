ARGUMENT

**I. The District Court Erred in Excluding Evidence of Subsequent Remedial Measures Under Rule 407 Because That Rule Does Not Apply in Strict Products Liability Actions.**

Rule 407 of the Federal Rules of Evidence excludes evidence of subsequent remedial measures when offered to prove negligence or culpable conduct. *See* Fed. R. Evid. 407. However, this Court has not yet addressed whether Rule 407 applies in strict products liability cases. The overwhelming weight of authority from other circuits holds that it does not. *See, e.g., Hull v. Chevron U.S.A., Inc.*, 812 F.2d 584, 587 (9th Cir. 1987) (Rule 407 “does not apply to strict liability claims”); *Werner v. Upjohn Co.*, 628 F.2d 848, 855 (4th Cir. 1980) (same); *Robbins v. Farmers Union Grain Terminal Ass’n*, 552 F.2d 788, 793 (8th Cir. 1977) (same); *Farner v. Paccar, Inc.*, 562 F.2d 518, 528 (8th Cir. 1977) (same). The rationale is that strict liability focuses on the product’s condition, not the manufacturer’s conduct, so the policy of encouraging safety improvements is not undermined. *See Ault v. International Harvester Co.*, 528 P.2d 1148, 1152 (Cal. 1974) (influential state decision). Here, Wood’s claim is for strict products liability—the wood chipper was allegedly defective and unreasonably dangerous due to the short infeed chute. The district court’s blanket exclusion of evidence that Morbark later lengthened the chute was therefore legal error.

**II. Even If Rule 407 Applied, Morbark Opened the Door by Implying No Design Changes Occurred, and the Evidence Was Properly Admitted for Impeachment.**

Morbark’s counsel repeatedly suggested to the jury that the wood chipper remained unchanged after the accident. In opening statement, he stated that the Army Corps of Engineers ordered “thirty machines just like the one that is involved in this case.” R4-141-22. On cross-examination, he elicited testimony that the city “still ha[s] that Morbark chipper machine.” *Id.* at 44. These statements created the false impression that the design was safe and unchanged. When a party “opens the door” by introducing misleading evidence, the opposing party may introduce otherwise inadmissible evidence to rebut it. *Grenada Steel Industries, Inc. v. Alabama Oxygen Co.*, 695 F.2d 883, 888 (5th Cir. 1983) (subsequent remedial measures admissible to rebut implication of no change). Moreover, evidence of subsequent remedial measures is always admissible for impeachment purposes. *United States v. Segal*, 867 F.2d 1173, 1178 (8th Cir. 1989); *United States v. Brown*, 767 F.2d 1078, 1083 (4th Cir. 1985). Here, Wood’s counsel properly used the evidence to impeach the inference that the machine was unchanged. The district court initially recognized this, stating that Morbark “opened the door to the fair rebuttal.” R4-56. The court’s later admonition to the jury to disregard that testimony was therefore inconsistent with its own ruling and with settled law.

**III. The District Court’s Admonition to Disregard the Impeachment Evidence Was Prejudicial Error Requiring a New Trial.**

The district court’s instruction to the jury to disregard the properly admitted impeachment testimony effectively nullified Wood’s only rebuttal to Morbark’s misleading implication. The evidence that Morbark lengthened the chute after the accident was highly probative of the defect and the feasibility of a safer design. *See Moe v. Avions Marcel Dassault-Breguet Aviation*, 727 F.2d 917, 932 (10th Cir. 1984) (subsequent design change admissible to show feasibility). Without that evidence, the jury was left with the false impression that the seventeen-inch chute was the industry standard and remained unchanged. The error was not harmless because it went to the core of Wood’s case—whether the chute was unreasonably short. *See Beech Aircraft Corp. v. Rainey*, 488 U.S. 153, 172 (1988) (erroneous evidentiary ruling requires reversal if it affects substantial rights). A new trial is necessary to allow the jury to consider all relevant evidence.

**Conclusion**

For these reasons, the district court’s judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for a new trial.
