The Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (OCSLA) does not extend Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act (LHWCA) coverage to workers injured on land, regardless of their connection to extractive operations on the outer Continental Shelf. The plain language of the OCSLA requires that the injury occur "as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf." 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The Fifth Circuit has correctly interpreted this provision to require a "situs-of-injury" test, which limits coverage to injuries that occur on the outer Continental Shelf or on the waters above it. Mills v. Dir., Office of Workers' Comp. Programs, 877 F.2d 356, 362 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc); see also Becker v. Tidewater, Inc., 586 F.3d 358, 366-67 (5th Cir. 2009); Pickett v. Petroleum Helicopters, Inc., 266 F.3d 366, 368 (5th Cir. 2001).

Although the Supreme Court has not directly addressed this issue, its dictum in Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207 (1986), suggests that the OCSLA's coverage is determined by "locale, not by the status of the individual injured or killed." Id. at 219. The Court also noted that § 1333(b) superimposes a status requirement on the OCSLA's situs requirement, implying that both the location of the injury and the employee's status are relevant to determining coverage. Id. at 219 n.2.

The Ninth Circuit's decision in this case, which adopted a "substantial nexus" test, is inconsistent with the plain language of the OCSLA and the Supreme Court's dictum in Tallentire. The "substantial nexus" test would extend LHWCA coverage to workers injured on land, as long as their injury is connected to extractive operations on the outer Continental Shelf. However, this interpretation ignores the statutory requirement that the injury occur "as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf." 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b).

Furthermore, the LHWCA's own provisions support a situs-of-injury requirement. The LHWCA requires that an injury occur on "navigable waters" or an "adjoining area" to be covered. 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). The OCSLA's extension of LHWCA coverage to the outer Continental Shelf should be interpreted consistently with these provisions.

In this case, Juan Valladolid's injury occurred on land, at Pacific Operations' onshore oil-processing facility, and not on the outer Continental Shelf. Therefore, his injury is not covered under the OCSLA, regardless of its connection to extractive operations on the outer Continental Shelf. The Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision and uphold the Benefits Review Board's denial of OCSLA benefits.

The Court should also affirm the denial of LHWCA benefits, as the Ninth Circuit correctly determined that La Conchita, the onshore facility where Valladolid was injured, is not a maritime situs. The facility is not an "adjoining area customarily used by an employer in loading, unloading, repairing, or building a vessel." 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). The facility's primary purpose is to process crude oil slurry, a non-maritime activity, and its use as a dumping ground for scrap metal from the offshore platforms does not convert it into a maritime situs. Herb's Welding, Inc. v. Gray, 470 U.S. 414, 422-24 (1985).

In conclusion, the OCSLA's extension of LHWCA coverage is limited to injuries that occur on the outer Continental Shelf or on the waters above it. The Ninth Circuit's "substantial nexus" test is inconsistent with the plain language of the OCSLA and relevant case law. The Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision and uphold the denial of OCSLA benefits.
