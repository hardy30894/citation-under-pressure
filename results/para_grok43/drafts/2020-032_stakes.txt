ARGUMENT

I. THE ELEVENTH CIRCUIT CORRECTLY HELD THAT THE REPEAL OF THE CHALLENGED POLICIES RENDERED THE CASE MOOT NOTWITHSTANDING THE REQUEST FOR NOMINAL DAMAGES.

A case becomes moot when the issues presented are no longer live or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome. Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc., 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013). When a defendant voluntarily ceases the challenged conduct, the case is moot if “subsequent events made it absolutely clear that the allegedly wrongful behavior could not reasonably be expected to recur.” Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Envtl. Servs. (TOC), Inc., 528 U.S. 167, 189 (2000) (quoting United States v. Concentrated Phosphate Export Ass’n, 393 U.S. 199, 203 (1968)). Georgia Gwinnett College’s revised policies eliminated the speech zones and permit requirements and removed the challenged code-of-conduct provision, satisfying that standard.

Nominal damages do not preserve a live controversy once equitable relief is unavailable. In Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs, 868 F.3d 1248, 1263 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc), the court held that “a prayer for nominal damages” alone “will not save an otherwise moot case.” That conclusion rests on the principle that nominal damages redress only the “abstract value of [a] constitutional right,” not an actual injury. Memphis Community School Dist. v. Stachura, 477 U.S. 299, 308 n.11 (1986); see also Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247, 266 (1978) (nominal damages available “without proof of actual injury”). Because the complaint alleged no concrete harm beyond the abstract violation itself, the request for nominal damages supplied no continuing stake in the litigation.

The distinction between facial and as-applied challenges does not alter the analysis. Once the policies are repealed, neither form of relief can affect the parties’ rights. See Lewis v. Continental Bank Corp., 494 U.S. 472, 477 (1990). The district court therefore properly dismissed the action as moot, and the Eleventh Circuit’s affirmance should be upheld.
