I. Furlow’s prior South Carolina crack-distribution conviction is not a categorical predicate under the ACCA or the career-offender guideline.

The ACCA requires a prior “serious drug offense,” meaning a state offense “involving manufacturing, distributing, or possessing with intent to manufacture or distribute, a controlled substance,” punishable by at least ten years. 18 U.S.C. § 924(e)(2)(A)(ii). The career-offender guideline requires a prior “controlled substance offense,” meaning an offense that prohibits manufacture, import, export, distribution, or possession with intent to distribute a controlled substance, punishable by more than one year. U.S.S.G. § 4B1.2(b). In both settings, courts use the categorical approach and ask whether the statute of conviction necessarily matches the federal definition. Taylor v. United States, 495 U.S. 575, 600-02 (1990); Descamps v. United States, 570 U.S. 254, 257-58 (2013); Mathis v. United States, 579 U.S. 500, 504-05 (2016).

South Carolina’s crack-distribution statute is broader than the federal definitions because South Carolina defines “distribute” to include a mere “delivery,” and “delivery” reaches a completed transfer without the federal limitations that cabin the ACCA and guideline predicates. See S.C. Code Ann. § 44-53-110(14), (17). This Court has held that South Carolina drug-distribution offenses are divisible as to the specific controlled substance, but the State’s controlled-substance schedules can sweep more broadly than federal law in material respects; where the state offense covers conduct outside the federal definition, it cannot serve as a categorical predicate absent a qualifying match in the Shepard documents. See United States v. Ward, 972 F.3d 364, 369-72 (4th Cir. 2020); Shepard v. United States, 544 U.S. 13, 16-17 (2005). Because the district court treated the conviction as automatically qualifying, it failed to apply the categorical inquiry required by Taylor, Descamps, and Mathis.

II. Furlow’s Georgia first-degree arson convictions are not predicates under the ACCA or the career-offender guideline.

Both the ACCA and the guideline enumerate “arson.” 18 U.S.C. § 924(e)(2)(B)(ii); U.S.S.G. § 4B1.2(a)(2). But “arson” is a generic offense, and a state conviction qualifies only if its elements are no broader than the generic definition. Taylor, 495 U.S. at 598-99; Mathis, 579 U.S. at 504-05. This Court has recognized that the relevant inquiry is whether the state statute’s elements “substantially correspond” to the generic offense. United States v. Knight, 606 F.3d 171, 174-75 (4th Cir. 2010).

Georgia’s first-degree arson statute criminalizes burning or causing damage by fire to a dwelling or certain other property, but it is not limited to the generic arson elements recognized at common law and in federal law. Compare Ga. Code Ann. § 16-7-60(a) with generic arson as described in United States v. Misleveck, 735 F.3d 983, 989-90 (7th Cir. 2013). Where a state statute sweeps more broadly than the generic offense, it cannot categorically qualify. Descamps, 570 U.S. at 261-62. The district court therefore erred in counting both Georgia arson convictions as ACCA violent felonies and guideline crimes of violence without first establishing that the statute of conviction matches generic arson.

III. Because the challenged convictions were necessary to both enhancements, the sentence must be vacated.

A defendant is an armed career criminal only if he has three qualifying prior convictions. 18 U.S.C. § 924(e)(1). He is a career offender only if he has two qualifying prior convictions and the instant offense is a qualifying offense. U.S.S.G. § 4B1.1(a). If either the South Carolina drug conviction or the Georgia arson convictions fails as a predicate, the district court’s enhancement determinations cannot stand. See United States v. Simmons, 649 F.3d 237, 246-47 (4th Cir. 2011) (en banc); United States v. Campbell, 22 F.4th 438, 441-45 (4th Cir. 2022).

This Court should vacate the sentence and remand for resentencing without the ACCA and career-offender enhancements.
