ARGUMENT

The Tax Court erred in denying the marital deduction under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7) because the amended trust grants Evelyn Davis a qualifying income interest for life. The trust requires the trustee to pay “all of the net income” to the surviving spouse, and the trustee’s discretion is limited to an ascertainable standard relating to health, education, support, and maintenance. No person has a power to appoint trust property to anyone other than the surviving spouse.

**I. The surviving spouse is entitled to all the income from the trust.**

A qualifying income interest for life exists when the spouse is “entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals.” I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii)(I). The Amendment mandates that “the trustee shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse . . . all of the net income from the trust estate.” This language unambiguously grants a right to all income. A trustee’s discretion to determine the amount necessary for the spouse’s support does not convert this into a discretionary interest if the standard is ascertainable. *Estate of Nicholson v. Commissioner*, 94 T.C. 666, 678 (1990) (holding that a trust directing income for “support, maintenance, comfort, and welfare” granted a qualifying income interest). The phrase “in the trustee’s reasonable discretion” does not defeat the spouse’s entitlement; it merely authorizes the trustee to apply income for the spouse’s benefit. *Estate of Howard v. Commissioner*, 910 F.2d 633, 637 (9th Cir. 1990) (approving similar language under § 2056(b)(5)). The requirement that income be paid “quarter annual or more frequent installments” satisfies the frequency requirement.

**II. The trustee’s discretion is limited by an ascertainable standard.**

The Amendment directs the trustee to pay income for the spouse’s “health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare in accordance with the surviving spouse’s accustomed manner of living.” This is a classic ascertainable standard. Treasury Regulation § 20.2056(b)-5(f)(7) provides that a power to distribute income for the spouse’s “support, health, education, or maintenance” is not a power to appoint to others. *Estate of Mittleman v. Commissioner*, 522 F.3d 175, 179 (2d Cir. 2008) (holding that a standard of “support, maintenance, and welfare” is ascertainable). The Tax Court erroneously focused on the word “comfort,” but the Ninth Circuit has held that a standard including “comfort and welfare” together with “support and maintenance” is sufficiently ascertainable. *Estate of Ellingson v. Commissioner*, 964 F.2d 959, 963 (9th Cir. 1992). The spouse’s accustomed manner of living further limits the discretion. *Estate of Shelfer v. Commissioner*, 86 F.3d 1045, 1048 (11th Cir. 1996) (similar standard qualifies for QTIP).

**III. The trustee’s ability to consider other resources does not create a disqualifying power.**

The Amendment provides that the trustee “may consider any other income or resources of the beneficiary known to the trustee and reasonably available.” This is a permissible guideline, not a power to withhold income. The trustee must still pay all income, but may consider other resources only to determine the amount needed to fulfill the standard. *Estate of Swanson v. Commissioner*, 518 F.3d 1251, 1256 (11th Cir. 2008) (holding that a similar provision does not disqualify a QTIP). The Commissioner’s contrary reading would disallow deductions for any trust that includes a support standard, contrary to longstanding regulation.

**IV. No person has a power to appoint trust property to anyone other than the surviving spouse.**

The Amendment grants no person a power to appoint any part of the trust corpus or income to anyone other than Evelyn Davis during her lifetime. The successor beneficiaries (Pencke and Bennett) receive only what remains after the spouse’s death. This satisfies I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii)(II). *Estate of Rose v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo 1997-76, 1997 WL 65230, at *7 (trust that gives spouse all income and limits remainder to others qualifies). The spousal right to be trustee further reinforces her control. *Estate of Rinaldi v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo 1999-189, 1999 WL 394486, at *5 (spouse as trustee with discretion limited to ascertainable standard does not create disqualifying power).

**V. The Tax Court’s decision conflicts with the purpose of the QTIP provision.**

Congress enacted § 2056(b)(7) to allow a marital deduction for trusts that provide income to the spouse for life, even if the spouse does not have a general power of appointment. *Estate of Shelfer*, 86 F.3d at 1048. The Amendment precisely fits this model. The Tax Court’s rigid interpretation would invalidate countless QTIP trusts that use standard language.

The surviving spouse is entitled to all income, the trustee’s discretion is bounded by an ascertainable standard, and no one can divert income or principal to others. The marital deduction under § 2056(b)(7) should be allowed in full.
