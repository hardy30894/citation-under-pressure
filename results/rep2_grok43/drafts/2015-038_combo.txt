ARGUMENT

The Property Clause empowers Congress to make all needful rules respecting federal territory. U.S. Const. art. IV, § 3, cl. 2; Camfield v. United States, 167 U.S. 518, 525 (1897). That power extends to the regulation of activities affecting federal lands, even when those activities occur on adjacent nonfederal property. Id. at 525-526. Regulations promulgated under this authority “have the force of law” and bind all persons within the area subject to federal jurisdiction. United States v. Grimaud, 220 U.S. 506, 521 (1911); Light v. United States, 220 U.S. 523, 537 (1911).

Title to submerged lands beneath navigable waters passed to the State upon admission, Pollard v. Hagan, 44 U.S. (3 How.) 212, 230 (1845), yet the United States retains its dominant navigational servitude and authority to protect its own holdings. The Daniel Ball, 77 U.S. (10 Wall.) 557, 563 (1870); Martin v. Waddell, 41 U.S. (16 Pet.) 367, 410 (1842). Federal power to regulate commerce on navigable waters is plenary and may be exercised within state boundaries. Gibbons v. Ogden, 22 U.S. (9 Wheat.) 1, 197 (1824). Consequently, a state’s ownership of the riverbed does not divest the United States of its ability to forbid uses that threaten the federal preserve. Irvine v. Marshall, 61 U.S. (20 How.) 558, 564 (1858).

When Congress creates a national preserve, it may impose uniform rules throughout the unit to fulfill the statutory purpose. Utah Power & Light Co. v. United States, 243 U.S. 389, 404 (1917). Such rules apply to all lands within the exterior boundaries, because the power to protect federal property does not depend on fee ownership of every acre. Gibson v. Chouteau, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 92, 99 (1872). Private or state inholdings therefore remain subject to reasonable federal regulation necessary to preserve the larger tract. Camfield, 167 U.S. at 525.

The hovercraft prohibition is a valid exercise of that authority. It applies uniformly to all waters within the preserve boundaries and is designed to protect the federal lands and waters from disturbance. Grimaud, 220 U.S. at 521. Because the regulation governs conduct affecting federal property rather than purporting to control solely state land, it remains enforceable notwithstanding state title to the submerged soil. Light, 220 U.S. at 537; Utah Power & Light, 243 U.S. at 404.

No pre-existing state right to operate hovercrafts overrides these federal rules. When the United States reserves land for a public purpose, subsequent state grants or titles are taken subject to that reservation. Pollard, 44 U.S. at 230; Martin, 41 U.S. at 410. The State therefore acquired its interest subject to the federal power to regulate activities within the preserve. Gibbons, 22 U.S. at 197.

Accordingly, the judgment should be affirmed.
