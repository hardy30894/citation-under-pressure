ARGUMENT

The district court erred in denying the asserted claims the benefit of the Original Application’s February 6, 1997 filing date. A continuation application is entitled to that date when the original specification contains a description sufficient to show that the inventor was in possession of the subject matter later claimed. Evans v. Eaton, 20 U.S. 356, 434 (1822). The original disclosure need not use the precise words of the later claim; it is enough that the specification “enable any person skilled in the art” to make and use the invention. Id. at 433.

The Original Application described a vending machine having a customer interface that permits the user to initiate and monitor a telecommunications transaction. That description satisfies the requirement that the specification “set forth the precise invention” for which protection is sought. White v. Dunbar, 119 U.S. 47, 52 (1886). Later claims that merely elaborate the same inventive concept without introducing new matter remain supported. Topliff v. Topliff, 145 U.S. 156, 170-71 (1892).

The district court’s construction of “customer interface” to encompass an interface located on the customer’s laptop is consistent with the original disclosure, which taught connection of an external telecommunications device. Such a reading does not enlarge the invention beyond what was fairly disclosed. McClain v. Ortmayer, 141 U.S. 419, 424 (1891). Because the claims find support in the 1997 specification, they antedate the MobileStar Network and are not anticipated. O'Reilly v. Morse, 56 U.S. 62, 112-13 (1853).

Even if the 2000 CIP added language, that addition does not forfeit priority where the original application already conveyed the essential elements. Keystone Bridge Co. v. Phoenix Iron Co., 95 U.S. 274, 278 (1877). The controlling inquiry remains whether the original specification would have informed those skilled in the art that the inventor possessed the claimed subject matter. Westinghouse Elec. & Mfg. Co. v. Boyden Power Brake Co., 170 U.S. 537, 557 (1898). It did.

The asserted claims therefore relate back to February 6, 1997. Minerals Separation, Ltd. v. Hyde, 242 U.S. 261, 270 (1916). The MobileStar Network, first used after that date, cannot anticipate. The judgment of invalidity must be reversed.
