The U.S. Bankruptcy Code does not exempt an inherited IRA from a debtor's estate in bankruptcy. The plain language of 11 U.S.C. §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) exempts "retirement funds" from creditors' claims, but an inherited IRA is not "retirement funds" in the hands of the beneficiary. As the Seventh Circuit correctly held, an inherited IRA is a "time-limited tax-deferral vehicle, but not a place to hold wealth for use after the new owner's retirement." Clark v. Rameker, 673 F.3d 706, 711 (7th Cir. 2013).

The statutory text and structure support this conclusion. Section 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) exempt funds that are "in a fund or account that is exempt from taxation under section...408" of the Internal Revenue Code. While an inherited IRA is tax-deferred, it is not a retirement fund for the beneficiary. The tax code requires the beneficiary to withdraw a minimum amount from the account each year, and the funds are not reserved for the beneficiary's retirement. See 26 U.S.C. §402(c)(11)(A), incorporating 26 U.S.C. §401(a)(9)(B).

The Seventh Circuit's decision is consistent with the purpose of the Bankruptcy Code's exemption provisions. Exempting inherited IRAs would allow debtors to shelter assets that are not truly retirement funds, but rather a source of current consumption. As the Seventh Circuit noted, if a decedent were to withdraw funds from an IRA and give them to a beneficiary, the funds would not be considered "retirement funds" in the beneficiary's hands. Clark, 673 F.3d at 712. It would be inconsistent to treat inherited IRAs differently.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Chilton, which held that an inherited IRA is exempt, is distinguishable. The Chilton court's reasoning relies on the fact that §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) do not explicitly require that the retirement funds be the debtor's. However, this omission does not imply that the funds need not be retirement funds in the debtor's hands. In re Chilton, 674 F.3d 486, 489 (5th Cir. 2012). The Seventh Circuit correctly rejected this argument, holding that the statute's language and structure imply that the funds must be retirement funds for the debtor. Clark, 673 F.3d at 711.

The Seventh Circuit's decision should be affirmed, as it is consistent with the statutory text and the purpose of the Bankruptcy Code's exemption provisions. The plain meaning of "retirement funds" requires that the funds be reserved for the debtor's retirement, and an inherited IRA does not meet this requirement. Therefore, an inherited IRA is not exempt from a debtor's estate in bankruptcy under 11 U.S.C. §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12).
