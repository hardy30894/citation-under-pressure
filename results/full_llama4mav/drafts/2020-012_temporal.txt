## Argument

The Railroad Retirement Act ("RRA") and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act ("RUIA") grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final decisions" of the United States Railroad Retirement Board ("the Board"). 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The Fifth Circuit below held that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application, relying on its prior decision in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003). However, Roberts was wrongly decided, as it misinterpreted the Supreme Court's holding in Califano v. In Szostak, the Second Circuit reasoned that the RRA's judicial review provision, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), does not explicitly limit review to decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits. 370 F.2d at 254-55. The court noted that the statute's language is broad enough to encompass review of a denial of a petition to reopen. Id. This interpretation is consistent with the principle that "a strong presumption exists in favor of judicial review of administrative action." See, e.g., Estep v. United States, 327 U.S. 114, 120 (1946) (noting that "the provision for judicial review . . . is a basic part of our scheme of government").

The Fifth Circuit should have followed Szostak and held that the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a final, reviewable decision. By dismissing Salinas's petition for lack of jurisdiction, the Fifth Circuit erred and perpetuated a circuit split on this issue.

This Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision and hold that the Board's denial of Salinas's request to reopen his 2006 application is subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).
