ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Lucero's Class Action Complaint for Lack of Subject Matter Jurisdiction Based on Mootness.

The district court's dismissal of Lucero's class action complaint for lack of subject matter jurisdiction was erroneous because it failed to consider the exception to the mootness doctrine for class actions. While it is true that "a suit brought as a class action must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified," Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins., 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir.2009) (internal quotation marks and citation omitted), this general rule is not without exception.

The Supreme Court has recognized that a class action lawsuit is not rendered moot when the named plaintiff's individual claims are satisfied if the plaintiff has a continuing personal stake in the litigation. See U.S. Parole Commission v. Geraghty, 445 U.S. 388, 404 (1980) ("We therefore hold that an action brought on behalf of a class does not become moot upon expiration of the named plaintiff's substantive claim, even though class certification has been denied."). Moreover, the Court has held that a plaintiff's motion for class certification can relate back to the filing of the complaint, thereby preserving the justiciability of the class claims. See Sosna v. Iowa, 419 U.S. 393, 402 n.11 (1975) ("There may be cases in which the controversy involving the named plaintiffs is 'capable of repetition, yet evading review,' ... and in such cases the ' mootness' exception for class actions should be applied.").

In this case, Lucero filed his class action complaint on April 20, 2009, and BCR served him with a Rule 68 offer of judgment on June 2, 2009. Aplt.App. 10, 13, 2, 64. Although Lucero did not file his motion for class certification until February 22, 2010, the parties had agreed to conduct discovery only on the class certification issue for the first six months, and the district court had adopted this plan. Aplt.App. 58-59. Under these circumstances, it is reasonable to relate Lucero's motion for class certification back to the filing of the complaint, as the delay in filing the motion was not undue. See Weiss v. Regal Collections, 385 F.3d 337, 347-48 (3d Cir.2004) (holding that a plaintiff's motion for class certification related back to the filing of the complaint where the plaintiff had diligently pursued class certification).

Furthermore, allowing defendants to "pick off" named plaintiffs in class actions by tendering an early Rule 68 judgment for the full amount of individual claims undermines the purpose of the class action device. See Deposit Guar. Nat'l Bank v. Roper, 445 U.S. 326, 339 (1980) ("Requiring multiple plaintiffs to bring separate actions, which effectively could be 'picked off' by a defendant's tender of judgment before an affirmative ruling on class certification could be obtained, obviously would frustrate the objectives of class actions."). As the Seventh Circuit has noted, "a defendant should not be permitted to thwart a class action by 'buying off' the named plaintiffs." Primax Recoveries, Inc. v. Sevilla, 324 F.3d 544, 546-47 (7th Cir.2003).

Other circuits have also recognized the need to prevent defendants from mooting class actions through early Rule 68 offers. See, e.g., Sandoz v. Cingular Wireless LLC, 553 F.3d 913, 920-21 (5th Cir.2008) (holding that a defendant's offer of judgment did not moot the class claims where the plaintiff had filed a motion for class certification); Pitts v. Terrible Herbst, Inc., 653 F.3d 1081, 1091-92 (9th Cir.2011) (same). See also, e.g., Damasco v. Clearwire Corp., 662 F.3d 891, 895 (7th Cir.2011) (noting that "a defendant should not be able to thwart a class action by 'picking off' the named plaintiff with an offer of judgment").

In light of these authorities, the district court erred in dismissing Lucero's class action complaint for lack of subject matter jurisdiction based on mootness. The court should have considered whether Lucero's motion for class certification related back to the filing of the complaint, or whether BCR's offer of judgment had terminated the justiciability of the class claims.

The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
