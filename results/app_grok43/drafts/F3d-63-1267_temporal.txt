I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW BECAUSE STELWAGON FAILED TO PROVE ACTUAL DAMAGES CAUSED BY ANY PRICE DISCRIMINATION.

A plaintiff seeking damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act must prove with reasonable certainty that the defendant's violation caused actual injury to its business or property. Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co., 282 U.S. 555, 562 (1931) (damages may not rest on "mere speculation or conjecture"). The evidence must furnish a basis for a reasonable inference that the violation produced the claimed loss; otherwise the verdict cannot stand. Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc., 327 U.S. 251, 264 (1946).

Stelwagon's proof fell short of this standard. Its witnesses testified only that competitors received lower prices and that Stelwagon lost some sales, without demonstrating that the price differential itself caused measurable injury. Such testimony supplies no "definite and certain" foundation for quantifying damages. Eastman Kodak Co. v. Southern Photo Materials Co., 273 U.S. 359, 379 (1927). The jury's award therefore rested on the very speculation the statute forbids.

The district court's remittitur did not cure the defect. Reducing an unsupported verdict cannot create the missing causal link between the discrimination and the claimed loss. See Keogh v. Chicago & N.W. Ry. Co., 260 U.S. 156, 165 (1922) (damages must be traced directly to the unlawful act). Because Stelwagon never supplied evidence from which a jury could find actual antitrust injury, the treble-damage judgment must be vacated.
