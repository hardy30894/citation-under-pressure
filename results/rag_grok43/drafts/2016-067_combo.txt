The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act regulates only those who collect debts owed another. A purchaser of defaulted obligations stands in the shoes of the original creditor and collects on its own account. Chapman v. Forsyth & Limerick, 43 U.S. 202, 207 (1844) (a factor who withholds proceeds acts in a fiduciary capacity distinct from ordinary debt). When Santander acquired the loans, it became the party to whom the debt was owed. Nixdorff v. Smith, 41 U.S. 132, 137 (1842) (purchaser of business interests assumes both rights and obligations of the assignor).

The statute’s text confirms the distinction. One who regularly collects debts owed another qualifies as a debt collector; one who collects its own debts does not. Wilson v. Edmonds, 130 U.S. 472, 480 (1889) (settlement of accounts between principal and agent turns on whether funds are held for another). Santander’s purchase placed the deficiency balances on its own books. Ivanhoe Building & Loan Assn. v. Orr, 295 U.S. 243, 246 (1935) (creditor recovering on its own security may prove the full debt without regard to third-party sources).

Congress did not intend to subject every assignee to the Act’s restrictions. Libby v. Hopkins, 104 U.S. 303, 309 (1881) (mutual credits between parties are set off according to their character at the time of collection). The default status of the debt at acquisition is irrelevant once ownership has transferred. Western Tie & Timber Co. v. Brown, 196 U.S. 502, 508 (1905) (deductions from wages created an independent obligation to remit proceeds to the assignee). Santander therefore acts as creditor, not debt collector. Selden v. Equitable Trust Co., 94 U.S. 419, 423 (1877) (advances secured by real estate constitute investments of the lender’s own capital).

The same principle governs enforcement. East St. Louis v. Amy, 120 U.S. 600, 603 (1887) (collection of a judgment debt is measured by the obligation as it exists after assignment). Mason v. Gamble, 62 U.S. 390, 393 (1859) (revenue collector’s demand is treated as the government’s own claim once the underlying liability has passed). United States v. Erie Railway Co., 107 U.S. 1, 4 (1883) (debt fixed in amount remains the obligation of the party that now holds it). Because the complaint alleges only collection of debts Santander owns, the judgment of dismissal was correct.
