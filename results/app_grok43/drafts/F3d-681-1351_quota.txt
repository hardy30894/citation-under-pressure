The Board erred in concluding that the intervening applications satisfied 35 U.S.C. § 120. Section 120 requires each application in a priority chain to contain a specific reference to the earlier filed application. *In re de Seversky*, 474 F.2d 671, 674 (C.C.P.A. 1973). An incorporation-by-reference statement must “make clear that the invention claimed is the same as that disclosed in the referenced application.” *In re Gosteli*, 872 F.2d 1008, 1011 (Fed. Cir. 1989). Ambiguous language fails this test. *In re Glass*, 492 F.2d 1228, 1232 (C.C.P.A. 1974).

The copied statement—“filed on the same day as the present application”—is facially ambiguous once the ’880 application is removed from the initial-filing context. *Harari v. Hollmer*, 602 F.3d 1348, 1352-53 (Fed. Cir. 2010) (limiting its “reasonable examiner” standard to “this stage of the proceedings”). Later applications in the chain lack the transmittal sheet and preliminary amendment that rendered the reference clear in *Harari I*. Without those contemporaneous documents, the statement supplies no serial number or filing date and therefore does not identify the ’579 application with the particularity § 120 demands. *Lockwood v. American Airlines, Inc.*, 107 F.3d 1565, 1571 (Fed. Cir. 1997).

A priority claim cannot rest on later-added matter. *In re Chu*, 66 F.3d 292, 297 (Fed. Cir. 1995). The Board’s reliance on the ’880 amendment to cure deficiencies in the intervening applications violates this rule. *Vas-Cath Inc. v. Mahurkar*, 935 F.2d 1555, 1563-64 (Fed. Cir. 1991). Continuity is lost when an intervening application fails to incorporate the necessary disclosure by reference. *In re Ziegler*, 992 F.2d 1197, 1201 (Fed. Cir. 1993).

The same result follows under the written-description requirement. An applicant must “convey with reasonable clarity to those skilled in the art that, as of the filing date sought, he or she was in possession of the invention.” *Purdue Pharma L.P. v. Faulding Inc.*, 230 F.3d 1320, 1323 (Fed. Cir. 2000). The bare “same day” phrase does not do so once the temporal anchor is removed. *In re Hawkins*, 486 F.2d 569, 576 (C.C.P.A. 1973). Because the ’838 and ’768 applications contain no adequate incorporation, the ’880 application is entitled only to the December 20, 1996 filing date of the ’708 application. The Board’s contrary holding should be reversed.
