**Argument**

The district court lacked subject matter jurisdiction. Federal courts are courts of limited jurisdiction, and the party invoking removal bears the burden of establishing jurisdiction. *Mulcahey v. Columbia Organic Chems. Co.*, 29 F.3d 148, 151 (4th Cir. 1994); *Darcangelo v. Verizon Commc’ns, Inc.*, 292 F.3d 181, 186 (4th Cir. 2002). Because removal statutes are strictly construed, any doubt must be resolved in favor of remand. *Mulcahey*, 29 F.3d at 151. That rule is dispositive here.

I. **This declaratory-judgment action does not “arise under” federal law under 28 U.S.C. § 1331.**

Federal-question jurisdiction exists only when a federal issue appears on the face of the well-pleaded complaint. *Louisville & Nashville R.R. v. Mottley*, 211 U.S. 149, 152 (1908); *Franchise Tax Bd. v. Constr. Laborers Vacation Tr.*, 463 U.S. 1, 9–10 (1983). A defendant cannot create federal jurisdiction by raising a federal defense, even if the defense is anticipated in the complaint. *Caterpillar Inc. v. Williams*, 482 U.S. 386, 392–93 (1987); *Aetna Health Inc. v. Davila*, 542 U.S. 200, 207 (2004).

The Pressls’ complaint seeks a declaration about the scope of a Virginia property easement and their right to build a dock. That is a state-law title and servitude dispute. The complaint does not plead a federal cause of action, nor does it necessarily require resolution of any federal issue. Under *Grable & Sons Metal Prods., Inc. v. Darue Eng’g & Mfg.*, 545 U.S. 308, 314 (2005), a state-law claim supports § 1331 only when it “necessarily raise[s]” a stated federal issue, actually disputed and substantial, that a federal forum may entertain without disrupting the federal-state balance approved by Congress. See also *Gunn v. Minton*, 568 U.S. 251, 258 (2013).

That standard is not met. The meaning of the 1960 flowage easement can be resolved by Virginia property law. The district court’s reliance on the FERC license, or APCO’s regulatory position, at most supplied a defense. But “a case may not be removed to federal court on the basis of a federal defense.” *Beneficial Nat’l Bank v. Anderson*, 539 U.S. 1, 6 (2003). The well-pleaded complaint rule forbids jurisdiction by anticipation. *Franchise Tax Bd.*, 463 U.S. at 12; *Merrell Dow Pharms. Inc. v. Thompson*, 478 U.S. 804, 808 (1986).

Nor does *Grable* rescue removal. In *Grable*, federal jurisdiction existed because the only contested issue was the meaning of a federal tax notice statute. 545 U.S. at 314–15. Here, by contrast, the dispute is over the scope of private easement rights under state law, and any federal-law overlay is contingent. That is precisely the sort of case that falls outside § 1331. See *Gunn*, 568 U.S. at 260–62.

II. **Section 825p does not confer jurisdiction over this private easement dispute.**

APCO also relied on 16 U.S.C. § 825p, but that provision does not create jurisdiction over every suit involving a FERC licensee. Section 825p gives the federal district courts jurisdiction over violations of the Federal Power Act and over “all suits in equity and actions at law brought to enforce any liability or duty created by” the Act. *See* 16 U.S.C. § 825p. The Supreme Court has cautioned that when Congress creates a limited grant of federal jurisdiction, courts may not expand it beyond the statute’s terms. *Shoshone Mining Co. v. Rutter*, 177 U.S. 505, 513–14 (1900); *Merrell Dow*, 478 U.S. at 809–10.

This suit does not seek to enforce any “liability or duty” created by the Federal Power Act. It asks only whether APCO’s claimed control over a dock on privately owned land exceeds the scope of a 1960 easement. That is a land-use dispute governed by state law. Nothing in § 825p converts every controversy involving property within a hydroelectric project boundary into a federal case. Where Congress wishes to displace state-law adjudication, it says so clearly. See *Shoshone Mining*, 177 U.S. at 513–14.

III. **Because jurisdiction was lacking, the district court’s merits dismissal cannot stand.**

When a court lacks subject matter jurisdiction, its only proper course is to vacate and remand. *Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Env’t*, 523 U.S. 83, 94 (1998); *Arbaugh v. Y & H Corp.*, 546 U.S. 500, 514 (2006). A dismissal on the merits entered without jurisdiction is void. *Ex parte McCardle*, 74 U.S. (7 Wall.) 506, 514 (1868).

The district court therefore erred in denying remand and in dismissing the complaint. The case belongs in state court, where the parties’ rights under the flowage easement can be determined under Virginia law. This Court should vacate the judgment and remand with instructions to return the case to the Virginia circuit court.
