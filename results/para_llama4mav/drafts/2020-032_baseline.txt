The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that a constitutional challenge to a school policy seeking nominal damages is rendered moot when the unconstitutional policy is revised during litigation. As this Court has held, a claim for nominal damages cannot save an otherwise moot case. Flanigan's Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs, 868 F.3d 1248, 1265-66 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc). The Eleventh Circuit's decision is consistent with the principle that a case becomes moot when the issues presented are no longer "live" or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome. Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc., 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013) (quoting Murphy v. Hunt, 455 U.S. 478, 481 (1982) (per curiam)).

Here, GGC revised its "Freedom of Expression Policy" and removed the challenged portion of its "Student Code of Conduct" during litigation, rendering the petitioners' claims for declaratory and injunctive relief moot. The district court correctly concluded that GGC had "unambiguously terminated the Prior Policies and there is no reasonable basis to expect that it will return to them." Uzuegbunam v. Preczewski, No. 18-12676, 2019 WL 2733683, at *5 (11th Cir. July 1, 2019). The petitioners' claim for nominal damages does not provide a sufficient basis to overcome mootness.

The Eleventh Circuit's decision is also consistent with this Court's precedent, which holds that a plaintiff must demonstrate a "continuing injury" or a "real and immediate threat of repeated injury" to maintain a claim for declaratory or injunctive relief. City of Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95, 102 (1983) (quoting O'Shea v. Littleton, 414 U.S. 488, 495-96 (1974)). The petitioners have not demonstrated a continuing injury or a real and immediate threat of repeated injury, as GGC has revised its policies and there is no indication that it will revert to its prior practices.

Furthermore, the district court did not abuse its discretion in denying the petitioners leave to amend their complaint. The petitioners failed to properly seek leave to amend, instead embedding their request within their response to the motion to dismiss. Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018). The district court's decision to enter judgment without allowing the petitioners to amend their complaint was not an abuse of discretion, as the petitioners had the opportunity to seek relief under Rules 59(e) or 60(b) after the district court's dismissal. Czeremcha v. Int'l Ass'n of Machinists & Aerospace Workers, AFL-CIO, 724 F.2d 1552, 1556 (11th Cir. 1984).

In sum, the Eleventh Circuit correctly held that the petitioners' constitutional challenge to GGC's policies was rendered moot by the revision of those policies during litigation. The decision is consistent with this Court's precedent and the principles of mootness. The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying the petitioners leave to amend their complaint. Therefore, the judgment of the Eleventh Circuit should be affirmed.
