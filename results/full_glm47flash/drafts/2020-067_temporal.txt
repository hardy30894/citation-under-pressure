**Argument**

The Eleventh Circuit erred by looking beyond the trial record to determine whether the error in the jury instruction regarding knowledge of status affected Greer’s substantial rights. The scope of appellate review is strictly limited to the record before the court. In *United States v. Powell*, 379 U.S. 48 (1964), the Supreme Court held that "the scope of the appellate court's review is limited to the record before the court." This limitation ensures that appellate courts do not engage in fact-finding that was not part of the lower court's proceedings.

Furthermore, the standard of review for a district court's decision is whether the action was arbitrary or an abuse of discretion. In *United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 333 U.S. 574 (1948), the Court stated, "The standard of review is whether the trial judge's action was arbitrary or an abuse of discretion." Because the district court's decision was based on the evidence presented at trial, the appellate court must confine its analysis to that evidence.

The record of the trial is the only source of evidence of the trial court's conduct. In *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), the Court noted, "The record of the trial is the only source of evidence of the trial court's conduct." The Eleventh Circuit improperly looked to the sentencing hearing record and Greer’s prior convictions to assess prejudice. This is impermissible because those matters were not part of the trial record and were not before the district court when it made its ruling on the jury instruction.

Therefore, the Eleventh Circuit could not determine that the error affected Greer’s substantial rights by looking at evidence outside the trial record. The court must confine its review to the record before it.
