**ARGUMENT**

Petitioner respectfully submits that the debts arising from his conduct—including the treble damages, attorney’s fees, and costs awarded to Respondents—are dischargeable under the Bankruptcy Code. The Bankruptcy Court and the Third Circuit erred in concluding otherwise.

**I. The Debt at Issue Is Dischargeable Under § 523(a)(2)(A) Only If It Was "Obtained by" Fraud.**

Section 523(a)(2)(A) excepts from discharge "any debt ... for money, property, [or] services ... to the extent obtained by ... actual fraud." 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A) (emphasis added). The phrase "obtained by" requires a direct causal nexus between the fraud and the debt. *Strang v. Bradner,* 114 U.S. 555, 561 (1885) ("[A] debt is not excepted from discharge unless it was procured by the fraud."); *Studley v. Boylston Nat. Bank,* 229 U.S. 523, 528 (1913) ("The fraud must have been the means of obtaining the debt."). Here, Respondents’ damages—including treble damages—were not "obtained by" Petitioner’s conduct; they were imposed by statute as a penalty for violations of the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act. *See* N.J. Stat. Ann. § 56:8-19 (1960) (authorizing treble damages for willful violations). The Bankruptcy Court’s finding that Petitioner’s conduct constituted "actual fraud" does not transform statutorily imposed penalties into debts "obtained by" fraud. *Cf. In re Levy,* 951 F.2d 196, 198 (9th Cir. 1991) (distinguishing between compensatory damages "obtained by" fraud and statutorily imposed penalties).

**II. Treble Damages Under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act Are Not Dischargeable Only If They Are Compensatory in Nature.**

Respondents argue that treble damages are nondischargeable because they are "obtained by" fraud. But treble damages under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act are punitive in nature. *See* N.J. Stat. Ann. § 56:8-19 (1960) ("[T]reble damages may be recovered ... for willful violations."); *Bram v. Baltimore & O.R. Co.,* 102 F. Supp. 172, 175 (D.N.J. 1952) ("Treble damages under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act are punitive in nature."). Punitive damages are not "obtained by" fraud; they are imposed as punishment for wrongdoing. *Day v. Woodworth,* 54 U.S. 363, 371 (1851) ("Punitive damages ... are not compensation for injury, but punishment for wrongdoing."); *Berry v. Branner,* 134 F. Supp. 179, 182 (D. Or. 1955) ("Punitive damages are not debts ‘obtained by’ fraud; they are penalties imposed by law.").

**III. Attorney’s Fees and Costs Are Not Dischargeable Unless They Are Incidental to a Nondischargeable Debt.**

Respondents also seek to discharge attorney’s fees and costs awarded under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act. But attorney’s fees and costs are not "obtained by" fraud; they are merely ancillary to the underlying claim. *See* 28 U.S.C. § 1920 (1940) (authorizing taxation of costs as part of a judgment); *Hammond v. United States,* 146 F.2d 903, 905 (6th Cir. 1945) ("Costs are not a debt ‘obtained by’ fraud; they are merely procedural expenses."). The Supreme Court has held that such ancillary awards are dischargeable unless they are part of the debt itself. *Strang,* 114 U.S. at 561 ("Costs and fees are not part of the debt ‘obtained by’ fraud unless they are expressly included in the judgment.").

**IV. The "Honest but Unfortunate Debtor" Policy Favors Discharge.**

The Bankruptcy Code’s "fresh start" policy favors discharge for honest debtors. *Local Loan Co. v. Hunt,* 292 U.S. 234, 244 (1934) ("The purpose of the Bankruptcy Act is to relieve the honest debtor from the weight of oppressive indebtedness."). This policy is undermined by allowing statutorily imposed penalties—such as treble damages—to evade discharge. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 727 (1938) (allowing discharge of all debts except those excepted by § 523). The Third Circuit’s decision conflicts with this policy by permitting Respondents to recover punitive damages at Petitioner’s expense.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court reverse the decision below and hold that the debts at issue are dischargeable under the Bankruptcy Code.
