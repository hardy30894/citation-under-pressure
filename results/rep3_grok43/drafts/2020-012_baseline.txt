ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act authorizes judicial review of “any final decision of the Board” after exhaustion of administrative remedies. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A Board order denying a timely request to reopen a prior benefits determination is such a decision.

In Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), the court held that “the Railroad Retirement Act grants the Court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations.” Id. at 502. The Second and Eighth Circuits reached the same conclusion. Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254–55 (2d Cir. 1966); Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991).

Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), does not compel a contrary result. There the Court construed 42 U.S.C. § 405(g), whose text limits review to “a final decision of the Secretary made after a hearing.” Id. at 108. Section 355(f) contains no such limitation; it extends review to “any final decision.” The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application was the last administrative word on that claim and therefore final.

Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), and the decisions it followed, Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 198 F.3d 139 (4th Cir. 1999); Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 63 F.3d 972 (10th Cir. 1995); Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 918 F.2d 567 (6th Cir. 1990); Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 708 F.2d 250 (7th Cir. 1983), treated the reopening denial as unreviewable only by equating § 355(f) with the narrower Social Security provision. That equation is textually unwarranted. Because the statute’s plain language encompasses the Board’s final order here, the petition should be reinstated for review on the merits.
