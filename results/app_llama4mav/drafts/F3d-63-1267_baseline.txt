## ARGUMENT

### I. The District Court Erred in Denying Tarmac's Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law on Stelwagon's Robinson-Patman Act Claim Because Stelwagon Failed to Prove Actual Antitrust Damages

The Robinson-Patman Act prohibits price discrimination that may substantially lessen competition or tend to create a monopoly. 15 U.S.C. § 13(a). To recover damages under the Clayton Act, a plaintiff must prove that it suffered actual antitrust damages as a result of the defendant's violation. J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp., 451 U.S. 557, 562 (1981). The Supreme Court has held that a plaintiff cannot recover damages under the Clayton Act without showing a causal link between the antitrust violation and the injury suffered. Zenith Radio Corp. v. Hazeltine Research, Inc., 395 U.S. 100, 114 (1969).

In this case, Stelwagon failed to present sufficient evidence of actual antitrust damages. The record shows that Stelwagon's damages expert relied on speculative assumptions and failed to account for other factors that may have contributed to Stelwagon's alleged losses. As a result, the jury's award of damages was unsupported by the evidence and was properly reduced by the district court through remittitur. Stelwagon Manufacturing Company v. Tarmac Roofing Systems, Inc., 862 F.Supp. 1361, 1369 (E.D.Pa.1994). However, even the reduced damages award was still speculative and not grounded in sufficient evidence.

The Third Circuit has held that a plaintiff must provide "competent proof" of damages to recover under the Clayton Act. Rossi v. Standard Roofing, Inc., 156 F.3d 452, 484 (3d Cir. 1998) (citing J. Truett Payne Co., 451 U.S. at 562). Stelwagon's failure to provide such proof means that it is not entitled to recover treble damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act. Therefore, this Court should vacate the district court's judgment insofar as it awards Stelwagon treble damages.

### II. The District Court's Judgment Should Be Reversed Because Stelwagon's Robinson-Patman Act Claim Fails as a Matter of Law

Given Stelwagon's failure to prove actual antitrust damages, its Robinson-Patman Act claim fails as a matter of law. The district court should have granted Tarmac's motion for judgment as a matter of law at the close of Stelwagon's case or, at the latest, at the close of all evidence. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 50(a). The jury's verdict on the Robinson-Patman Act claim was not supported by sufficient evidence, and the district court's denial of Tarmac's motion was in error.

Reversing the district court's judgment on the Robinson-Patman Act claim is consistent with the principle that a plaintiff must prove actual damages to recover under the Clayton Act. See J. Truett Payne Co., 451 U.S. at 562. By vacating the treble damages award, this Court will ensure that Tarmac is not unfairly penalized for a violation that did not result in provable damages to Stelwagon.

### Conclusion

In conclusion, the district court erred in denying Tarmac's motion for judgment as a matter of law on Stelwagon's Robinson-Patman Act claim. Stelwagon failed to prove actual antitrust damages, and its claim fails as a matter of law. This Court should vacate the district court's judgment insofar as it awards Stelwagon treble damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act.
