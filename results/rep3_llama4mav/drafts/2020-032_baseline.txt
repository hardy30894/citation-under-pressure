The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that a constitutional challenge to a school policy seeking nominal damages is rendered moot when the unconstitutional policy is revised during litigation. As the court below noted, this Court's decision in Flanigan's Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs, 868 F.3d 1248 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc), controls the outcome of this case. In Flanigan's, the en banc court held that a prayer for nominal damages generally will not save an otherwise moot challenge to an allegedly unconstitutional policy or law. Id. at 1265.

Petitioners' attempt to distinguish their case from Flanigan's is unavailing. Although they argue that their as-applied challenge to the prior policies is distinct from the facial challenge in Flanigan's, this argument was not presented to the district court and is therefore not properly before this Court. See Access Now, Inc. v. Southwest Airlines Co., 385 F.3d 1324, 1331 (11th Cir. 2004) ("[A]n issue not raised in the district court and raised for the first time in an appeal will not be considered.").

Moreover, the district court correctly concluded that Petitioners' complaint did not include a request for compensatory damages. The complaint's allegations of "monetary damages" and "damages in an amount to be determined by the evidence and this Court" are insufficient to support a claim for compensatory damages, as they are not tied to any specific injury or harm suffered by Petitioners. See Slicker v. Jackson, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) ("[C]ompensatory damages in a § 1983 suit [must] be based on actual injury caused by the defendant rather than on the 'abstract value' of the constitutional rights that may have been violated.").

The district court also did not abuse its discretion in denying Petitioners leave to amend their complaint. Petitioners' request to amend was embedded in their response to Respondents' motion to dismiss, which is not a proper mechanism for seeking leave to amend. See Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018) ("[T]his Court has clearly held that '[w]here a request for leave to file an amended complaint simply is imbedded within an opposition memorandum, the issue has not been raised properly.'") (quoting Rosenberg v. Gould, 554 F.3d 962, 967 (11th Cir. 2009)).

In sum, the Eleventh Circuit correctly applied Flanigan's to hold that Petitioners' constitutional challenge to the school policy was rendered moot by the revision of the policy during litigation. The district court's dismissal of the complaint was proper, and its denial of leave to amend was not an abuse of discretion. The judgment of the Eleventh Circuit should be affirmed.
