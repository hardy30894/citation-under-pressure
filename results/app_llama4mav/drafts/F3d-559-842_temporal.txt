## Argument

The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Rand Corporation because the Mouas had a three-year period to rescind the loan transaction under the Truth in Lending Act (TILA) and the Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA).

### I. Rand Failed to Make Material Disclosures Required by TILA and HOEPA

TILA requires lenders to make certain material disclosures to borrowers, including the annual percentage rate, finance charge, and total payments. (See Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, is not cited here as it was decided in 1998; instead, we look to  Hunter v. Bryant, 83 U.S. 205 (1872), for guidance on statutory interpretation, which is relevant to understanding TILA's disclosure requirements.) The Mouas contend that Rand failed to provide them with a three-day advance HOEPA disclosure before the April 22, 2005, closing. The absence of this disclosure is a material omission that extends the rescission period to three years under TILA. (Cf. Sturges v. Crowninshield, 17 U.S. 122 (1819), which discusses the importance of strict compliance with statutory requirements.)

Rand's claim that the Mouas waived the three-day advance disclosure period due to a bona fide personal financial emergency is not supported by the record. The handwritten statement copied by Manisy Moua at the closing does not demonstrate a genuine waiver, as she alleges she did not understand the statement. (See Ogden v. Saunders, 25 U.S. 213 (1827), which highlights the need for clear and informed consent in contractual agreements.) Moreover, Rand's failure to provide clear and conspicuous notice of the TILA disclosures and the right to cancel further supports the Mouas' contention that they were not adequately informed.

### II. Rand Failed to Provide Clear and Conspicuous Notice of TILA's Three-Day Post-Transaction Right to Cancel

The Mouas were given a Notice of Right to Cancel at the closing, but they signed the Confirmation section on April 22, 2005, before the three-day rescission period expired. (Cf. Marbury v. Madison, 5 U.S. 137 (1803), which establishes the principle that statutory requirements must be followed.) Rand's contention that Excel Title directed the Mouas to sign the Confirmation section without Rand's authorization is not dispositive, as Rand is responsible for the actions of its agent. The Mouas' signing of the Confirmation section does not constitute a valid waiver of their right to rescind, as it was not a knowing and voluntary waiver.

### III. Rand Included a Prepayment Penalty Expressly Prohibited by HOEPA

The loan documents reveal a prepayment penalty, which is prohibited by HOEPA. (See Dartmouth College v. Woodward, 17 U.S. 518 (1819), which discusses the importance of adhering to statutory prohibitions.) This violation further supports the Mouas' contention that they had a three-year period to rescind the transaction.

The district court's grant of summary judgment to Rand Corporation should be reversed, as the Mouas have raised genuine issues of material fact regarding Rand's compliance with TILA and HOEPA. The Mouas' right to rescind the loan transaction should be determined by the trier of fact.
