**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Did Not Abuse Its Discretion in Enhancing the Lodestar Based on Exceptional Performance and Results.**

The Eleventh Circuit correctly affirmed the district court’s enhancement of the lodestar fee under 42 U.S.C. § 1988. The Supreme Court has repeatedly recognized that district courts possess “broad discretion” in determining reasonable attorney’s fees under fee-shifting statutes, *Hensley v. Eckerhart*, 461 U.S. 424, 437 (1983), and that enhancements may be appropriate in “rare” and “exceptional” cases where the lodestar fails to reflect the quality of representation or the results achieved. *Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air v. Pennsylvania (Delaware Valley I)*, 478 U.S. 546, 565 (1986); *Blum v. Stenson*, 465 U.S. 886, 898-99 (1984).

Contrary to Petitioners’ contention, the Supreme Court has never categorically barred enhancements based on superior performance or exceptional results. In *Blum*, the Court explicitly left open the possibility of an upward adjustment where the applicant presents “specific evidence” that the lodestar does not adequately compensate for “superior performance” and “exceptional success.” *Id.* at 899, 104 S.Ct. at 1549. The Court reiterated this principle in *Delaware Valley I*, stating that enhancements remain permissible in “rare” and “exceptional” cases supported by detailed findings. 478 U.S. at 565. The Court’s subsequent decision in *Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air v. Commonwealth of Pennsylvania (Delaware Valley II)*, 483 U.S. 711 (1987), did not alter this framework; it merely clarified that enhancements could not be based solely on the contingent nature of the litigation. *Id.* at 731.

The Eleventh Circuit has consistently applied this standard, affirming enhancements where the district court made specific, record-supported findings of exceptional performance and results. *Kenny A. ex rel. Winn v. Perdue*, 532 F.3d 1209, 1242 (11th Cir. 2008); *NAACP v. City of Evergreen*, 812 F.2d 1332, 1336-37 (11th Cir. 1987); *Norman v. Housing Auth. of Montgomery*, 836 F.2d 1292, 1302 (11th Cir. 1988). As the concurring opinion in *Kenny A.* correctly noted, the Supreme Court’s decisions in *Blum* and *Delaware Valley I* establish that enhancements are permissible where the lodestar fails to reflect the true value of the services rendered. 532 F.3d at 1242 (Wilson, J., concurring).

**II. The District Court’s Findings Were Sufficiently Specific and Supported by the Record.**

Petitioners argue that the district court’s enhancement was impermissible because it relied on the judge’s own observations rather than “specific evidence.” This contention misapprehends the nature of fee determinations. The Supreme Court has expressly recognized that district courts are uniquely positioned to evaluate the quality of advocacy and the significance of results. *Delaware Valley I*, 478 U.S. at 565; *Blum*, 465 U.S. at 899. The district court here provided detailed findings, noting that plaintiffs’ counsel achieved “exceptional success” in securing comprehensive reforms for foster children and that their performance was “stellar” throughout the litigation. *Kenny A. ex rel. Winn v. Perdue*, 454 F.Supp.2d 1260, 1286, 1290 (N.D. Ga. 2006).

This Court has repeatedly upheld enhancements where the district court’s findings were similarly grounded in its observations of counsel’s performance and the impact of the litigation. *See, e.g., Geier v. Sundquist*, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); *Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co.*, 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000); *Quaratino v. Tiffany & Co.*, 166 F.3d 422, 425 (2d Cir. 1999). The Eleventh Circuit has likewise affirmed enhancements where the district court’s findings were based on its firsthand experience with the litigation. *Kenny A.*, 532 F.3d at 1242.

**III. No Circuit Split Exists on This Issue.**

Petitioners mischaracterize the state of the law by suggesting a circuit split. In fact, every circuit to consider the issue has permitted enhancements in “rare” and “exceptional” cases where the lodestar fails to reflect superior performance or exceptional results. *See, e.g., Geier*, 372 F.3d at 794-95; *Van Gerwen*, 214 F.3d at 1046; *Quaratino*, 166 F.3d at 425; *Forshee v. Waterloo Indus., Inc.*, 178 F.3d 527, 532 (8th Cir. 1999); *Hyatt v. Apfel*, 195 F.3d 188, 192 (4th Cir. 1999); *Roe v. Cheyenne Mountain Conference Resort, Inc.*, 124 F.3d 1221, 1233 n.8 (10th Cir. 1997); *Shipes v. Trinity Indus.*, 987 F.2d 311, 320 (5th Cir. 1993). The Eleventh Circuit’s decision in *Kenny A.* is fully consistent with these authorities.

**Conclusion**

The district court did not abuse its discretion in enhancing the lodestar based on exceptional performance and results. The Supreme Court has expressly permitted such enhancements in “rare” and “exceptional” cases, and the Eleventh Circuit has consistently affirmed them where supported by specific, record-based findings. The judgment below should be affirmed.
