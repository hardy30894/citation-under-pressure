**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE MERIT SYSTEMS PROTECTION BOARD’S DISMISSAL OF PETITIONER’S APPEAL FOR LACK OF JURISDICTION IS A “MIXED CASE” SUBJECT TO REVIEW IN DISTRICT COURT.**

The Civil Service Reform Act of 1978 (“CSRA”) provides that a federal employee who alleges discrimination in connection with an adverse employment action over which the Merit Systems Protection Board (“Board”) has jurisdiction may seek judicial review in district court. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2); *Kloeckner v. Solis*, 568 U.S. 41, 47 (2012) (“[W]hen an employee brings a ‘mixed case’—a case appealable to the Board and alleging discrimination—she may seek review in district court.”). This Court has long recognized that the term “mixed case” encompasses not only cases in which the Board reaches the merits of a discrimination claim, but also cases in which the Board dismisses the appeal for lack of jurisdiction where the employee’s claim of involuntariness is inextricably intertwined with allegations of discrimination. *See, e.g., Williams v. Dep’t of the Army*, 715 F.3d 1360, 1363 (Fed. Cir. 2013) (holding that a jurisdictional dismissal based on voluntariness is a mixed case subject to district court review).

The Board’s dismissal of Petitioner’s appeal for lack of jurisdiction falls squarely within this definition. Petitioner’s claim that his retirement and suspension were involuntary was predicated on allegations that the agency discriminated against him on the basis of his race, age, and disability, and retaliated against him for engaging in protected activity. *See* Pet. App. 3a-4a. The Board’s jurisdictional determination thus turned on the very same factual and legal issues that underlie Petitioner’s discrimination claim. As this Court has explained, when “the Board’s jurisdiction hinges on the validity of [the employee’s] contention that the settlement—and hence, the resulting suspension and retirement—had been involuntary,” the case is a mixed case subject to district court review. *Perry v. Merit Sys. Prot. Bd.*, 842 F.3d 1345, 1348 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (citing *Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 47).

The D.C. Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Powell v. Department of Defense*, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), is inconsistent with *Kloeckner* and must be rejected. *Powell* held that a jurisdictional dismissal is not a mixed case, but the Supreme Court in *Kloeckner* expressly declined to address whether its holding applied to jurisdictional dismissals. *See Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 603 n.3 (noting that the circuits were split on whether procedural dismissals should be reviewed in district court, and reserving the question of jurisdictional dismissals). Moreover, the Supreme Court’s reasoning in *Kloeckner* supports the conclusion that jurisdictional dismissals based on voluntariness are mixed cases. The Court emphasized that the CSRA’s “mixed-case” provision “refers to cases in which an employee has been affected by an action which she may appeal to the Board and alleges that a basis for the action was discrimination.” *Id.* at 604 (quoting 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)). Here, Petitioner was affected by an action (his retirement and suspension) that he alleged was the result of discrimination, and the Board’s jurisdiction turned on whether that action was voluntary. *See id.* Thus, Petitioner’s case is a mixed case subject to district court review.

II. **THE D.C. CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT *KLOECKNER* DID NOT OVERRULE *POWELL*.**

The D.C. Circuit in this case erroneously concluded that *Kloeckner* did not overrule *Powell*. *See Perry*, 842 F.3d at 1350-51. This conclusion is inconsistent with the Supreme Court’s holding in *Kloeckner* and the plain text of the CSRA.

The Supreme Court in *Kloeckner* held that when the Board dismisses a mixed case on procedural grounds, judicial review lies in district court. *Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 607. The Court reasoned that the CSRA’s “mixed-case” provision “refers to cases in which an employee has been affected by an action which she may appeal to the Board and alleges that a basis for the action was discrimination.” *Id.* at 604. The Court further explained that the term “mixed case” encompasses cases in which the Board dismisses the appeal for lack of jurisdiction where the employee’s claim of involuntariness is intertwined with allegations of discrimination. *Id.* at 604-05.

The D.C. Circuit’s attempt to distinguish *Kloeckner* on the ground that it involved a procedural dismissal rather than a jurisdictional dismissal is unavailing. The Supreme Court’s reasoning in *Kloeckner* applies with equal force to jurisdictional dismissals. The Court’s focus was on whether the employee’s claim of discrimination was intertwined with the Board’s jurisdictional determination. *See id.* at 604-05. Here, Petitioner’s claim of involuntariness was inextricably intertwined with his discrimination claim, and the Board’s jurisdiction turned on that very issue. Thus, Petitioner’s case is a mixed case subject to district court review.

III. **UNIFORMITY AND EFFICIENCY FAVOR DISTRICT COURT REVIEW.**

The D.C. Circuit’s holding creates a circuit split and undermines the uniformity and efficiency of judicial review under the CSRA. The Federal Circuit has consistently held that jurisdictional dismissals based on voluntariness are mixed cases subject to district court review. *See, e.g., Williams*, 715 F.3d at 1363; *Conforto v. Merit Sys. Prot. Bd.*, 713 F.3d 1115, 1118 n.1 (Fed. Cir. 2013). The D.C. Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Powell* is an outlier and should be rejected.

Moreover, district court review is more efficient and consistent with the purposes of the CSRA. District courts are better equipped to handle discrimination claims, which often involve complex factual and legal issues. *See Kloeckner*, 568 U.S. at 607 (explaining that district courts are better suited to handle discrimination claims). The D.C. Circuit’s holding forces employees to litigate their discrimination claims in the Federal Circuit, which lacks expertise in discrimination law and is ill-equipped to handle such claims. *See id.*

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court reverse the decision of the D.C. Circuit and hold that the Board’s dismissal of Petitioner’s appeal for lack of jurisdiction is a mixed case subject to review in district court.
