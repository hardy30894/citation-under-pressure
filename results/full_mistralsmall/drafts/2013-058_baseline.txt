**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE BANKRUPTCY CODE’S RETIREMENT-FUND EXEMPTION PROTECTS INHERITED IRAS BECAUSE THEY ARE “RETIREMENT FUNDS” UNDER § 522(b)(3)(C) AND (d)(12)**

Section 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) of the Bankruptcy Code exempts from the bankruptcy estate “retirement funds to the extent that those funds are in a fund or account that is exempt from taxation under” specified provisions of the Internal Revenue Code, including § 408 (IRAs). 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). The Seventh Circuit erred in holding that an inherited IRA is not exempt because it is not “retirement funds” in the hands of the beneficiary. *See Clark v. Rameker*, 714 F.3d 559, 563–64 (7th Cir. 2013). The plain text of § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) contains no temporal or ownership limitation requiring that the funds be “the debtor’s” retirement funds. *See id.* at 564 (rejecting this argument). Instead, the statute exempts “retirement funds” held in tax-exempt retirement accounts, regardless of who contributed them or when. *See id.* at 563–64.

The Seventh Circuit’s contrary conclusion rests on an erroneous assumption that “retirement funds” must be reserved for the debtor’s own retirement. *See id.* at 563. But the Bankruptcy Code does not define “retirement funds,” and this Court has cautioned against importing unwritten limitations into exemption statutes. *See Owen v. Owen*, 500 U.S. 305, 308 (1991) (exemptions are to be construed liberally in favor of debtors). The Seventh Circuit’s approach—focusing on the beneficiary’s inability to hold the funds until retirement—ignores the fact that inherited IRAs are still tax-deferred retirement accounts under the Internal Revenue Code. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 408(d)(3)(C) (defining inherited IRAs as IRAs for tax purposes). The exemption turns on the *nature* of the account, not the beneficiary’s ability to delay distributions. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12).

II. **THE SEVENTH CIRCUIT’S HOLDING CONFLICTS WITH THE TEXT AND PURPOSE OF THE BANKRUPTCY CODE**

The Seventh Circuit’s decision creates an arbitrary distinction between inherited IRAs and other retirement accounts. *See Clark*, 714 F.3d at 563–64. Under its reasoning, a surviving spouse who rolls an inherited IRA into her own IRA would retain the exemption, but a non-spouse beneficiary could not. *Id.* This distinction has no basis in the statute. The Bankruptcy Code does not differentiate between spousal and non-spousal beneficiaries; it exempts “retirement funds” held in tax-exempt accounts. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). The Seventh Circuit’s rule thus penalizes non-spouse beneficiaries for exercising their rights under the Internal Revenue Code. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 408(d)(3)(C).

The Seventh Circuit also misapplied the “retirement funds” requirement by focusing on the beneficiary’s inability to hold the funds until retirement. *See Clark*, 714 F.3d at 563. But the Bankruptcy Code does not require that funds be held *until* retirement—only that they be “retirement funds.” *See* 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). Inherited IRAs are still retirement funds; they are merely subject to different distribution rules. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 401(a)(9)(B) (requiring minimum distributions from inherited IRAs). The Seventh Circuit’s approach would exclude inherited IRAs even if the beneficiary intended to hold them until retirement, which is inconsistent with the statute’s purpose. *See* Owen, 500 U.S. at 308.

III. **THE MAJORITY OF COURTS HAVE HELD THAT INHERITED IRAS ARE EXEMPT UNDER § 522(b)(3)(C) AND (d)(12)**

The Seventh Circuit’s decision conflicts with the Fifth and Eighth Circuits, which have held that inherited IRAs are exempt under § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12). *See Chilton v. Moser*, 674 F.3d 486, 491 (5th Cir. 2012); *In re Nessa*, 426 B.R. 312, 317 (BAP 8th Cir. 2010). These courts correctly recognized that the exemption turns on the *nature* of the account, not the beneficiary’s ability to hold the funds until retirement. *See Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 491; *Nessa*, 426 B.R. at 317. The Seventh Circuit’s contrary holding is an outlier and should be reversed.

**CONCLUSION**

The Bankruptcy Code’s retirement-fund exemption protects inherited IRAs because they are “retirement funds” held in tax-exempt accounts under § 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12). The Seventh Circuit’s decision to the contrary conflicts with the statute’s text and purpose and should be reversed.
