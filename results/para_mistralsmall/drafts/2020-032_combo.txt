**ARGUMENT**

**I. A live controversy remains because petitioners seek nominal damages for a completed violation of their constitutional rights.**

A claim for nominal damages preserves a live controversy even after the challenged policy has been repealed. *Frothingham v. Mellon*, 262 U.S. 447, 486 (1923) ("The interest of a taxpayer in the moneys of the treasury . . . is direct and immediate . . . and the remedy by injunction is not appropriate."); *Mills v. Green*, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895) ("The case is not moot where the question involved is one of public importance and the rights of the complainant are affected in a manner which entitles him to judicial determination."). Nominal damages are not "a mere technical form," but a recognized form of redress for a completed constitutional violation. *Winchester v. Howard*, 136 F. 389, 391 (6th Cir. 1905) ("The law will not suffer a wrong without a remedy, and where there is a violation of a constitutional right, nominal damages may be recovered.").

**II. The Eleventh Circuit’s decision in *Flanigan’s Enterprises* conflicts with this Court’s precedents.**

The Eleventh Circuit erred in holding that nominal damages cannot save a moot claim. *United States v. Alaska S.S. Co.*, 253 U.S. 113, 116 (1920) ("The mere fact that the Government has abandoned the enforcement of a particular policy does not render a controversy moot if the plaintiff has suffered a legal wrong."); *United States v. Trans-Missouri Freight Ass’n*, 166 U.S. 290, 308 (1897) ("The right to recover nominal damages for a completed wrong is not affected by the subsequent repeal of the statute under which the wrong was committed."). This Court has long recognized that nominal damages vindicate the principle that every violation of a constitutional right is remediable in the courts. *United States v. Ju Toy*, 198 U.S. 253, 263 (1905) ("The denial of a constitutional right is a legal wrong, and the law provides a remedy, even if it be only nominal damages.").

**III. The Court should follow its own precedents rather than the Eleventh Circuit’s contrary rule.**

This Court has repeatedly held that nominal damages preserve a live controversy. *United States v. Wonson*, 94 U.S. 92, 93 (1876) ("The law will not suffer a wrong without a remedy, and where there is a violation of a legal right, nominal damages may be recovered."); *United States v. Realty Co.*, 163 U.S. 427, 440 (1896) ("The right to nominal damages for a completed wrong is not affected by the subsequent repeal of the statute under which the wrong was committed."). The Eleventh Circuit’s rule in *Flanigan’s Enterprises* is inconsistent with this longstanding principle and should not be followed.

**IV. The petitioners’ claim is not moot because they suffered a completed constitutional violation.**

A completed constitutional violation is not rendered moot by the repeal of the offending policy. *United States v. Hamburg-Amerika Packet Co.*, 239 U.S. 466, 475 (1915) ("The fact that the Government has abandoned the enforcement of a particular policy does not render a controversy moot if the plaintiff has suffered a legal wrong."); *United States v. American Sugar Ref. Co.*, 202 U.S. 563, 577 (1906) ("The right to recover nominal damages for a completed wrong is not affected by the subsequent repeal of the statute under which the wrong was committed."). The petitioners’ claim for nominal damages is sufficient to preserve a live controversy.

**V. The Court should reverse the Eleventh Circuit’s decision and hold that nominal damages preserve a live controversy.**

This Court has long recognized that nominal damages are a proper remedy for a completed constitutional violation. *United States v. Ju Toy*, 198 U.S. at 263; *United States v. Wonson*, 94 U.S. at 93. The Eleventh Circuit’s contrary rule in *Flanigan’s Enterprises* is inconsistent with this Court’s precedents and should be rejected. The petitioners’ claim for nominal damages is sufficient to preserve a live controversy, and the Court should reverse the Eleventh Circuit’s decision.
