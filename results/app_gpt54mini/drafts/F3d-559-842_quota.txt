## ARGUMENT

### I. The district court erred in holding that the Mouas’ rescission right expired after three days.

Under TILA, a borrower whose lender fails to deliver the required material disclosures and notice of the right to cancel may rescind for up to three years. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a), (f); *Beach v. Ocwen Federal Bank*, 523 U.S. 410, 417-19 (1998); *Jesinoski v. Countrywide Home Loans, Inc.*, 574 U.S. 259, 262-63 (2015). Regulation Z likewise provides that the rescission period extends when the creditor fails to provide “the notice of the right to rescind or material disclosures.” 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(a)(3); *Semar v. Platte Valley Federal Savings & Loan Association*, 791 F.2d 699, 702-03 (9th Cir. 1986).

Here, the record permits a finding that Rand failed in multiple, independent ways.

First, the Mouas presented evidence that Rand did not provide the required HOEPA disclosures three business days before consummation. HOEPA requires advance disclosures in covered transactions, and its protections are to be strictly enforced. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b); *Bojarczuk v. Provident Bank*, 399 F.3d 276, 279-80 (3d Cir. 2005). Rand’s assertion of waiver cannot defeat summary judgment because TILA permits waiver of the waiting period only in the narrow circumstance of a bona fide personal financial emergency, and the creditor bears the burden of showing strict compliance with the statutory requirements. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(d); 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(e); *Rodash v. AIB Mortgage Co.*, 16 F.3d 1142, 1148-49 (11th Cir. 1994), abrogated on other grounds by statute; *In re Porter*, 961 F.2d 1066, 1077-78 (3d Cir. 1992). Whether the handwritten waiver here reflected a genuine emergency or was merely a lender-prepared form created because of scheduling problems is at minimum a disputed fact issue.

Second, the Notice of Right to Cancel was not shown to have been provided in a manner that clearly and conspicuously preserved the three-day rescission right. TILA requires that the creditor “clearly and conspicuously” disclose the right to rescind. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a); *Smith v. Highland Bank*, 108 F.3d 1325, 1328-29 (11th Cir. 1997). A creditor’s use of documents that blur when rescission rights expire or create confusion as to whether the borrower has already waived them can violate the Act. *Rodash*, 16 F.3d at 1147-50; *Wiggins v. Avco Financial Services*, 62 F.3d 396, 399-400 (11th Cir. 1995). The “Confirmation” signature page stating that “more than 3 business days have elapsed” is especially problematic because, on this record, it was signed on the day of closing—before the rescission period expired. A document requiring the borrower to certify the passage of time that had not yet occurred cannot be reconciled with TILA’s command that rescission rights be clearly disclosed.

Third, the inclusion of a prepayment penalty prohibited by HOEPA independently extended the rescission period. HOEPA bars certain prepayment penalties in high-cost mortgages. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c); 12 C.F.R. § 226.32(d)(6); *Cetto v. LaSalle Bank National Association*, 518 F.3d 263, 270-72 (4th Cir. 2008). Where a creditor fails to provide the material terms required by TILA and HOEPA, the borrower’s rescission period extends to three years. *Jackson v. First Federal Savings of Arkansas, F.A.*, 709 F.2d 183, 187-88 (3d Cir. 1983). Rand’s promissory note expressly describes an interest-rate and payment structure that the Mouas contend included a prohibited prepayment penalty. That dispute alone defeats summary judgment.

### II. The district court further erred by treating the release-like “Confirmation” as an effective waiver of statutory rescission rights.

TILA does not permit a borrower to prospectively waive the rescission right in ordinary course; the Act allows waiver only by a written statement describing a bona fide personal financial emergency. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(d); 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(e). Courts construe that exception narrowly because TILA is a remedial statute “designed to protect consumers.” *Mourning v. Family Publications Service, Inc.*, 411 U.S. 356, 377 (1973); *Palmer v. Champion Mortgage*, 465 F.3d 24, 29-30 (1st Cir. 2006). The lender must show the emergency was real, the waiver was spontaneous, and the consumer had a genuine need for immediate funds. *Rodash*, 16 F.3d at 1148-49; *Harris v. OSI Financial Services, Inc.*, 595 F. Supp. 2d 884, 889-90 (N.D. Ill. 2009).

The record here does not compel that conclusion. The handwritten waiver was drafted by the closer, not by the borrowers; the circumstances were tied to a delayed payoff statement and lender scheduling, not an emergency caused by the borrowers; and Manisy testified she did not understand the statement she was told to copy. Those facts are inconsistent with a knowing waiver. At minimum, they create a triable issue of fact.

### III. Summary judgment was improper because rescission under TILA turns on disputed material facts, and the remedy is effective upon notice.

The Supreme Court has made clear that a borrower rescinds by giving notice, not by obtaining a judicial declaration first. *Jesinoski*, 574 U.S. at 262-63. Once a borrower timely exercises rescission, the court then determines whether the lender complied and how unwinding should proceed. *Beach*, 523 U.S. at 419-21; *Yamamoto v. Bank of New York*, 329 F.3d 1167, 1172-73 (9th Cir. 2003). Because the Mouas introduced evidence of undisclosed HOEPA terms, defective notice, and an invalid waiver, the district court should have denied Rand’s motion and allowed rescission to proceed. The judgment should be reversed.
