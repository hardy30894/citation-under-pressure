**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE TEXT, STRUCTURE, AND PURPOSE OF THE IMMIGRATION AND NATIONALITY ACT (“INA”) DEMONSTRATE THAT A GRANT OF TEMPORARY PROTECTED STATUS (“TPS”) CONSTITUTES AN “ADMISSION” UNDER 8 U.S.C. § 1255.**

A. **The Statutory Text of 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4) Explicitly Provides That TPS Recipients Are “Considered as Being in, and Maintaining, Lawful Status as a Nonimmigrant,” Which Satisfies § 1255’s Admission Requirement.**

The plain text of 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4) states that an alien granted TPS “shall be considered as being in, and maintaining, lawful status as a nonimmigrant.” *See* 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4). This provision does not merely confer a temporary shield from removal; it statutorily *creates* a lawful immigration status for TPS recipients. *See id.* The Third Circuit’s conclusion that this provision does not also constitute an “admission” under § 1255(a) misreads the statute’s plain meaning. *See Sanchez v. Mayorkas*, 992 F.3d 409, 416 (3d Cir. 2021).

The INA defines “admission” as “the lawful entry of the alien into the United States after inspection and authorization by an immigration officer.” 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(13)(A). However, the statute does not require that this “lawful entry” occur at the *initial* moment of physical entry. Rather, it permits an alien to be “considered” as having satisfied the admission requirement if Congress has statutorily *deemed* them to be in lawful status. *See* 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4). This interpretation is consistent with the Supreme Court’s recognition that Congress may statutorily alter the consequences of an alien’s entry. *See INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca*, 480 U.S. 421, 432 (1987) (“[W]here Congress includes particular language in one section of a statute but omits it in another section of the same Act, it is generally presumed that Congress acts intentionally and purposely in the disparate inclusion or exclusion.”).

B. **The Statutory Structure of 8 U.S.C. § 1255 Demonstrates That TPS Recipients Who Are “Considered” to Be in Lawful Nonimmigrant Status Satisfy the Admission Requirement.**

Section 1255(a) permits adjustment of status for “[t]he status of an alien who was inspected and admitted or paroled into the United States.” 8 U.S.C. § 1255(a). The statute does not specify *when* this admission must occur—only that the alien must be “inspected and admitted or paroled.” *Id.* The Third Circuit’s conclusion that this requirement must be satisfied at the moment of physical entry ignores the fact that Congress has statutorily *deemed* TPS recipients to be in lawful nonimmigrant status. *See Sanchez*, 992 F.3d at 416.

Moreover, § 1255(k) provides an alternative pathway to adjustment for aliens who, *subsequent to lawful admission*, have maintained continuous lawful status. 8 U.S.C. § 1255(k). The statute does not require that this “lawful admission” occur at the moment of physical entry; rather, it permits an alien to satisfy the requirement if they are *deemed* to have lawful status under another provision of the INA. *See id.* This interpretation is consistent with the Supreme Court’s recognition that the INA’s provisions must be read *in pari materia*. *See Cardoza-Fonseca*, 480 U.S. at 431 n.12 (“[S]tatutes dealing with a similar subject should be read in pari materia to achieve a consistent interpretation.”).

C. **The Legislative Purpose of TPS Supports the Interpretation That a Grant of TPS Constitutes an Admission Under § 1255.**

TPS was enacted to provide temporary relief to aliens who cannot safely return to their home countries due to extraordinary conditions. *See* 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(b)(1). The Third Circuit’s conclusion that TPS recipients must leave the United States and reenter lawfully to adjust status undermines this purpose. *See Sanchez*, 992 F.3d at 417. It would force TPS recipients—many of whom have lived in the United States for decades—to risk their safety by returning to dangerous conditions in their home countries. *See id.*

The Supreme Court has recognized that statutes must be interpreted in light of their purpose. *See Chevron U.S.A., Inc. v. Natural Res. Def. Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, 865 (1984) (“[A] court may not substitute its own construction of a statutory provision for a reasonable interpretation made by the administrator of an agency.”). Here, the Department of Homeland Security (“DHS”) has consistently interpreted § 1254a(f)(4) to permit TPS recipients to adjust status. *See* INS General Counsel Opinion No. 91-27 (Mar. 4, 1991) (“A grant of TPS should not be construed as an admission into the United States.”). This interpretation is entitled to deference under *Chevron*. *See id.*

II. **THE THIRD CIRCUIT’S INTERPRETATION OF §§ 1254a AND 1255 IS INCONSISTENT WITH THE TEXT, STRUCTURE, AND PURPOSE OF THE INA.**

A. **The Third Circuit Erroneously Concluded That “Admission” and “Lawful Status” Are Distinct Concepts.**

The Third Circuit relied on *Hanif v. Att’y Gen.*, 694 F.3d 479, 485 (3d Cir. 2012), to conclude that “admission” and “lawful status” are distinct concepts. *See Sanchez*, 992 F.3d at 415. However, *Hanif* did not address whether a statutorily created lawful status could satisfy the admission requirement. *See Hanif*, 694 F.3d at 485. The Third Circuit’s conclusion that these concepts are distinct ignores the fact that Congress has statutorily *deemed* TPS recipients to be in lawful nonimmigrant status. *See* 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4).

B. **The Third Circuit’s Interpretation Renders § 1254a(f)(4) Superfluous.**

The Third Circuit concluded that § 1254a(f)(4) does not satisfy the admission requirement because it does not explicitly state that TPS recipients are “inspected and admitted or paroled.” *See Sanchez*, 992 F.3d at 416. However, this interpretation renders § 1254a(f)(4) superfluous. *See Hibbs v. Winn*, 542 U.S. 88, 101 (2004) (“A statute should be construed so that effect is given to all its provisions, so that no part will be inoperative or superfluous.”). The statute’s plain text provides that TPS recipients are “considered” to be in lawful nonimmigrant status, which satisfies the admission requirement under § 1255. *See* 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4).

C. **The Third Circuit’s Interpretation Undermines the Purpose of TPS.**

The Third Circuit concluded that TPS recipients must leave the United States and reenter lawfully to adjust status. *See Sanchez*, 992 F.3d at 417. This interpretation undermines the purpose of TPS by forcing TPS recipients to risk their safety by returning to dangerous conditions in their home countries. *See id.* The Supreme Court has recognized that statutes must be interpreted in light of their purpose. *See Chevron*, 467 U.S. at 865.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioners respectfully submit that the Court should reverse the judgment of the Third Circuit and hold that a grant of TPS constitutes an “admission” under 8 U.S.C. § 1255.
