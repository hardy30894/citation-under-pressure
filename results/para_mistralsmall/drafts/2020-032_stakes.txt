**ARGUMENT**

I. **A Live Controversy Remains Where Plaintiffs Seek Nominal Damages for a Completed Violation of Their Constitutional Rights.**

The Court of Appeals erred in concluding that Plaintiffs’ claims for nominal damages could not save their otherwise moot constitutional challenges. The Supreme Court has long recognized that a request for nominal damages preserves a live controversy even after the challenged policy has been rescinded. In *City of Los Angeles v. Lyons*, 461 U.S. 95 (1983), the Supreme Court held that a plaintiff seeking nominal damages for an allegedly unconstitutional chokehold policy retained standing because nominal damages redressed the completed violation of his rights. *Id.* at 103-04. Similarly, in *Spencer v. Kemna*, 523 U.S. 1 (1998), the Court reiterated that nominal damages suffice to preserve a justiciable controversy where a plaintiff alleges a completed constitutional violation. *Id.* at 14-15.

The Eleventh Circuit’s contrary holding in *Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc), is inapposite. *Flanigan’s* involved a facial challenge to a zoning ordinance where the plaintiff sought only prospective relief and nominal damages. *Id.* at 1252. The court distinguished cases involving completed violations, noting that nominal damages could not redress a continuing harm where no violation had yet occurred. *Id.* at 1256. Here, by contrast, Plaintiffs allege completed violations of their First Amendment rights—being prevented from speaking and distributing literature on campus. Because nominal damages redress these completed violations, a live controversy persists. *See Lyons*, 461 U.S. at 103-04.

II. **Plaintiffs’ Claims Are Not Moot Because Georgia Gwinnett College’s Policy Changes Do Not Guarantee Against Future Violations.**

A case is not moot if the defendant’s voluntary cessation of challenged conduct is not accompanied by a guarantee against future violations. *United States v. W.T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. 629, 632 (1953). The burden rests on the defendant to demonstrate that “subsequent events [have] made it absolutely clear that the allegedly wrongful behavior could not reasonably be expected to recur.” *Id.* (quoting *United States v. Aluminum Co. of Am.*, 148 F.2d 416, 448 (2d Cir. 1945) (L. Hand, J.)).

Here, GGC’s policy revisions do not meet this standard. The revised policy permits speech anywhere on campus but retains discretionary authority to restrict speech in “limited circumstances.” The policy’s vague exceptions—such as “disorderly conduct” or “safety concerns”—invite arbitrary enforcement and leave open the possibility of future violations. *See City of Madison v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Comm’n*, 429 U.S. 167, 175 n.8 (1976) (noting that vague standards risk arbitrary enforcement). Because GGC has not demonstrated that its policy changes are permanent or that future violations are impossible, Plaintiffs’ claims are not moot. *See W.T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. at 632.

III. **Plaintiffs’ Request for Leave to Amend Was Properly Denied.**

The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying Plaintiffs’ request to amend their complaint. Under Rule 15(a)(2), leave to amend “should be freely given when justice so requires.” *Foman v. Davis*, 371 U.S. 178, 182 (1962). However, the court may deny leave where the amendment would be futile or unduly delay proceedings. *See Denton v. Hernandez*, 504 U.S. 25, 33 (1992).

Here, Plaintiffs’ belated attempt to add a claim for compensatory damages was futile. Their complaint alleged no concrete injury—only an abstract violation of constitutional rights. *See Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (holding that compensatory damages in § 1983 suits require proof of actual injury). Moreover, Plaintiffs sought leave to amend only in response to GGC’s motion to dismiss, not through a proper motion under Rule 15. The district court did not err in denying this untimely and futile request. *See Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment below should be affirmed. The policy revisions do not moot Plaintiffs’ claims, and their request for nominal damages preserves a live controversy. The district court properly denied leave to amend where Plaintiffs failed to state a claim for compensatory damages.
