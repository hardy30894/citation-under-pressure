**INTRODUCTION**

The Freedom of Information Act embodies “a general philosophy of full agency disclosure unless information is exempted under clearly delineated statutory language.” *U.S. Dep’t of State v. Ray*, 502 U.S. 164, 173 (1991). The nine exemptions are “explicitly exclusive” and “must be narrowly construed.” *U.S. Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989); *Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 361 (1976). The Ninth Circuit’s expansion of Exemption 2 into a catch-all for “predominantly internal” documents ignores both the statutory text and this Court’s instruction that exemptions not be expanded by implication. *John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp.*, 493 U.S. 146, 152 (1989).

**I. EXEMPTION 2 IS LIMITED TO “INTERNAL PERSONNEL RULES AND PRACTICES”**

Exemption 2 shields only matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The word “solely” confines the exemption to documents that are exclusively about personnel rules—not to every internal agency record. *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 363–64. The Supreme Court has repeatedly warned that FOIA exemptions are to be interpreted narrowly. *FBI v. Abramson*, 456 U.S. 615, 630 (1982). The Ninth Circuit abandoned that principle when it adopted a “predominantly internal” test. That test deletes “solely” from the statute and substitutes an amorphous balancing standard wholly absent from Congress’s language.

**II. ESQD ARC MAPS ARE NOT PERSONNEL RULES OR PRACTICES**

The withheld documents are Explosive Safety Quantity Distance data—technical maps and formulas showing blast radiuses. They do not govern agency employees’ conduct, working conditions, or duties. They are engineering calculations used to design and construct ammunition storage facilities. They are no more “internal personnel rules” than the goshawk nesting data this Court refused to exempt in *Maricopa Audubon Society v. U.S. Forest Service*, 108 F.3d 1082, 1085 (9th Cir. 1997). There, the Court held that the data did not relate “solely, or even predominantly, to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” *Id.* at 1085 (quoting 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2)). The same is true here.

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), is misplaced. *Hardy* involved an agency raid manual—a classic personnel rule governing officers’ enforcement conduct. *Id.* at 656. This Court has never expanded Exemption 2 to non-personnel matters, and *Hardy* did not do so, either. As the Ninth Circuit itself recognized, *Maricopa Audubon* treated “law enforcement” as merely one sufficient way to satisfy Exemption 2, not as authorization to extend the exemption to unrelated technical data. *Maricopa Audubon*, 108 F.3d at 1085–87.

**III. THE RISK OF CIRCUMVENTION CANNOT BROADEN THE EXEMPTION’S SCOPE**

The Ninth Circuit held that ESQD maps were exempt because their disclosure would “risk circumvention of agency regulation.” But the circumvention inquiry arises only after the document qualifies as an internal personnel rule. *See Rose*, 425 U.S. at 369. It cannot transform otherwise disclosable documents into personnel rules. The agency must first show the document is “related solely to internal personnel rules and practices”; only then may it show that disclosure would risk circumvention. The Ninth Circuit inverted that order, using the risk of circumvention to define the document as “predominantly internal.” That approach eviscerates the statutory threshold.

This Circuit has long recognized that Exemption 2 applies only to matters involving “personnel rules and practices,” not to all internal agency information. *Church of Scientology of California v. U.S. Dep’t of Justice*, 612 F.2d 417, 427 (9th Cir. 1979). The ESQD data are not about how the Navy’s personnel perform their duties; they are about the physical effects of explosives. They are external-facing safety information, not employment rules.

**IV. THE NAVY HAS OTHER EXEMPTIONS FOR GENUINE SECURITY CONCERNS**

If the Navy genuinely fears that disclosure will endanger national security, it may invoke Exemption 1 for classified documents or Exemption 7(F) for law-enforcement records that could endanger individuals. The Navy chose not to classify the ESQD maps and did not establish that they were compiled for law enforcement purposes. It cannot circumvent those specific, narrow exemptions by stretching Exemption 2 into a general national-security catch-all. Exemptions are “explicitly exclusive,” *Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. at 151, and each must be applied independently. *John Doe Agency*, 493 U.S. at 152.

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test violates the plain text of Exemption 2 and this Court’s directive that FOIA exemptions be narrowly construed. The ESQD arc maps and related documents are not “internal personnel rules and practices” of the Navy. The judgment should be reversed.
