**Argument**

The district court erred in holding that Perkins was required to prove reasonable diligence before he could invoke actual innocence as a basis for equitable tolling. This Court has already held that “actual innocence, if proved, serves as a gateway through which a petitioner may pass” despite AEDPA’s limitations period. *Souter v. Jones*, 395 F.3d 577, 602 (6th Cir. 2005). That rule remains controlling unless and until the Supreme Court clearly rejects it. *United States v. Moore*, 131 F.3d 595, 598 (6th Cir. 1997). Nothing in *Pace v. DiGuglielmo*, 544 U.S. 408 (2005), compels a different result.

I. **A credible showing of actual innocence equitably tolls AEDPA’s statute of limitations in this Circuit.**

In *Souter*, this Court held that “a credible claim of actual innocence may equitably toll the statute of limitations” because “in the event that a petitioner can show actual innocence, the miscarriage of justice exception is implicated.” 395 F.3d at 599, 602. The Court explained that the actual-innocence gateway prevents the statute of limitations from barring federal review where doing so would risk the continued incarceration of one who is probably innocent. Id. at 599-600. That reasoning is rooted in the Supreme Court’s recognition that “in an extraordinary case, where a constitutional violation has probably resulted in the conviction of one who is actually innocent,” a habeas court may reach the merits notwithstanding a procedural bar. *Murray v. Carrier*, 477 U.S. 478, 496 (1986); see also *Schlup v. Delo*, 513 U.S. 298, 327 (1995).

Perkins presented the sort of newly discovered evidence that at least warrants a gateway innocence inquiry: multiple affidavits suggesting that Jones, not Perkins, confessed to the killing and attempted to conceal bloodied clothing. At this stage, the question is not whether Perkins has definitively proven innocence, but whether his showing is sufficiently credible to excuse AEDPA’s time bar. *House v. Bell*, 547 U.S. 518, 538 (2006). The district court should have evaluated the innocence claim under *Souter* rather than collapsing the inquiry into a diligence requirement.

II. **Pace did not overrule Souter or impose diligence as an additional element to actual-innocence tolling.**

The district court relied on *Pace*, but *Pace* addressed a different question: whether a petitioner’s confusion about state timeliness could constitute “extraordinary circumstances” warranting equitable tolling. 544 U.S. at 418. The Court stated that a litigant seeking equitable tolling must show that he pursued his rights diligently and that some extraordinary circumstance stood in his way. Id. at 418-19. But *Pace* did not involve actual innocence, and it did not purport to overrule the miscarriage-of-justice cases. See *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 326-27; *House*, 547 U.S. at 536-37.

Indeed, the Supreme Court later confirmed that actual innocence functions as a distinct gateway doctrine. In *McQuiggin v. Perkins*, 569 U.S. 383, 386 (2013), the Court held that “actual innocence, if proved, serves as a gateway through which a petitioner may pass” even when the petition is untimely under AEDPA. The Court specifically rejected the argument that a showing of diligence is a prerequisite to that gateway, explaining that “unexplained delay in presenting new evidence bears on the determination whether the petitioner has made the requisite showing,” but “diligence is not dispositive.” Id. at 399-400. Although *McQuiggin* post-dates the judgment below, it confirms that *Pace* never announced the rule the Warden urges here.

III. **Requiring diligence as a threshold bar would defeat the equitable function of the actual-innocence gateway.**

Equitable tolling exists to prevent strict limitations rules from producing unjust forfeitures. *Holland v. Florida*, 560 U.S. 631, 645-49 (2010). But the actual-innocence gateway serves an even more fundamental purpose: to avoid the incarceration of the innocent. *Schlup*, 513 U.S. at 324-27. If diligence were a threshold requirement, a petitioner with persuasive proof of innocence could still be barred solely because the evidence surfaced late or because counsel failed to develop it sooner. That would invert the doctrine. As the Supreme Court explained, “a federal habeas court must entertain such an otherwise barred constitutional claim” when a petitioner meets the actual-innocence standard. *House*, 547 U.S. at 536.

The district court’s approach also creates an irreconcilable tension with this Court’s precedent. *Souter* allows tolling based on actual innocence; *Pace* does not address innocence; and no controlling decision requires the petitioner to prove diligence before the court may consider whether the miscarriage-of-justice exception applies. This Court should therefore reverse and remand for the district court to assess Perkins’s innocence gateway claim under the proper standard.

**Conclusion**

Perkins respectfully requests that the Court reverse the judgment below and remand for consideration of his habeas petition on the merits.
