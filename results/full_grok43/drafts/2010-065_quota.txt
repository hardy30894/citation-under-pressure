ARGUMENT

Bond lacks standing to challenge her conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 229 on Tenth Amendment grounds. In Tennessee Electric Power Co. v. Tennessee Valley Authority, 306 U.S. 118, 144 (1939), the Court held that private parties “absent the states or their officers, have no standing in this suit to raise any question under the [Tenth] Amendment.”

Federal courts must resolve jurisdictional questions before reaching the merits. Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment, 523 U.S. 83, 101-02 (1998). The Tenth Amendment reserves powers to the States, and a criminal defendant may not assert the States’ sovereign interests. See Screws v. United States, 325 U.S. 91, 109 (1945).

Five circuits have applied Tennessee Electric to deny private-party standing. United States v. Hacker, 565 F.3d 522, 525-27 (8th Cir. 2009); Oregon v. Legal Services Corp., 552 F.3d 965, 971-72 (9th Cir. 2009); Brooklyn Legal Services Corp. v. Legal Services Corp., 462 F.3d 219, 234-35 (2d Cir. 2006); Medeiros v. Vincent, 431 F.3d 25, 33-36 (1st Cir. 2005); United States v. Parker, 362 F.3d 1279, 1284-85 (10th Cir. 2004). The Eleventh Circuit has likewise denied standing where the claim presents only an “undifferentiated, generalized grievance.” Dillard v. Chilton County Commission, 495 F.3d 1324, 1335 (11th Cir. 2007).

Although two circuits once permitted such challenges, Atlanta Gas Light Co. v. U.S. Department of Energy, 666 F.2d 1359, 1368 n.16 (11th Cir. 1982); Gillespie v. City of Indianapolis, 185 F.3d 693, 703-04 (7th Cir. 1999), subsequent decisions have retreated from that position. See Dillard, 495 F.3d at 1335; Seniors Civil Liberties Ass’n v. Kemp, 965 F.2d 1030, 1034 n.6 (11th Cir. 1992). The weight of authority confirms that only a State or its officers may invoke the Tenth Amendment against federal legislation. Bond therefore cannot press the claim here.
