**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT CORRECTLY HELD THAT THE BURTONS WERE NOT “EMPLOYERS” OF LUXAMA’S CREW UNDER THE AWPA OR FLSA.

The AWPA borrows the broad “employ” definition from the FLSA, which includes “to suffer or permit to work.” 29 U.S.C. §§ 203(g), 1802(5). But that definition, while expansive, is not boundless. In *Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb*, 331 U.S. 722, 730-33 (1947), the Supreme Court held that worker status turns on the “circumstances of the whole activity,” not on isolated indicia of control. The Eleventh Circuit likewise has recognized that the ultimate inquiry is whether the putative employer exercised “substantial control” over the workers’ meaningful terms and conditions of employment. *Antenor v. D & S Farms, 88 F.3d 925, 933-34 (11th Cir. 1996); Aimable v. Long & Scott Farms, 20 F.3d 434, 439-40 (11th Cir. 1994).*  

Applying those principles, the district court properly concluded that the Burtons did not jointly employ Luxama’s workers. The undisputed facts show that Luxama recruited, transported, paid, directed, and supervised his own crew; he determined the per-box rate paid to each worker; and he managed the day-to-day harvest. The Burtons merely identified the field to be harvested and observed progress a few times a day. Those limited interactions are not enough to transform a grower into an employer under the AWPA. See *Aimable*, 20 F.3d at 444-46 (no joint employment where grower did not control hiring, firing, pay, supervision, or transportation); *Antenor*, 88 F.3d at 939-42 (looking to the totality of circumstances, especially control over workers’ labor).  

Nor did the Burtons’ contractual arrangement with Little Rock Produce Company alter that conclusion. The record shows a crop-sharing and marketing arrangement, with Little Rock supplying seed, boxes, and a trailer and the Burtons retaining responsibility for growing and harvesting the beans. But economic involvement in a crop, without operational control over the labor force, does not create an employment relationship under the AWPA. See *Antenor*, 88 F.3d at 940-42; *Charles v. Burton*, 169 F.3d 1322, 1328-30 (11th Cir. 1999). Because Luxama retained the core prerogatives of an independent farm labor contractor, the district court properly entered summary judgment on the joint-employment issue.

II. THE BURTONS’ FAILURE TO VERIFY LUXAMA’S REGISTRATION VIOLATED THE AWPA, BUT THE DISTRICT COURT PROPERLY LIMITED RECOVERY TO STATUTORY DAMAGES.

The AWPA requires growers who “utilize” the services of a farm labor contractor to verify that the contractor is properly registered. 29 U.S.C. § 1842. Congress created a private right of action with specific remedies, including statutory damages where actual damages are not proved. 29 U.S.C. § 1854(c)(1). In *DeLeon-Granados v. Eller & Sons Trees, Inc.*, 581 F.3d 632, 635-38 (11th Cir. 2009), the court explained that AWPA’s remedial scheme is statutory and carefully calibrated: where Congress specifies statutory damages, courts should not expand the remedy beyond the statute’s terms.  

The district court therefore correctly held that, even if the Burtons failed to inspect Luxama’s registration as required, the workers could not recover actual damages attributable to that violation absent proof of injury caused by the registration lapse itself. The record instead shows that the catastrophic injuries were caused by the truck accident and the absence of insurance or a liability bond under 29 U.S.C. § 1841, not by the registration violation under § 1842. In *Mata v. Young Bros. Farm, Inc.*, 731 F.2d 1308, 1311-13 (5th Cir. 1984), the court recognized that statutory agricultural-worker protections do not create open-ended tort recovery untethered to the statute’s injury and causation requirements. Likewise, *Flores v. Albertson’s, Inc.*, 19 F.3d 1100, 1104-06 (5th Cir. 1994), holds that AWPA remedies must be tied to the statutory violation and the loss proven.  

Here, the district court correctly separated the two violations. The Burtons’ failure to verify registration supported liability under § 1842, but not actual damages for the later transportation accident. Statutory damages are the remedy Congress provided for that violation. 29 U.S.C. § 1854(c)(1); *DeLeon-Granados*, 581 F.3d at 637-38.

III. THE JUDGMENT SHOULD BE AFFIRMED.

Summary judgment is appropriate where there is no genuine dispute of material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 247-52 (1986).* On this record, no reasonable factfinder could conclude that the Burtons jointly employed Luxama’s crew, and the district court properly confined the remedy for any registration-verification violation to the damages authorized by the AWPA. The judgment should be affirmed.
