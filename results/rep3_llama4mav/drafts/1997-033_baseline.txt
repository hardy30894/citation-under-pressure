The District Court's decision to invoke 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the clear statutory mandate of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Section 1407(a) explicitly requires that each action transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel" to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The language of the statute is unequivocal, leaving no room for interpretation that would permit the transferee court to retain the case for trial. 

As Judge Kozinski noted in his dissent below, the federal courts have consistently disregarded this statutory command, starting with the Second Circuit's decision in Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971). However, the plain language of § 1407(a) precludes the transferee court from transferring the case to itself for trial under § 1404(a). The authority granted to the transferee court under § 1407 is limited to pretrial proceedings, and venue transfer motions under § 1404(a) that relate to those proceedings.

The practice of allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial undermines the policy choice reflected in § 1407, which ensures that plaintiffs retain their right to select the forum for trial. In re Air Crash Disaster Near Hanover, New Hampshire, 342 F.Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H. 1971), acknowledged that there is no direct authority in the language of §§ 1407(a) or 1404(a) for a transferee judge to transfer cases to their district for trial. 

The Ninth Circuit's decision below, affirming the District Court's order, was erroneous. The court failed to recognize the clear statutory mandate of § 1407(a) and instead relied on case law that has perpetuated an unlawful practice. As this Court has held, "the starting point for interpreting a statute is the language of the statute itself." Consumer Product Safety Comm'n v. GTE Sylvania, Inc., 447 U.S. 102, 108 (1980). Here, the language of § 1407(a) is clear, and it requires remand to the original district at or before the conclusion of pretrial proceedings.

The judgment of the Ninth Circuit should be reversed, and the case should be remanded to the Northern District of Illinois for trial, as mandated by § 1407(a).
