## ARGUMENT

The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that petitioners’ constitutional challenge became moot when Georgia Gwinnett College revised its policies, and that their claim for nominal damages could not revive an otherwise moot case. This Court should affirm.

### I. The Revision of the Challenged Policies Rendered the Case Moot

A case becomes moot when “the issues presented are no longer ‘live’ or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome.” *Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc.*, 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013) (quoting *Murphy v. Hunt*, 455 U.S. 478, 481 (1982)). Here, GGC repealed the speech-zone and code-of-conduct policies and replaced them with policies permitting expressive activity anywhere on campus. Petitioners do not challenge the district court’s finding that “there is no reasonable basis to expect that [GGC] will return to” the prior policies. That finding satisfies the voluntary-cessation standard: the defendant bears the burden of showing the challenged conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur, *Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Envtl. Servs. (TOC), Inc.*, 528 U.S. 167, 189 (2000), and GGC met that burden by unambiguously terminating the policies and implementing permanent reforms. Because petitioners no longer face any threat of enforcement, their claims for declaratory and injunctive relief are moot. *See Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona*, 520 U.S. 43, 67 (1997) (mootness when “there is no reasonable expectation that the alleged violation will recur”).

### II. Nominal Damages Cannot Save an Otherwise Moot Case

Petitioners argue that their request for nominal damages preserves a live controversy. But this Court has squarely held that “a prayer for nominal damages generally will not save an otherwise moot challenge to an allegedly unconstitutional policy or law.” *Flanigan’s Enters., Inc. of Ga. v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248, 1255 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc). That holding is consistent with Article III’s requirement that a plaintiff must have “a personal stake in the outcome of the controversy” throughout the litigation. *Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Env’t*, 523 U.S. 83, 102 (1998). Once the challenged policy is gone, no ongoing injury exists, and nominal damages—which merely “recognize the denial of a right” without compensating for any continuing harm, *Carey v. Piphus*, 435 U.S. 247, 266 (1978)—cannot create a live case or controversy. *See Van de Kamp v. Goldstein*, 555 U.S. 335, 348 (2009) (nominal damages available only if plaintiff proves a constitutional violation, but that does not overcome mootness when the violation is no longer ongoing). The Eleventh Circuit’s rule in *Flanigan’s* is sound: nominal damages do not “retroactively create a live controversy where none exists.” 868 F.3d at 1256.

### III. Petitioners Did Not Properly Plead Compensatory Damages

Petitioners contend that their complaint sought compensatory damages, but the district court correctly rejected that argument. To recover compensatory damages under § 1983, a plaintiff must allege “actual injury caused by the defendant.” *Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000). The complaint here alleged only “irreparable harm” and “injury” without specifying any concrete loss—no monetary loss, physical pain, emotional distress, or reputational harm. *Id.* at 1231. Such conclusory allegations fail to state a plausible claim for compensatory damages. *See Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009) (pleading must contain “factual content that allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable”). The district court therefore properly treated the complaint as seeking only nominal damages.

### IV. The District Court Did Not Abuse Its Discretion in Denying Leave to Amend

Petitioners sought leave to amend only in a response to a motion to dismiss, without filing a proper motion or attaching a proposed amendment. This Court has held that “[w]here a request for leave to file an amended complaint simply is imbedded within an opposition memorandum, the issue has not been raised properly.” *Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018). Moreover, petitioners did not specify what additional factual allegations they would include to support compensatory damages. *See Newton v. Duke Energy Fla., LLC*, 895 F.3d 1270, 1277 (11th Cir. 2018) (plaintiff must “set forth the substance of the proposed amendment”). Even if the request had been procedurally proper, amendment would have been futile because petitioners could not allege actual injury from a policy that was no longer in effect. *See Foman v. Davis*, 371 U.S. 178, 182 (1962) (leave may be denied for futility). The district court therefore acted within its discretion.

### Conclusion

Because the challenged policies were repealed and petitioners cannot maintain a live controversy through nominal damages alone, the case is moot. The judgment of the Eleventh Circuit should be affirmed.
