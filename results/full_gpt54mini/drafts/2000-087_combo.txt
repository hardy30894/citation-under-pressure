The statute does not authorize indefinite detention beyond the 90-day removal period.

The Court should construe 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(6) against a backdrop in which “[f]reedom from imprisonment—from government custody, detention, or other forms of physical restraint—lies at the heart of the liberty” protected by the Constitution. Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71, 80 (1992). Long before Congress enacted the present immigration scheme, this Court held that detention may not be used as a substitute for punishment without adequate limiting principles. See Wong Wing v. United States, 163 U.S. 228, 237 (1896) (holding that even aliens may not be subjected to punishment “without a judicial trial”); Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356, 369 (1886) (“The Fourteenth Amendment to the Constitution is not confined to the protection of citizens.”).

The text of § 1231(a)(6) permits detention “beyond the removal period,” but nothing in the statutory language compels the extraordinary reading that Congress authorized imprisonment without end.
