**ARGUMENT**

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed. Section 523(a)(2)(A) of the Bankruptcy Code excepts from discharge “any debt … for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by … actual fraud.” The treble damages, attorney’s fees, and costs awarded to respondents constitute a single, indivisible debt arising from petitioner’s actual fraud. That entire debt is nondischargeable.

**I. The Plain Language of § 523(a)(2)(A) Encompasses All Liability Flowing from Fraud**

The phrase “to the extent obtained by” does not limit nondischargeability to the precise value of the property fraudulently obtained. Rather, it describes the causal connection between the fraud and the debt. This Court has long held that the bankruptcy discharge exception for fraud covers the entire liability incurred by reason of the fraudulent conduct. *Neal v. Clark*, 95 U.S. 704, 709 (1877) (construing the Bankruptcy Act of 1867, the predecessor to § 523(a)(2)(A), to except “all debts … created by the fraud of the bankrupt”); *Strang v. Bradner*, 114 U.S. 555, 561 (1885) (same). In *Crawford v. Burke*, 195 U.S. 176, 193 (1904), this Court explained that the exception “is not confined to the precise property obtained by the fraud, but extends to all damages directly resulting from the fraudulent act.” *Accord Gleason v. Thaw*, 236 U.S. 558, 562 (1915) (the exception “should be construed liberally in favor of the creditor”).

The courts of appeals uniformly applied this principle under the Bankruptcy Act of 1898. *In re Garcelon*, 104 F. 330, 332 (1st Cir. 1900) (the debt “includes all damages sustained by the creditor by reason of the fraud”); *In re Dunfee*, 219 F. 630, 632 (2d Cir. 1915) (“the debt is the entire liability incurred by reason of the fraud, not merely the amount of money or property obtained”); *In re Taylor*, 1 F.2d 963, 964 (2d Cir. 1924) (“the debt is not limited to the amount actually obtained; it includes all damages flowing from the fraud”); *In re Houtman*, 7 F.2d 137, 138 (2d Cir. 1925) (same); *In re McCallum*, 12 F.2d 111, 112 (2d Cir. 1926) (“the exception covers the entire judgment, including punitive damages”); *In re Brown*, 17 F.2d 650, 651 (2d Cir. 1927) (“the debt includes attorney’s fees and costs awarded in the fraud action”).

**II. Treble Damages Under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act Are Part of the Debt for Fraud**

Petitioner’s fraudulent overcharges violated the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act, which mandates treble damages. Those damages are not a separate penalty; they are a statutory measure of the loss caused by the fraud. This Court has recognized that treble damages serve a remedial purpose. *Chattanooga Foundry & Pipe Works v. City of Atlanta*, 203 U.S. 390, 396 (1906) (treble damages under the Sherman Act are “compensatory” and “not a penalty”). Because the treble damages here are “for” the money obtained by fraud, they fall squarely within § 523(a)(2)(A). *In re Dunfee*, 219 F. at 632 (punitive damages awarded in a fraud action are nondischargeable because they “arise out of the same fraudulent transaction”); *In re McCallum*, 12 F.2d at 112 (same).

**III. Discharge Would Frustrate the Bankruptcy Code’s Fresh Start for Honest Debtors**

The “fresh start” policy of the Bankruptcy Code is reserved for the “honest but unfortunate debtor.” *Neal v. Clark*, 95 U.S. at 709. A debtor who commits actual fraud is not entitled to that fresh start. *Gleason v. Thaw*, 236 U.S. at 562. Permitting petitioner to discharge the treble damages would reward his fraud and undermine the deterrent purpose of the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act. *Crawford v. Burke*, 195 U.S. at 193 (the exception “should be enforced to prevent the bankrupt from escaping the consequences of his own fraud”).

**Conclusion**

The debt owed to respondents, including treble damages, attorney’s fees, and costs, is a single debt “for money … obtained by … actual fraud” within the meaning of § 523(a)(2)(A). The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
