The district court erred in concluding that the asserted claims lack written-description support in the Original Application and therefore are not entitled under 35 U.S.C. § 120 to its February 6, 1997 filing date. An earlier application supplies the required support when it “convey[s] with reasonable clarity to those skilled in the art that, as of the filing date sought, [the inventor] was in possession of the invention.” Vas-Cath Inc. v. Mahurkar, 935 F.2d 1555, 1563-64 (Fed. Cir. 1991). Possession is shown by any disclosure that “reasonably conveys to the artisan” the subject matter later claimed. In re Curtis, 354 F.3d 1347, 1357 (Fed. Cir. 2004).

The Original Application describes a vending machine that supplies telecommunications access to a customer’s external device and that receives payment and status information from that customer. These passages necessarily include an interface permitting the customer to interact with the machine through the very device being connected. That disclosure is sufficient even if the precise phrase “customer interface” does not appear. See Lockwood v. American Airlines, Inc., 107 F.3d 1565, 1572 (Fed. Cir. 1997) (“the specification need not contain the exact terms of the claims”).

Because the Original Application supplies written-description support, the asserted claims are entitled to its filing date. That date antedates the MobileStar Network, which therefore cannot anticipate under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b). See In re Schreiber, 128 F.3d 1473, 1477 (Fed. Cir. 1997). The grant of summary judgment of invalidity must be reversed.
