The Ninth Circuit erred in exempting documents relating to the effects of explosions from disclosure under the FOIA because they are "predominantly" for internal use and present a risk of circumventing agency regulation. The court's adoption of the "predominantly internal" standard for Exemption 2 is unfounded and contravenes the statutory text and Supreme Court precedent.

Exemption 2 shields matters "related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency." 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Supreme Court has interpreted this exemption narrowly, emphasizing that it applies to materials that are not of "genuine and significant public interest" and pose a risk of circumvention of agency regulation. Dep't of the Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S. 352, 369 (1976). The Ninth Circuit's prior caselaw, including Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), limited Exemption 2 to "law enforcement materials" that risk circumvention of agency regulation. 

The Ninth Circuit's adoption of the D.C. Circuit's "predominantly internal" standard in Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 670 F.2d 1051 (D.C. Cir. 1981), is not supported by the statutory text or Supreme Court precedent. The phrase "law enforcement" is not found in the text of § 552(b)(2), and the Supreme Court in Rose did not use this phrase to define the scope of Exemption 2. Instead, the Court emphasized that Exemption 2 applies to materials that are "used for internal purposes" and are not of "genuine and significant public interest." Rose, 425 U.S. at 369.

Furthermore, the Navy failed to demonstrate that the ESQD arc maps are exempt under Exemption 7(F), as they were not "compiled for law enforcement purposes." The Navy's own description of the ESQD arcs as a "long-term planning tool" for designing and constructing ammunition storage facilities indicates that they are not law enforcement materials. See Church of Scientology v. Dep't of Army, 611 F.2d 738, 748 (9th Cir. 1979).

The Navy's reluctance to release the ESQD arc maps appears to be driven by concerns about public reaction rather than national security. The Navy voluntarily disclosed similar documents related to the Bangor base, which is located farther from populated areas than NMII. The Navy's failure to classify the ESQD arc maps for NMII, despite claims that their disclosure could pose national security risks, further undermines its argument for exemption.

In light of the statutory text, Supreme Court precedent, and the Navy's own actions, the Ninth Circuit's decision to exempt the ESQD arc maps from disclosure under Exemption 2 was erroneous. The judgment should be reversed, and the Navy should be required to disclose the requested documents.
