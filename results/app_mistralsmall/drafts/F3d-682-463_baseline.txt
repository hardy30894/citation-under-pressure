**ARGUMENT**

I. **WASEK’S HARASSMENT CLAIMS ARE NOT BARRED BY THE FACT THAT HE CANNOT SHOW THE HARASSMENT WAS BASED ON HIS GENDER.**

A. **Title VII prohibits harassment that is “because of . . . sex,” but does not require that the harasser be motivated by the victim’s gender.** *Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc.*, 523 U.S. 75, 80 (1998) (“[T]he prohibition of discrimination on the basis of sex . . . prevents the workplace from being permeated with discriminatory intimidation, ridicule, and insult.”). The Supreme Court has made clear that harassment is actionable under Title VII when it is “based on sex,” not when it is motivated by the victim’s sexual orientation or perceived sexual orientation. *Id.* at 79-80. Thus, even if Ottobre’s conduct was motivated by Wasek’s perceived bisexuality rather than his gender, Wasek’s claims are not barred.

B. **Michigan’s Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act (“ELCRA”) likewise prohibits harassment “because of . . . sex,” and does not require proof that the harasser was motivated by the victim’s gender.** *Mickelson v. Barbour*, 593 N.W.2d 90, 95 (Mich. 1999) (interpreting ELCRA’s prohibition on sex discrimination to include harassment). The Michigan Supreme Court has held that ELCRA’s protections are not limited to discrimination based on gender stereotypes or sexual orientation. *Id.* at 94-95. Thus, Wasek’s claims under ELCRA are not barred by his inability to show that Ottobre’s conduct was motivated by Wasek’s gender.

II. **WASEK’S HARASSMENT CLAIMS ARE NOT BARRED BY THE FACT THAT HE CANNOT SHOW HE WAS TREATED DIFFERENTLY THAN OTHER EMPLOYEES.**

A. **Title VII does not require a plaintiff to show that he was treated differently than other employees to establish a hostile work environment claim.** *Harris v. Forklift Sys., Inc.*, 510 U.S. 17, 21 (1993) (“[T]he plaintiff need not prove that [the harassment] seriously affected his psychological well-being . . . .”). The Supreme Court has held that a plaintiff must show that the workplace was “permeated with discriminatory intimidation, ridicule, and insult” that was “sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of the victim’s employment.” *Id.* at 21-22 (quoting *Meritor Sav. Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, 67 (1986)).

B. **Wasek has alleged sufficient facts to survive summary judgment on his hostile work environment claim.** *See, e.g., Burlington N. & Santa Fe Ry. Co. v. White*, 548 U.S. 53, 67-68 (2006) (holding that a plaintiff must show that the harassment was “severe or pervasive” enough to create an objectively hostile work environment). Wasek has alleged that Ottobre subjected him to repeated sexual touching, sexually explicit comments, and other harassing behavior over a period of months. *See, e.g., R. 26-1 at 52-58.* These allegations, if proven, would be sufficient to establish a hostile work environment.

III. **WASEK’S CLAIMS ARE NOT BARRED BY THE FACT THAT HE DID NOT REPORT THE HARASSMENT TO HIGHER-LEVEL MANAGEMENT.**

A. **Title VII does not require a plaintiff to report harassment to higher-level management to preserve his claims.** *Faragher v. City of Boca Raton*, 524 U.S. 775, 807 (1998) (“[A] plaintiff need not always alert upper management in order to preserve a claim.”). The Supreme Court has held that an employer is liable for harassment by a supervisor if the employer “failed to provide a reasonable avenue of complaint or to act after receiving notice.” *Id.* at 807-08.

B. **Wasek has alleged sufficient facts to survive summary judgment on his failure-to-act claims.** *See, e.g., Burlington Indus., Inc. v. Ellerth*, 524 U.S. 742, 765 (1998) (holding that an employer is liable for a supervisor’s harassment if the employer “failed to provide a reasonable avenue of complaint or to act after receiving notice”). Wasek has alleged that he reported the harassment to his rig boss, Pat Tripp, who failed to act and even threatened Wasek with retaliation. *See, e.g., R. 26-1 at 52-58.* These allegations, if proven, would be sufficient to establish that Arrow Energy failed to provide a reasonable avenue of complaint or to act after receiving notice.
