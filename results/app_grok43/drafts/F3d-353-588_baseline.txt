ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Erred in Concluding That Epps Failed to Show He Was Regarded as Disabled.

Under the ADA, a plaintiff establishes that he was regarded as disabled by showing the employer “mistakenly believes that the person has a substantially limiting impairment.” Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., 527 U.S. 471, 489 (1999). The same standard governs MHRA claims. See Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.010(5); Medley v. Valentine Radford Communications, Inc., 173 S.W.3d 315, 320 (Mo. Ct. App. 2005).

Chief Hardy’s September 29, 1999 memorandum expressly cited Dr. Mirkin’s note that Epps suffered degenerative disk disease and “would very likely need to seek a different type of occupation,” together with Epps’s workers’ compensation filings and missed work, to conclude Epps could not perform his duties. Hardy’s March 17, 2000 memorandum repeated the same references and added that no light-duty positions existed and that officers must bend, squat, and run. The Mayor and Board of Aldermen adopted these recommendations and terminated Epps. This evidence creates a genuine issue whether Pine Lawn regarded Epps as substantially limited in the major life activity of working. See Sutton, 527 U.S. at 491; see also Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986) (summary judgment inappropriate where evidence permits reasonable inference of disputed fact).

II. The District Court Erred in Holding That Sovereign Immunity Bars Epps’s Retaliation and Intentional-Infliction Claims.

Missouri municipalities enjoy sovereign immunity only for governmental functions performed in a non-proprietary capacity. See Jones v. State Highway Comm’n, 557 S.W.2d 225, 229 (Mo. 1977). Section 287.780 creates a statutory cause of action for retaliatory discharge that applies to all employers, including political subdivisions. See Crabtree v. Bugby, 967 S.W.2d 66, 71 (Mo. 1998). Because the statute expressly imposes liability on any “employer,” it abrogates immunity for claims arising under its terms. See State ex rel. Taylor v. Sanders, 23 S.W.3d 762, 765 (Mo. Ct. App. 2000).

Epps’s intentional-infliction claim likewise survives. Missouri has waived immunity for intentional torts committed by employees acting within the scope of employment when the municipality maintains liability insurance. See Mo. Rev. Stat. § 537.600(1); see also Jungerman v. City of Raytown, 925 S.W.2d 202, 204 (Mo. 1996). The record contains no evidence that Pine Lawn lacked such coverage. Accordingly, summary judgment on both state-law counts was improper.

CONCLUSION

The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for trial.
