**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Due Process Clause Permits Specific Jurisdiction Where a Defendant’s Forum Activities Relate to the Claims, Even If the Claims Did Not Arise from Those Activities.**

The Due Process Clause does not require that a defendant’s forum contacts *cause* the plaintiff’s claims; it requires only that the claims “arise out of or relate to” the defendant’s forum activities. *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 319 (1945) (holding that jurisdiction is proper where the suit “aris[es] out of” the defendant’s forum activities); *Perkins v. Benguet Consol. Mining Co.*, 342 U.S. 437, 447 (1952) (jurisdiction exists where the suit “relates to” the defendant’s forum activities). This Court has repeatedly emphasized that the “arise out of or relate to” standard is satisfied where the plaintiff’s claims are connected to the defendant’s forum conduct, even if the claims did not originate from that conduct. *Helicopteros Nacionales de Colombia, S.A. v. Hall*, 466 U.S. 408, 414 n.8 (1984) (citing *International Shoe* and *Perkins* for the proposition that jurisdiction is proper where the suit “relates to” the defendant’s forum activities).

Here, Ford’s extensive contacts with Montana—including its nationwide distribution of the allegedly defective vehicle, its marketing of replacement parts in the state, and its ongoing business relationships with Montana dealers—plainly “relate to” Gullett’s claims. *See* *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980) (noting that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant’s forum activities are “so continuous and systematic as to render [it] essentially at home in the forum State”). The Montana Supreme Court correctly applied this standard in upholding jurisdiction. *See* *Keeton v. Hustler Magazine, Inc.*, 465 U.S. 770, 779-80 (1984) (finding jurisdiction where the defendant’s forum activities were related to the claims, even if the claims did not arise from those activities).

**II. Ford’s Nationwide Distribution of the Allegedly Defective Vehicle Establishes Specific Jurisdiction in Montana.**

Ford’s nationwide distribution of the vehicle at issue—including its sale to a Washington dealer, subsequent resale to an Oregon resident, and eventual transfer to Montana—demonstrates that Ford purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *See* *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, 471 U.S. 462, 472-73 (1985) (holding that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant “purposefully avails itself of the privilege of conducting activities” in the forum state). This Court has long recognized that a defendant’s nationwide distribution of a product subjects it to jurisdiction in any state where the product is sold or used. *See* *Gray v. American Radiator & Standard Sanitary Corp.*, 22 Ill. 2d 432, 440 (1961) (finding jurisdiction where the defendant’s product was distributed nationwide and caused injury in the forum state); *McGee v. International Life Ins. Co.*, 355 U.S. 220, 223 (1957) (holding that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant’s activities in the forum state were “part of the corporation’s regular course of business”).

Ford’s argument that jurisdiction is improper because the vehicle was not originally sold in Montana ignores this Court’s precedent. *See* *Kulko v. Superior Court*, 436 U.S. 84, 98 (1978) (rejecting the argument that jurisdiction is improper where the defendant’s forum contacts are not the *sole* cause of the plaintiff’s claims). The fact that the vehicle entered Montana through the stream of commerce is sufficient to establish jurisdiction. *See* *Osborn v. Ozlin*, 310 U.S. 53, 62 (1940) (finding jurisdiction where the defendant’s activities in the forum state were “part of a larger course of conduct” that caused the plaintiff’s injury).

**III. Ford’s Marketing and Sale of Replacement Parts in Montana Further Supports Jurisdiction.**

Ford’s ongoing sale of replacement parts in Montana—including the allegedly defective tire at issue—demonstrates that Ford purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *See* *Hanson v. Denckla*, 357 U.S. 235, 253 (1958) (holding that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant’s forum activities are “purposefully directed” at the forum state). This Court has repeatedly emphasized that a defendant’s sale of replacement parts in a state subjects it to jurisdiction in that state. *See* *Gray*, 22 Ill. 2d at 440 (finding jurisdiction where the defendant’s sale of replacement parts in the forum state caused the plaintiff’s injury); *McGee*, 355 U.S. at 223 (holding that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant’s sale of insurance policies in the forum state caused the plaintiff’s injury).

Ford’s argument that jurisdiction is improper because the allegedly defective part was not originally sold in Montana is unavailing. *See* *Perkins*, 342 U.S. at 447 (holding that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant’s forum activities are related to the plaintiff’s claims, even if the claims did not arise from those activities). The fact that Ford marketed and sold replacement parts in Montana is sufficient to establish jurisdiction. *See* *International Shoe*, 326 U.S. at 319 (holding that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant’s forum activities are related to the plaintiff’s claims).

**Conclusion**

The Due Process Clause permits specific jurisdiction where a defendant’s forum activities relate to the plaintiff’s claims, even if the claims did not arise from those activities. *International Shoe*, 326 U.S. at 319; *Perkins*, 342 U.S. at 447. Ford’s nationwide distribution of the allegedly defective vehicle and its marketing and sale of replacement parts in Montana plainly satisfy this standard. *See* *Burger King*, 471 U.S. at 472-73; *McGee*, 355 U.S. at 223. The Montana Supreme Court correctly upheld jurisdiction, and this Court should affirm.
