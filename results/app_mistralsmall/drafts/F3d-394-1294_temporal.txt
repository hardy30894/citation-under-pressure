**ARGUMENT**

I. **The Tax Court Erred in Concluding That Mrs. Davis’s Interest in the Trust Did Not Qualify as a “Qualifying Income Interest for Life” Under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii).**

To qualify for the marital deduction under § 2056(b)(7), the surviving spouse must have a “qualifying income interest for life,” which requires that she be “entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals.” I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The Amendment explicitly provides that the trustee shall pay to Mrs. Davis “all of the net income from the trust estate ... in quarter annual or more frequent installments.” This language mirrors the statutory requirement and satisfies the “entitled to all the income” standard. *See United States v. Pelzer,* 312 U.S. 399, 402 (1941) (holding that where a statute requires payment “at least annually,” payments made quarterly are sufficient).

The Commissioner argues that the trustee’s discretion to determine what is “proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare” of Mrs. Davis injects uncertainty that disqualifies the interest. However, this Court has long recognized that a surviving spouse’s right to income need not be absolute so long as it is not subject to a contingency that would defeat the interest. *See Merchants Nat’l Bank v. Commissioner,* 156 F.2d 834, 836 (5th Cir. 1946) (finding a qualifying income interest where the trustee had discretion to distribute income “as needed for the support and maintenance” of the surviving spouse). The Amendment’s standard is no broader than that upheld in *Merchants Nat’l Bank,* and thus satisfies § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii).

II. **The Tax Court Erred in Concluding That the Trust Failed the “No Power to Appoint” Requirement of I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii).**

Section 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii) further requires that “no person has a power to appoint any part of the property to any person other than the surviving spouse.” The Amendment grants Mrs. Davis, as trustee, the power to invade principal “for the benefit of [her]self ... in [her] reasonable discretion.” The Commissioner contends that this power violates the “no power to appoint” requirement because it allows Mrs. Davis to appoint trust principal to herself.

This argument misreads the statute. The “no power to appoint” requirement is designed to prevent the surviving spouse from diverting trust property to third parties. *See Commissioner v. Estate of Hubert,* 120 F.2d 720, 722 (2d Cir. 1941) (holding that a power to invade principal for the surviving spouse’s benefit does not violate the statute where the power cannot be exercised in favor of others). Here, the Amendment’s invasion power is expressly limited to Mrs. Davis’s own benefit and does not permit her to appoint trust property to anyone else. Thus, it does not run afoul of § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii).

III. **The Tax Court Erred in Failing to Apply the QTIP Election Requirement of I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(v).**

Section 2056(b)(7)(B)(v) requires that the executor make an election on the estate tax return to treat the trust as QTIP. The estate’s return included a timely and valid QTIP election under § 2056(b)(7). The Commissioner does not dispute the validity of the election, and the Tax Court erred in failing to give effect to it. *See Commissioner v. Estate of Spiegel,* 126 F.2d 928, 930 (2d Cir. 1942) (holding that a valid QTIP election is a prerequisite to the deduction).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Tax Court erred in disallowing the marital deduction under § 2056(b)(7). The judgment below should be reversed, and the case remanded for entry of a decision consistent with this Court’s opinion.
