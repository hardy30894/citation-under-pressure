**ARGUMENT**

I. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE “PREDOMINANTLY INTERNAL” AND RISK CIRCUMVENTING AGENCY REGULATION UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 2.

The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” test for Exemption 2 is contrary to the plain text of the statute and this Court’s precedent. Exemption 2 shields only “matters that are related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). This Court has long held that Exemption 2 applies only to “trivial” or “housekeeping” matters, not to documents that have substantial public significance. *Department of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 363-64 (1976) (citing S. Rep. No. 813, 89th Cong., 1st Sess. 8 (1965)). The ESQD arc maps at issue here are not “internal personnel rules and practices” but rather technical safety data used to assess risks to surrounding communities. *See id.* at 369 (Exemption 2 does not apply to documents that “significantly affect[] the public’s understanding of the operations or activities of the government”).

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 670 F.2d 1051 (D.C. Cir. 1981), is misplaced. *Crooker* applied a “predominantly internal” test to law enforcement manuals, but this Court has never adopted such a broad interpretation of Exemption 2. *See Maricopa Audubon Soc’y v. United States Forest Serv.*, 108 F.3d 1082, 1085 (9th Cir. 1997) (rejecting a similar “predominantly internal” argument and holding that Exemption 2 applies only to “trivial” matters). The D.C. Circuit’s approach conflates Exemption 2 with Exemption 7(F), which specifically protects law enforcement techniques that could endanger safety. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F); *Church of Scientology v. Department of Defense*, 611 F.2d 738, 748 (D.C. Cir. 1979) (Exemption 7(F) applies to “law enforcement techniques and procedures”).

Moreover, the Ninth Circuit’s holding conflicts with *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), which limited Exemption 2 to “law enforcement materials” that risk circumvention of agency regulation. *Id.* at 656. The ESQD arc maps are not law enforcement materials; they are safety planning tools used to mitigate risks to the public. *See id.* (Exemption 2 does not apply to documents that “do not relate to the enforcement of the law”).

II. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT THE ESQD ARC MAPS COULD REASONABLY BE EXPECTED TO ENDANGER LIFE OR PHYSICAL SAFETY UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 7(F).

Exemption 7(F) applies only to records “compiled for law enforcement purposes” that “could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of any individual.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F). The ESQD arc maps are not compiled for law enforcement purposes; they are technical safety documents used for long-term planning. *See Church of Scientology*, 611 F.2d at 748 (Exemption 7(F) does not apply to “non-law-enforcement materials”).

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp.*, 493 U.S. 146 (1989), is misplaced. *John Doe* involved records compiled for law enforcement purposes, not safety planning documents. *Id.* at 155-56. The Court has repeatedly emphasized that FOIA exemptions must be narrowly construed. *See Department of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. at 361; *United States Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989).

III. THE NINTH CIRCUIT’S HOLDING DISTORTS CONGRESS’S CAREFUL BALANCE IN FAVOR OF GOVERNMENT SECRECY.

This Court has repeatedly cautioned against expansive interpretations of FOIA exemptions. *See Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. at 151 (exemptions are “explicitly exclusive” and must be narrowly construed). The Ninth Circuit’s holding effectively creates a new exemption for technical safety data, undermining FOIA’s core purpose of promoting transparency. *See Department of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. at 360-61 (FOIA reflects “a general philosophy of full agency disclosure”).

The dissenting opinion in the Ninth Circuit correctly noted that the Navy’s refusal to classify the ESQD arc maps under Exemption 1 suggests that the claimed security risks are overstated. *See Milner v. Dep’t of the Navy*, 563 F.3d 1111, 1125 (9th Cir. 2009) (Berzon, J., dissenting). If the maps truly posed a national security risk, the Navy would have classified them. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(1).

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that the ESQD arc maps are exempt under FOIA Exemptions 2 or 7(F). The judgment below should be reversed, and the case remanded for disclosure of the requested documents.
