# ARGUMENT

## I. THE PLAIN TEXT OF ANILCA § 103(c) CONFIRMS THAT NPS RETAINS REGULATORY AUTHORITY OVER THE NATION RIVER

Statutory interpretation begins and ends with the text where, as here, the text is unambiguous. *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, 842-43 (1984). Section 103(c) provides that "only those lands within the boundaries of a conservation system unit which are public lands . . . shall be deemed to be included as a portion of such unit." 16 U.S.C. § 3103(c). The statute further specifies that non-public lands "shall not be subject to the regulations applicable solely to public lands within such units." *Id.* (emphasis added).

Petitioner's argument fails at the threshold because the hovercraft ban is not a regulation "applicable solely to public lands within" conservation system units. To the contrary, 36 C.F.R. § 2.17(e) prohibits hovercraft use throughout the National Park System, on federal and non-federal lands alike, wherever they may be located. A regulation of universal applicability across the entire Park System cannot simultaneously be one "applicable solely to public lands" within Alaska's conservation units. This Court has repeatedly instructed that courts must give effect to every word of a statute and avoid interpretations that render statutory text superfluous. *TRW Inc. v. Andrews*, 534 U.S. 19, 31 (2001) ("It is a cardinal principle of statutory construction that a statute ought, upon the whole, to be so construed that, if it can be prevented, no clause, sentence, or word shall be superfluous, void, or insignificant."). Petitioner's reading would require this Court to strike the word "solely" from the statute—a result Congress plainly did not intend.

## II. ANILCA § 1319'S SAVINGS CLAUSE PRESERVES NPS'S PRE-EXISTING REGULATORY AUTHORITY

Section 1319 of ANILCA confirms that Congress did not intend to strip NPS of its ordinary regulatory authority. That provision states that "[n]othing in [the statute] shall be construed as . . . superseding, modifying, or repealing, except as specifically set forth in this Act, existing laws applicable to the various Federal agencies which are authorized to . . . exercise licensing or regulatory functions." 16 U.S.C. § 3207. This Court has long recognized that savings clauses of this nature preserve background regulatory authority absent clear congressional intent to displace it. *See Wyeth v. Levine*, 555 U.S. 555, 574-75 (2009) (courts should not infer preemption or displacement of existing regulatory schemes without clear textual command). Nothing in § 103(c) "specifically" abrogates NPS's authority to regulate hovercraft use as a uniform, nationwide safety and resource-protection measure.

## III. THE FEDERAL GOVERNMENT RETAINS A DOMINANT NAVIGATIONAL SERVIENT INTEREST OVER THE WATERWAYS AT ISSUE INDEPENDENT OF TITLE TO THE SUBMERGED LANDS

Even assuming, as the Ninth Circuit did, that title to the submerged lands beneath the Nation River passed to Alaska under the Submerged Lands Act, 43 U.S.C. § 1301 *et seq.*, and the equal-footing doctrine, *see Alaska v. United States*, 545 U.S. 75, 79-80 (2005), that conveyance does not divest the federal government of its authority to regulate navigation and activity on those waters. As Justice Scalia recognized in his separate opinion in that very case, "[i]f title to submerged lands passed to Alaska, the Federal Government would still retain significant authority to regulate activities in the waters of Glacier Bay by virtue of its dominant navigational servitude, other aspects of the Commerce Clause, and even the treaty power." *Alaska v. United States*, 545 U.S. 75, 110 (2005) (Scalia, J., concurring in part and dissenting in part). The federal navigational servitude has been a fixture of this Court's jurisprudence since *The Daniel Ball*, 77 U.S. (10 Wall.) 557, 563 (1871), which recognized federal authority over "waters that are navigable in fact" as "public property of the nation." Ownership of the riverbed is not the touchstone of federal regulatory power; the government's servitude "is a servitude with respect to which the compensation clause of the Fifth Amendment does not apply." *United States v. Rands*, 389 U.S. 121, 123 (1967). Thus, even crediting petitioner's premise that Alaska owns the submerged lands, that fact alone does not preclude NPS from regulating hovercraft operation on the surface waters flowing through federal conservation units.

## IV. PETITIONER HAS ESTABLISHED STANDING, BUT THAT DOES NOT AID HIS MERITS ARGUMENT

Respondent does not dispute that petitioner has satisfied Article III's requirements. Petitioner alleged "an intention to engage in a course of conduct arguably affected with a constitutional interest, but proscribed by a statute, and there exists a credible threat of prosecution thereunder." *Susan B. Anthony List v. Driehaus*, 573 U.S. 149, 158 (2014) (quoting *Babbitt v. United Farm Workers Nat'l Union*, 442 U.S. 289, 298 (1979)). Threatened enforcement, without more, suffices to create an Article III injury: "an actual arrest, prosecution, or other enforcement action is not a prerequisite to challenging the law." *Id.* Petitioner's justiciable injury, however, does not translate into a meritorious statutory claim. The threshold question of standing is analytically distinct from the merits question of whether § 103(c) forecloses the hovercraft regulation. *See Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment*, 523 U.S. 83, 89 (1998) ("The requirement that jurisdiction be established as a threshold matter 'spring[s] from the nature and limits of the judicial power of the United States' and is 'inflexible and without exception.'" (quoting *Mansfield, C. & L.M.R. Co. v. Swan*, 111 U.S. 379, 382 (1884))). That petitioner may properly bring this challenge does not mean the challenge succeeds.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed. The plain text of ANILCA § 103(c), reinforced by the savings clause in § 1319 and the federal government's independent navigational servitude over the Nation River, confirms that NPS retained authority to enforce its uniform hovercraft regulation against petitioner.
