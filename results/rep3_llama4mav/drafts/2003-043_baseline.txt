The government's argument that it can collect a partnership's tax debt from individual partners without assessing the partners or obtaining a judgment against them is unpersuasive. The Internal Revenue Code requires the IRS to assess a tax against a "taxpayer" within three years of the filing of the taxpayer's return. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a). The Code defines a "taxpayer" as "any person subject to any internal revenue tax," and a "person" includes "an individual, a trust, estate, partnership, association, company or corporation." 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14), (a)(1). As the Ninth Circuit correctly held, a partnership and its individual partners are separate "taxpayers" under the Code. Galletti v. United States (In re Galletti), 298 F.3d 1107, 1112 (9th Cir.2002).

The IRS's assessment against the partnership does not extend the statute of limitations for collecting the tax from the individual partners. The Code's assessment and collection provisions presume that the taxpayer against whom a deficiency has been assessed is the same taxpayer for whom the statute of limitations is extended. 26 U.S.C. §§ 6203, 6502(a). Since the IRS did not assess the individual partners, the statute of limitations for collecting the tax from them remains three years. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a).

The Ninth Circuit's decision in Young v. Riddell, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir.1960), does not support the government's position. The district court's holding in Young was limited to the issue of whether a partner who had paid the tax was entitled to a refund when the assessment was made against the partnership, not the partner. Young v. Riddell, 60-1 U.S. Tax Cas. (CCH) ¶ 9831, at 76,054 (S.D.Cal.1959). The court acknowledged that the IRS would have had to either assess the tax against the partner or bring a court action to collect it. Id. at 76,050.

Furthermore, the Ninth Circuit's decision in United States v. Coson, 286 F.2d 453 (9th Cir.1961), supports the respondents' position. In Coson, the court held that an assessment against a partnership did not create a valid lien against a partner's property when the partner was not individually assessed. Coson v. United States, 169 F.Supp. 671, 676 (S.D.Cal.1958). Although the Ninth Circuit affirmed on different grounds, the district court's reasoning is persuasive. Id.

Under California law, a creditor cannot collect a partnership debt from an individual partner without first obtaining a judgment against the partner. Cal. Corp.Code § 16307(c). The IRS did not obtain a judgment against the respondents, and the time for doing so has expired. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a). Therefore, the IRS's claim against the respondents is unenforceable, and the bankruptcy court correctly disallowed it. 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1).

In conclusion, the IRS's failure to assess the individual partners or obtain a judgment against them bars the government from collecting the partnership's tax debt from the respondents. The Ninth Circuit's decision in Galletti is consistent with the Code's provisions and relevant case law, and the respondents respectfully request that this Court affirm the judgment below.
