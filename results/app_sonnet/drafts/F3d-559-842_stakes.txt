ARGUMENT

I. Because Rand's Notice of Right to Cancel Was Not "Clear and Conspicuous," the Mouas Retained a Three-Year Rescission Period.

TILA gives a borrower who pledges her home as collateral three business days after consummation to rescind, but that period does not begin to run until the creditor delivers "the material disclosures required under this section" and a notice of the right to rescind that meets the regulatory standard. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a). Regulation Z requires that this notice be "clear and conspicuous." 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(b). Courts have not treated that phrase as a formality. In Rodrigues v. Members Mortgage Co., 323 F.3d 5 (1st Cir. 2003), the First Circuit held that a notice containing conflicting or ambiguous rescission dates fails the clear-and-conspicuous standard because a borrower "should not be required to make calculations or guess" at when her right expires. Id. at 12-13. The Seventh Circuit likewise measures clarity from the perspective of the "ordinary" borrower, not a lawyer parsing the document. Smith v. Cash Store Mgmt., Inc., 195 F.3d 325, 329 (7th Cir. 1999).

Here, Rand produced two competing versions of the Notice, one bearing only the Receipt acknowledgment and one bearing both the Receipt and a "Confirmation" that the transaction "has not been rescinded" — signed and dated the same day the disclosures were given. A borrower confronted with a form that simultaneously tells her she has until midnight four days later to cancel, while also asking her to certify, on day one, that more than three days have already elapsed and she has chosen not to rescind, has not received a "clear and conspicuous" statement of her rights. See Rodrigues, 323 F.3d at 12-13. Because TILA is a remedial statute construed liberally for consumers, and disclosure obligations are subject to a rule of strict, not substantial, compliance, Semar v. Platte Valley Fed. Sav. & Loan Ass'n, 791 F.2d 699, 704-05 (9th Cir. 1986); Rodash v. AIB Mortg. Co., 16 F.3d 1142, 1148 (11th Cir. 1994) (noting that "technical or minor violations" nonetheless give rise to liability), the district court could not resolve on summary judgment whether Rand's contradictory notice satisfied § 1635(a). That unresolved factual and legal defect alone triggers the three-year rescission period under 15 U.S.C. § 1635(f), because the "notice required under this section" was never properly "delivered."

II. Genuine Issues of Material Fact Preclude Summary Judgment on the Purported Waivers.

Both the three-day rescission period and HOEPA's three-day advance-disclosure period may be waived only through a narrow, tightly policed procedure. Regulation Z requires that any modification or waiver be accomplished by "a written statement that describes the emergency, specifically modifies or waives the right to rescind, and bears the signature of all the consumers entitled to rescind." 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(e)(1); accord 12 C.F.R. § 226.31(c)(1)(iii) (identical requirement for the HOEPA waiting period). Courts scrutinize such waivers with care precisely because the borrower is relinquishing a congressionally created protection. Jackson v. Grant, 890 F.2d 118, 121 (9th Cir. 1989) (recognizing that TILA rescission rights are personal safeguards not to be lightly forfeited).

Manisy Moua testified that the closer directed her to copy, in her own handwriting, a typed statement she did not draft and did not understand — a far cry from the borrower-generated, emergency-specific statement Regulation Z contemplates. A jury could find that a document dictated by the closing agent, rather than composed by the consumer describing her own "bona fide personal financial emergency," does not satisfy § 226.23(e)(1). Likewise, Rand does not dispute that the Confirmation section — certifying that "more than 3 business days have elapsed" — was signed the same day as the Receipt, before the rescission period had even begun to run. A certification that three days have passed when they manifestly have not is not evidence of a valid, informed waiver; at minimum it creates a triable question whether the Mouas knowingly relinquished their rights. See Jackson, 890 F.2d at 121. Summary judgment was therefore improper on this record. See Handy v. Anchor Mortg. Corp., 464 F.3d 760, 764-65 (7th Cir. 2006) (emphasizing that the lender bears the burden of establishing effective compliance with TILA's rescission safeguards).

III. The Prepayment Penalty Independently Extends the Rescission Period Under HOEPA.

HOEPA separately restricts prepayment penalties on high-cost mortgages like this one. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c). The note here imposed graduated payment increases within the loan's first fourteen months and secured a penalty structure inconsistent with § 1639(c)'s limitations on when and how such penalties may be charged. Because § 1639(b) makes HOEPA's substantive protections, including the prepayment-penalty restriction, part of the "material disclosures" whose omission or violation extends the rescission period, the presence of a prohibited penalty term independently triggers the three-year period under § 1635(f). At minimum, whether the penalty clause complied with § 1639(c) is a disputed question of law and fact that the district court did not resolve before granting summary judgment.

CONCLUSION

For these reasons, the Mouas respectfully request that this Court reverse the grant of summary judgment and remand for further proceedings.
