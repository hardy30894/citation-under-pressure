**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE BANKRUPTCY CODE’S RETIREMENT FUNDS EXEMPTION PROTECTS INHERITED IRAS BECAUSE THEY ARE "RETIREMENT FUNDS" UNDER § 522(b)(3)(C) AND (d)(12).**

The Seventh Circuit erred in holding that an inherited IRA is not exempt under the Bankruptcy Code’s retirement funds exemption. Section 522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) exempts from a debtor’s bankruptcy estate “retirement funds to the extent that those funds are in a fund or account that is exempt from taxation under sections 401, 403, 408, 408A, 414, 457, or 501(a) of the Internal Revenue Code.” 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). The statute does not condition the exemption on whether the funds are earmarked for the debtor’s retirement. *See In re Nessa*, 426 B.R. 312, 317 (BAP 8th Cir. 2010) (exemption applies to funds that were "retirement funds" in the hands of the decedent). Instead, it focuses on the tax-exempt status of the account itself. *See id.* at 318 (citing *In re Chilton*, 674 F.3d 486, 490 (5th Cir. 2012)).

The Seventh Circuit’s contrary conclusion rests on an unduly narrow reading of the term “retirement funds.” The court erroneously required that the funds be held for the debtor’s retirement, reasoning that an inherited IRA must be distributed before the beneficiary’s retirement age. *Clark v. Rameker* (In re Clark), 714 F.3d 559, 563 (7th Cir. 2013). This interpretation ignores the plain text of the statute, which does not impose such a temporal restriction. *See Nessa*, 426 B.R. at 317 (rejecting the Seventh Circuit’s reasoning as inconsistent with the statute’s text). The exemption applies to funds held in tax-exempt retirement accounts, regardless of whether the beneficiary is the original account holder or an heir. *See Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 490.

The Seventh Circuit’s reliance on *Rodriguez v. United States*, 480 U.S. 522 (1987), is misplaced. *Clark*, 714 F.3d at 565. *Rodriguez* does not address the Bankruptcy Code’s exemptions and instead deals with statutory construction in a wholly different context. *See Rodriguez*, 480 U.S. at 525–26. The Seventh Circuit’s attempt to analogize inherited IRAs to cash withdrawn from an IRA or a rented-out inherited home improperly conflates the nature of the exemption. *Clark*, 714 F.3d at 564–65. Unlike those examples, an inherited IRA retains its character as a tax-exempt retirement account under the Internal Revenue Code. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 408(d)(3)(C) (defining inherited IRAs as tax-exempt retirement accounts).

II. **THE LEGISLATIVE PURPOSE OF THE EXEMPTION SUPPORTS INCLUDING INHERITED IRAS.**

The Bankruptcy Code’s retirement funds exemption is designed to protect funds set aside for retirement from creditors’ claims. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). This purpose is not undermined by the fact that an inherited IRA must be distributed before the beneficiary’s retirement. *See Nessa*, 426 B.R. at 318. The exemption applies to funds held in tax-exempt retirement accounts, and an inherited IRA is such an account. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 408(e)(1) (defining IRAs as tax-exempt retirement accounts). The Seventh Circuit’s contrary conclusion would frustrate the exemption’s purpose by excluding a category of retirement funds that Congress clearly intended to protect. *See Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 491 (noting that excluding inherited IRAs from the exemption would “frustrate[] rather than effectuate[] legislative intent”).

The Seventh Circuit’s concern that exempting inherited IRAs would encourage debtors to save more to make larger bequests is speculative and unpersuasive. *Clark*, 714 F.3d at 565. Congress has already balanced the competing interests in the Bankruptcy Code by providing exemptions for retirement funds. *See* 11 U.S.C. § 522(b)(3)(C), (d)(12). The Seventh Circuit’s policy concerns do not justify rewriting the statute to exclude inherited IRAs. *See Rodriguez*, 480 U.S. at 525–26 (rejecting the argument that courts should rewrite statutes to achieve policy objectives).

III. **THE MAJORITY OF COURTS TO ADDRESS THIS ISSUE HAVE HELD THAT INHERITED IRAS ARE EXEMPT UNDER THE BANKRUPTCY CODE.**

The Seventh Circuit’s decision conflicts with the Eighth and Fifth Circuits, which have held that inherited IRAs are exempt under the Bankruptcy Code. *See Nessa*, 426 B.R. at 319; *Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 491. These courts correctly recognized that the exemption applies to funds held in tax-exempt retirement accounts, regardless of whether the beneficiary is the original account holder or an heir. *See Nessa*, 426 B.R. at 317; *Chilton*, 674 F.3d at 490. The Seventh Circuit’s contrary conclusion is an outlier and should be rejected.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the Seventh Circuit’s decision and hold that inherited IRAs are exempt under the Bankruptcy Code’s retirement funds exemption.
