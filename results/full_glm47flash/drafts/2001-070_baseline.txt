**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE EIGHTH AMENDMENT DOES NOT CATEGORICALLY PROHIBIT THE EXECUTION OF THE MENTALLY RETARDED.**

The Eighth Amendment does not categorically prohibit the execution of the mentally retarded. The Supreme Court has never held that mental retardation is a per se bar to the death penalty. In *Ford v. Wainwright*, 477 U.S. 399 (1986), the Court held that a categorical bar applies only to those who are "unaware of the punishment they are about to suffer." *Id.* at 409. This standard is satisfied by a finding of insanity, not mild mental retardation. Similarly, in *Stanford v. Kentucky*, 492 U.S. 361 (1989), the Court rejected a categorical bar for juveniles, holding that the death penalty is not per se unconstitutional for those who commit crimes while under the age of eighteen. *Id.* at 369. The Court has consistently held that the Eighth Amendment requires the sentencer to consider mitigating evidence, but it does not categorically prohibit the execution of the mentally retarded. *Penry v. Lynaugh*, 492 U.S. 302, 319 (1989). *Penry* is the controlling precedent for this case, and it explicitly rejected the argument that the Eighth Amendment categorically bars the execution of the mentally retarded.

**II. THE STATE PROVIDED A FULL AND FAIR OPPORTUNITY TO PRESENT MITIGATING EVIDENCE, AND THE JURY CONSIDERED IT.**

The State provided a full and fair opportunity to present mitigating evidence, and the jury considered it. *Lockett v. Ohio*, 438 U.S. 586, 604 (1978), held that a sentencer may not impose the death penalty unless it considers every aspect of the defendant's character and record. *Id.* at 604. The defense presented a forensic psychologist who testified that Atkins was mildly mentally retarded. The jury heard this evidence and weighed it against the aggravating factors. The record shows that Atkins brutally beat Sara Lee Odom with a belt buckle, causing extensive injuries, and then fled the hospital and the jurisdiction. The jury found that the mitigating evidence was insufficient to outweigh the heinous nature of the crime. Therefore, the death penalty was not imposed in violation of the Eighth Amendment.
