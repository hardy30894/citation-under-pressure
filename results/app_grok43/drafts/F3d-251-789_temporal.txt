The district court's finding that Gonzalez-Caballero consented to Danelsy's removal and retention is clearly erroneous. "A finding is 'clearly erroneous' when although there is evidence to support it, the reviewing court on the entire evidence is left with the definite and firm conviction that a mistake has been committed." United States v. United States Gypsum Co., 333 U.S. 364, 395 (1948).

Consent to surrender custody must be shown by clear, voluntary, and unequivocal acts; conditional or temporary permission does not suffice. See Meyer v. Nebraska, 262 U.S. 390, 399 (1923) (recognizing the fundamental right of parents to custody "and to control the education of their own"). Gonzalez-Caballero's provision of documents and accompaniment to the airport occurred only after Mena promised immigration assistance and a prompt return; her immediate reports to Panamanian authorities and the police upon non-return demonstrate that any agreement was expressly limited. Such conditional assent cannot establish consent to permanent retention. See Hartford Fire Ins. Co. v. Wilson, 187 U.S. 467, 474 (1903) (holding that consent given upon unfulfilled conditions "is not effective").

The district court's contrary conclusion rests on disputed testimony that, viewed as a whole, leaves a definite conviction of mistake. The finding must therefore be reversed.
