ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act (RRA) and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (RUIA) grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final" decisions of the United States Railroad Retirement Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review. The Fifth Circuit, bound by its own precedent in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Board, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), held that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Manfredo Salinas's 2006 application for a disability annuity. However, this Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision and hold that the Board's denial of Salinas's request to reopen his prior claim is a final, reviewable decision.

The RRA and RUIA grant jurisdiction to review "final decisions" of the Board without explicitly defining what constitutes a "final decision." 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). In Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), this Court held that a decision by the Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare (now the Commissioner of Social Security) not to reopen a prior claim is not reviewable under the Social Security Act, absent a colorable constitutional claim. However, the Court's reasoning in Sanders was based on the specific statutory framework of the Social Security Act, which differs from the RRA and RUIA. See Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254-55 (2d Cir. 1966) (distinguishing the RRA from the Social Security Act).

The D.C. Circuit, in Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), correctly held that the RRA grants circuit courts jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations. The court reasoned that the RRA's judicial review provision, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), is broader than the Social Security Act's, and that the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim is a "final decision" within the meaning of the statute. Stovic, 826 F.3d at 504-05.

Other circuits have reached a similar conclusion. In Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991), the Eighth Circuit held that a Board decision denying a request to reopen a prior claim is reviewable, relying on Szostak, 370 F.2d at 254-55. The Second Circuit, in Szostak, had previously held that a Board decision denying a request to reopen is reviewable, reasoning that the RRA's judicial review provision is "broader than that contained in the Social Security Act." Szostak, 370 F.2d at 255.

In contrast, the majority of circuits, including the Fifth Circuit in Roberts, have held that a Board decision not to reopen a prior claim is not reviewable. See, e.g., Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999); Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995); Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990). However, these decisions rely heavily on the reasoning in Sanders, which is distinguishable.

This Court should adopt the reasoning of the D.C. and Eighth Circuits and hold that the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a final, reviewable decision. The RRA's judicial review provision is broader than the Social Security Act's, and the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim is a "final decision" within the meaning of the statute. See Stovic, 826 F.3d at 504-05; Sones, 933 F.2d at 638; Szostak, 370 F.2d at 254-55.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Roberts, while binding on that court, is not controlling here. This Court should consider the reasoning of the circuits that have held that a Board decision not to reopen a prior claim is reviewable and reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision. By doing so, this Court will ensure that the RRA is interpreted consistently with its statutory text and purpose. See, e.g., United States v. Stewart, 311 U.S. 60, 63 (1940) (statutes should be interpreted to achieve their purpose).

In conclusion, the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a final, reviewable decision. This Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision and hold that the circuit courts have jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen a prior benefits claim.
