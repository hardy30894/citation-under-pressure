The District Court's decision to invoke 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the clear statutory mandate of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Section 1407(a) explicitly requires that each action transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel" to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The language of the statute is unequivocal, leaving no room for interpretation (In re Air Crash Disaster Near Hanover, New Hampshire, 342 F.Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H.1971)).

Despite this clear directive, some courts have disregarded the statutory mandate, allowing transferee courts to transfer cases to themselves for trial under § 1404(a) (Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir.1971); In Re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation, 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3rd Cir.1982)). However, this practice is not supported by the statutory text and undermines the policy choice reflected in § 1407(a), which ensures that plaintiffs retain their right to select the forum for trial (In re Air Crash Disaster, 342 F.Supp. at 909).

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation's Rule 14(b) and the Federal Judicial Center's Manual for Complex Litigation (Third) § 21.61 (1995) have perpetuated this erroneous practice. Nevertheless, it is the duty of the courts to respect the statutory text and policy choices made by Congress (United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989)).

The Ninth Circuit's decision in this case, denying mandamus relief, was based on an incorrect assessment that the District Court's order was not "clearly erroneous as a matter of law" (Bauman v. United States Dist. Court, 557 F.2d 650, 653 (9th Cir.1977)). However, as Judge Kozinski's dissent highlights, the District Court's decision is part of an "oft-repeated error" that has developed over the years, ignoring the clear statutory command (KOZINSKI, Circuit Judge, dissenting).

The practice of allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial under § 1404(a) is not only contrary to the statutory text but also undermines the principles of venue and the rights of plaintiffs to choose their forum (Varsic v. U.S. Dist. Ct., 607 F.2d 245, 252 (9th Cir.1979); Sunshine Beauty Supplies v. U.S. Dist. Court, 872 F.2d 310, 311 (9th Cir.1989)). The courts should respect the statutory scheme and policy choices made by Congress, rather than perpetuating an erroneous practice that has developed through judicial precedent (Wash. Pub. Util. Group v. U.S. Dist. Court, 843 F.2d 319, 323 (9th Cir.1987)).

In conclusion, the District Court's decision to invoke § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial is contrary to the clear statutory mandate of § 1407(a). The courts should respect the statutory text and policy choices made by Congress, and reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision denying mandamus relief.
