**ARGUMENT**

**I. SECTION 523(a)(2)(A) RENDERS NONDISCHARGEABLE ALL DEBT ARISING FROM FRAUD, INCLUDING TREBLE DAMAGES, ATTORNEY’S FEES, AND COSTS AWARDED UNDER STATE CONSUMER PROTECTION LAW.**

The plain text of 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A) excepts from discharge “any debt … for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by … false pretenses, a false representation, or actual fraud.” The phrase “any debt” unambiguously encompasses the entirety of a liability arising from fraudulent conduct, including both compensatory and non-compensatory awards. *See Cohen v. de la Cruz*, 523 U.S. 213, 218-19 (1998). Because Cohen’s fraud resulted in an award of treble damages, attorney’s fees, and costs under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act, N.J.S.A. 56:8-1 *et seq.*, the entire judgment is nondischargeable.

**A. The Bankruptcy Code’s Text and Structure Extend Nondischargeability to All Damages Flowing from Fraud.**

Section 523(a)(2)(A) does not limit dischargeability to the precise value of money or property obtained by fraud. Instead, it uses the broad phrase “to the extent obtained by,” which encompasses all consequences of the fraud. *Cohen*, 523 U.S. at 218-19. The Court in *Cohen* expressly rejected the argument that only the portion of a debt equal to the amount directly taken is nondischargeable, holding that “any debt” means the whole debt, not merely its compensatory component. *Id.* at 220. As the Court explained, “the language of § 523(a)(2)(A) treats a debt ‘for’ money obtained by fraud as including all liability arising from the fraudulent conduct, not merely the value of the money obtained.” *Id.*

Indeed, *Cohen* squarely resolves this case. There, the tenants were awarded treble damages under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act based on the debtor’s overcharging of rent. *Id.* at 215-16. The Court affirmed that such treble damages are part of the debt “for” money obtained by fraud and are therefore nondischargeable. *Id.* at 221-22. Because the debt here arises from identical conduct under the same state statute, *Cohen* controls.

**B. Punitive Damages and Statutory Penalties Are Not Exempted from Nondischargeability.**

The debtor may argue that treble damages are punitive and thus dischargeable, but *Cohen* forecloses that distinction. The Court reasoned that § 523(a)(2)(A) focuses on the debtor’s fraudulent acquisition of property, not on the label attached to the resulting award. *Cohen*, 523 U.S. at 219-20. A debtor who commits fraud should not receive a fresh start while the victim bears the costs of deterrence. *Id.* at 221 (citing *Grogan v. Garner*, 498 U.S. 279, 287 (1991)). The “fresh start” policy is reserved for the “honest but unfortunate debtor,” not for those who perpetrate fraud. *Grogan*, 498 U.S. at 286-87.

Moreover, treble damages under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act are mandatory following a finding of fraud, N.J.S.A. 56:8-19, thus representing a fixed consequence of the debtor’s misconduct. As the Third Circuit recognized below, such a codified award is entirely foreseeable to the fraudfeasor and is properly classified as debt “for” the fraud itself. *See In re Cohen*, 106 F.3d 525, 529 (3d Cir. 1997), *aff’d*, 523 U.S. 213 (1998). The foreseeability of mandatory penalties underscores that they are part of the debt incurred by the fraudulent act, not a separate punitive exaction.

**C. Attorney’s Fees and Costs Are Likewise Nondischargeable.**

Section 523(a)(2)(A) encompasses attorney’s fees and costs awarded as a result of the fraudulent conduct. *Cohen*, 523 U.S. at 222 n.5. The debtor’s fraud forced the tenants to litigate to vindicate their rights; the fees and costs are thus “incurred in connection with the fraud” and are nondischargeable. *Id.*

**II. THE RECORD AMPLY DEMONSTRATES THAT COHEN COMMITTED ACTUAL FRAUD WITHIN THE MEANING OF § 523(a)(2)(A).**

The bankruptcy court found that Cohen implicitly represented that he was charging lawful rents, that he recklessly disregarded the truth of that representation, and that the tenants justifiably relied to their detriment. *In re Cohen*, 185 B.R. 171, 175-76 (Bankr. D.N.J. 1994). The Third Circuit affirmed, holding that fraudulent intent may be inferred from reckless indifference. *Cohen*, 106 F.3d at 529. This Court has endorsed that standard, noting that actual fraud under § 523(a)(2)(A) is not limited to misrepresentations but includes any fraudulent scheme. *Cohen*, 523 U.S. at 217-18.

Cohen’s claim that he made no affirmative misrepresentation is irrelevant. Fraud by silence or omission satisfies § 523(a)(2)(A) when the debtor has a duty to disclose. *Id.* at 218. Moreover, his deliberate ignorance of rent control laws—despite multiple opportunities to learn the law—constitutes reckless conduct that establishes the requisite intent. *Cohen*, 106 F.3d at 529.

**CONCLUSION**

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed. Section 523(a)(2)(A) renders nondischargeable the entire debt arising from Cohen’s fraud, including treble damages, attorney’s fees, and costs.
