The Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act of 1980 (ANILCA) grants the National Park Service (NPS) authority to regulate activities within the boundaries of National Park System units in Alaska, including those on state-owned lands and waters. The plain text of ANILCA § 103(c) supports this interpretation.

The statute provides that "only those lands within the boundaries of [conservation system units] which are public lands ... are deemed to be included as a portion of such unit." 16 U.S.C. § 3103(c). However, this provision does not preclude the NPS from regulating activities on non-public lands within the unit. The language of § 103(c) does not explicitly limit the NPS's regulatory authority to public lands.

In fact, the Supreme Court has long recognized that the federal government has the authority to regulate activities on non-federal lands within national parks. In United States v. Gettysburg Electric Ry. Co., 160 U.S. 668 (1896), the Court held that Congress has the power to establish national parks and regulate activities within them, even if the land is not federally owned. The Court stated that "the power to preserve and protect the Gettysburg battlefield ... is not questioned" and that "the power to do so is clearly within the power of the Federal government." Id. at 682.

Similarly, in Camfield v. United States, 167 U.S. 518 (1897), the Court upheld the federal government's authority to regulate private land within a national forest reserve. The Court held that "the government has, with respect to the public domain, the rights of an ordinary proprietor, to maintain its possession and to prosecute trespassers." Id. at 525-26.

These precedents establish that the federal government has the authority to regulate activities within national parks, including those on non-federal lands. ANILCA § 103(c) does not limit this authority, and the NPS's regulation of hovercraft use on state-owned waters within the Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve is a valid exercise of its authority.

The petitioner's argument that ANILCA § 103(c) prohibits the NPS from regulating activities on state-owned lands and waters within the preserve is unpersuasive. The statute's language does not support this interpretation, and the precedents cited above establish the federal government's authority to regulate activities within national parks.

Therefore, the NPS's regulation of hovercraft use on state-owned waters within the Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve is lawful, and the district court's grant of summary judgment in favor of the respondents should be affirmed.
