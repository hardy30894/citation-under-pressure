Argument

The Tax Court erred in denying the Davis estate a marital deduction under Internal Revenue Code section 2056(b)(7) for the trust income passing to Evelyn L. Davis. To qualify for a QTIP deduction, the surviving spouse must have a "qualifying income interest for life," meaning she must be "entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals." I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The Amendment to the Declaration of Trust satisfies this requirement.

The language of the Amendment, specifically paragraph 2, indicates that the trustee shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse "all of the net income from the trust estate" in quarter annual or more frequent installments. Although the trustee has discretion to determine what is "proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare" of the surviving spouse, this discretion does not limit the spouse's entitlement to all the income. The trustee's discretion is merely a guideline for the application of the income, not a limitation on the spouse's right to receive it.

The court's decision in Commissioner v. O'Keeffe, 118 F.2d 639 (1st Cir. 1941), supports this interpretation. In O'Keeffe, the court held that a trust provision requiring the trustee to pay the income to the beneficiary "as she may need it" did not limit the beneficiary's right to the income, as the trustee's discretion was not absolute. Similarly, in the present case, the trustee's discretion under the Amendment is not absolute, as it is guided by the surviving spouse's "accustomed manner of living." See also, Merchants National Bank v. Commissioner, 320 U.S. 256 (1943) (holding that a trust provision giving the trustee discretion to invade principal for the beneficiary's "support and maintenance" did not limit the beneficiary's right to the income).

Furthermore, the fact that the trustee may consider "other income or resources of the beneficiary" in making distributions does not negate the surviving spouse's entitlement to all the income. As the court held in Geyer v. Bookwalter, 193 F. Supp. 57 (W.D. Mo. 1961), a trust provision allowing the trustee to consider the beneficiary's other resources in determining distributions did not limit the beneficiary's right to the income.

Therefore, the Davis estate is entitled to a marital deduction under section 2056(b)(7) for the trust income passing to Evelyn L. Davis, and the Tax Court's decision to the contrary should be reversed.
