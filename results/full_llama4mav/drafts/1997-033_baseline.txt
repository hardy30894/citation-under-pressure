The District Court's decision to invoke 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the clear statutory mandate of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Section 1407(a) explicitly requires that each action transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel" to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The language of the statute is unequivocal, leaving no room for interpretation (In re Air Crash Disaster Near Hanover, New Hampshire, 342 F.Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H.1971)).

Despite this clear directive, the Courts of Appeals for the Second and Third Circuits have condoned the practice of transferee courts invoking § 1404(a) to retain cases for trial (Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir.1971); In Re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation, 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3rd Cir.1982)). However, as Judge Kozinski's dissent in the lower court opinion noted, this practice is a clear departure from the statutory text and has been justified on grounds of policy or convenience rather than statutory fidelity.

The statutory scheme under § 1407 reflects an important policy choice: cases may be transferred to remote locations, but only for the duration of pretrial procedures. When it comes to conducting a trial, plaintiffs retain their ancient right to select the forum (Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach, 61 F.3d 911, 916 (9th Cir. 1995) (Kozinski, J., dissenting)). Allowing transferee courts to invoke § 1404(a) to retain cases for trial undermines this policy choice and grants district judges unwarranted discretion to subvert the Panel's responsibility.

The Ninth Circuit's decision in this case perpetuates this erroneous practice, despite the clear statutory language to the contrary. This Court should grant review to correct this oft-repeated error and reaffirm the statutory mandate that cases transferred under § 1407(a) be remanded to their original district for trial. By doing so, this Court will ensure that the important policy underlying § 1407 is respected and that district judges are held to the statutory limits of their authority.
