**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON APPELLANT’S ADA AND MHRA CLAIMS BECAUSE PINE LAWN REGARDED HIM AS DISABLED.**

To establish a claim under the Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) and the Missouri Human Rights Act (“MHRA”), a plaintiff must show, *inter alia*, that the employer regarded him as disabled. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 12102(2)(C) (ADA); *Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.010(12)* (MHRA). The district court erred in concluding that Appellant failed to raise a genuine issue of material fact on this element. Pine Lawn’s own actions—particularly Chief Hardy’s memoranda and the Mayor and Board’s termination decision—demonstrate that it regarded Appellant as disabled.

Under the ADA and MHRA, an individual is “regarded as” disabled if the employer perceived him as having a physical impairment that substantially limits a major life activity. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 12102(2)(C); *Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.010(12)*. The Supreme Court has held that an employer’s belief that an employee cannot perform a particular job due to a perceived impairment suffices to establish “regarded as” disabled. *See School Bd. of Nassau Cty. v. Arline*, 480 U.S. 273, 284 (1987) (citing *29 C.F.R. § 1630.2(j)(3)(i)* (1985)). Here, Chief Hardy’s memoranda explicitly relied on Dr. Mirkin’s assessment that Appellant had degenerative disk disease and would likely need to seek a different occupation. *See* Hardy’s Sept. 29, 1999, and Mar. 17, 2000, memoranda. The Mayor and Board’s termination decision, which accepted Hardy’s recommendation, further evidences Pine Lawn’s belief that Appellant could not perform the duties of a police officer due to his perceived disability. *See* *EEOC v. Sears, Roebuck & Co.*, 417 F. Supp. 242, 250 (N.D. Ill. 1976) (employer’s reliance on medical opinion in terminating employee supports “regarded as” disabled claim).

The district court’s reliance on *Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc.*, 527 U.S. 471 (1999), is misplaced. *Sutton* addressed whether mitigating measures must be considered in determining whether an individual is disabled, not whether an employer regarded an individual as disabled. *Id.* at 482-83. Pine Lawn’s perception of Appellant’s disability, not his actual physical condition, is the relevant inquiry. *See* *School Bd. of Nassau Cty. v. Arline*, 480 U.S. at 284.

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON APPELLANT’S WORKERS’ COMPENSATION RETALIATION CLAIM BECAUSE PINE LAWN’S SOVEREIGN IMMUNITY DEFENSE FAILS.**

The district court held that Missouri’s sovereign immunity statute barred Appellant’s workers’ compensation retaliation claim. *See* Mo. Rev. Stat. § 537.600. However, this statute does not immunize municipal employers from liability for violations of workers’ compensation laws. *See* *State ex rel. City of St. Louis v. Gaertner*, 616 S.W.2d 81, 83 (Mo. Ct. App. 1981) (municipal corporations are not immune from liability for workers’ compensation claims). The Missouri Supreme Court has long held that municipal corporations are subject to the same liabilities as private employers under the workers’ compensation statutes. *See* *City of St. Louis v. Industrial Comm’n*, 325 Mo. 119, 28 S.W.2d 329, 331 (1930). Pine Lawn’s sovereign immunity defense is therefore without merit.

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON APPELLANT’S INTENTIONAL INFLICTION OF EMOTIONAL DISTRESS CLAIM BECAUSE PINE LAWN’S CONDUCT WAS EXTREME AND OUTRAGEOUS.**

The district court held that Missouri’s sovereign immunity statute barred Appellant’s intentional infliction of emotional distress claim. However, this claim is not barred by sovereign immunity. *See* *State ex rel. State Highway Comm’n v. Mummert*, 623 S.W.2d 77, 80 (Mo. Ct. App. 1981) (sovereign immunity does not bar claims for intentional torts). To establish a claim for intentional infliction of emotional distress, Appellant must show that Pine Lawn’s conduct was extreme and outrageous. *See* *Ford v. Revlon, Inc.*, 534 S.W.2d 685, 687 (Mo. Ct. App. 1976). Chief Hardy’s memoranda and the Mayor and Board’s termination decision, which relied on Appellant’s workers’ compensation history and perceived disability, are sufficient to raise a genuine issue of material fact on this element. *See* *Restatement (Second) of Torts* § 46 cmt. d (1965) (employer’s use of false or exaggerated reasons for termination may be extreme and outrageous conduct).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in granting summary judgment on Appellant’s ADA, MHRA, workers’ compensation retaliation, and intentional infliction of emotional distress claims. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
