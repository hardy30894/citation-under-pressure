## ARGUMENT

The Fifth Circuit correctly dismissed Salinas’s petition for lack of jurisdiction. Under the Railroad Retirement Act and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), a court of appeals may review only “final decisions of the Board on the merits of a claim for benefits after administrative appeals have been exhausted.” *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003). A Board decision declining to reopen a prior benefits claim is not a final decision on the merits and therefore falls outside the scope of § 355(f).

### I. The Plain Language of § 355(f) Limits Review to Merits Decisions

Section 355(f) provides that a petitioner may obtain review of “any final decision of the Board” in a circuit court. But this Court has consistently interpreted that language in light of the administrative scheme. The Board’s reopening decisions are collateral to the merits of a benefits claim. They do not determine entitlement to benefits; they merely decide whether to revisit a prior final decision. *See Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 140–41. The Fifth Circuit in *Roberts* joined the majority of circuits holding that “we have no jurisdiction to review the Board’s decision not to reopen a prior claim for benefits.” *Id.* at 140; *see also Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999); *Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995); *Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990); *Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 708 F.2d 250, 254–55 (7th Cir. 1983).

### II. The Board’s Reopening Regulations Confirm the Non-Reviewable Nature of Reopening Decisions

The Board’s regulations permit reopening only within limited timeframes and for specific reasons, such as new and material evidence or administrative error. 20 C.F.R. § 261.2. A decision not to reopen is discretionary and does not constitute a “final decision” on the underlying claim. *See Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 107–09 (1977) (holding that a refusal to reopen a Social Security disability claim is not a final decision reviewable under 42 U.S.C. § 405(g)). The same principle applies here. The Board’s reopening decision is not a merits determination; it is an administrative decision about whether to revisit a prior final decision. Judicial review of such decisions would undermine the finality of Board determinations and the efficient administration of the benefits system.

### III. The Fifth Circuit’s Precedent Controls and Is Consistent with the Majority View

Salinas invited the Fifth Circuit to follow the D.C. Circuit’s decision in *Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), which held that the court had jurisdiction to review a Board decision denying a reopening request. But *Stovic* represents a minority view and is inconsistent with the weight of authority. The Fifth Circuit in *Roberts* expressly considered and rejected the reasoning of the minority circuits, including the Second Circuit’s decision in *Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 253 (2d Cir. 1966), and the Eighth Circuit’s decision in *Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 933 F.2d 636 (8th Cir. 1991). *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141. The court found the majority view “persuasive” and adopted it. *Id.*

Under the rule of orderliness, the Fifth Circuit was bound by *Roberts* and could not overrule it. *See Vaughan v. Anderson Reg’l Med. Ctr.*, 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir.), *cert. denied*, 138 S. Ct. 101 (2017). Salinas’s invitation to follow *Stovic* was therefore properly rejected.

### IV. Salinas Exhausted His Administrative Remedies Only on the Reopening Issue, Not on the Merits of the 2006 Claim

Salinas did not pursue administrative review of the denial of his 2006 application. That denial became final in 2007. He later sought reopening, but the Board’s decision on reopening is not a decision on the merits of the underlying claim. *See Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 140. Because Salinas never exhausted his administrative remedies on the merits of the 2006 claim, there is no final decision on that claim for the court to review. The Board’s reopening decision is the only decision before the court, and it is not reviewable.

### Conclusion

The Fifth Circuit correctly dismissed Salinas’s petition for lack of jurisdiction. The Board’s decision not to reopen a prior benefits claim is not a final decision on the merits under § 355(f). This Court should affirm the dismissal.
