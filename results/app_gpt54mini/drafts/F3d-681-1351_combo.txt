**ARGUMENT**

The Board erred in holding that the intervening applications in Harari’s chain satisfy 35 U.S.C. § 120. Section 120 grants the benefit of an earlier filing date only when each later application is “filed before the patenting or abandonment of or termination of proceedings on the first application” and “contains or is amended to contain a specific reference to the earlier filed application.” 35 U.S.C. § 120. The statute is strictly construed because a patentee who claims an earlier date seeks a priority advantage over intervening rights. *Miller v. Brass Co.*, 104 U.S. 350, 355-56 (1881); *Eames v. Andrews*, 122 U.S. 40, 65-66 (1887). Harari did not carry that burden.

The Supreme Court has long held that priority depends on what the application itself discloses and preserves. A later application may obtain the earlier date only if it is sufficiently connected to the original disclosure and does not introduce new matter. *Schriber-Schroth Co. v. Cleveland Trust Co.*, 311 U.S. 211, 217-18 (1940); *Mackay Radio & Tel. Co. v. Radio Corp. of Am.*, 306 U.S. 86, 94 (1939). That rule is fatal here. The intervening applications must themselves provide a legally effective reference chain to the “Multi-State EEprom Read and Write Circuits and Techniques” application. If they do not, then the '880 application is limited to the 1996 filing date of the '708 application, and Hollmer’s priority prevails.

The Board relied on *Harari I* for the proposition that ambiguity in the original filing could be cured by later-filed papers. But *Harari I* was expressly confined to the “initial filing stage” and turned on whether “the identity of the incorporated reference is clear to a reasonable examiner in light of the documents presented.” *Harari v. Hollmer*, 602 F.3d 1348, 1352-54 (Fed. Cir. 2010). That holding does not eliminate the statutory requirement that each application in the chain satisfy § 120. Nor does it permit the Board to treat a later clarification as if it had been present from the outset. The Court has repeatedly rejected attempts to enlarge a patent’s effective disclosure by hindsight reconstruction. *Sutter v. Robinson*, 119 U.S. 530, 541-42 (1886); *Crown Cork & Seal Co. v. Aluminum Stopper Co.*, 108 U.S. 366, 371-72 (1883).

Here, the critical incorporation statement in the '566 application said the relevant reference was an application “filed on the same day as the present application.” That statement was false once the matter is analyzed through the later chain, because the issue is not whether the '579 application could be identified years later, but whether the intervening applications themselves unmistakably incorporated it in a manner that preserved continuity. A patent applicant must “point out and distinctly claim” what he seeks to protect, and the public is entitled to rely on the record as filed. *Merrill v. Yeomans*, 94 U.S. 568, 573-74 (1877); *Westinghouse v. Boyden Power-Brake Co.*, 170 U.S. 537, 561-62 (1898). The intervening papers here did not do so with the clarity required by § 120.

Nor can Harari salvage the chain by invoking liberal rules of construction. The Supreme Court has never permitted a party to use later documents to rewrite an earlier application’s priority posture after the fact. *Hobbs v. Beach*, 180 U.S. 383, 394-95 (1901); *Topliff v. Topliff*, 145 U.S. 156, 174-75 (1892). The question is not whether a skilled reader might eventually infer what Harari intended; it is whether the priority chain was properly established in the applications themselves. *Goodyear Dental Vulcanite Co. v. Davis*, 102 U.S. 222, 227-28 (1880). Because the Board answered that question by extending *Harari I* beyond its narrow holding, its decision cannot stand.

The Board’s judgment should therefore be reversed. Harari failed to prove uninterrupted compliance with § 120 from the '566 application to the '880 application. Without that chain, the '880 application does not reach back to 1989, and Hollmer’s earlier invention date controls. *Miller v. Brass Co.*, 104 U.S. at 355-56; *Schriber-Schroth Co.*, 311 U.S. at 217-18.
