**Argument**

**I. The Equal Protection Clause Protects Individuals from Arbitrary Discrimination, Even in the Absence of a Formal Class.**

The Fourteenth Amendment’s Equal Protection Clause guarantees that no state shall "deny to any person within its jurisdiction the equal protection of the laws." *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. 562, 565 (2000). While this guarantee traditionally requires a plaintiff to demonstrate that the government treated members of a protected class differently, the Supreme Court has recognized an exception for "class of one" claims. Under this doctrine, a plaintiff may allege a violation of the Equal Protection Clause by asserting that the state treated them differently from others without a legitimate state purpose. *Olech v. Village of Willowbrook*, 220 F.3d 493, 495 (7th Cir. 2000). The Seventh Circuit has held that a plaintiff can allege an equal protection violation by asserting that state action was motivated solely by a "spiteful effort to 'get' him for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective." *Id.* (quoting *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. 562, 565 (2000)).

**II. Olech’s Complaint Sufficiently Alleges a "Class of One" Violation.**

Grace Olech’s complaint sufficiently alleges a "class of one" violation by demonstrating that the Village of Willowbrook treated her differently from other property owners without a rational basis. Olech sought to connect her property to the municipal water supply. The Village conditioned this connection on her granting a 33-foot easement. However, the record shows that the Village required only a 15-foot easement from other property owners seeking access to the same water supply. This disparate treatment, without a legitimate state objective justifying the additional 18-foot demand, satisfies the "class of one" standard. *Olech*, 220 F.3d at 495. Because Olech alleges that the Village’s actions were motivated by a desire to spite her rather than a legitimate governmental interest, she has stated a cognizable claim under the Equal Protection Clause. *Id.*
