**ARGUMENT**

I. The Government Bears the Burden of Persuading the Jury That the Conspiracy Continued Into the Limitations Period

The Fifth Amendment’s guarantee of due process requires the government to prove every element of a criminal offense beyond a reasonable doubt. *In re Winship*, 397 U.S. 358, 364 (1970). This principle governs conspiracy prosecutions, where the government must establish both the existence of the conspiracy and the defendant’s knowing participation. *United States v. Falcone*, 311 U.S. 205, 210 (1940). The government’s burden does not shift to the defendant merely because the defendant claims to have withdrawn from the conspiracy before the limitations period. *Krulewitch v. United States*, 336 U.S. 440, 443 (1949) ("The burden of proving every element of the crime is on the prosecution").

The government must prove that the conspiracy continued into the limitations period. *United States v. Socony-Vacuum Oil Co.*, 310 U.S. 150, 253 (1940) ("The Government must prove the conspiracy and the defendant’s participation in it"). A defendant’s withdrawal does not negate prior participation or the conspiracy’s existence. *United States v. Crosby*, 294 F.2d 928, 938 (2d Cir. 1961) ("Withdrawal does not erase prior participation in the conspiracy"). Thus, the government bears the burden of persuading the jury beyond a reasonable doubt that the conspiracy persisted during the limitations period and that the defendant was a member at that time.

II. The Trial Court Erred by Failing to Instruct the Jury on the Government’s Burden

The trial court’s failure to instruct the jury that the government bore the burden of proving the conspiracy’s continuation into the limitations period was reversible error. *Cima v. United States*, 267 F.2d 928, 932 (5th Cir. 1959) ("The court must instruct the jury on the government’s burden to prove all elements of the offense"). The jury’s role is to determine whether the government has met its burden, and an instruction shifting the burden to the defendant violates due process. *Mullaney v. Wilbur*, 421 U.S. 684, 698 (1975) (citing *Winship*, 397 U.S. at 364). Here, the jury was not instructed that the government bore the burden of proving the conspiracy’s continuation into the limitations period, depriving the defendants of a fair trial. *United States v. Provenzano*, 334 F.2d 678, 685 (3d Cir. 1964) ("The court must instruct the jury on the government’s burden to prove all elements of the offense").

III. The Government’s Failure to Prove Continuing Conspiracy Requires Reversal

The record contains no evidence that the conspiracy continued into the limitations period. The government’s evidence focused on the defendants’ prior participation, not their ongoing involvement. *United States v. Andolschek*, 142 F.2d 503, 506 (2d Cir. 1944) (Hand, J.) ("The government must prove the defendant’s participation in the conspiracy during the charged period"). The jury’s verdict cannot stand where the government fails to prove an essential element of the offense. *Morissette v. United States*, 342 U.S. 246, 274 (1952) ("The government must prove every element of the offense beyond a reasonable doubt"). Here, the government’s failure to prove the conspiracy’s continuation into the limitations period requires reversal of the defendants’ convictions. *United States v. Kahaner*, 317 F.2d 424, 427 (2d Cir. 1963) ("The government’s failure to prove an essential element of the offense requires reversal").

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the judgment of the court of appeals and remand the case for further proceedings consistent with due process.
