The district court exceeded its authority by retaining this action for trial. Section 1407(a) empowers the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation to transfer civil actions sharing common questions of fact "to any district for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings." 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). The statute then commands that "[e]ach action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." Id. (emphasis added). This directive is "clear" and "mandatory." See In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litig., 685 F.2d 810, 820 n.7 (3d Cir. 1982).

Section 1404(a) supplies no authority to circumvent that command. The transferee court's power under § 1407(a) is expressly limited to pretrial proceedings; once those proceedings end, the statute requires remand to the transferor district. Allowing a transferee judge to invoke the general venue statute to assign the case to itself for trial would nullify the Panel's exclusive authority to order transfers and the statutory obligation to remand. See Bauman v. United States Dist. Court, 557 F.2d 650, 654 (9th Cir. 1977) (recognizing that § 1407 transfers are confined to pretrial matters).

The decisions permitting self-transfer rest on policy considerations rather than statutory text. Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971), and similar cases acknowledged that nothing in §§ 1404(a) or 1407(a) "directly allows" a transferee court to retain a case for trial, yet departed from the statute's plain terms. Such departures are foreclosed. When Congress has spoken with precision, courts must implement the enacted language rather than substitute their own views of efficiency. See, e.g., Wash. Pub. Util. Group v. United States Dist. Court, 843 F.2d 319, 326 (9th Cir. 1988).

The Panel's Rule 14(b) cannot enlarge the authority granted by statute. An administrative rule is invalid to the extent it conflicts with the governing statute. The result here is that the Arizona court was required to request remand, and the judgment must be reversed.
