**ARGUMENT**

I. THE OCSLA EXTENDS LONGSHORE ACT COVERAGE ONLY TO INJURIES OCCURRING ON THE OUTER CONTINENTAL SHELF ITSELF.

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that § 1333(b) of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act ("OCSLA") extends Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act ("LHWCA") coverage to injuries occurring on land where there is a "substantial nexus" to extractive operations on the shelf. *Valladolid v. Pacific Operations Offshore, LLP*, 604 F.3d 1134, 1143 (9th Cir. 2010). This interpretation contravenes the plain text and structure of the OCSLA, as well as controlling Supreme Court precedent.

A. The Text and Structure of the OCSLA Require a Situs-of-Injury Requirement.

Section 1333(b) of the OCSLA provides that LHWCA coverage extends to "the disability or death of an employee resulting from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf for the purpose of exploring for, developing, removing, or transporting by pipeline the natural resources of the outer Continental Shelf." 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) (emphasis added). The phrase "occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf" unambiguously ties coverage to injuries that occur *on* the outer Continental Shelf. *See generally* *Russello v. United States*, 464 U.S. 16, 23 (1983) ("[W]here Congress includes particular language in one section of a statute but omits it in another section of the same Act, it is generally presumed that Congress acts intentionally and purposely in the disparate inclusion or exclusion.").

The Ninth Circuit’s "substantial nexus" test ignores the statutory text’s focus on the *location* of the operations, not the *nexus* between the injury and those operations. *Valladolid*, 604 F.3d at 1143. The statute does not extend coverage to injuries that merely *result from* extractive operations; it extends coverage to injuries that occur *as a result of operations conducted on the shelf*. The distinction is critical. *See Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs*, 877 F.2d 356, 362 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc) ("The plain language of § 1333(b) requires that the injury occur on the outer Continental Shelf.").

B. Supreme Court Precedent Supports a Situs-of-Injury Requirement.

The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized that OCSLA coverage is geographically limited. In *Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire*, 477 U.S. 207 (1986), the Court explained that "Congress determined that the general scope of OCSLA’s coverage . . . would be determined principally by locale, not by the status of the individual injured or killed." *Id.* at 219. Although the Court’s footnote regarding § 1333(b) was dictum, it reflects the Court’s understanding that OCSLA coverage is tied to the outer Continental Shelf itself. *Id.* at 219 n.2 ("Only one provision of OCSLA superimposes a status requirement on the otherwise determinative OCSLA situs requirement; § 1333(b)...").

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Curtis v. Schlumberger Offshore Service, Inc.*, 849 F.2d 805 (3d Cir. 1988), is misplaced. The Third Circuit’s "but for" test conflates causation with coverage. *Id.* at 811. The Supreme Court has rejected such an expansive interpretation of OCSLA coverage. *See Tallentire*, 477 U.S. at 219 ("[T]he general scope of OCSLA’s coverage . . . would be determined principally by locale.").

II. THE LHWCA DOES NOT COVER INJURIES OCCURRING ON NON-MARITIME SITUSES, EVEN IF THE EMPLOYEE IS ENGAGED IN MARITIME EMPLOYMENT.

The Ninth Circuit also erred in holding that La Conchita, Pacific Operations’ onshore processing facility, qualifies as a maritime situs under § 903(a) of the LHWCA. *Valladolid*, 604 F.3d at 1159. The LHWCA’s coverage is limited to injuries occurring on "navigable waters of the United States" or on an "adjoining area customarily used by an employer in loading, unloading, repairing, or building a vessel." 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). La Conchita does not meet this standard.

A. La Conchita Is Not an "Adjoining Area" Under § 903(a).

The Ninth Circuit’s "functional relationship" test for determining whether a facility qualifies as an "adjoining area" is inconsistent with the LHWCA’s text and precedent. *See Brady-Hamilton Stevedore Co. v. Herron*, 568 F.2d 137, 141 (9th Cir. 1978). The Supreme Court has made clear that the LHWCA’s coverage is geographically limited to maritime situses. *See Director, Office of Workers’ Comp. Programs v. Perini North River Assocs.*, 459 U.S. 297, 305 (1983) ("The Act’s coverage is limited to injuries occurring on navigable waters or on an adjoining area customarily used for maritime activity.").

La Conchita is separated from the nearest navigable water by a highway and railroad tracks, and it has no direct access to any pier, dock, or loading facility. *Valladolid*, 604 F.3d at 1159. The scrap metal handling that occurred there was not an "intermediate step[]" in moving cargo between ship and land transportation, as required by *P.C. Pfeiffer Co. v. Ford*, 444 U.S. 69, 83 (1979). Instead, the scrap metal was transported three miles from the Casitas Pass Pier—a maritime situs—before being dumped at La Conchita. *Id.* at 1160.

B. The Ninth Circuit’s Expansion of LHWCA Coverage Is Contrary to Congressional Intent.

The LHWCA was designed to provide compensation for workers injured in inherently maritime occupations. *See Northeast Marine Terminal Co. v. Caputo*, 432 U.S. 249, 264 (1977). The Ninth Circuit’s holding would extend LHWCA coverage to virtually any onshore facility connected to offshore operations, a result Congress did not intend. *See Mills*, 877 F.2d at 362 ("Congress did not intend to cover workers injured on land, no matter how closely their work is connected to maritime activities.").

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit’s decision and hold that the OCSLA extends LHWCA coverage only to injuries occurring on the outer Continental Shelf itself. The Court should also affirm the denial of LHWCA benefits because La Conchita is not a maritime situs under § 903(a).
