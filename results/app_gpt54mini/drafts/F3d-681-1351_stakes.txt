**ARGUMENT**

The Board erred in holding that the ’880 application is entitled to the April 13, 1989 filing date of the ’566 application. Section 120 requires that each application in the chain “contain or be amended to contain a specific reference to the earlier filed application.” 35 U.S.C. § 120. The Supreme Court has explained that the benefit of an earlier filing date is available only when the statutory requirements are satisfied, because “[e]ach of the applications in the chain must comply with the statutory requirements.” *Medtronic CoreValve, LLC v. Edwards Lifesciences Corp.*, 741 F.3d 1359, 1364 (Fed. Cir. 2014); see also *In re Gosteli*, 872 F.2d 1008, 1010–11 (Fed. Cir. 1989) (strictly enforcing § 120’s requirements).

Here, the intervening ’838 and ’768 applications did not make the necessary specific reference to the ’579 application by a clear incorporation by reference. The original ’566 application’s language—“one entitled ‘Multi-State EEprom Read and Write Circuits and Techniques,’ filed on the same day as the present application”—was ambiguous on its face. J.A. 1204. As this Court held in *Harari v. Hollmer*, the proper inquiry is whether “the identity of the incorporated reference is clear to a reasonable examiner in light of the documents presented.” 602 F.3d 1350, 1352–53 (Fed. Cir. 2010). That holding, however, was expressly limited to the “initial filing stage” and to whether the preliminary amendment introduced new matter. Id. at 1352–54. It did not excuse compliance with § 120 in later applications in the chain.

The Board’s remand decision improperly treated the previous appeal as resolving the § 120 issue. It did not. A conclusion that the ’579 application could be identified for purposes of avoiding a new-matter objection in the ’880 application does not establish that the ’838 and ’768 applications each “contain” a legally sufficient specific reference to the ’566 application and the incorporated subject matter. Section 120 requires continuity through each link in the chain. *In re Chu*, 66 F.3d 292, 295–96 (Fed. Cir. 1995) (“To obtain the benefit of the filing date of an earlier application, the later application must satisfy the requirements of § 120.”); *Medtronic CoreValve*, 741 F.3d at 1364.

Nor can the ’880 application rely on the ’708 application’s undisputed disclosure of the ’579 application to cure defects in the earlier links. The statute does not permit a later application to reach backward through an invalid chain and resurrect priority that the intermediate applications failed to preserve. *In re Daniels*, 144 F.3d 1452, 1456–57 (Fed. Cir. 1998) (priority depends on whether the chain of applications satisfies the statutory requirements for each application); *In re Rosuvastatin Calcium Patent Litig.*, 703 F.3d 511, 516–17 (Fed. Cir. 2012) (earlier filing date unavailable absent compliance with § 120). The Board’s contrary approach effectively reads the specific-reference requirement out of the statute.

The Board also erred to the extent it relied on the copied incorporation statement itself as sufficient. Incorporation by reference is a “drafting tool,” not a substitute for statutory compliance. *Advanced Display Sys., Inc. v. Kent State Univ.*, 212 F.3d 1272, 1282 (Fed. Cir. 2000). An incorporated reference must be identified with enough specificity that a skilled reader can locate and understand the incorporated material. *Id.* at 1282–83. Here, the copied statement referred to an application “filed on the same day as the present application,” a description that ceased to be accurate as the chain progressed. J.A. 1204. Because the later applications merely repeated that inaccurate language, they did not provide the clear, specific reference that § 120 demands. *In re Wertheim*, 646 F.2d 527, 537–38 (C.C.P.A. 1981) (specific reference must be unmistakable and satisfy the statute as written).

Finally, this Court should reject any attempt to expand *Harari I* into a binding determination that the ’579 application was properly incorporated in every application in the chain. *Harari I* addressed only whether the preliminary amendment in the ’880 application added new matter; it did not decide entitlement to the 1989 filing date under § 120. 602 F.3d at 1353–54. The Board’s decision on remand therefore rested on a legal error: it assumed that a limited new-matter holding supplied the missing statutory continuity. It did not. Because Harari failed to establish uninterrupted compliance with § 120, the ’880 application is entitled at most to the December 20, 1996 filing date of the ’708 application. The Board’s judgment should be reversed.
