**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE JURY’S VERDICT ON ROBINSON-PATMAN LIABILITY WAS NOT SUSTAINED BY SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE AND MUST BE REVERSED.**

A.  To establish a prima facie case under § 2(a) of the Robinson-Patman Act, 15 U.S.C. § 13(a), Stelwagon was required to prove: (1) that Tarmac discriminated in price between different purchasers of commodities of like grade and quality; (2) that the commodities were sold for use, consumption, or resale within the United States; (3) that the effect of the discrimination may be substantially to lessen competition or tend to create a monopoly in any line of commerce; and (4) that the discrimination occurred in the context of contemporaneous sales. *J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp.*, 451 U.S. 557, 562 (1981); *FTC v. Morton Salt Co.*, 334 U.S. 37, 46–47 (1948).

B.  The record contains no competent evidence that Tarmac sold MAPs to Standard or Celotex at prices lower than those charged Stelwagon for contemporaneous sales of like products.  Stelwagon’s damages expert conceded that he could not identify any specific invoice showing a lower price to Standard or Celotex.  Without such evidence, no reasonable jury could find that Tarmac engaged in price discrimination.  *See Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat, Inc.*, 429 U.S. 477, 486 (1977) (“[T]he plaintiff must prove *antitrust injury*, which is to say injury of the type the antitrust laws were intended to prevent and that flows from that which makes defendants’ acts unlawful.”).

C.  Even assuming arguendo that Stelwagon established a prima facie violation, it failed to prove antitrust injury or damages proximately caused by any alleged discrimination.  The Supreme Court has held that “a plaintiff seeking damages under the antitrust laws bears the burden of proving *antitrust injury*, which is to say injury of the type the antitrust laws were intended to prevent and that flows from that which makes defendants’ acts unlawful.” *Brunswick*, 429 U.S. at 489.  Here, Stelwagon’s claimed damages—lost profits allegedly caused by Tarmac’s sales to Standard and Celotex—are not the type of injury the Robinson-Patman Act seeks to redress.  *See Cargill, Inc. v. Monfort of Colo., Inc.*, 479 U.S. 104, 117 (1986) (plaintiff must show that the antitrust violation caused the injury of which it complains).

D.  Moreover, Stelwagon’s damages model was fatally flawed.  It assumed that every sale by Tarmac to Standard or Celotex displaced a sale by Stelwagon, without accounting for alternative explanations such as market expansion or shifts in customer preference.  This Court has repeatedly rejected damages models that fail to isolate the effect of the alleged violation.  *See In re Sch. Asbestos Litig.*, 977 F.2d 764, 780 (3d Cir. 1992) (“Damages must be proved with reasonable certainty; speculation or guesswork is not enough.”) (citing *Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc.*, 327 U.S. 251, 264 (1946)).

**II. THE JURY’S BREACH-OF-CONTRACT VERDICT WAS BASED ON AN UNENFORCEABLE ORAL AGREEMENT AND MUST BE REVERSED.**

A.  The alleged exclusive distributorship agreement was oral and indefinite in duration.  Under Pennsylvania law, which governs this diversity action, an oral agreement that is not to be performed within one year is unenforceable under the Statute of Frauds.  42 Pa. Cons. Stat. Ann. § 512(1); *Wright v. Newman*, 599 A.2d 300, 302 (Pa. Super. Ct. 1991).  Because the agreement, if it existed, was terminable at will by either party, it lacked consideration and was unenforceable.  *See Gilbert v. City of Philadelphia*, 70 A.3d 829, 835 (Pa. Commw. Ct. 2013).

B.  Even if the agreement were enforceable, Stelwagon failed to prove damages with reasonable certainty.  The jury’s award of $74,242 was based on a speculative projection of lost profits, unsupported by any reliable financial data.  This Court has held that “[d]amages for breach of contract must be established with reasonable certainty and cannot be based on mere speculation or conjecture.” *Hoffman v. Hockessin Jet., Inc.*, 834 A.2d 114, 120 (Del. 2003) (applying Pennsylvania law).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Tarmac respectfully requests that this Court reverse the judgment below in its entirety and remand with instructions to enter judgment in favor of Tarmac.
