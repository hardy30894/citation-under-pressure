ARGUMENT

A district court's omission to advise a defendant of appeal rights under former Rule 32(a)(2) supplies no ground for collateral relief when the record establishes that the defendant already knew of the right and deliberately forwent an appeal. The purpose of the rule is to ensure awareness; once awareness is shown, the constitutional interest protected by the rule has been satisfied. Canizio v. New York, 327 U.S. 82, 85 (1946) (due-process inquiry turns on whether the prisoner "was not represented by counsel" and "the court neither asked him whether he desired counsel to be assigned, nor advised him of his right to counsel").

This Court has repeatedly refused to vacate judgments where the asserted procedural defect caused no actual prejudice because the defendant possessed independent knowledge of the right in question. Gryger v. Burke, 334 U.S. 728, 730 (1948); Williams v. Oklahoma, 358 U.S. 576, 583 (1959). The same principle governs here. Peguero's own testimony confirmed he understood his appellate rights at sentencing. That knowledge renders the omitted advice immaterial, just as the absence of counsel at sentencing did not invalidate a conviction when the defendant had already been fully informed of the consequences. McConnell v. Rhay, 393 U.S. 2, 3 (1968); Mempa v. Rhay, 389 U.S. 128, 134 (1967).

Collateral attack further requires a showing that the claimed error "amounted to a denial of due process of law in violation of the Fourteenth Amendment." Woods v. Nierstheimer, 328 U.S. 211, 216 (1946). No such denial occurs when the defendant, fully aware of his options, elects to stand on the judgment. Smith v. O'Grady, 312 U.S. 329, 334 (1941). Federal habeas therefore does not lie to correct a procedural lapse that the prisoner himself has already overcome by actual knowledge. Darr v. Burford, 339 U.S. 200, 210 (1950).

The Third Circuit correctly applied these settled principles. Because Peguero knew of his right to appeal and chose not to exercise it, the district court's failure to recite the rule worked no prejudice and supplied no basis for vacating the sentence. The judgment should be affirmed.
