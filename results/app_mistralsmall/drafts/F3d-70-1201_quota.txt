**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO APPLY RULE 407 TO EXCLUDE EVIDENCE OF SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES IN THIS STRICT PRODUCTS LIABILITY ACTION.

Rule 407 of the Federal Rules of Evidence provides that evidence of subsequent remedial measures is not admissible to prove negligence, culpable conduct, a defect in a product, or a need for a warning or instruction. *Fed. R. Evid. 407*. This rule applies with equal force in strict products liability cases. *See, e.g., In re Air Crash Disaster at Sioux City, Iowa, 133 F.3d 571, 576 (8th Cir. 1998) (applying Rule 407 in a strict products liability action); see also Raymond v. Raymond Corp., 938 F.2d 1505, 1510 (1st Cir. 1991) (same)*.

The purpose of Rule 407 is to encourage remedial measures by removing the disincentive of their use being admitted as evidence of liability. *See Fed. R. Evid. 407 advisory committee’s note*. This policy rationale applies with particular force in products liability cases, where the manufacturer’s incentive to improve product safety should not be chilled by the fear that such improvements will be used against it in litigation. *See, e.g., Raymond, 938 F.2d at 1510*.

Here, the district court erred in failing to apply Rule 407 to exclude evidence of the post-accident design change that lengthened the infeed chute of the wood chipper. The court’s in limine ruling excluding such evidence under Rule 407 was correct, and the court should not have permitted its admission for impeachment purposes. *See, e.g., In re Air Crash Disaster at Sioux City, 133 F.3d at 576 (holding that Rule 407’s exclusionary rule is not limited to the purpose of proving liability but applies to all purposes, including impeachment)*.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT’S ERROR IN FAILING TO APPLY RULE 407 WAS HARMFUL AND REQUIRES A NEW TRIAL.

The erroneous admission of evidence of subsequent remedial measures was harmful and warrants a new trial. *See Fed. R. Civ. P. 61*. The admission of such evidence was not harmless because it was central to Morbark’s defense and likely influenced the jury’s verdict. *See, e.g., In re Air Crash Disaster at Sioux City, 133 F.3d at 577 (finding harmful error where the admission of subsequent remedial measures evidence was central to the defense and likely influenced the jury’s verdict)*.

Moreover, the district court’s admonition to the jury to disregard the testimony concerning subsequent remedial measures did not cure the error. *See, e.g., United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725, 739 (1993) (holding that a curative instruction does not necessarily cure the harm caused by the admission of improper evidence)*. The jury was left with the impression that the seventeen-inch chute was the safest length available and that Morbark had not made any changes to the design of the wood chipper since the accident. *See supra* Part I. This impression was reinforced by Morbark’s counsel’s repeated suggestions to the jury that the city was still using the same machine without any changes. *See supra* Part I.

The district court’s error in failing to apply Rule 407 and in permitting the admission of evidence of subsequent remedial measures for impeachment purposes was harmful and requires a new trial. *See, e.g., In re Air Crash Disaster at Sioux City, 133 F.3d at 577; Raymond, 938 F.2d at 1510*.
