The Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act, 43 U.S.C. § 1333(a)(2)(A), incorporates the civil and criminal laws of adjacent States only “to the extent that they are applicable and not inconsistent with this subchapter or with other Federal laws.” This language requires a gap in federal coverage before state law may serve as surrogate federal law. See Continental Oil Co. v. London S.S. Owners’ Mut. Ins. Ass’n, 417 F.2d 1030, 1036 (5th Cir. 1969) (state law applies only upon “necessity to fill a significant void or gap”).

The Supreme Court has confirmed that Congress enacted the OCSLA to supply “a body of law applicable to the seabed, the subsoil, and the fixed structures” on the outer Continental Shelf. Rodrigue v. Aetna Cas. & Sur. Co., 395 U.S. 352, 355 (1969). Federal law was viewed as incomplete, yet Congress rejected both maritime law and direct state sovereignty, choosing instead a federal enclave model in which state law would operate only where federal law was silent. Shell Oil Co. v. Iowa Dep’t of Revenue, 488 U.S. 19, 26-27 (1988); see also United States v. Sharpnack, 355 U.S. 286, 289-90 (1958) (assimilative statutes fill gaps without delegating legislative power).

The Fifth Circuit has consistently applied this gap-filling rule. Union Texas Petroleum Corp. v. PLT Eng’g, Inc., 895 F.2d 1043, 1050 (5th Cir. 1990) (state law adopted only if federal law contains “a significant void or gap”); Breton Energy, L.L.C. v. Mariner Energy Res., Inc., 764 F.3d 394, 398 (5th Cir. 2014) (same). Later decisions have reaffirmed that comprehensive federal statutes leave no room for state supplementation. Barker v. Hercules Offshore, Inc., 713 F.3d 208, 214 (5th Cir. 2013); Demette v. Falcon Drilling Co., 280 F.3d 492, 498 (5th Cir. 2002).

The Fair Labor Standards Act supplies a comprehensive wage-and-hour regime that expressly addresses meal periods, overtime, and minimum wages. Its savings clause preserves more protective state laws only within the territorial jurisdiction of the States; it does not authorize their extraterritorial extension onto the outer Continental Shelf. Because the FLSA leaves no “significant void or gap,” California wage-and-hour provisions are not assimilated under § 1333(a)(2)(A). Valladolid v. Pac. Operations Offshore, LLP, 604 F.3d 1126, 1130 (9th Cir. 2010) (OCSLA jurisdiction is exclusive federal enclave jurisdiction).

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary reading—that state law applies whenever it is “applicable and not inconsistent”—would convert the OCSLA into a general choice-of-law statute rather than a gap-filling measure. That construction contradicts the statute’s text, its legislative history, and the uniform approach of the Fifth Circuit. The district court therefore correctly held that Newton’s claims are governed exclusively by federal law.
