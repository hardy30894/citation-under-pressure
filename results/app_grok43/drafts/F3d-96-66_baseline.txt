ARGUMENT

The district court erred in granting summary judgment. Genuine issues of material fact exist as to whether Bora breached the active-operations duty recognized in Scindia Steam Navigation Co. v. De Los Santos, 451 U.S. 156, 101 S.Ct. 1614, 68 L.Ed.2d 1 (1981).

In Scindia the Supreme Court held that a vessel “must exercise reasonable care to avoid exposing longshoremen to harm from hazards they may encounter in areas, or from equipment, under the active control of the vessel during the stevedoring operation.” Id. at 167. The Court explained that this duty is “a simple one: to exercise reasonable care under the circumstances.” Id. at 166. The Third Circuit has repeatedly applied the same standard. See, e.g., Kirsch v. Plovidba, 971 F.2d 1026, 1029 (3d Cir. 1992); Derr v. Kawasaki Kisen K.K., 835 F.2d 490, 496 (3d Cir. 1987).

The snatch blocks were equipment under the vessel’s active control. The crew alone operated the block-and-pulley system; the blocks were fixed to the hatch covers and could be moved only by the crew. After opening the hatch covers, the crew was required to return each block to the vertical position and secure it. On the morning of the accident the crew left one block in the horizontal position atop a cargo unit. That placement created two hazards: it prevented safe removal of the fruit beneath it and exposed the hookup man to falling objects. Because the blocks remained under the crew’s exclusive control, Bora owed Serbin the Scindia active-operations duty with respect to them.

A reasonable jury could find that Bora breached that duty. The crew knew or should have known that a block left down would obstruct cargo operations and endanger longshoremen. Serbin’s decision to correct the condition does not, as a matter of law, relieve Bora of liability. Scindia imposes on the vessel an affirmative obligation to keep its equipment in a safe condition; it does not shift that obligation to longshoremen merely because they attempt to mitigate an obvious hazard created by the vessel. See Scindia, 451 U.S. at 167-68; see also Davis v. Portline Transportes Maritime Internacional, 16 F.3d 532, 540-41 (3d Cir. 1994).

The record therefore contains triable issues on breach and causation. Summary judgment was improper.
