**ARGUMENT**

The District’s categorical exclusion of Lamb’s Chapel’s religious film series from school facilities violates the First Amendment’s guarantee of free speech. By opening its property to a broad array of community groups, the District created a public forum from which it may not exclude speech based on its religious viewpoint.

**I. The District Created a Public Forum, and Once It Did So, It Could Not Exclude Speech Based on Religious Content.**

The District’s policy and practice allowed a wide range of secular organizations—including the “Southern Harmonizers,” a “Psychology and The Unknown” lecture series, and other civic and cultural groups—to use school premises for expressive activities. This affirmative opening of the forum to the public for discussion of diverse topics transformed the property into a public forum. *Schneider v. State*, 308 U.S. 147, 163 (1939) (holding that a municipality “may not, by its ordinances, close the public streets and parks against the orderly and peaceful use of them for the communication of information and opinion”).
