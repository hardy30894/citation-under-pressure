The U.S. Bankruptcy Code does not exempt an inherited IRA from a debtor's estate in bankruptcy. The plain language of 11 U.S.C. §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) exempts "retirement funds" from creditors' claims, but an inherited IRA does not qualify as "retirement funds" in the hands of the beneficiary.

The Seventh Circuit correctly held that an inherited IRA is not exempt under §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) because it is not a fund reserved for the owner's retirement. Clark v. Rameker, 716 F.3d 1028 (7th Cir. 2013). As the court noted, an inherited IRA is subject to different rules than a traditional IRA, including a requirement that the beneficiary withdraw a minimum amount from the account each year, and a prohibition on new contributions. Id. at 1030-31.

The Seventh Circuit's decision is consistent with the purpose of the Bankruptcy Code's exemption provisions, which is to protect debtors' retirement savings. Rousey v. Jacoway, 544 U.S. 320, 332 (2005) ("The Bankruptcy Code's exemption provisions are designed to 'protect a debtor's essential needs' and 'give a debtor a fresh start.'"). An inherited IRA, however, is not a retirement savings vehicle for the beneficiary, but rather a tax-deferral vehicle that must be distributed within a certain timeframe. Clark, 716 F.3d at 1031.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Chilton, which held that an inherited IRA is exempt under §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12), is distinguishable. In re Chilton, 674 F.3d 486 (5th Cir. 2012). The Fifth Circuit's reasoning relies on a broad interpretation of "retirement funds" that is not supported by the statutory text or legislative history. In contrast, the Seventh Circuit's decision in Clark is grounded in a careful analysis of the statutory language and the characteristics of an inherited IRA.

Furthermore, exempting inherited IRAs from creditors' claims would create an anomaly in the Bankruptcy Code. As the Seventh Circuit noted, if a decedent were to withdraw funds from an IRA and give them to a beneficiary, those funds would not be exempt from creditors' claims. Clark, 716 F.3d at 1032. It would be inconsistent to treat inherited IRAs differently simply because the funds were transferred directly from the decedent's IRA to the beneficiary's inherited IRA.

In conclusion, the Seventh Circuit correctly held that an inherited IRA is not exempt from a debtor's estate in bankruptcy under 11 U.S.C. §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12). The plain language of the statute, the characteristics of an inherited IRA, and the purpose of the Bankruptcy Code's exemption provisions all support this conclusion.
