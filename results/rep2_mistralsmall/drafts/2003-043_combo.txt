**ARGUMENT**

I. THE GOVERNMENT’S FAILURE TO ASSESS THE INDIVIDUAL PARTNERS WITHIN THE THREE-YEAR LIMITATION PERIOD BARS COLLECTION FROM THEM

The Internal Revenue Code requires the government to assess a tax “against the taxpayer” within three years after the return is filed. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a) (1954). An assessment is “made by recording the liability of the taxpayer in the office of the Secretary.” 26 U.S.C. § 6203 (1954). The statutory term “taxpayer” includes “an individual,” 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14) (1954), and each partner is a separate “individual” and “taxpayer.” See United States v. Coson, 286 F.2d 453, 458 (9th Cir. 1961) (“[T]he assessment at issue named only the partnership and ‘an unknown number of unidentified taxpayers.’”); cf. United States v. Benedict, 338 U.S. 692, 696 (1950) (“The tax is imposed upon the individual partners, not upon the partnership.”).

The government’s assessment against the partnership did not constitute an assessment against the partners. As this Court recognized in Coson, “[a] lien does not exist against a particular individual’s property ... unless the underlying tax obligation has been assessed against him under § 6203.” 286 F.2d at 458. The Supreme Court has likewise held that an assessment must identify the taxpayer whose liability is being recorded. See United States v. Ahrens, 340 U.S. 273, 274 (1951) (“The assessment is made by recording the liability of the taxpayer in the office of the Secretary.”) (quoting § 6203). Because the partners were not assessed, the three-year statute of limitations applicable to them was never tolled. See Lucas v. Pilliod Lumber Co., 155 F.2d 92, 94 (8th Cir. 1946) (“The statute of limitations begins to run against a cause of action at the time the cause of action accrues.”).

II. THE TEN-YEAR EXTENSION IN § 6502(a) APPLIES ONLY TO THE PARTNERSHIP, NOT TO THE INDIVIDUAL PARTNERS

Section 6502(a) extends the limitations period for collection to ten years “after the assessment of the tax,” but only “[w]here the assessment of any tax ... has been made within the period of limitation properly applicable thereto.” 26 U.S.C. § 6502(a) (1954). The assessment here was made against the partnership, not the partners. See Coson, 286 F.2d at 458. The statutory phrase “the taxpayer” in § 6502(a) refers back to the taxpayer assessed under § 6203. See Ahrens, 340 U.S. at 274. Thus, the ten-year extension runs only in favor of the partnership, not the partners. See Lucas, 155 F.2d at 94 (limitations period runs separately as to each taxpayer).

III. CALIFORNIA PARTNERSHIP LAW DOES NOT CREATE AN ENFORCEABLE CLAIM AGAINST THE PARTNERS WITHOUT A JUDGMENT

Under California law, a judgment against a partnership is not a judgment against the partners. Cal. Corp. Code § 16307(c) (1947); see Witkin, Summary of California Law § 60V (1948) (“A judgment against a partnership may not be satisfied from a partner’s assets unless there is also a judgment against the partner.”). The government has not obtained such a judgment. See United States v. Hibernia Bank, 156 F.Supp. 872, 874 (N.D. Cal. 1957) (“A judgment against a partnership is not a judgment against the individual partners.”). Without a judgment, the government’s claim is unenforceable against the partners’ property. See Lucas, 155 F.2d at 94.

IV. THE GOVERNMENT’S RELIANCE ON YOUNG V. RIDDELL IS MISPLACED

The government cites Young v. Riddell, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir. 1960), for the proposition that an assessment against the partnership suffices to bind the partners. But Young did not address whether the government could collect from partners without an individual assessment. The district court in Young merely held that a partner who had paid his share of the tax could not recover a refund because he was liable under state law. Young v. Riddell, 60-1 U.S. Tax Cas. (CCH) ¶ 9831, at 76,054 (S.D. Cal. 1959), aff’d, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir. 1960). The Ninth Circuit affirmed on that narrow ground. 283 F.2d at 910. Young does not support the government’s position here.

V. THE STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS BARS THE GOVERNMENT’S CLAIMS

Because the government did not assess the partners within three years, and did not obtain a judgment against them, its claims are time-barred. See Lucas, 155 F.2d at 94; Hibernia Bank, 156 F.Supp. at 874. The bankruptcy court correctly disallowed the claims. 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1) (1952).

**CONCLUSION**

The judgment below should be affirmed.
