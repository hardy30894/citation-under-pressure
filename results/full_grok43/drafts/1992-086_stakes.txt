I. The School District’s Refusal to Permit Lamb’s Chapel’s Use of Its Facilities Constitutes Viewpoint Discrimination in a Limited Public Forum.

The School District’s facilities constitute a limited public forum. Perry Education Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983). Although the government need not open such a forum, once it does so “it must respect the lawful boundaries it has itself set.” Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund, Inc., 473 U.S. 788, 800 (1985). Restrictions “must be reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum” and “must not discriminate against speech on the basis of its viewpoint.” Id. at 806.

The District permitted after-hours use of its premises for lectures, musical performances, and films addressing family life and child-rearing. It nevertheless barred Lamb’s Chapel solely because the proposed films presented those subjects “from a Christian perspective.” This exclusion turns on the speaker’s viewpoint, not on any subject-matter limitation the District had consistently enforced. See Board of Educ. of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, 250 (1990) (plurality opinion) (noting that “the prohibition of discrimination against religious speech” prevents government from “favoring one viewpoint about religion over others”).

Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263 (1981), confirms that religious speech on topics otherwise permitted in a forum may not be singled out for exclusion. There the Court held that a university could not “open its facilities to a wide range of uses” yet “close them to religious groups.” Id. at 267. The same principle applies here: once the District opened its facilities for community discussion of family values, it could not deny access because the discussion would occur through a religious lens.

The District’s reliance on New York Education Law § 414 and Local Rule 7 does not alter the analysis. A state statute or local rule “may not be applied in a manner that violates the First Amendment.” Widmar, 454 U.S. at 276. Because the denial rests on viewpoint discrimination rather than a neutral, consistently applied subject-matter restriction, it fails constitutional scrutiny.

II. The Second Circuit’s Distinction Between “Religious Purposes” and Permissible Uses Cannot Be Sustained.

The court below concluded that prior uses of the facilities lacked “religious purposes” and therefore did not open the forum to Lamb’s Chapel. That distinction collapses when the subject matter—family life and child-rearing—is identical. The District allowed secular presentations on the same topics; it excluded only the religious perspective. Such selectivity is the hallmark of viewpoint discrimination. See Perry, 460 U.S. at 46 (content-based restrictions in limited forums must be “viewpoint neutral”).

No compelling interest justifies the exclusion. The District’s asserted interest in avoiding an Establishment Clause violation is insufficient where, as here, equal access to a limited forum is at issue. See Widmar, 454 U.S. at 271 (“[T]he Establishment Clause does not bar a policy of equal access.”). The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
