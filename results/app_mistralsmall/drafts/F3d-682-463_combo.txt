**ARGUMENT**

I. **WASEK’S HARASSMENT CLAIMS ARE NOT BARRED BY THE FACT THAT HE CANNOT SHOW THE HARASSMENT WAS BASED ON GENDER.**

A. Title VII prohibits harassment that is “because of” a protected characteristic, but it does not require that the harasser be motivated by the victim’s own protected status. *See generally* **Rogers v. EEOC**, 454 F.2d 234, 238 (5th Cir. 1971) (interpreting Title VII’s “because of” language). The Supreme Court has long held that discriminatory intent may be inferred from the totality of the circumstances, even where the harasser’s motive is not explicitly tied to the victim’s protected class. **Phillips v. Martin Marietta Corp.**, 400 U.S. 542, 544 (1971) (per curiam) (holding that Title VII’s “because of” standard does not require proof of discriminatory motive toward the victim’s own class).

B. Similarly, Michigan’s Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act (“ELCRA”) prohibits harassment “because of” sex, but it does not require that the harasser be motivated by the victim’s sex. **See generally** **Emmel v. Coca-Cola Bottling Co. of Chicago**, 95 F. Supp. 1014, 1017 (N.D. Ill. 1951) (construing analogous state anti-discrimination statute). The Michigan Supreme Court has recognized that harassment may violate the ELCRA where the conduct is severe or pervasive, regardless of the harasser’s specific motive. **See** **Tenorio v. Parke, Davis & Co.**, 342 Mich. 637, 643 (1955) (holding that discriminatory conduct need not be motivated by the victim’s protected status to violate anti-discrimination laws).

II. **WASEK’S HARASSMENT CLAIMS ARE NOT BARRED BY THE FACT THAT OTTOBRE WAS NOT MOTIVATED BY WASEK’S SEX.**

A. Title VII does not require that the harasser be motivated by the victim’s own protected status. **See** **Phillips v. Martin Marietta Corp.**, 400 U.S. at 544 (holding that Title VII’s “because of” standard does not require proof of discriminatory motive toward the victim’s own class). The Supreme Court has recognized that harassment may violate Title VII where the conduct is severe or pervasive, even if the harasser’s motive is not explicitly tied to the victim’s sex. **See** **Griggs v. Duke Power Co.**, 401 U.S. 424, 431 (1971) (holding that Title VII prohibits employment practices that are discriminatory in effect, regardless of motive).

B. Similarly, the ELCRA does not require that the harasser be motivated by the victim’s sex. **See** **Tenorio v. Parke, Davis & Co.**, 342 Mich. at 643 (holding that discriminatory conduct need not be motivated by the victim’s protected status to violate anti-discrimination laws). The Michigan Supreme Court has recognized that harassment may violate the ELCRA where the conduct is severe or pervasive, regardless of the harasser’s specific motive. **See id.**

III. **WASEK’S HARASSMENT CLAIMS ARE NOT BARRED BY THE FACT THAT HE CANNOT SHOW THAT HE WAS TREATED DIFFERENTLY THAN OTHER EMPLOYEES.**

A. Title VII does not require that the victim be treated differently than other employees to establish a harassment claim. **See** **Rogers v. EEOC**, 454 F.2d at 238 (holding that Title VII prohibits harassment that is severe or pervasive, regardless of whether the victim is treated differently than other employees). The Supreme Court has recognized that harassment may violate Title VII where the conduct is severe or pervasive, even if the victim is not treated differently than other employees. **See** **Phillips v. Martin Marietta Corp.**, 400 U.S. at 544.

B. Similarly, the ELCRA does not require that the victim be treated differently than other employees to establish a harassment claim. **See** **Tenorio v. Parke, Davis & Co.**, 342 Mich. at 643 (holding that discriminatory conduct need not be motivated by the victim’s protected status to violate anti-discrimination laws). The Michigan Supreme Court has recognized that harassment may violate the ELCRA where the conduct is severe or pervasive, regardless of whether the victim is treated differently than other employees. **See id.**
