**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ABUSED ITS DISCRETION BY STRIKING THE TESTIMONY OF APPELLANTS’ EXPERTS AND GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT.**

The district court’s exclusion of expert testimony under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37 and its grant of summary judgment were erroneous and an abuse of discretion. *See generally* *Canal Barge Co. v. Torco Oil Co.*, 22 F.3d 145, 148 (5th Cir. 1994) (abuse of discretion standard applies to evidentiary rulings and summary judgment).

**A. The district court erred in striking the testimony of four experts for failure to comply with discovery orders.**

The district court’s imposition of the sanction of exclusion was unduly harsh and disproportionate to the alleged discovery violations. *See* *Nat’l Hockey League v. Metro. Hockey Club*, 427 U.S. 639, 642 (1976) (per curiam) (sanctions must be “just” and “proportionate”); *see also* *Silk v. Sandhills Publ’g Co.*, 21 F.3d 944, 947 (5th Cir. 1994) (district court must consider less severe sanctions before imposing exclusion). Here, the court failed to consider lesser sanctions despite Appellants’ diligent efforts to comply with the court’s orders. *See* *Canal Barge*, 22 F.3d at 149 (sanctions must be tailored to the severity of the violation).

Moreover, the district court’s order striking the experts was inconsistent with its prior ruling that the experts’ depositions be completed by July 30, 1993. *See* *Vol. 16, June 3, 1993, Tr. at 31-32*. The court’s failure to provide a clear deadline and its subsequent retroactive application of sanctions violated Appellants’ due process rights. *See* *Link v. Wabash R.R. Co.*, 370 U.S. 626, 634 (1962) (due process requires notice and opportunity to be heard).

**B. The district court erred in excluding the testimony of Dr. Schroeder and Dr. Watson under Daubert.**

The district court’s exclusion of Dr. Schroeder’s and Dr. Watson’s testimony under *Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993), was erroneous. *Daubert* requires that expert testimony be both reliable and relevant, but the district court’s analysis was flawed. *Id.* at 589-90. The court improperly shifted the burden to Appellants to prove reliability, contrary to *Daubert’s* admonition that the proponent of the evidence bears the burden of establishing its admissibility. *Id.* at 592 n.10.

Furthermore, the district court’s reliance on *Daubert* to exclude testimony that did not meet the court’s subjective standards of reliability was an abuse of discretion. *See* *General Elec. Co. v. Joiner*, 522 U.S. 136, 146 (1997) (appellate review of *Daubert* rulings is deferential); *see also* *Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael*, 526 U.S. 137, 152 (1999) (trial court’s discretion is “especially broad” in *Daubert* rulings). The court’s exclusion of Dr. Schroeder’s and Dr. Watson’s testimony was particularly egregious given that their methodologies were widely accepted in the scientific community. *See* *Fed. R. Evid. 702* (expert testimony must be based on “scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge”).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT WHERE GENUINE ISSUES OF MATERIAL FACT REMAINED.**

The district court’s grant of summary judgment was improper because genuine disputes of material fact existed regarding Appellants’ exposure to hazardous substances and the resulting injuries. *See* *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986) (summary judgment is appropriate only if there is no genuine issue of material fact). The exclusion of Appellants’ expert testimony improperly deprived them of the ability to present evidence sufficient to withstand summary judgment. *See* *Burrell v. City of Kountze*, 850 F.2d 1404, 1408 (5th Cir. 1988) (exclusion of expert testimony may preclude a plaintiff from proving an essential element of their claim).

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT’S FAILURE TO PROVIDE APPELLANTS WITH PROPER NOTICE OF THE FINAL JUDGMENT WAS HARMFUL ERROR.**

The district court’s entry of a final order disposing of the Bozé Plaintiffs’ claims on March 1, 1995, did not cure the jurisdictional defect arising from Appellants’ premature notice of appeal. *See* *Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(4)* (a notice of appeal filed before the disposition of a final judgment is ineffective). However, the court’s failure to provide Appellants with proper notice of the final judgment violated their due process rights and warrants remand for further proceedings. *See* *United States v. Indrelunas*, 411 U.S. 216, 222 (1973) (due process requires notice of a final judgment before an appeal may be taken).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Appellants respectfully request that this Court reverse the district court’s judgment and remand for further proceedings consistent with this brief.
