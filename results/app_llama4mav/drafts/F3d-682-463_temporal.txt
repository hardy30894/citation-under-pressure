## Argument

The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Arrow Energy Services, Inc. ("Arrow Energy") on Harold Wasek's claims under Title VII and Michigan's Elliot-Larsen Civil Rights Act ("ELCRA"). Wasek presented sufficient evidence to create a genuine issue of material fact as to whether he was subjected to a hostile work environment because of his sex.

### I. Wasek Was Subjected to a Hostile Work Environment Because of His Sex

To establish a hostile work environment claim, Wasek must show that he was subjected to unwelcome harassment that was sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of his employment. See Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson, 477 U.S. 57, 67 (citing Henson v. Dundee, 682 F.2d 897, 902 (11th Cir. 1982), which in turn cites Rogers v. EEOC, 454 F.2d 234 (5th Cir. 1971)). The harassment Wasek endured at the hands of Paul Ottobre was both severe and pervasive. Ottobre repeatedly touched Wasek in a sexual manner, made explicit comments about Wasek's appearance, and told graphic stories. This behavior was not merely "boorish or offensive"; it was objectively hostile and created an abusive work environment. See Williams v. Saxbe, 413 F. Supp. 654, 657-58 (D.D.C. 1976) (citing Rogers, 454 F.2d at 238).

The fact that Ottobre's behavior was motivated by a sexual or quasi-sexual interest in Wasek is sufficient to establish that the harassment was "because of sex." See Bundy v. Jackson, 641 F.2d 934, 945-46 (D.C. Cir. 1981) (citing Barnes v. Costle, 561 F.2d 983 (D.C. Cir. 1977)). Wasek's testimony that he believed Ottobre was bisexual and that Ottobre's behavior was driven by a sexual attraction to him further supports this conclusion.

### II. Arrow Energy Is Liable for the Hostile Work Environment

Arrow Energy is liable for the hostile work environment created by Ottobre because it knew or should have known about the harassment and failed to take prompt and effective remedial action. See Miller v. Bank of America, 418 F. Supp. 233, 236 (N.D. Cal. 1976) (citing Calcote v. Texas Educational Foundation, Inc., 458 F.2d 46 (5th Cir. 1972)). Wasek reported Ottobre's behavior to his supervisor, Pat Tripp, who not only failed to intervene but also advised Wasek not to report the incidents to superiors. When Wasek eventually complained to Dick Smillie and Bill Strong, they responded with hostility and inaction. Smillie's suggestion that Wasek "kick [Ottobre's] ass" and Strong's advice that Wasek and Ottobre "duke it out" demonstrate a reckless disregard for Wasek's well-being and a failure to take Wasek's complaints seriously.

The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Arrow Energy because Wasek presented sufficient evidence to create a genuine issue of material fact as to whether he was subjected to a hostile work environment because of his sex and whether Arrow Energy is liable for that environment. The judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.

## Conclusion

For the foregoing reasons, the district court's grant of summary judgment to Arrow Energy should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
