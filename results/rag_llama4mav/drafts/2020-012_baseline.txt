The Railroad Retirement Act (RRA) and the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (RUIA) grant federal circuit courts jurisdiction to review "final decisions" of the Railroad Retirement Board (Board). 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The question presented is whether a decision by the Board denying a request to reopen a prior benefits claim constitutes a "final decision" subject to judicial review.

The Fifth Circuit correctly acknowledged a circuit split on this issue, with the majority of circuits, including the Fifth Circuit itself in Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), holding that they lack jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen a prior claim for benefits. However, the D.C. Circuit's decision in Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), presents a compelling minority view that the RRA grants circuit courts jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations.

The Supreme Court's decision in Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), is often cited as supporting the majority view. However, Sanders is distinguishable because it involved a different statutory scheme—the Social Security Act—which has a more restrictive judicial review provision than the RRA. The RRA's judicial review provision, 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), is broader and does not contain the same limiting language as the Social Security Act's § 205(g). 

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has recognized that different statutory schemes for administrative review can have distinct implications for judicial review. For example, in Lindahl v. Office of Personnel Management, 470 U.S. 768 (1985), the Court noted the complexities and overlaps between different statutory schemes governing federal employee benefits. Similarly, the Court has emphasized the importance of understanding the specific statutory context in cases involving judicial review of administrative decisions, as seen in Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala, 525 U.S. 449 (1999).

In light of these considerations, the Fifth Circuit should reconsider its precedent in Roberts. The D.C. Circuit's reasoning in Stovic, which emphasizes the plain meaning of the RRA and the importance of judicial review in ensuring the integrity of the administrative process, is persuasive. Adopting this view would align with the principle that statutory provisions for judicial review should be interpreted to effectuate their purpose, as discussed in cases like Dada v. Mukasey, 554 U.S. 1 (2008), and Heimeshoff v. Hartford Life & Accident Ins. Co., 571 U.S. 99 (2013).

The Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application is a final decision that is subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit should reconsider its jurisdictional bar to reviewing the Board's decision, in line with the D.C. Circuit's decision in Stovic. This Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's dismissal of Salinas's petition for lack of jurisdiction.
