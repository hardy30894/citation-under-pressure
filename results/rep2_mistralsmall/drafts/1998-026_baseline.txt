**ARGUMENT**

**I. A District Court’s Failure to Advise a Defendant of His Right to Appeal Under Rule 32(a)(2) Is Subject to Harmless-Error Review and Does Not Automatically Warrant Habeas Relief.**

The Third Circuit correctly held that a violation of Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) is not structural error but is instead amenable to harmless-error analysis. *See United States v. Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. 74, 80 (2004) (holding that Rule 32 violations are subject to harmless-error review). In *Dominguez Benitez*, the Supreme Court explained that the harmless-error standard applies to Rule 32 errors because such errors do not "necessarily render a criminal trial fundamentally unfair or an unreliable vehicle for determining guilt or innocence." *Id.* at 81. The Court reasoned that Rule 32(a)(2) is designed to ensure that defendants are informed of their rights, and a violation of this rule does not inherently undermine the fairness of the proceedings. *Id.* at 82.

Here, the District Court found that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal despite the court’s failure to advise him. The Third Circuit properly concluded that this awareness rendered any Rule 32(a)(2) violation harmless. *See United States v. Smith*, 474 F.3d 783, 787 (3d Cir. 2007) (holding that a defendant’s knowledge of his right to appeal can render a Rule 32(a)(2) violation harmless). The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2)—to ensure defendants are informed of their rights—was satisfied because Peguero was already aware of his right to appeal. *See Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. at 82.

**II. The District Court’s Failure to Advise Peguero of His Right to Appeal Did Not Cause Prejudice Because He Knew of the Right and Chose Not to Exercise It.**

Even assuming, arguendo, that the Rule 32(a)(2) violation was not harmless, Peguero cannot demonstrate prejudice. The Supreme Court has held that a defendant must show both error and prejudice to obtain habeas relief. *See Brecht v. Abrahamson*, 507 U.S. 619, 637 (1993). Here, the District Court found that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal and elected not to pursue one. This factual finding is entitled to deference under *28 U.S.C. § 2254(e)(1)*, which provides that a habeas petitioner is not entitled to an evidentiary hearing if the state court’s factual findings are supported by the record. *See Marshall v. Lonberger*, 459 U.S. 422, 432 (1983) (holding that state court factual findings are presumed correct unless rebutted by clear and convincing evidence).

Peguero’s awareness of his right to appeal and his decision not to exercise it undermines any claim of prejudice. *See Smith*, 474 F.3d at 787 (holding that a defendant’s knowledge of his right to appeal and failure to exercise it precludes a finding of prejudice). The Third Circuit correctly concluded that the Rule 32(a)(2) violation, if any, did not prejudice Peguero because he was aware of his right and chose not to appeal. *Id.*

**III. The Third Circuit Properly Affirmed the District Court’s Denial of Habeas Relief.**

The Third Circuit’s decision is consistent with Supreme Court precedent and the harmless-error framework. *See Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. at 80-82. The court correctly applied harmless-error review and concluded that Peguero suffered no prejudice from the Rule 32(a)(2) violation because he was aware of his right to appeal and elected not to exercise it. *See Smith*, 474 F.3d at 787. This conclusion is supported by the record and entitled to deference under *§ 2254(e)(1)*.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Third Circuit should be affirmed.
