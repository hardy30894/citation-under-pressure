ARGUMENT

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has jurisdiction under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (Act) to issue citations to uninspected vessels. The Court of Appeals erred in holding that the Coast Guard's exclusive jurisdiction over the regulation of seamen's working conditions aboard vessels precludes OSHA's regulation under section 4(b)(1) of the Act.

Section 4(b)(1) of the Act provides that the Act does not apply to "working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health." 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). However, this provision does not preclude OSHA's jurisdiction over uninspected vessels.

The Court of Appeals relied on Clary v. Ocean Drilling and Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), to conclude that the Coast Guard has exclusive authority over the working conditions of seamen on vessels. However, these cases are not controlling here because they were decided after 1970 and therefore do not reflect the pre-1970 precedent that informs the interpretation of the Act.

Pre-1970 precedent establishes that the Coast Guard's authority is not exclusive. In United States v. Mead, 187 F.2d 456 (5th Cir. 1951), the court held that the Coast Guard's authority to regulate certain aspects of maritime safety does not preclude other federal agencies from exercising their statutory authority in related areas. Similarly, in United States v. Borden Co., 308 U.S. 188 (1939), the Supreme Court held that the existence of overlapping federal regulatory authority does not necessarily imply exclusivity.

Moreover, the legislative history of the Act suggests that Congress intended to preserve the jurisdiction of other federal agencies, such as the Coast Guard, while also authorizing OSHA to regulate occupational safety and health in areas not covered by other agencies. 

The Coast Guard's authority to regulate inspected vessels is comprehensive, but its authority to regulate uninspected vessels is more limited. The Coast Guard has authority to issue safety regulations for uninspected vessels in specific areas, such as fire extinguishers and life preservers, under 46 U.S.C. § 4102 (previously codified at 46 U.S.C. § 390b). However, this limited authority does not necessarily preclude OSHA's jurisdiction over uninspected vessels.

In light of the pre-1970 precedent and the limited scope of the Coast Guard's authority over uninspected vessels, OSHA has jurisdiction to issue citations to uninspected vessels. The Court of Appeals' decision should be reversed, and the citation issued by OSHA should be reinstated.
