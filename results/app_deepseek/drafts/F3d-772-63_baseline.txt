**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Foley’s Claims Under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A and ch. 244**

**A. Foley Plausibly Alleged That Wells Fargo Violated the Settlement Agreement’s Pre-Foreclosure Modification Requirements, Stating a Claim Under Chapter 93A**

The district court erred in concluding that Foley failed to state a claim under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A. Chapter 93A prohibits “unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce.” *See* Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A, § 2(a). A breach of contract, standing alone, does not violate Chapter 93A. *See Morris v. BAC Home Loans Servicing, L.P.,* 775 F. Supp. 2d 255, 259 (D. Mass. 2011). However, a breach that is “egregious” or “aggravated” and that “falls within the penumbra of some common-law, statutory, or other established concept of unfairness” may state a claim. *See Mass. Eye & Ear Infirmary v. QLT Phototherapeutics, Inc.,* 552 F.3d 47, 69 (1st Cir. 2009) (quoting *PMP Assocs., Inc. v. Globe Newspaper Co.,* 366 Mass. 593, 596 (1975)).

Here, Foley alleged that Wells Fargo failed to consider him for a HAMP modification before initiating foreclosure, as required by the settlement agreement. The agreement mandated that Settlement Class B members “first shall be considered for a HAMP modification” before any other modification or foreclosure. Wells Fargo’s conduct—delaying Foley’s HAMP application for seven months, failing to return calls, and sending misleading letters about short sales while foreclosure loomed—constitutes the type of “egregious” conduct that Chapter 93A was designed to remedy. *See Klairmont v. Gainsboro Rest., Inc.,* 465 Mass. 165, 175 (2013) (holding that conduct “in disregard of known contractual obligations” may be unfair under Chapter 93A). The district court’s dismissal of this claim was therefore erroneous.

**B. Foley Plausibly Alleged That Wells Fargo Violated Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244, § 35A**

Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244, § 35A requires a mortgagee to provide a borrower with a “right to cure” notice before accelerating the mortgage or commencing foreclosure. The statute mandates that the notice include “a statement that the borrower has the right to cure the default.” *See* Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244, § 35A(b). Foley alleged that Wells Fargo failed to provide such a notice before scheduling foreclosure. The district court dismissed this claim on the ground that Foley did not allege that he was in default. But Foley’s complaint explicitly states that he “stopped making timely payments in-full” as of October 2010, and that he made only partial payments through April 2011. These allegations, taken as true, establish that Foley was in default. *See Watterson v. Page,* 987 F.2d 1, 3 (1st Cir. 1993). The district court’s dismissal of this claim was therefore improper.

**II. Foley’s Claims Are Not Preempted by the Home Owners’ Loan Act**

Wells Fargo argues that Foley’s state-law claims are preempted by the Home Owners’ Loan Act (“HOLA”), 12 U.S.C. § 1461 *et seq.*, and its implementing regulations. However, HOLA preemption applies only to federal savings associations, not to national banks like Wells Fargo. *See* 12 C.F.R. § 560.2 (applying to “federal savings associations”). Wells Fargo is a national bank, not a federal savings association. *See Watters v. Wachovia Bank, N.A.,* 550 U.S. 1, 6 (2007) (distinguishing between national banks and federal savings associations). Moreover, even if HOLA applied, the statute preempts only state laws that “obstruct, impair, or condition” a federal savings association’s lending activities. *See* 12 C.F.R. § 560.2(a). Foley’s claims—which seek to enforce contractual and statutory obligations that Wells Fargo voluntarily assumed—do not obstruct or impair any federal lending activity. *See Silvas v. E*Trade Mortg. Corp.,* 514 F.3d 1001, 1005 (9th Cir. 2008) (holding that HOLA does not preempt state-law claims based on a lender’s own contractual promises). The district court’s preemption ruling was therefore erroneous.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the district court’s dismissal of Foley’s claims under Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A and ch. 244, and remand for further proceedings.
