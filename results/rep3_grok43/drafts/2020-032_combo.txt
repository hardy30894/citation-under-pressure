The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that the revision of Georgia Gwinnett College’s policies during litigation rendered the action moot. When intervening events make it impossible for a court to grant effectual relief, the case ceases to present a justiciable controversy. Mills v. Green, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895); Ex parte McCardle, 74 U.S. (7 Wall.) 506, 514 (1868). The district court found that the college had “unambiguously terminated the Prior Policies and there is no reasonable basis to expect that it will return to them.” That determination ended any live dispute over declaratory or injunctive relief. United States v. Alaska S.S. Co., 253 U.S. 113, 116 (1920); California v. San Pablo & Tulare R. Co., 149 U.S. 308, 314 (1893).

A bare request for nominal damages does not preserve jurisdiction once the underlying controversy has vanished. The Constitution limits federal courts to “cases” and “controversies” in which the parties maintain adverse legal interests of “sufficient immediacy and reality.” Maryland Casualty Co. v. Pacific Coal & Oil Co., 312 U.S. 270, 273 (1941); Aetna Life Ins. Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227, 240–41 (1937). Nominal damages, being symbolic only, supply no concrete stake once the challenged conduct has permanently ceased. See Golden v. Zwickler, 394 U.S. 103, 108 (1969); Hall v. Beals, 396 U.S. 45, 48 (1969).

The capable-of-repetition exception likewise fails. That doctrine applies only when the same controversy is reasonably likely to recur between the same parties and the duration of the challenged action is too short to permit full litigation. Southern Pac. Terminal Co. v. ICC, 219 U.S. 498, 515 (1911); Liner v. Jafco, Inc., 375 U.S. 301, 304–05 (1964). Uzuegbunam’s graduation and the college’s permanent policy change eliminate any such prospect. Preiser v. Newkirk, 422 U.S. 395, 402–03 (1975) (applying earlier authorities); see also St. Pierre v. United States, 319 U.S. 41, 42–43 (1943).

Because the complaint alleged no actual injury beyond the abstract violation of constitutional rights, the request for nominal damages added nothing to the justiciability analysis. The district court therefore properly dismissed the action, and the judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
