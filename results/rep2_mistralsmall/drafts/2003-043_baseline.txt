**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE IRS MUST INDIVIDUALLY ASSESS EACH PARTNER TO EXTEND THE STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS FOR COLLECTION.**

The Internal Revenue Code requires the IRS to assess tax liabilities against each taxpayer within three years of the return’s filing to extend the limitations period for collection. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a) (2000); *United States v. Coson*, 286 F.2d 453, 458 (9th Cir. 1961). A "taxpayer" includes "an individual," 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14), and the IRS’s assessment must "provide identification of the taxpayer," 26 C.F.R. § 301.6203-1 (2003). Here, the IRS assessed the partnership—not the individual partners—so the statute of limitations for collecting from the partners remained the original three years. *See Coson*, 286 F.2d at 458 (holding that an assessment must name the taxpayer to be valid).

The IRS’s reliance on *United States v. Wright*, 57 F.3d 561 (7th Cir. 1995), is misplaced. In *Wright*, the IRS had assessed both the partnership *and* the individual partners, extending the limitations period for all. *Id.* at 562. Here, no such individualized assessment occurred. The Ninth Circuit has consistently required separate assessments for each taxpayer to extend the limitations period. *See id.*; *Coson*, 286 F.2d at 458.

**II. CALIFORNIA PARTNERSHIP LAW DOES NOT ALLOW COLLECTION WITHOUT A JUDGMENT AGAINST THE PARTNER.**

Under California law, a judgment against a partnership does not automatically bind a partner’s assets. *See* Cal. Corp. Code § 16307(c) (West 2002); *Witkin, Summary of California Law* § 60V (9th ed. 2001). The IRS must first obtain a judgment against the partner to collect. *Id.* Here, the IRS neither assessed the partners nor obtained a judgment, so its claims are unenforceable. *See Coson*, 286 F.2d at 458 (requiring an assessment to enforce liability).

**III. THE IRS’S CLAIMS ARE UNENFORCEABLE IN BANKRUPTCY.**

The Bankruptcy Code disallows claims that are "unenforceable against the debtor." 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1) (2000). Since the IRS failed to assess or obtain a judgment against the partners, its claims are unenforceable. *See Coson*, 286 F.2d at 458; *In re First T.D. & Inv., Inc.*, 253 F.3d 520, 526 (9th Cir. 2001) (reviewing de novo).

**CONCLUSION**

The IRS’s failure to individually assess the partners or obtain a judgment against them bars collection. The Ninth Circuit’s precedents and the Code require individualized assessments to extend the limitations period. The IRS’s claims are unenforceable in bankruptcy. The judgment below should be affirmed.
