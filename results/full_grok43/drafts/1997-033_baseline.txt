**ARGUMENT**

Section 1407(a) provides that actions transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings “shall be remanded by the panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). The word “shall” creates a mandatory obligation. Anderson v. Yungkau, 329 U.S. 482, 485 (1947) (“shall” is “the language of command”). Once pretrial proceedings end, the statute leaves the transferee court no authority to retain the case for trial.

Section 1404(a) supplies no contrary power. That provision authorizes transfer only when venue and convenience considerations permit; it does not repeal or qualify the express remand command of § 1407(a). Where a specific statute addresses a subject, a general statute does not control. Morton v. Mancari, 417 U.S. 535, 550-51 (1974). Section 1407(a) is the specific statute governing multidistrict litigation and its terminal step; § 1404(a) cannot be invoked to nullify the required remand.

The Judicial Panel’s Rule 14(b), which purports to authorize self-transfer under § 1404(a), cannot enlarge the statutory grant. An administrative rule inconsistent with the governing statute is invalid. See, e.g., United States v. Larionoff, 431 U.S. 864, 873 (1977). The Panel’s authority is limited to “coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings,” after which remand is compulsory.

Allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial would also undermine the venue rights preserved by § 1407. Plaintiffs select the original forum; the statute permits only temporary displacement for pretrial purposes. See Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612, 622 (1964) (transfer statutes do not alter substantive rights attached to proper venue). The district court’s order assigning the case to itself for trial therefore exceeded its authority under § 1407(a).
