The District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2), does not provide a basis for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court should affirm the judgment of the Court of Appeals.

The plain language of Rule 32 does not specify the consequences of a court's failure to advise a defendant of their right to appeal. However, this Court has established that not every violation of the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure automatically entitles a defendant to relief. See, e.g., Hill v. Lockhart, 474 U.S. 52 (1985) (holding that a defendant must demonstrate prejudice resulting from counsel's deficient performance to obtain habeas relief).

In this case, the District Court found that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing, despite the court's failure to advise him. This finding is consistent with the principle that a defendant's knowledge of their rights can mitigate the effects of a court's error. See Canizio v. New York, 327 U.S. 82 (1946) (holding that a defendant's awareness of their rights can render a court's failure to advise them harmless).

The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation, concluding that the rule's purpose had been served since Peguero was aware of his right to appeal. This approach is consistent with this Court's precedent, which emphasizes the importance of considering the actual prejudice suffered by a defendant. See Lane v. Williams, 455 U.S. 624 (1982) (holding that a habeas petitioner must demonstrate ongoing prejudice resulting from a constitutional violation).

Peguero's claim relies heavily on the idea that a Rule 32 violation is per se grounds for habeas relief. However, this Court has rejected a similar argument in the context of other procedural rules. See, e.g., Swain v. Pressley, 430 U.S. 372 (1977) (holding that a habeas petitioner must demonstrate that a statutory remedy is "inadequate or ineffective" to warrant relief).

In the absence of any prejudice resulting from the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal, habeas relief is not warranted. The Court should affirm the judgment of the Court of Appeals and hold that a Rule 32 violation does not automatically entitle a defendant to relief when they were aware of their right to appeal and elected not to exercise it.
