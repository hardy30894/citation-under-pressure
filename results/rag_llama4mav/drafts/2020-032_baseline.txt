The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that a constitutional challenge to a school policy seeking nominal damages is rendered moot when the unconstitutional policy is revised during litigation. The mootness doctrine is rooted in Article III's requirement that federal courts only decide live cases or controversies. When GGC revised its "Freedom of Expression Policy" and removed the challenged portion of its "Student Code of Conduct," it rendered the claims for declaratory and injunctive relief moot, as there was no longer a live controversy regarding the prior policies.

The Eleventh Circuit's decision in Flanigan's Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs, 868 F.3d 1248 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc), controls this case. In Flanigan's, the court held that a prayer for nominal damages generally will not save an otherwise moot challenge to an allegedly unconstitutional policy or law. Uzuegbunam and Bradford's complaint, which sought only nominal damages in addition to declaratory and injunctive relief, falls squarely within the Flanigan's holding.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that a case becomes moot when the challenged conduct ceases and there is no reasonable expectation that it will recur. See, e.g., Princeton University v. Schmid, 455 U.S. 100 (1982); Iron Arrow Honor Society v. Heckler, 464 U.S. 67 (1983). GGC's revision of its policies and Uzuegbunam's graduation rendered the claims for declaratory and injunctive relief moot, and the nominal damages claim cannot save the case.

The district court correctly applied Flanigan's when it dismissed the case as moot. Uzuegbunam and Bradford's arguments that their case is distinguishable from Flanigan's are unpersuasive. The district court also did not abuse its discretion in denying leave to amend, as Appellants failed to properly seek amendment via a filed motion and did not specify the substance of their proposed amendment.

The judgment of the Eleventh Circuit should be affirmed, as the district court correctly applied the mootness doctrine and the holding in Flanigan's. The revision of GGC's policies and Uzuegbunam's graduation rendered the case moot, and the nominal damages claim is insufficient to save the case.
