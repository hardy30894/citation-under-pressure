**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE RELEASE DOES NOT BAR BANCORP’S CLAIMS BECAUSE ITS LANGUAGE IS NARROWLY LIMITED TO CLAIMS “ARISING FROM OR WITH RESPECT TO THE DECISION … TO CLOSE THE BANK.”**

A release is a contract whose scope is determined by its plain language. *See Bank of Am. Nat’l Trust & Sav. Ass’n v. Gillaizeau*, 766 F.2d 709, 712 (2d Cir. 1985). The release executed by Bancorp discharges the FDIC only from claims “arising from or with respect to the decision of the [OCC] to close the Bank on June 21, 1985.” This language is unambiguous and must be given its ordinary meaning. *See W.W.W. Assocs., Inc. v. Giancontieri*, 77 N.Y.2d 157, 162 (1990).

Bancorp’s claims—for unjust enrichment from the FDIC’s payment of post-insolvency interest, breach of fiduciary duty in selling assets below “true franchise value,” waste of corporate assets, and inadequate accounting—do not arise from the OCC’s *decision* to close the Bank. Rather, they challenge the FDIC’s *post-closure conduct* as receiver. The OCC, not the FDIC, made the decision to close the Bank. *See Golden Pac. Bancorp v. Clarke*, 837 F.2d 509, 511 (D.C. Cir. 1988). The release’s narrow language cannot be stretched to immunize the FDIC’s independent, post-receivership actions. *Cf. Consarc Corp. v. Marine Midland Bank, N.A.*, 996 F.2d 568, 573 (2d Cir. 1993) (releases must be construed strictly against the drafter). The FDIC drafted the release; any ambiguity must be resolved against it. *See Jacobson v. Sassower*, 66 N.Y.2d 638, 639 (1985).

**II. THE STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS DID NOT BEGIN TO RUN UNTIL THE RECEIVERSHIP TERMINATED ON NOVEMBER 1, 1995.**

New York’s six-year statute of limitations for unjust enrichment, breach of fiduciary duty, and corporate waste, N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 213(7), does not accrue until the plaintiff has a complete and present cause of action. *See Kronos, Inc. v. AVX Corp.*, 81 N.Y.2d 90, 94 (1993). For claims against a receiver, the limitations period runs from the termination of the receivership, because only then can the plaintiff ascertain the full extent of the receiver’s alleged misconduct. *See FDIC v. Bracero*, 695 F. Supp. 711, 714 (E.D.N.Y. 1988) (citing *Matter of Union Bank of Brooklyn*, 178 N.Y. 1, 6 (1904)). The FDIC terminated the receivership on November 1, 1995; Bancorp filed suit on October 31, 1995—one day before. The action is timely.

The FDIC’s self-payment of post-insolvency interest began on March 15, 1991, but Bancorp could not have known the full scope of the FDIC’s alleged self-dealing until the receivership ended. *See Zola v. Gordon*, 601 F.2d 942, 944 (2d Cir. 1979) (statute of limitations does not begin until the plaintiff discovers, or should have discovered, the injury). Moreover, the FDIC’s fiduciary duty to Bancorp as a shareholder continued throughout the receivership. *See FDIC v. Bierman*, 2 F.3d 1424, 1435 (7th Cir. 1993) (receiver owes fiduciary duties to creditors and shareholders). A fiduciary’s self-dealing is a continuing wrong, and the statute of limitations does not begin until the fiduciary relationship ends. *See Golden Pac. Bancorp v. FDIC*, 273 F.3d 509, 518 (2d Cir. 2001) (recognizing that the limitations period may be tolled during a receivership).

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN ALTERNATIVELY HOLDING THAT THE RELEASE BARS ALL CLAIMS.**

Even if the release were ambiguous, extrinsic evidence shows that the parties intended it to cover only claims related to the OCC’s closure decision, not the FDIC’s subsequent conduct. *See Hartford Accident & Indem. Co. v. Wesolowski*, 33 N.Y.2d 169, 172 (1973). The release was executed as part of a settlement of the *Chuang* and *May 1986* actions, neither of which involved Bancorp’s current claims. *See FDIC v. Chuang*, 690 F. Supp. 192, 193 (S.D.N.Y. 1988). The FDIC cannot now expand the release’s scope beyond what was bargained for. *See Mangini v. McClurg*, 24 N.Y.2d 556, 563 (1969).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
