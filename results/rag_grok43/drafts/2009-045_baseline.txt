**ARGUMENT**

The lodestar figure calculated under a federal fee-shifting statute such as 42 U.S.C. § 1988 may be enhanced for superior performance and exceptional results in rare and exceptional cases when supported by specific evidence. *Blum v. Stenson*, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984), held that “an upward adjustment may be justified in the ‘rare case where the fee applicant offers specific evidence to show that the quality of service rendered was superior to that one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged’ and resulted in ‘exceptional’ success.” The Court expressly rejected the argument “that an upward adjustment to an attorney’s fee is never appropriate under § 1988.” *Id.* at 901.

*Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air*, 478 U.S. 546 (1986), reaffirmed this principle. The Court stated that “upward adjustments of the lodestar figure are still permissible … in certain ‘rare’ and ‘exceptional’ cases, supported by both ‘specific evidence’ on the record and detailed findings by the lower courts.” *Id.* at 565 (quoting *Blum*, 465 U.S. at 898-901). It further observed that “the lodestar figure includes most, if not all, of the relevant factors constituting a ‘reasonable’ attorney’s fee,” yet left open the possibility of enhancement “for superior performance” in extraordinary circumstances. *Id.* at 566.

Subsequent decisions confirm that quality-of-representation and results-obtained enhancements remain available on this narrow basis. *See City of Burlington v. Dague*, 505 U.S. 557, 562-63 (1992) (distinguishing permissible performance enhancements from impermissible contingency enhancements); *Hensley v. Eckerhart*, 461 U.S. 424, 435 (1983) (“in some cases of exceptional success an enhancement award may be justified”).

Here the district court made detailed findings, based on its observation of counsel throughout the litigation and comparison with prevailing Atlanta rates, that the representation was “stellar” and produced results “far exceeding” what the lodestar alone captured. These findings satisfy *Blum*’s evidentiary requirement. Every circuit to address the issue has recognized that such enhancements are permissible in rare cases. *See, e.g.*, *Geier v. Sundquist*, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); *Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co.*, 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000).

Because the question presented asks only whether enhancement is ever permissible on these grounds, the answer is yes. The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
