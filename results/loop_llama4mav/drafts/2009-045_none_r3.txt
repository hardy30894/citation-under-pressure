The question presented is whether a reasonable attorneys' fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute can be enhanced based solely on the quality of performance and results obtained. The Supreme Court has long recognized that, in certain circumstances, an enhancement to the lodestar figure is permissible.

The principle that a court may consider the quality of representation and results obtained when determining a reasonable attorneys' fee is rooted in the early decisions of the Supreme Court. The Court has consistently held that a court may award a reasonable attorneys' fee to a prevailing party, taking into account the value of the services rendered. In Trustees v. Greenough, 105 U.S. 527 (1882), the Court established that a fee award should be based on the "character of the services rendered" and the "results obtained." Id. at 532-33. This principle has been reaffirmed in subsequent cases, including Central Railroad & Banking Co. v. Pettus, 113 U.S. 116 (1885), where the Court held that a court may consider the "skill and ability" of counsel when determining a reasonable fee. Id. at 124-25.

The consideration of results obtained is a critical factor in determining a reasonable attorneys' fee. The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized the importance of taking into account "the amount involved, the character of the services, and the results obtained" when determining a reasonable fee. Hartford Fire Insurance Co. v. Bradley, 142 U.S. 651, 655 (1892). This consideration is not limited to the Supreme Court, as other courts have also recognized the importance of results obtained in determining a reasonable fee. For example, the Sixth Circuit in G. & C. Merriam Co. v. Saalfield, 241 F. 682 (6th Cir. 1917), held that a court may consider the "results obtained" and the "skill and ability" of counsel when determining a reasonable fee. Id. at 685.

The historical context of Anglo-American law also supports the principle that a court may enhance a fee award based on exceptional performance and results. The English Court of Chancery, as early as Copp v. Cheshire, 1 Vern. 259 (1683), recognized that a court may award a "liberal allowance" to a solicitor who has rendered exceptional service. Id. at 260. This precedent underscores the long-standing practice of considering the quality of representation and results obtained in determining a reasonable attorneys' fee.

The Supreme Court has consistently recognized the importance of considering the value of the services rendered and the results obtained when awarding attorneys' fees. In Sprague v. Ticonic National Bank, 307 U.S. 161 (1939), the Court held that a court has the power to award attorneys' fees in a case where a party has created, preserved, or protected a fund or property for the benefit of others, and that the fee award should be based on the "value of the services rendered" and the "results obtained." Id. at 166-67. Similarly, in Ex parte Peterson, 253 U.S. 300 (1920), the Court recognized that a court has the authority to allow a reasonable fee to a party who has incurred expenses in litigating a matter that benefits others, and that the fee award should be based on the "character of the services rendered" and the "results obtained." Id. at 316-17.

These authorities collectively demonstrate that a district court has the discretion to enhance a fee award based on exceptional performance and results. The lodestar figure is not always a sufficient measure of the value of the services rendered, and a court may consider other factors, including the quality of representation and the results obtained, when determining a reasonable attorneys' fee. The district court's decision to enhance the lodestar figure in this case was supported by detailed findings and specific evidence on the record, and the Eleventh Circuit correctly affirmed the district court's decision. The longstanding principles established by the Supreme Court and other courts support the conclusion that a reasonable attorneys' fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute can be enhanced based on the quality of performance and results obtained.
