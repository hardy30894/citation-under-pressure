A. Constitutionality of 18 U.S.C. § 1951

Congress lacks authority under the Commerce Clause to criminalize the robbery at issue. The Hobbs Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1951, reaches only conduct that affects interstate commerce. As construed, however, the statute criminalizes a purely local robbery whose connection to interstate commerce is both attenuated and insubstantial.

The Supreme Court has repeatedly held that Congress may regulate intrastate activity only when that activity “substantially affect[s] interstate commerce.” United States v. Lopez, 514 U.S. 549, 559 (1995). The Court has rejected the view that “Congress may regulate any activity that it found was related to the economic productivity of individual citizens,” because such a rule would “convert congressional authority under the Commerce Clause to a general police power of the sort retained by the States.” Id. at 564. The robbery of a single restaurant in Wichita does not satisfy this substantial-effects test. The government’s proof—that the restaurant purchased some ingredients from out-of-state suppliers and that its business declined after the crime—establishes at most an indirect and remote relationship to interstate commerce. That relationship is indistinguishable from the “but-for” causal chain the Court found insufficient in Lopez. Id. at 567.

Nor can the conviction be sustained on the theory that Congress may regulate an entire class of robberies. The Lopez Court expressly distinguished statutes that regulate economic activity from those that criminalize “violent crime” lacking any commercial nexus. Id. at 561. Section 1951, as applied here, falls on the latter side of that line. Because the statute contains no jurisdictional element that would ensure, “through case-by-case inquiry,” that the particular robbery affects interstate commerce in a substantial way, id. at 561, the conviction exceeds the limits of Article I.

The district court therefore erred in denying the motion to dismiss Count 1. This Court should reverse the conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 1951.
