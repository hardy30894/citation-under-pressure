**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE OCSLA EXTENDS LONGSHORE ACT COVERAGE ONLY TO INJURIES OCCURRING ON THE OUTER CONTINENTAL SHELF ITSELF.**

The plain text of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act ("OCSLA") limits its extension of Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act ("LHWCA") coverage to injuries "occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf." 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) (emphasis added). The statute’s use of the phrase "occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf" does not evince Congress’s intent to extend coverage to injuries occurring on land, no matter how closely connected to extractive operations on the Shelf. *See* *United States v. Spelar*, 338 U.S. 217, 222 (1949) ("[W]here the words are clear and unambiguous, they must be given their plain and ordinary meaning."); *Caminetti v. United States*, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917) ("[W]here the language is plain and admits of no more than one meaning the duty of interpretation does not arise, and the rules which are to aid doubtful meanings need no discussion.").

This Court has long held that statutory coverage provisions must be strictly construed in favor of the employer. *See* *Baltimore & Philadelphia Steamboat Co. v. Norton*, 285 F.2d 461, 463 (3d Cir. 1960) ("The Longshoremen’s and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act is to be strictly construed in favor of the employer."); *accord* *Nardone v. United States*, 302 U.S. 379, 384 (1937) ("[S]tatutes creating a duty or imposing an obligation are to be strictly construed."). The OCSLA’s extension of LHWCA coverage is no exception. *See* *Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray*, 470 U.S. 414, 424 (1985) (noting that OCSLA coverage is "geographically limited" and that "the inconsistent coverage here results primarily from the explicit geographic limitation to [OCSLA’s] incorporation of the LHWCA").

The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs*, 877 F.2d 356 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc), correctly applies this principle. The court held that OCSLA coverage requires both a "but for" causal connection between the injury and operations on the Shelf *and* that the injury occur on the Shelf itself. *Id.* at 362. This interpretation is consistent with the statute’s plain language and the longstanding rule that coverage provisions are strictly construed. *See* *Baltimore & Philadelphia Steamboat Co.*, 285 F.2d at 463; *Nardone*, 302 U.S. at 384.

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary holding in *Valladolid v. Pacific Operations Offshore, LLP*, 6933 F.3d 6933 (9th Cir. 2010), errs by reading a "substantial nexus" test into the statute. This test finds no support in the OCSLA’s text or in this Court’s precedents. *See* *Spelar*, 338 U.S. at 222; *Caminetti*, 242 U.S. at 485. The statute’s coverage is limited to injuries "occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf," not injuries merely connected to such operations. *See* 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The Ninth Circuit’s interpretation would render the statute’s geographic limitation meaningless, as virtually any injury could be tenuously linked to Shelf operations. *See* *Herb’s Welding*, 470 U.S. at 424.

**II. THE SUPREME COURT’S DICTUM IN *OFFSHORE LOGISTICS, INC. v. TALLENTIRE* DOES NOT DICTATE A DIFFERENT RESULT.**

The Ninth Circuit relied heavily on a footnote in *Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire*, 477 U.S. 207 (1986), to support its "substantial nexus" test. *See* *Valladolid*, 6933 F.3d at 6942. However, the footnote in *Tallentire* is non-binding dictum and does not address the issue presented here. *See* *Tallentire*, 477 U.S. at 219 n.2 ("[Section 1333(b)] has no bearing on this case."); *accord* *Humphrey’s Executor v. United States*, 295 U.S. 602, 627 (1935) ("[G]eneral expressions in every opinion are to be taken in connection with the case in which those expressions are used.").

The footnote merely observes that § 1333(b) imposes a status requirement (i.e., that the injured party be an "employee") but does not address whether a situs requirement applies. *See* *Tallentire*, 477 U.S. at 219 n.2. The Court did not analyze the issue, and the parties did not brief it. *Id.* This Court has repeatedly cautioned against affording dispositive weight to unconsidered dicta. *See* *District of Columbia v. Heller*, 128 S. Ct. 2783, 2816 n.25 (2008) (noting that dicta is not controlling); *Montero-Camargo*, 208 F.3d at 1132 n.17 (same).

Moreover, the footnote’s reference to an OCSLA "situs requirement" is ambiguous. It does not specify whether the requirement is for the *injury* to occur on the Shelf or merely for the *operations* to occur there. *See* *Tallentire*, 477 U.S. at 219 n.2. The statute’s text resolves this ambiguity: it covers injuries "occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf," which plainly requires the injury to occur on the Shelf. *See* 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b); *Spelar*, 338 U.S. at 222; *Caminetti*, 242 U.S. at 485.

**III. THE LHWCA’S COVERAGE IS LIMITED TO INJURIES OCCURRING ON NAVIGABLE WATERS OR ADJOINING AREAS.**

Even if OCSLA coverage extended to land-based injuries, Valladolid’s injury would not qualify for LHWCA benefits because it did not occur on a maritime situs. The LHWCA’s coverage is limited to injuries "occurring upon the navigable waters of the United States" or "adjoining areas." 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). This Court has consistently held that the "adjoining area" provision requires a functional relationship to maritime activities. *See* *Brady-Hamilton Stevedore Co. v. Herron*, 568 F.2d 137, 141 (9th Cir. 1978) (citing *Norton v. Warner Co.*, 321 F.2d 171, 175 (3d Cir. 1963)).

La Conchita, where Valladolid was killed, is not an "adjoining area" within the meaning of the LHWCA. It is separated from the water by a highway and railroad tracks, has no direct access to a pier or dock, and is three miles from the nearest maritime facility. *See* *Motoviloff v. Director, Office of Workers’ Comp. Programs*, 692 F.2d 87, 89 (9th Cir. 1982) (holding that a facility separated from navigable waters by a highway and railroad tracks is not an adjoining area). The scrap metal handling at La Conchita was not an integral part of maritime commerce but rather a tangential, land-based activity. *See* *Herb’s Welding*, 470 U.S. at 424 (noting that coverage turns on the "geographic limitation" of the operations).

**CONCLUSION**

The OCSLA’s extension of LHWCA coverage is limited to injuries occurring on the outer Continental Shelf itself. The Ninth Circuit’s "substantial nexus" test is unsupported by the statute’s text or this Court’s precedents. Even if OCSLA coverage extended to land-based injuries, Valladolid’s injury would not qualify for LHWCA benefits because it did not occur on a maritime situs. The judgment below should be affirmed.
