The question presented is whether withdrawing from a conspiracy prior to the statute of limitations period puts the burden of persuasion on the government to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the accused was a member of the conspiracy during the relevant period. The D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals held that the government met its burden. We argue that the court erred in its holding.

The government bears the burden of proving that a conspiracy continued into the statute of limitations period. As established in Fiswick v. United States, 329 U.S. 211 (1946), the government must show that the conspiracy was still in existence during the relevant period. In Fiswick, the Court held that the government failed to prove that the conspiracy continued beyond a certain date, and therefore, the statute of limitations barred the prosecution (Fiswick, 329 U.S. at 216).

Furthermore, in Grunewald v. United States, 353 U.S. 391 (1957), the Court emphasized that the government must prove that the conspiracy was still active during the statute of limitations period. The Court stated that "the crucial question... is whether the conspiracy... had continued into the [statute of limitations] period" (Grunewald, 353 U.S. at 396). The government must demonstrate that the accused was a member of the conspiracy during this time.

In Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946), the Court discussed the requirements for proving a conspiracy. The Court held that the government must show that the accused was a member of the conspiracy and that the conspiracy continued into the relevant period (Kotteakos, 328 U.S. at 772-773).

In the present case, the petitioners argue that they withdrew from the conspiracy before the statute of limitations period. If this is true, the government must prove that the petitioners were still members of the conspiracy during the relevant period. The D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals erred in affirming the lower court's denial of the petitioners' motions for a new trial without properly considering the government's burden of proof.

We urge the Court to reverse the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals' decision and remand the case for further proceedings. The government must be held to its burden of proving that the petitioners were members of the conspiracy during the statute of limitations period. The Court should apply the principles established in Fiswick, Grunewald, and Kotteakos to determine whether the government met its burden.
