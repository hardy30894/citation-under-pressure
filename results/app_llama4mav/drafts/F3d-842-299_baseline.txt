Argument

I. The District Court Lacked Subject Matter Jurisdiction Over This Case.

The district court's denial of the Pressls' motion to remand was erroneous because neither 28 U.S.C. § 1331 nor 16 U.S.C. § 825p conferred subject matter jurisdiction over this case.

A. The Complaint Does Not Arise Under Federal Law.

Federal question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 is lacking because the Pressls' complaint asserts only state law claims. The complaint seeks a declaration of the parties' rights under a flowage easement, which is a matter of state property law. See Virginia Elec. & Power Co. v. Haden, 203 Va. 693, 127 S.E.2d 374 (1962). The fact that APCO operates the Smith Mountain hydroelectric project under a FERC license does not transform the Pressls' state law claims into federal questions. As the Supreme Court has held, "the mere presence of a FERC license does not automatically confer federal jurisdiction over all disputes related to the licensed project." See California v. FERC, 495 U.S. 490, 497 (1990). The Pressls' complaint does not raise a substantial federal question, and therefore, the district court lacked jurisdiction under § 1331. See Gunn v. Minton, 133 S. Ct. 1059, 1065 (2013) (federal jurisdiction requires a "substantial" federal issue).

B. 16 U.S.C. § 825p Does Not Provide an Independent Basis for Jurisdiction.

The district court also erred in concluding that 16 U.S.C. § 825p provided an independent basis for jurisdiction. Section 825p grants federal courts jurisdiction over suits arising under the Federal Power Act ("FPA"). However, the Pressls' complaint does not arise under the FPA; it is a state law property dispute. APCO's reliance on § 825p is misplaced because the statute does not confer jurisdiction over state law claims that merely relate to a FERC-licensed project. See Panola Land Buyers Ass'n v. Shuman, 762 F.2d 1550, 1556 (11th Cir. 1985) (holding that § 825p does not provide jurisdiction over state law claims that do not arise under the FPA).

II. The District Court's Dismissal of the Complaint Must Be Vacated.

Because the district court lacked subject matter jurisdiction, its dismissal of the Pressls' complaint must be vacated. See Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment, 523 U.S. 83, 94 (1998) ("Without jurisdiction the court cannot proceed at all in any cause."). The case should be remanded to the district court with instructions to remand it to state court. See 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c).

In conclusion, the district court erred in denying the Pressls' motion to remand and dismissing their complaint. This Court should vacate the district court's order and remand the case with instructions to remand it to state court.
