**ARGUMENT**

**I. Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial**

The plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) requires that any action transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." This statutory command is unambiguous and admits of no exception. *See* *United States v. Oregon*, 295 U.S. 1, 28 (1935) ("Where the terms of a statute are plain and unambiguous, the courts are not at liberty to look beyond the statute to ascertain its meaning."); *Caminetti v. United States*, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917) ("[W]here the language is plain and admits of no more than one meaning the duty of interpretation does not arise."). The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation has no authority to retain transferred actions for trial, nor may a transferee court invoke § 1404(a) to circumvent the remand requirement. *See* *In re United States Steel Corp.*, 479 F.2d 489, 492 (3d Cir. 1973) (holding that § 1407(a) "unambiguously requires remand" and rejecting the argument that § 1404(a) permits retention for trial); *In re Air Crash Disaster Near Chicago, Ill.*, 309 F.Supp. 1115, 1117 (J.P.M.L. 1970) ("The Panel’s authority is limited to pretrial proceedings; it has no power to retain a case for trial.").

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810 (3d Cir. 1982)—cited by the court below—is erroneous and unsupported by pre-1970 authority. *See* *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971) (acknowledging that § 1407(a) "requires remand" but justifying retention for trial as a matter of "practical necessity"); *In re Air Crash Disaster Near Hanover, N.H.*, 342 F.Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H. 1971) ("I must recognize candidly that there is nothing in the language of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) or § 1404(a) which directly allows, or even suggests, that the transferee judge has the power to transfer cases to his district, or any district, for purposes of trial."). These cases improperly elevate judicial convenience over statutory text. *See* *United States v. Shreveport Grain & Elevator Co.*, 287 U.S. 77, 82 (1932) ("The power of the federal courts to deal with matters brought before them is limited by the Constitution and by the laws passed in pursuance thereof.").

**II. Section 1404(a) Does Not Authorize a Transferee Court to Retain a Case for Trial**

Section 1404(a) permits transfer of a case "for the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice" to "any other district or division where it might have been brought." However, this provision does not empower a transferee court to retain a case for trial after the Panel has ordered remand under § 1407(a). *See* *Ex parte Collett*, 337 U.S. 55, 61 (1949) ("The power of a court to transfer a case to another district is limited by the terms of the statute under which the transfer is made."); *Goldlawr, Inc. v. Heiman*, 369 U.S. 463, 466 (1962) (holding that § 1404(a) "is not a grant of authority to transfer a case to a district where it could not have been brought originally"). A transferee court’s authority under § 1404(a) is derivative of the Panel’s transfer order, which is itself limited to pretrial proceedings. *See* *United States v. National City Lines*, 337 U.S. 78, 80 (1949) ("The power to transfer is not an independent grant of jurisdiction but is limited to the purposes for which it was conferred.").

The cases relied upon by the court below—*Pfizer*, 447 F.2d at 125, and *In re Fine Paper*, 685 F.2d at 820—are inapposite. They misread § 1404(a) as permitting a transferee court to "transfer" a case to itself for trial, but this interpretation ignores the statutory text and the Panel’s exclusive authority under § 1407(a). *See* *Chicago, R.I. & P.R. Co. v. Schwyhart*, 227 U.S. 184, 194 (1913) ("A statute should be construed so as to give effect to all its provisions, so that no part will be inoperative or superfluous."). The Panel’s remand order is mandatory, and a transferee court may not use § 1404(a) to frustrate it. *See* *United States v. Rice*, 327 U.S. 742, 749 (1946) ("Where a statute commands that something be done, it is ordinarily to be done.").

**III. The Panel’s Rule 14(b) Cannot Expand the Court’s Statutory Authority**

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation’s Rule 14(b)—which purports to authorize transferee courts to retain cases for trial—is ultra vires. *See* *Marbury v. Madison*, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137, 177 (1803) ("An act of the Legislature repugnant to the Constitution is void."). The Panel has no authority to adopt rules that contradict the plain terms of § 1407(a). *See* *United States v. Smith*, 286 U.S. 7, 12 (1932) ("The power to make rules of practice and procedure is not a power to alter substantive rights."). Rule 14(b) is therefore invalid to the extent it purports to authorize retention for trial. *See* *United States v. Shreveport Grain & Elevator Co.*, 287 U.S. at 82 ("The power of the federal courts to deal with matters brought before them is limited by the Constitution and by the laws passed in pursuance thereof.").

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the judgment below and hold that § 1407(a) mandates remand to the transferor district for trial. The transferee court lacked authority to invoke § 1404(a) to retain the case.
