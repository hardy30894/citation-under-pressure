**ARGUMENT**

Petitioner respectfully submits that the Ninth Circuit erred in affirming the district court’s grant of summary judgment in favor of Respondent Department of the Navy. The lower courts misapplied FOIA Exemption 2, 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2), by construing it to shield documents that are not “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” The Navy’s claimed exemption fails under the plain text of the statute and controlling Supreme Court precedent.

**I. FOIA Exemption 2 Applies Only to Documents “Related Solely to the Internal Personnel Rules and Practices of an Agency.”**

The text of Exemption 2 is unambiguous: it exempts only matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2) (emphasis added). This Court has long held that exemptions under FOIA must be narrowly construed. *Weiss v. United States*, 308 U.S. 169, 173 (1939) (“Exemptions from disclosure are to be narrowly construed.”). The Supreme Court has emphasized that FOIA’s exemptions are “explicitly exclusive,” meaning they must be applied strictly and not expanded beyond their plain meaning. *Adm’r, FAA v. Robertson*, 422 U.S. 255, 262 (1975) (quoting S. Rep. No. 89-813, at 3 (1965)).

The Navy’s ESQD arc maps and related documents are not “internal personnel rules and practices.” They are operational safety data used to design and secure ammunition storage facilities. As such, they fall outside the scope of Exemption 2. *See Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 360-61 (1976) (holding that FOIA reflects a “general philosophy of full agency disclosure unless information is exempted under clearly delineated statutory language”).

**II. The Navy’s Interpretation of Exemption 2 Violates the Statute’s Plain Meaning and Legislative History.**

The legislative history of FOIA confirms that Exemption 2 was intended to cover only mundane matters such as parking regulations, lunch hours, and sick leave—not operational safety data. *See* S. Rep. No. 89-813, at 8 (1965) (explaining that Exemption 2 was designed to protect “trivial matters” such as “parking facilities, lunch hours, and sick leave”). The Supreme Court has recognized that Exemption 2 was not intended to shield sensitive operational information. *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 363 (citing the Senate Report’s narrow construction of Exemption 2).

The Navy’s attempt to expand Exemption 2 to cover ESQD arc maps—documents that are neither personnel rules nor practices—is inconsistent with the statute’s plain meaning and legislative intent. *See id.* at 361-62 (holding that FOIA exemptions must be narrowly construed and strictly applied).

**III. The Navy’s Reliance on “Predominantly Internal” Standard is Contrary to Supreme Court Precedent.**

The Ninth Circuit adopted a “predominantly internal” standard for Exemption 2, but this standard has no basis in Supreme Court precedent. The Supreme Court has repeatedly cautioned against expanding FOIA exemptions beyond their plain text. *See Robertson*, 422 U.S. at 262 (holding that exemptions must be narrowly construed and explicitly exclusive). The “predominantly internal” test is inconsistent with the statute’s requirement that documents be “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2).

Moreover, the Supreme Court has rejected attempts to broaden FOIA exemptions under the guise of protecting “internal” matters. *See Rose*, 425 U.S. at 361-62 (rejecting an expansive interpretation of Exemption 6, which, like Exemption 2, is to be narrowly construed). The Navy’s interpretation of Exemption 2 would eviscerate FOIA’s disclosure requirements by allowing agencies to withhold any document they deem “predominantly internal,” regardless of its actual content.

**IV. The Navy’s Documents Are Not Exempt Under Any FOIA Provision.**

The Navy has not demonstrated that the ESQD arc maps fall within any FOIA exemption. Exemption 2 does not apply because the documents are not “internal personnel rules and practices.” Exemption 1 does not apply because the documents are not classified. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(1). Exemption 7(F) does not apply because the documents were not compiled for law enforcement purposes. *See id.* § 552(b)(7)(F).

The Navy’s attempt to withhold these documents under Exemption 2 is an improper expansion of the exemption that conflicts with the statute’s plain text and Supreme Court precedent. The judgment below should be reversed.
