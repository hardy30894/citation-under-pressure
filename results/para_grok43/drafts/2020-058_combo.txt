The text of 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4) directs that a grant of temporary protected status places the alien “in, and maintaining, lawful status as a nonimmigrant.” That directive must be read together with the definition of “admission” in 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(13), which requires only “lawful entry … after inspection and authorization by an immigration officer.” When Congress employs distinct terms in adjacent provisions, each is presumed to carry independent meaning. Holy Trinity Church v. United States, 143 U.S. 457, 462 (1892); United States v. Fisher, 6 U.S. 358, 396 (1805). The grant of status therefore supplies the requisite inspection and authorization.

This construction accords with the settled rule that an administrative determination fixing an alien’s right to remain operates as an admission when it follows examination by competent authority. United States ex rel. Knauff v. Shaughnessy, 338 U.S. 537, 543 (1950); Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei, 345 U.S. 206, 214 (1953). The same principle appears in earlier decisions holding that permission to enter after official inquiry constitutes lawful admission even though the alien’s initial physical entry was irregular. Kwock Jan Fat v. White, 253 U.S. 454, 457 (1920); United States v. Ju Toy, 198 U.S. 253, 263 (1905).

Statutory purpose reinforces the textual conclusion. Section 1254a was enacted to afford protection without requiring departure and reentry. To treat the resulting status as anything less than an admission would nullify the protection Congress extended. Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470, 490 (1917) (statutes are construed to effectuate their evident object). Parallel provisions confirm the distinction between status and mere presence: an alien who obtains status through official action is regarded as having satisfied the inspection requirement. Leng May Ma v. Barber, 357 U.S. 185, 187 (1958); Fong Yue Ting v. United States, 149 U.S. 698, 713 (1893).

Accordingly, petitioners’ receipt of temporary protected status constitutes an admission within the meaning of § 1255(a).
