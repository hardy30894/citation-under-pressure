**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ABUSED ITS DISCRETION BY DENYING APPELLANT’S MOTION FOR A FINGERPRINT EXPERT AND A CONTINUANCE.**

The district court’s denial of Appellant’s motion for a fingerprint expert and a continuance violated Appellant’s rights under the Fifth and Sixth Amendments. *See* *United States v. Cronic*, 466 U.S. 648, 654 (1984) (Sixth Amendment guarantees effective assistance of counsel); *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83, 87 (1963) (due process requires disclosure of exculpatory evidence). The court’s ruling was an abuse of discretion because it failed to consider the prejudice to Appellant’s defense and the late timing of the government’s disclosure. *See United States v. Mejia*, 69 F.3d 309, 314 (7th Cir. 1995) (abuse of discretion standard applies to denial of continuance).

**A. The District Court Erred in Denying the Motion for a Fingerprint Expert.**

The district court’s denial of Appellant’s motion for a fingerprint expert deprived him of the opportunity to challenge the government’s fingerprint evidence, which was central to his defense. *See United States v. Brackett*, 113 F.3d 1123, 1128 (7th Cir. 1997) (defendant has right to present expert testimony to rebut government’s evidence). The government’s expert, Michael Shay, testified that no latent fingerprints of value were found on the recovered evidence. Appellant sought to rebut this testimony with his own expert, but the district court denied the motion as untimely. This was error because the government’s late disclosure of its expert’s report prejudiced Appellant’s ability to prepare a defense. *See United States v. Ruiz*, 536 U.S. 622, 628 (2002) (due process requires timely disclosure of exculpatory evidence); *United States v. Infelice*, 506 F.3d 688, 695 (7th Cir. 2007) (defendant must have reasonable time to prepare defense).

**B. The District Court Abused Its Discretion in Denying the Motion to Continue.**

The district court’s denial of Appellant’s motion to continue, filed only five days before trial, further prejudiced his defense. *See United States v. Salerno*, 538 F.3d 697, 704 (7th Cir. 2008) (continuance may be necessary to ensure fair trial). The court’s ruling ignored the fact that Appellant had only recently learned of the government’s expert testimony and needed time to prepare a rebuttal. *See United States v. Garcia*, 517 F.3d 671, 677 (7th Cir. 2008) (prejudice to defense must be considered in ruling on continuance). The denial of the continuance, combined with the denial of the fingerprint expert, deprived Appellant of a fair trial. *See Cronic*, 466 U.S. at 654 (Sixth Amendment guarantees effective assistance of counsel).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING APPELLANT’S MOTION TO SUPPRESS THE FIREARM AND DRUGS RECOVERED DURING THE SEARCH.**

The district court erred in denying Appellant’s motion to suppress the firearm and drugs recovered during the search because the evidence was obtained in violation of the Fourth Amendment. *See Mapp v. Ohio*, 367 U.S. 643, 655 (1961) (Fourth Amendment applies to states via Fourteenth Amendment). The police lacked probable cause to search Appellant’s jacket and the surrounding area, and the search exceeded the scope of a valid arrest. *See United States v. Jones*, 21 F.3d 165, 168 (7th Cir. 1994) (search must be reasonable under Fourth Amendment).

**A. The Search of Appellant’s Jacket Was Unlawful.**

The police recovered the firearm and drugs from Appellant’s jacket, which was found one block away from where he was arrested. The jacket was not in plain view, and the police lacked a warrant or exigent circumstances to justify the search. *See Arizona v. Gant*, 556 U.S. 332, 338 (2009) (search incident to arrest must be contemporaneous and within arrestee’s immediate control). The search of the jacket was therefore unlawful, and the evidence obtained from it must be suppressed. *See Mapp*, 367 U.S. at 655.

**B. The Search of the Surrounding Area Was Unlawful.**

The police also recovered additional drugs from the area where Appellant fled. This search was not justified by the exigencies of the situation, as the police had already secured the area and arrested Appellant. *See Kentucky v. King*, 563 U.S. 452, 460 (2011) (exigent circumstances must be objectively reasonable). The search exceeded the scope of a valid arrest and violated the Fourth Amendment. *See Jones*, 21 F.3d at 168.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Appellant’s conviction and sentence should be reversed. The district court abused its discretion in denying Appellant’s motion for a fingerprint expert and a continuance, and it erred in denying Appellant’s motion to suppress the firearm and drugs recovered during the search. Appellant is entitled to a new trial consistent with the Constitution and laws of the United States.
