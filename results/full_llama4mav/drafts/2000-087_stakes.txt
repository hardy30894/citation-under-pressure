The post-removal-period statute, 8 U.S.C. § 1231(a)(6), does not authorize the Attorney General to detain a removable alien indefinitely beyond the 90-day removal period. The Supreme Court has held that the statute, when read in light of the Constitution's demands, contains an implicit "reasonable time" limitation. Zadvydas v. Davis, 533 U.S. 678, 682 (2001). The Court's interpretation is grounded in the principle that indefinite detention of aliens who have been admitted to the United States but subsequently ordered removed raises serious constitutional concerns, particularly with respect to substantive due process. Id. at 690-91.

The Court's decision in Zadvydas was driven by the recognition that § 1231(a)(6) applies broadly to aliens ordered removed for various reasons, not just those deemed particularly dangerous. Id. at 691. In contrast to cases involving suspected terrorists or other special circumstances where heightened deference to the political branches may be warranted, the detention of aliens like Zadvydas, who are removable due to criminal convictions, does not implicate the same national security concerns. Id. at 696.

To determine whether detention is authorized, the habeas court must assess whether the detention exceeds a period reasonably necessary to secure removal. Id. at 699. The Court established a presumptively reasonable period of six months, after which the government must respond with evidence sufficient to rebut an alien's showing that there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future. Id. at 701.

In this case, Zadvydas has been detained far beyond the six-month period, and the government has not provided sufficient evidence to rebut his showing that removal is not reasonably foreseeable. The District Court's finding that Zadvydas "will never be deported because there is no place to send him" necessarily implies that he will not be removed in the reasonably foreseeable future. Zadvydas v. Caplinger, 986 F. Supp. 1011, 1027 (E.D. La. 1997).

The government's efforts to effectuate Zadvydas' removal have been unsuccessful, and the prospects for removal remain uncertain. As the Supreme Court noted, the fact that Zadvydas' Lithuanian citizenship reapplication was still pending at the time of the Court's decision underscores the uncertainty surrounding his removal. Zadvydas, 533 U.S. at 687.

In light of the Supreme Court's decision in Zadvydas, the Attorney General's detention of Zadvydas beyond a reasonable period necessary to secure his removal is not authorized by § 1231(a)(6). The judgment of the District Court ordering Zadvydas' release should be affirmed, with the modification that it does not preclude the INS from seeking to return Zadvydas to custody or modify the conditions of his release if there is a substantial likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future.
