ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN ITS ADMONITION TO THE JURY TO DISREGARD TESTIMONY CONCERNING SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES PROPERLY ADMITTED FOR IMPROPER PURPOSES.

The district court initially granted Morbark's motion in limine to exclude evidence of subsequent remedial measures under Rule 407 of the Federal Rules of Evidence. However, when Morbark's counsel opened the door to the issue of subsequent design changes during trial, the court permitted Wood's counsel to rebut the implication that the wood chipper's design had not been altered since the accident. See Towner v. State Farm Fire & Cas. Co., 854 F.2d 1310, 1312 (11th Cir. 1988) (holding that a party who "opens the door" to a particular issue cannot complain when the opposing party seeks to rebut the implication).

During Infinger's cross-examination, Morbark's counsel suggested that the City of DeFuniak Springs was still using the same wood chipper, implying that its design was safe. Wood's counsel was then permitted to ask Infinger about changes made to the machine, revealing that the infeed chute had been lengthened. See Werner v. Upjohn Co., 628 F.2d 848, 856-57 (4th Cir. 1980) (holding that evidence of subsequent remedial measures is admissible for impeachment purposes).

The district court erred in admonishing the jury to disregard Infinger's testimony regarding the subsequent design change. By doing so, the court effectively undermined the impeachment value of the testimony and potentially misled the jury. See United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45, 52 (1984) (noting that impeachment evidence can be crucial in assessing a witness's credibility).

Furthermore, the district court's admonition was insufficient to cure the prejudice caused by Morbark's counsel's earlier statements. See United States v. Simon, 964 F.2d 1082, 1086 (11th Cir. 1992) (holding that a curative instruction may not be sufficient to remedy prejudice caused by improper statements). As a result, the jury was left with an incomplete and misleading understanding of the evidence.

II. RULE 407 DOES NOT BAR EVIDENCE OF SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES IN STRICT PRODUCTS LIABILITY CASES.

The district court's initial ruling excluding evidence of subsequent remedial measures under Rule 407 was erroneous. Rule 407 does not explicitly bar such evidence in strict products liability cases. See Ault v. International Harvester Co., 528 P.2d 1148, 1151-52 (Cal. 1974) (holding that Rule 407 does not apply in strict products liability cases). In fact, many courts have held that evidence of subsequent remedial measures is admissible in strict products liability cases to prove product defect or causation. See, e.g., Sutkowski v. Universal Marion Corp., 281 N.E.2d 749, 752-53 (Ill. App. Ct. 1972); Caprara v. Chrysler Corp., 417 N.E.2d 545, 552-53 (N.Y. 1981); see also Flaminio v. Honda Motor Co., Ltd., 733 F.2d 463, 468-69 (7th Cir. 1984) (noting that the exclusionary rule of Rule 407 may not apply in strict products liability cases).

Moreover, the policy underlying Rule 407 - encouraging defendants to take remedial measures without fear of admission in subsequent litigation - is not implicated in strict products liability cases, where the defendant is typically a manufacturer or supplier, rather than a negligent actor. See Robbins v. Farmers Union Grain Terminal Ass'n, 552 F.2d 788, 793 (8th Cir. 1977) (noting that the policy underlying Rule 407 is not relevant in strict liability cases).

The district court's denial of Wood's motion for a new trial was therefore erroneous. See Hewitt v. B.F. Goodrich Co., 732 F.2d 1554, 1556 (11th Cir. 1984) (holding that a new trial is warranted when the district court's error affects the substantial rights of a party). This Court should reverse the district court's order and remand for a new trial.

In conclusion, the district court erred in its admonition to the jury and in denying Wood's motion for a new trial. Rule 407 does not bar evidence of subsequent remedial measures in strict products liability cases, and such evidence is admissible for impeachment purposes. This Court should reverse the district court's order and remand for a new trial.
