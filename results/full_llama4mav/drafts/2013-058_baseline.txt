The U.S. Bankruptcy Code does not exempt an inherited IRA from a debtor's estate in bankruptcy. The plain language of 11 U.S.C. §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) requires that exempt "retirement funds" be held in a tax-exempt account, but it does not explicitly state that the funds must be the debtor's own retirement savings. However, the Seventh Circuit correctly interpreted the statute to mean that the funds must be held for the debtor's retirement to qualify for the exemption.

As the Seventh Circuit noted, an inherited IRA is subject to different rules under the Internal Revenue Code than a traditional IRA. For example, the beneficiary of an inherited IRA is required to withdraw a minimum amount from the account each year, and the account cannot be rolled over or merged with another account (26 U.S.C. §408(d)(3)(C); 26 U.S.C. §402(c)(11)(A), incorporating 26 U.S.C. §401(a)(9)(B)). This means that the funds in an inherited IRA are not being held for the debtor's retirement, but rather are being distributed to the beneficiary.

The Seventh Circuit's decision is consistent with the purpose of the Bankruptcy Code's exemption provisions, which is to protect debtors' necessary assets, such as retirement savings, from creditor claims. As the court observed, treating an inherited IRA as exempt would "shelter from creditors a pot of money that can be freely used for current consumption" (Clark v. Rameker, 7th Cir., No. 12-1241, at 4). This is not the intended purpose of the exemption.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in In re Chilton, 674 F.3d 486 (5th Cir. 2012), which held that an inherited IRA is exempt under §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12), is distinguishable. The Chilton court focused on the fact that the statute does not explicitly require that the retirement funds be the debtor's own, but it failed to consider the functional differences between an inherited IRA and a traditional IRA.

The Supreme Court has recognized that statutory interpretation requires consideration of the statute's purpose and context (Rodriguez v. United States, 480 U.S. 522, 525-26 (1987)). In this case, the Seventh Circuit's interpretation of §522(b)(3)(C) and (d)(12) is consistent with the purpose of the Bankruptcy Code's exemption provisions and the functional characteristics of inherited IRAs. Therefore, the judgment of the Seventh Circuit should be affirmed.
