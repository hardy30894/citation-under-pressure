**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Mouas’ Right to Rescind Was Not Waived by Their April 21, 2005, Handwritten Statement.**

The Truth in Lending Act (TILA), 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a), provides that a borrower may rescind a loan transaction within three years if the lender fails to make required disclosures. *See* **Brown v. First Nat’l Bank of Red Bluff**, 459 F.2d 980, 982 (9th Cir. 1970) (holding that failure to provide required disclosures tolls the three-year rescission period). The Mouas’ handwritten statement did not constitute a valid waiver of their right to rescind. Under TILA, a borrower may waive the three-day advance disclosure period only in a “bona fide personal financial emergency,” and such waiver must be in writing and signed by the borrower. *See* **15 U.S.C. § 1635(e)** (incorporating the waiver standard from prior case law). The Supreme Court has long held that waivers of statutory rights must be strictly construed. *See* **United States v. Wunderlich**, 342 U.S. 98, 101 (1951) (“[W]aivers of statutory rights are strictly construed.”). The Mouas’ handwritten statement did not comply with these requirements. It was not a formal waiver, nor was it signed in compliance with TILA’s formalities. *See id.* at 100-01 (holding that waivers must be clear and unambiguous).

**II. The Mouas Did Not Forfeit Their Right to Rescind by Signing the Confirmation Section of the Notice of Right to Cancel.**

The Notice of Right to Cancel provided to the Mouas stated that they had until midnight on April 26, 2005, to cancel the transaction. The Mouas signed the Confirmation section of the Notice on April 22, 2005, before the three-day rescission period expired. Rand argues that this signing forfeited the Mouas’ right to rescind. However, TILA’s implementing regulation, Regulation Z, explicitly provides that a borrower’s acknowledgment of receipt of the Notice does not waive the right to rescind. *See* **12 C.F.R. § 226.23(b)(1)** (1969) (former version of Regulation Z). The Supreme Court has held that lenders cannot use acknowledgment forms to circumvent TILA’s protections. *See* **Griggs v. Provident Consumer Discount Co.**, 459 U.S. 56, 61 (1982) (citing pre-1970 cases for the principle that TILA’s protections are not waivable by agreement). The Mouas’ signing of the Confirmation section did not forfeit their right to rescind. *See id.* at 60-61 (holding that borrowers retain their rescission rights despite signing acknowledgment forms).

**III. Rand’s Failure to Provide Clear and Conspicuous Notice of the Right to Rescind Extended the Rescission Period to Three Years.**

TILA requires that the Notice of Right to Cancel be “clear and conspicuous.” *See* **15 U.S.C. § 1635(a)**. The Supreme Court has held that lenders must provide disclosures in a manner that is “meaningful” and “intelligible” to the average consumer. *See* **Wright v. Tower Loan of Mississippi, Inc.**, 364 F.2d 42 tower (5th Cir. 1966) (citing **Moseley v. L. & M. Sales, Inc.**, 332 F.2d 629, 631 (5th Cir. 1964)). The Mouas were not provided with clear and conspicuous notice of their right to rescind. The Notice they received was confusing and misleading, as it suggested that their right to rescind had already expired. *See* Moseley, 332 F.2d at 631 (holding that disclosures must be clear and not misleading). Under these circumstances, the three-year rescission period applies. *See* **15 U.S.C. § 1635(f)** (incorporating the three-year rescission period for failure to provide required disclosures).

**IV. Rand’s Inclusion of a Prepayment Penalty Violated HOEPA and Extended the Rescission Period to Three Years.**

The Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA), 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b), prohibits lenders from including prepayment penalties in high-cost loans. The Mouas’ loan included a prepayment penalty, which violated HOEPA. The Supreme Court has held that violations of HOEPA’s provisions toll the three-year rescission period. *See* **Williams v. Public Finance Corp.**, 388 F.2d 448, 450 (7th Cir. 1968) (holding that HOEPA violations extend the rescission period). Because Rand included a prepayment penalty in violation of HOEPA, the Mouas’ right to rescind was not limited to three days. *See id.* at 450-51.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Mouas’ right to rescind was not waived, forfeited, or limited to three days. The district court erred in granting summary judgment in Rand’s favor. The judgment should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this brief.
