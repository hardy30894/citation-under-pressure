## ARGUMENT

The Board correctly determined that Harari's '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the '566 application because the intervening applications in the priority chain failed to properly incorporate the '579 application by reference.

The key issue in this appeal is whether the incorporation by reference language in the '566 application, which was copied into the subsequent applications, sufficiently identified the '579 application to satisfy the continuity requirements of 35 U.S.C. § 120. The language at issue states that the disclosure of a copending application "filed on the same day as the present application, by Sanjay Mehrotra and Dr. Eliyahou Harari" is incorporated by reference. J.A. 1204.

As this Court previously held in Harari I, the proper standard for evaluating the sufficiency of incorporation by reference language at the initial filing stage is whether the identity of the incorporated reference is clear to a reasonable examiner in light of the documents presented. Harari I, 602 F.3d at 1352-53. However, this standard is not directly applicable to the issue at hand, which involves the intervening applications in the priority chain.

Instead, we look to the principles established in Ex parte Hall, 83 USPQ 256 (P.O.B.A. 1948), and In re Heritage, 150 F.2d 554 (C.C.P.A. 1945), which held that an incorporation by reference must be clear and unambiguous to be effective. In Ex parte Hall, the Board of Appeals held that a reference to a copending application must be sufficient to identify the application with certainty. 83 USPQ at 257. Similarly, in In re Heritage, the Court of Customs and Patent Appeals held that an incorporation by reference must be "clear and unambiguous" to satisfy the requirements of the statute. 150 F.2d at 557.

Applying these principles, it is clear that the incorporation by reference language in the '566 application and subsequent applications is ambiguous and does not clearly identify the '579 application. The language refers to an application "filed on the same day as the present application," which is not a clear identifier, as the filing date of the subsequent applications changed. 

Furthermore, the Supreme Court has long held that patent applicants must comply with the statutory requirements for claiming the benefit of an earlier filing date. See Crown Cork & Seal Co. v. Ferdinand Gutmann Co., 304 U.S. 159 (1938). In Crown Cork, the Court emphasized the importance of strict compliance with the patent statutes, including the requirements for claiming priority. Id. at 165.

In this case, Harari's failure to clearly identify the '579 application in the intervening applications means that the '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date. The Board's decision on remand was correct, and this Court should affirm.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Board should be affirmed.
