**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT’S FAILURE TO ADVISE THE DEFENDANT OF HIS RIGHT TO APPEAL WAS HARMLESS ERROR.**

The Court of Appeals correctly applied the standard of harmless error to the District Court’s failure to advise the defendant of his right to appeal. Under the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, errors in the sentencing process are subject to harmless error analysis rather than automatic reversal. *United States v. Olano*, 507 U.S. 725, 734 (1993). The purpose of Rule 32 is to ensure that the defendant is aware of his right to appeal. *United States v. Hare*, 513 U.S. 105, 115 (1994). Because the record demonstrates that the defendant was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing, the violation of Rule 32(a)(2) was harmless.

A.  **Rule 32(a)(2) violations are subject to harmless error analysis.**

The Supreme Court has established that errors in the sentencing process are subject to the harmless error standard articulated in *United States v. Olano*. *Id.* at 734. Under this standard, the government must demonstrate that the error was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. *Id.* at 732. This framework applies to the failure to advise a defendant of his right to appeal, as such a failure is a procedural error rather than a structural defect that requires automatic reversal.

B.  **The purpose of the rule was served because the defendant was aware of his right to appeal.**

The specific purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that the defendant is aware of his right to appeal. *United States v. Hare*, 513 U.S. at 115. The rule is not intended to punish the government for a technical omission if the defendant was already aware of his right. *Id.* at 115. In the present case, the District Court held an evidentiary hearing and found that the defendant, Manuel D. Peguero, was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing. This finding satisfies the *Hare* standard, as the rule’s purpose was served despite the court’s failure to provide the statutory advice. Consequently, the error was harmless.
