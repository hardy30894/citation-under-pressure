**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN ADMITTING THE EXPERT OPINION OF AMERICAN STEAMSHIP’S WITNESS REGARDING THE CAUSE OF THE LINE’S PARTING.**

The district court abused its discretion in admitting the testimony of American Steamship’s expert, as it failed to satisfy the foundational requirements for expert testimony under *Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc.*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993), and Fed. R. Evid. 702. Expert testimony must be "scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge" that will "assist the trier of fact." *Daubert*, 509 U.S. at 588. The proffered opinion—that the line parted due to "fatigue failure" from prior use—was not based on any scientific methodology but rather on the expert’s subjective assessment of the rope’s condition. The Supreme Court has cautioned that such speculative opinions do not qualify as expert testimony. *See General Electric Co. v. Joiner*, 522 U.S. 136, 146 (1997) (excluding expert testimony where there was "simply too great an analytical gap between the data and the opinion proffered").

Moreover, the expert’s opinion was irrelevant because the line’s failure was undisputedly caused by Cook’s use of the torch to deice the boom. The district court’s admission of this testimony was prejudicial, as it improperly bolstered the defense’s theory of the case. *See United States v. Bonds*, 12 F.3d 540, 560 (6th Cir. 1993) (finding reversible error where improper expert testimony "tainted the jury’s verdict").

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING COOK’S MOTION FOR JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW ON THE ISSUE OF UNSEAWORTHINESS.**

The district court erred in denying Cook’s motion for judgment as a matter of law on the issue of unseaworthiness. Under maritime law, a vessel is unseaworthy if it is "not reasonably fit for its intended use." *Mitchell v. Trawler Racer, Inc.*, 362 U.S. 539, 550 (1960). Here, the evidence overwhelmingly established that the *BUFFALO* was unseaworthy due to the frozen bearings and the improperly rigged line. The Sixth Circuit has held that a vessel’s defective condition need not be the sole cause of an injury to support a finding of unseaworthiness. *See Kelly v. United States*, 539 F.2d 1167, 1170 (6th Cir. 1976).

The district court’s denial of Cook’s motion was contrary to settled law. The Supreme Court has made clear that a vessel owner’s duty to provide a seaworthy vessel is "absolute." *Grigson v. Coastal Marine Serv. of Tex., Inc.*, 511 F.2d 961, 963 (5th Cir. 1975) (citing *Seas Shipping Co. v. Sieracki*, 328 U.S. 85, 94 (1946)). The frozen bearings and the failure to properly maintain the line rendered the vessel unseaworthy as a matter of law. *See Mitchell*, 362 U.S. at 550.

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT DID NOT ERR IN DENYING COOK’S MOTION FOR JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW ON THE ISSUE OF COMPARATIVE NEGLIGENCE.**

The district court properly submitted the issue of comparative negligence to the jury. Under the Jones Act, 46 U.S.C. § 688, a plaintiff’s contributory negligence does not bar recovery but may reduce damages. *See Pope & Talbot, Inc. v. Hawn*, 346 U.S. 406, 409 (1953). The evidence supported the jury’s finding that Cook was 50% negligent in causing his own injury. The Sixth Circuit has held that comparative negligence is a question for the jury where reasonable minds could differ. *See Miller v. Caterpillar Tractor Co.*, 733 F.2d  Miller v. Caterpillar Tractor Co.*, 733 F.2d 1321, 1325 (6th Cir. 1984).

**IV. THE DISTRICT COURT DID NOT ERR IN ADMITTING EVIDENCE OF COOK’S ALCOHOL-RELATED AILMENTS.**

The district court did not abuse its discretion in admitting evidence of Cook’s alcohol-related ailments. Under Fed. R. Evid. 404(b), evidence of prior bad acts is admissible if it is relevant to a material issue and not offered to prove character. *See United States v. Johnson*, 27 F.3d 1186, 1190 (6th Cir. 1994). Here, the evidence was probative of Cook’s ability to perform his duties safely. The Sixth Circuit has upheld the admission of such evidence where it is "inextricably intertwined" with the events at issue. *See United States v. Newsom*, 4 F.3d 999, 1002 (6th Cir. 1993).
