**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED BY GRANTING A MANDATORY INJUNCTION WITHOUT THE HEIGHTENED SHOWING THIS COURT REQUIRES**

A preliminary injunction issues only where the movant demonstrates irreparable harm and either a likelihood of success on the merits, or "sufficiently serious questions going to the merits to make them a fair ground for litigation and a balance of hardships tipping decidedly" in the movant's favor. *Jackson Dairy, Inc. v. H.P. Hood & Sons, Inc.*, 596 F.2d 70, 72 (2d Cir. 1979). Where, as here, the injunction is mandatory—ordering Cadman Towers to award Shapiro a specific ground-floor space out of turn, ahead of others on the waiting list—this Court demands a heightened showing of entitlement before ordering affirmative relief pendente lite. *Abdul Wali v. Coughlin*, 754 F.2d 1015, 1025-26 (2d Cir. 1985). The district court did not apply that heightened standard, and its order cannot stand on the ordinary, more lenient formula it employed.

**II. SHAPIRO FAILED TO SHOW A LIKELIHOOD OF SUCCESS ON HER REASONABLE-ACCOMMODATION CLAIM**

*A. Reasonable accommodation does not require the fundamental alteration of a neutral allocation system.*

Section 3604(f)(3)(B) requires only "reasonable accommodations ... necessary to afford [a handicapped] person equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling." 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B). "Reasonable" carries the same limiting force the Supreme Court recognized in the analogous Rehabilitation Act context: an accommodation need not work a "fundamental alteration in the nature of [the] program." *Southeastern Community College v. Davis*, 442 U.S. 397, 410 (1979). Nor does the duty to accommodate require an entity to disregard the legitimate, competing interests of third parties. *Alexander v. Choate*, 469 U.S. 287, 300-01 (1985). Cadman Towers' first-come/first-served waiting list allocates a scarce, subsidized resource among hundreds of cooperators, including elderly and disabled residents. Ordering Cadman Towers to move Shapiro to the front of that line does not merely accommodate her; it fundamentally alters the neutral system governing the entire building and necessarily displaces another cooperator's settled expectancy.

*B. The FHAA requires an individualized weighing of costs and burdens that the district court never undertook.*

Courts must engage in a "case by case" inquiry into "the cost of the accommodation" and its "effect on the operation" of the entity involved. *School Bd. of Nassau Cty. v. Arline*, 480 U.S. 273, 287-88 & n.17 (1987). Applying this framework to the FHAA specifically, the Ninth Circuit has held that an accommodation is unreasonable where it imposes "undue financial and administrative burdens" on the provider. *United States v. California Mobile Home Park Mgmt. Co.*, 29 F.3d 1413, 1416 (9th Cir. 1994). Similarly, the Eleventh Circuit has recognized that the FHAA does not compel accommodations that would work a "fundamental alteration" of an entity's established operations. *Elliott v. City of Athens*, 960 F.2d 975, 981 (11th Cir. 1992). Here, immediate reassignment necessarily displaces whoever stands next on the list—quite possibly another disabled or elderly resident who has waited years for a space. The district court made no finding identifying that person or assessing the burden of displacement, and Shapiro therefore failed to establish that her requested accommodation, rather than some lesser measure, was the "reasonable" one required by the statute.

**III. THE BALANCE OF HARDSHIPS DOES NOT TIP DECIDEDLY TOWARD SHAPIRO**

Even under the alternative *Jackson Dairy* prong, Shapiro needed to show the equities favor her decidedly. 596 F.2d at 72. They do not. Shapiro already holds a city-issued disability placard permitting street parking notwithstanding local restrictions—an accommodation she herself used for years. By contrast, immediate reassignment permanently deprives another cooperator of an anticipated space and forces Cadman Towers to abandon the seniority system that renders its waitlist administrable for hundreds of households. Where relief to one member of a protected class comes directly at the expense of another member of that same class waiting in line, the equities counsel restraint rather than judicial compulsion. Cf. *Doe v. New York Univ.*, 666 F.2d 761, 773 (2d Cir. 1981) (irreparable-harm showing must be weighed against the injury an injunction inflicts on others).

**IV. CONCLUSION**

Because Shapiro failed to make the clear showing required for mandatory preliminary relief, *Abdul Wali*, 754 F.2d at 1025-26, and because the FHAA does not require Cadman Towers to fundamentally alter its neutral waiting-list system at another cooperator's expense, *Southeastern Community College*, 442 U.S. at 410; *Alexander*, 469 U.S. at 300-01, this Court should vacate the preliminary injunction and remand for further proceedings under the proper reasonableness standard.
