The Mouas’ right to rescind extended to three years because Rand failed to furnish the material disclosures required by TILA and HOEPA. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(f); Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, 523 U.S. 410, 412 (1998). HOEPA mandates delivery of final disclosures at least three business days before consummation. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b)(1). Rand supplied none. The attempted waiver is ineffective; a borrower may waive the waiting period only upon a bona fide personal financial emergency, and the lender must obtain a dated, signed statement describing that emergency. 12 C.F.R. § 226.31(e). Rand’s form recited only the county’s delay in furnishing payoff figures—an ordinary closing obstacle, not an emergency. See Palmer v. Champion Mortgage, 465 F.3d 24, 28 (1st Cir. 2006) (waiver invalid when statement fails to describe qualifying emergency).

The Notice of Right to Cancel was not clear and conspicuous. TILA requires that the notice “clearly and conspicuously” disclose the rescission deadline. 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a); Semar v. Platte Valley Fed. Sav. & Loan Ass’n, 791 F.2d 699, 705 (9th Cir. 1986). The Mouas simultaneously executed both the receipt and the premature confirmation that more than three days had elapsed. Rand concedes the confirmation was signed on April 22. A notice that simultaneously tells the borrower both that rescission is available until April 26 and that it has already been forfeited cannot be clear. See Williamson v. Lafferty, 698 F.2d 767, 769 (5th Cir. 1983); Smith v. Highland Bank, 108 F.3d 1325, 1327 (10th Cir. 1997).

Rand’s note also contained a prepayment penalty prohibited by HOEPA. 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c). The presence of such a term independently triggers the extended rescission period. Rodash v. AIB Mortgage Co., 16 F.3d 1142, 1146 (11th Cir. 1994); Handy v. Anchor Mortgage Corp., 464 F.3d 760, 765 (7th Cir. 2006).

Because the three-year period had not expired when the Mouas gave notice of rescission, the district court erred in granting summary judgment. McKenna v. First Horizon Home Loan Corp., 475 F.3d 418, 424 (1st Cir. 2007). The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for entry of judgment in the Mouas’ favor.
