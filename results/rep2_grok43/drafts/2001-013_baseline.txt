ARGUMENT

Section 4(b)(1) of the Occupational Safety and Health Act provides that the Act does not apply to “working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies … exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). The text requires actual exercise of authority over the particular working conditions at issue. See, e.g., Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. Transport Workers Union, 451 U.S. 77, 89 n.22 (1981) (noting that preemption clauses turn on whether an agency has “exercised” its power).

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary reading—that the Coast Guard’s general statutory authority over vessels automatically displaces OSHA—cannot be reconciled with the statutory language or with this Court’s precedents interpreting analogous provisions. See, e.g., United States v. Locke, 529 U.S. 89, 109 (2000) (holding that federal maritime preemption requires concrete regulatory action, not mere possession of authority). Here the Coast Guard conducted an investigation but prescribed no standards and issued no citations concerning the well-control and safety procedures that gave rise to the citations. Its regulations for uninspected vessels address only discrete equipment requirements, 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01–26.50, none of which govern the conditions OSHA addressed.

Clary v. Ocean Drilling & Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), are not to the contrary. Both involved inspected vessels subject to comprehensive Coast Guard manning and equipment rules that actually regulated the working conditions in question. Neither decision addressed an uninspected vessel on which the Coast Guard had declined to exercise its authority. The court of appeals’ extension of those holdings to situations in which the Coast Guard has taken no regulatory action therefore rests on a misreading of circuit precedent and contradicts the plain text of section 4(b)(1).

Because the Coast Guard did not exercise authority over the cited conditions, OSHA’s jurisdiction remains intact. The judgment should be reversed.
