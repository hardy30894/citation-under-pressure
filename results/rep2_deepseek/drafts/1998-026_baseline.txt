**ARGUMENT**

**I. A District Court’s Failure to Advise a Defendant of His Right to Appeal Under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32 Is Subject to Harmless-Error Review.**

The Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure do not create substantive rights enforceable through collateral attack. *Hill v. United States*, 368 U.S. 424, 428 (1962). A violation of Rule 32’s advice requirement is a procedural error, not a jurisdictional defect. *United States v. Timmreck*, 441 U.S. 780, 784 (1979). Accordingly, such an error is subject to harmless-error analysis under 28 U.S.C. § 2111 and Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(a). *See Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, 22 (1967) (applying harmless-error standard to constitutional errors; procedural errors receive even more deferential review). The Court of Appeals correctly held that a Rule 32 violation does not automatically entitle a defendant to habeas relief. Instead, the reviewing court must assess whether the error affected the defendant’s substantial rights. *Kotteakos v. United States*, 328 U.S. 750, 764–65 (1946).

**II. The Error Was Harmless Because Peguero Had Actual Knowledge of His Right to Appeal and Elected Not to Appeal.**

The sole purpose of Rule 32’s advice requirement is to ensure that a defendant knows of his right to appeal. Fed. R. Crim. P. 32 advisory committee’s note to 1994 amendment. That purpose is fully satisfied when the defendant already possesses that knowledge. Here, the District Court found after an evidentiary hearing that Peguero “knew of his right to appeal when the sentencing hearing occurred.” That finding is entitled to deference. *Anderson v. Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 573–74 (1985). Because Peguero knew of his right, the court’s failure to repeat the advice could not have caused him to forgo an appeal. The error therefore had no “substantial and injurious effect” on his rights. *Brecht v. Abrahamson*, 507 U.S. 619, 623 (1993). A defendant who is already aware of his appellate rights suffers no prejudice from the omission of a redundant advisory. *Cf. United States v. Vonn*, 535 U.S. 55, 62 (2002) (holding that harmless-error review applies to Rule 11 violations and that a defendant’s actual knowledge can render an omission harmless).

**III. Habeas Relief Is Not Available for a Technical Violation That Did Not Prejudice the Defendant.**

Collateral relief under 28 U.S.C. § 2255 is reserved for errors that “fundamentally” undermine the legality of the sentence. *Hill*, 368 U.S. at 428. A mere failure to recite the appeal right, when the defendant already knows of it, does not rise to that level. *Timmreck*, 441 U.S. at 784. Peguero does not claim that he was unaware of his right or that he would have appealed but for the court’s omission. The District Court’s factual finding that he knew of his right and chose not to appeal forecloses any claim of prejudice. Because the error did not affect Peguero’s decision-making, it cannot justify vacating his sentence. *See United States v. Olano*, 507 U.S. 725, 734 (1993) (error is harmless if it did not affect the outcome). The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
