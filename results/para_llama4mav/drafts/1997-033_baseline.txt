The District Court's decision to invoke 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the clear statutory mandate of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Section 1407(a) explicitly requires that each action transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel" to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The language of the statute is unequivocal, leaving no room for interpretation that would permit the transferee court to retain the case for trial. 

As Judge Kozinski noted in his dissent below, the federal courts have consistently disregarded this statutory command, starting with the Second Circuit's decision in Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971). However, the plain language of § 1407(a) precludes the transferee court from transferring the case to itself for trial under § 1404(a). The authority granted to the transferee court under § 1407 is limited to pretrial proceedings, and venue transfer motions under § 1404(a) that relate to those proceedings.

The practice of allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial undermines the policy choice reflected in § 1407, which ensures that plaintiffs retain their right to select the forum for trial. As the Supreme Court has emphasized, "the plain meaning of legislation should be conclusive, except in the 'rare cases [in which] the literal application of a statute will produce a result demonstrably at odds with the intentions of its drafters.'" United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235, 242 (1989) (quoting Griffin v. Oceanic Contractors, Inc., 458 U.S. 564, 571 (1982)). This is not such a case; rather, the literal application of § 1407(a) is consistent with its purpose and the intentions of its drafters.

The Ninth Circuit's decision below, affirming the District Court's order, was erroneous. The court failed to recognize the clear statutory mandate of § 1407(a) and instead relied on case law that has perpetuated an unlawful practice. As Judge Kozinski observed, "seldom have so many been so consistently wrong." The time has come to correct this error and respect the policy choice embodied in § 1407.

Therefore, this Court should reverse the judgment of the Ninth Circuit and hold that a federal district court conducting "pretrial proceedings" under 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) may not invoke § 1404(a) to assign a transferred case to itself for trial. The case should be remanded to the original district, the Northern District of Illinois, for trial.
