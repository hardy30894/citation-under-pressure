**Argument**

**I. The Sixth Amendment Right to Counsel Does Not Apply to Tribal Court Proceedings.**
The Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies only to federal and state courts. *Santa Clara Pueblo v. Martinez*, 436 U.S. 49, 71 (1978). The Indian Civil Rights Act governs the procedures of tribal courts, not the United States Constitution. *Id.* at 71. Because Bryant’s prior convictions were obtained in a tribal court, the Sixth Amendment was not implicated, and the convictions are constitutionally valid.

**II. A Recidivist Statute Penalizes Only the Last Offense, Not the Prior Convictions.**
Even assuming the Sixth Amendment applied to the tribal proceedings, the use of prior convictions as predicates for a recidivist charge does not violate the Sixth Amendment. *United States v. Rodriquez*, 553 U.S. 377, 386 (2008). The Supreme Court has held that recidivist statutes "penalize only the last offense committed by the defendant." *Id.* The prior convictions serve only to establish the defendant’s status as a recidivist; they are not the basis of the punishment. *Id.* Furthermore, *United States v. Shelton*, 535 U.S. 654, 661 (2002), established that while a prison sentence may be invalid without counsel, the underlying conviction remains valid. Thus, the validity of the tribal convictions is irrelevant to the validity of the current charge.

**III. *Nichols v. United States* Authorizes the Use of Prior Convictions for Predicate Purposes.**
The Supreme Court in *Nichols v. United States*, 511 U.S. 738 (1994), addressed the use of prior convictions in sentencing. The Court affirmed the use of a prior uncounseled misdemeanor conviction to enhance a sentence, provided the government proves its existence by a preponderance of the evidence. *Id.* at 748. This standard is lower than the "beyond a reasonable doubt" standard required to prove the elements of the current offense. *Id.* at 746. *Nichols* explicitly rejected the argument that the use of prior convictions violates the Sixth Amendment. *Id.* at 746-47.

**IV. The Ninth Circuit’s Precedent in *Ant* Is Inconsistent with Supreme Court Law.**
The Ninth Circuit’s decision relies on *United States v. Ant*, 882 F.2d 1389 (9th Cir. 1989). However, *Ant* predates *Nichols* and *Shelton* and is inconsistent with the Supreme Court’s modern understanding of recidivist statutes. *Rodriquez* clarifies that the prior convictions are not the basis of punishment. *Ant*’s reasoning is therefore flawed.
