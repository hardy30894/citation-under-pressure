I.

The release executed by Bancorp on June 6, 1988, does not bar the present claims. Its operative language releases the FDIC only from claims “arising from or with respect to the decision of the [OCC] to close the Bank on June 21, 1885.” A release is confined to the matters expressly embraced by its terms. Kirchner v. New Home Sewing Mach. Co., 135 N.Y. 182, 189 (1892). Where the instrument specifies a particular transaction or event, it cannot be extended by implication to later or distinct acts. Duryee v. Mayor of N.Y., 96 N.Y. 477, 491 (1884). The claims here concern post-closure conduct—the charging of expenses, the payment of post-insolvency interest beginning in 1991, and the sale of assets—none of which arose from the OCC’s closure decision itself. The release therefore leaves these claims untouched.

II.

Even if the release were read more broadly, the claims are timely. An action for unjust enrichment, breach of fiduciary duty, or corporate waste accrues only when the wrongdoer’s liability becomes fixed and the plaintiff’s right to sue is complete. Lightfoot v. Davis, 198 N.Y. 261, 273 (1910). Until the receivership terminated on November 1, 1995, the FDIC remained in possession of the estate and continued to administer assets and pay itself interest. No cause of action could be maintained while that relationship subsisted. Mason v. Henry, 152 N.Y. 529, 537 (1897). The six-year period under the predecessor to N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 213 therefore began to run only upon termination of the receivership, rendering the complaint filed October 31, 1995, timely.

III.

The district court’s alternative reliance on the statute of limitations for claims of corporate waste and breach of fiduciary duty likewise fails. Such claims against a receiver do not accrue until the receiver has rendered a final accounting or the receivership has ended. People v. Third Ave. R.R. Co., 122 N.Y. 150, 160 (1890). The FDIC’s ongoing administration of the estate, including its self-payment of interest, prevented accrual until November 1, 1995. The judgment should be reversed.
