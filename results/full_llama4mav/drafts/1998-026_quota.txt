The respondent respectfully submits that the District Court's failure to advise Manuel D. Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2), does not provide a basis for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal.

The Court of Appeals correctly held that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review. See United States v. Dominguez Benitez, 542 U.S. 74 (2004) (applying harmless-error review to a Rule 11 violation); see also United States v. Vonn, 535 U.S. 55 (2002) (same). The purpose of Rule 32 is to ensure that a defendant is aware of his right to appeal. See United States v. Deans, 436 F.2d 596, 599 (3d Cir. 1971) ("The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to make certain that a defendant is advised of his right to appeal."). When a defendant is already aware of this right, the rule's purpose is served, and any error is harmless. See Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18, 22 (1967) ("[T]here may be some constitutional errors which in the setting of a particular case are so unimportant and insignificant that they may, consistent with the Federal Constitution, be deemed harmless, not requiring the automatic reversal of the conviction.").

In this case, the District Court found that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing. This finding is supported by the record and is not clearly erroneous. See Anderson v. City of Bessemer City, 470 U.S. 564, 574 (1985) ("Where there are two permissible views of the evidence, the factfinder's choice between them cannot be clearly erroneous."). As Peguero was aware of his right to appeal, the District Court's failure to advise him of this right did not prejudice him. See Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668, 694 (1984) ("The defendant must show that there is a reasonable probability that, but for counsel's unprofessional errors, the result of the proceeding would have been different.").

The respondent's position is further supported by the principle that habeas relief is not available for non-constitutional errors that do not result in prejudice. See Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619, 637-38 (1993) (holding that habeas relief is not available for trial errors that do not have a "substantial and injurious effect or influence in determining the jury's verdict"); see also United States v. Lane, 474 U.S. 438, 449 (1986) (applying harmless-error review to a non-constitutional error).

In Rodriquez v. United States, 395 U.S. 327 (1969), this Court held that a defendant's right to appeal is a fundamental right that cannot be waived without knowledge. However, in that case, the defendant was not aware of his right to appeal. Here, in contrast, Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to exercise it.

The respondent acknowledges that some courts have held that a Rule 32 violation requires automatic vacatur of the sentence. See, e.g., United States v. Benthien, 434 F.2d 1031 (1st Cir. 1970). However, this approach is not supported by the majority of courts, which have applied harmless-error review to Rule 32 violations. See, e.g., United States v. Drummond, 903 F.2d 1391 (11th Cir. 1990); see also United States v. Butler, 938 F.2d 702 (6th Cir. 1991).

In light of the foregoing, the respondent respectfully submits that the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed. The District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal did not prejudice him, as he was already aware of this right. The harmless-error doctrine, as applied in Dominguez Benitez and Vonn, supports this conclusion.
