ARGUMENT

Stelwagon failed to prove actual antitrust damages causally linked to the Robinson-Patman violation. Section 4 of the Clayton Act requires a plaintiff to establish “actual damages” resulting from the violation. J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp., 451 U.S. 557, 562 (1981). Proof must rest on evidence, not speculation. Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc., 327 U.S. 251, 264 (1946).

The record shows only that Tarmac sold MAPs to Standard and Celotex at lower prices. No evidence quantified lost sales or profits Stelwagon suffered from those differentials. See Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co., 282 U.S. 555, 563 (1931) (damages must be proved with reasonable certainty). Stelwagon’s damage model instead aggregated losses from the breach of the exclusive distributorship, conflating contractual and antitrust harms. Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat, Inc., 429 U.S. 477, 489 (1977) (antitrust injury must flow from the anticompetitive aspect of the conduct).

A plaintiff cannot rely on the mere existence of price discrimination to infer injury. Texaco Inc. v. Hasbrouck, 496 U.S. 543, 556 (1990). Nor may damages be inferred from the fact of violation alone. Zenith Radio Corp. v. Hazeltine Research, Inc., 395 U.S. 100, 123-24 (1969). The jury’s original $2,272,000 award, later reduced by the district court, rested on precisely such an inference. Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp., 475 U.S. 574, 587 (1986) (antitrust plaintiffs must present evidence tending to exclude non-antitrust explanations).

Because Stelwagon presented no competent proof separating antitrust damages from contract damages, the evidence was insufficient as a matter of law. The district court therefore erred in denying Tarmac’s renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law on the treble-damage award. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 50; Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Prods., Inc., 530 U.S. 133, 150 (2000) (judgment required when evidence permits only one reasonable conclusion). The judgment awarding $1,423,392.50 in trebled damages must be vacated.
