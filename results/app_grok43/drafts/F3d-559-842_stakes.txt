**ARGUMENT**

The district court erred in granting summary judgment because genuine issues of material fact exist as to whether Rand’s violations of TILA and HOEPA extended the Mouas’ rescission period to three years. See 15 U.S.C. § 1635(f)(3); Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, 523 U.S. 410, 412 (1998) (“[T]he borrower’s right of rescission shall expire three years after the date of consummation of the transaction” when required disclosures are not made).

Rand failed to furnish the Mouas with the HOEPA disclosure statement three business days before consummation, as mandated by 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b)(1). The Mouas’ unrebutted testimony establishes they received no such advance disclosure. Rand’s reliance on an alleged waiver is unavailing. The handwritten statement Manisy Moua copied at closing does not satisfy the regulatory requirements for a bona fide personal financial emergency waiver under 12 C.F.R. § 226.31(c)(1)(iii), and the pending redemption period alone does not constitute the requisite emergency.

The Notice of Right to Cancel was not “clear and conspicuous.” See 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a). The Mouas signed both the receipt and confirmation sections on April 22, 2005—the same day as closing—thereby purporting to waive their rescission rights before the statutory three-day period had run. Rand does not dispute this simultaneous execution. Such premature confirmation renders the notice defective. See Rodash v. AIB Mortgage Co., 16 F.3d 1142, 1146 (11th Cir. 1994) (acknowledgment signed contemporaneously with closing violates TILA timing requirements).

Finally, the loan documents included a prepayment penalty prohibited by HOEPA. See 15 U.S.C. § 1639(c)(1). This additional violation independently triggers the extended rescission period.

Because these violations are supported by record evidence creating triable issues, summary judgment was improper. The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for further proceedings.
