**ARGUMENT**

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that FOIA Exemption 2, 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2), shields the ESQD documents because they are “predominantly internal” and their disclosure risks circumvention of agency regulation. That standard has no basis in the statutory text or controlling precedent.

FOIA mandates disclosure unless material falls within one of nine “explicitly exclusive” exemptions that “are to be interpreted narrowly.” *U.S. Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989) (quoting *Adm’r FAA v. Robertson*, 422 U.S. 255, 262 (1975)); *Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 361 (1976). Exemption 2 covers only matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The phrase “personnel rules and practices” refers to “employee-related matters such as hiring and firing, work rules and discipline, compensation and benefits.” *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 362-63. It does not encompass operational documents like ESQD arc maps used to site ammunition storage facilities.

In *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), this Court held that Exemption 2 protects “law enforcement materials, the disclosure of which may risk circumvention of agency regulation.” *Id.* at 656. The Court did not adopt a broader “predominantly internal” test. *Maricopa Audubon Soc’y v. U.S. Forest Serv.*, 108 F.3d 1082 (9th Cir. 1997), likewise treated the law-enforcement category as sufficient but not necessary only after first confirming that the requested data did not “relate ‘solely,’ or even predominantly, ‘to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.’” *Id.* at 1085 (quoting 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2)).

The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” formulation from *Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 670 F.2d 1051 (D.C. Cir. 1981) (en banc), therefore conflicts with both the statute and this Court’s precedent. The ESQD documents are not personnel rules; they are technical planning tools for facility design and ordnance placement. Their disclosure cannot be withheld under Exemption 2.

The judgment should be reversed.
