**ARGUMENT**

The text of 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) extends Longshore Act coverage only to disability or death “resulting from any injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf.” The phrase “occurring as the result of operations conducted on” the Shelf imposes both a geographic and a causal limitation. The injury must arise from Shelf operations and must itself occur on the Shelf.

In *Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire*, 477 U.S. 207 (1986), the Court observed that “Congress determined that the general scope of OCSLA’s coverage . . . would be determined principally by locale, not by the status of the individual injured or killed.” *Id.* at 219. The accompanying footnote confirmed that only one provision—§ 1333(b)—“superimposes a status requirement on the otherwise determinative OCSLA situs requirement.” *Id.* at 219 n.2. Although dictum, the statement reflects the statute’s explicit geographic premise.

The Fifth Circuit correctly applied this principle in *Mills v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs*, 877 F.2d 356 (5th Cir. 1989) (en banc). There the court held that an employee injured onshore while constructing a platform destined for the Shelf was ineligible because “the injury must occur on an OCS platform or on the waters above the OCS.” *Id.* at 362. The same rule bars recovery when, as here, the fatal injury occurred at an onshore processing facility three miles from the coast.

The Third Circuit’s contrary “but for” approach in *Curtis v. Schlumberger Offshore Service, Inc.*, 849 F.2d 805 (3d Cir. 1988), cannot be reconciled with the statutory language. Section 1333(b) does not say “resulting from operations that are connected to” the Shelf; it requires that the injury occur “as the result of operations conducted on” the Shelf. The Fifth Circuit’s reading respects that distinction and the “explicit geographic limitation” recognized in *Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray*, 470 U.S. 414, 421 (1985).

Petitioner’s proposed “substantial nexus” test would erase the geographic boundary Congress placed in the statute. Because Juan Valladolid’s death occurred on land outside the outer Continental Shelf, § 1333(b) does not apply. The judgment of the Benefits Review Board should be affirmed.
