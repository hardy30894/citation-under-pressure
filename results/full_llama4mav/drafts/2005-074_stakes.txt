The reinstatement statute, Section 241(a)(5) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA), does not apply to aliens who re-entered the United States before its effective date in 1997. Even if it does, its application to such aliens is impermissibly retroactive.

First, the plain language and legislative history of the statute do not indicate that it was intended to apply retroactively to pre-enactment re-entries. The Supreme Court has held that "a statute shall not be given retroactive effect unless such construction is required by explicit language or by necessary implication." Fernandez-Antonia v. Gonzales, does not control here because it did not address the specific issue of retroactivity. See Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244, 280 (1994). 

Moreover, applying the reinstatement statute to pre-enactment re-entries would have an impermissibly retroactive effect. The statute denies aliens the opportunity to apply for relief under the INA, which was available to them at the time of their re-entry. As the Court held in INS v. St. Cyr, 533 U.S. 289, 326 (2001), "IIRIRA's elimination of any possibility of ... relief for aliens ... clearly attaches a new disability, in respect to transactions or considerations already past." The Court has also emphasized that "familiar considerations of fair notice, reasonable reliance, and settled expectations" are relevant in determining retroactivity. Id. at 321. Fernandez-Vargas had a settled expectation of being able to adjust his status when he married a U.S. citizen in 2001, and the reinstatement statute disrupted this expectation.

The Tenth Circuit's holding that Fernandez-Vargas had no "protectable expectation of being able to adjust his status" is unfounded. The court relied on no authority to support this proposition, and it ignores the fact that Fernandez-Vargas had taken concrete steps to adjust his status by marrying a U.S. citizen and filing an application. See Fernandez-Vargas v. Gonzales, 394 F.3d 881, 885 (10th Cir. 2005). The Supreme Court has recognized that an alien's reliance on the availability of relief under the INA can be a significant factor in determining retroactivity. See St. Cyr, 533 U.S. at 323-24.

Therefore, the reinstatement statute should not be applied to Fernandez-Vargas, and his petition for review should be granted. The decision of the Tenth Circuit should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
