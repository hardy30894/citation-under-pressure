**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Mouas’ Right to Rescind Did Not Expire After Three Days Because Rand Failed to Make Material Disclosures Required by TILA and HOEPA.**

Under the Truth in Lending Act (TILA), 15 U.S.C. § 1601 *et seq.*, a borrower’s right to rescind a transaction secured by a principal dwelling may be extended from three days to three years if the lender fails to make material disclosures required by the Act. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1635(f); *Keiran v. Home Capital, Inc.,* 720 F.3d 721, 725 (8th Cir. 2013) (citing *McOmie-Gray v. Bank One Corp.,* 383 F.3d 532, 536 (6th Cir. 2004)). The Supreme Court has held that the three-year extension applies where the lender’s failure to disclose is “not only a violation of the statute but also a material one.” *Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank,* 523 U.S. 410, 415 (1998). A disclosure is material if it “could have changed the consumer’s decision to rescind.” *Id.* at 415-16.

Here, Rand concedes it failed to provide the Mouas with the three-day advance HOEPA disclosure required by 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b). This failure was material because it deprived the Mouas of the opportunity to consider the terms of the loan before committing to a transaction that ultimately imposed significantly higher payments. *See id.* at 415 (holding that failure to disclose the right to rescind is material where it could have affected the borrower’s decision to proceed). The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Rand on this ground.

**II. Rand’s Failure to Provide Clear and Conspicuous Notice of TILA’s Right to Cancel Extended the Rescission Period.**

TILA requires lenders to provide borrowers with a clear and conspicuous notice of the right to rescind, and failure to do so may extend the rescission period to three years. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a); *Keiran,* 720 F.3d at 725. The notice must be “separate from the other documents” and must “clearly and conspicuously” state the right to rescind. *Id.* (quoting 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a)). The Eighth Circuit has held that a lender’s failure to provide a proper notice may toll the three-day period. *See id.* at 726 (citing *McOmie-Gray,* 383 F.3d at 536).

In this case, the Mouas were given a Notice of Right to Cancel at the closing, but the version they signed included a “Confirmation” section stating that more than three business days had elapsed and that they had not rescinded. The Mouas contend they signed this section simultaneously with the Receipt, before the three-day period expired. Even if Rand did not direct the inclusion of the Confirmation section, its presence on the form rendered the notice unclear and misleading. *See id.* at 726 (holding that a notice that misleads the borrower about the right to rescind is insufficient). The district court erred in failing to consider whether the notice provided was clear and conspicuous.

**III. The Inclusion of a Prepayment Penalty Prohibited by HOEPA Extended the Rescission Period.**

HOEPA prohibits lenders from including prepayment penalties in high-cost mortgages. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1639(b). The Eighth Circuit has held that a violation of HOEPA’s provisions may extend the rescission period to three years. *See Keiran,* 720 F.3d at 725 (citing *McOmie-Gray,* 383 F.3d at 536). Here, the Mouas’ loan included a prepayment penalty, which is expressly prohibited by HOEPA. This violation is material because it deprived the Mouas of the ability to refinance the loan without penalty, a right they would have considered had they been properly informed. *See Beach,* 523 U.S. at 415-16. The district court erred in failing to consider this violation as a basis for extending the rescission period.

**IV. The Mouas Did Not Validly Waive Their Right to the Three-Day Advance Disclosure.**

TILA permits borrowers to waive the three-day advance disclosure period only in the case of a “bona fide personal financial emergency.” 15 U.S.C. § 1635(e). The Eighth Circuit has held that a waiver is valid only if the lender demonstrates the borrower was “fully apprised of the right being waived” and that the waiver was “knowingly and voluntarily made.” *Keiran,* 720 F.3d at 727 (quoting *McOmie-Gray,* 383 F.3d at 537). Here, the Mouas were not provided with the required advance disclosures, and the waiver they signed was obtained under duress due to the impending foreclosure sale. The district court erred in finding the waiver valid under these circumstances.

**Conclusion**

The district court erred in granting summary judgment to Rand. The Mouas’ right to rescind was not limited to three days because Rand failed to make material disclosures required by TILA and HOEPA, failed to provide clear and conspicuous notice of the right to rescind, and included a prepayment penalty prohibited by HOEPA. Additionally, the Mouas did not validly waive their right to the three-day advance disclosure. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
