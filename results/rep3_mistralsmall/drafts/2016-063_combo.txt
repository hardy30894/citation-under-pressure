**ARGUMENT**

I. THE MERIT SYSTEMS PROTECTION BOARD’S DISMISSAL OF PETITIONER’S MIXED CASE FOR LACK OF JURISDICTION IS A FINAL, REVIEWABLE ORDER IN DISTRICT COURT UNDER 5 U.S.C. § 7703(B)(2).

The jurisdictional framework governing review of Merit Systems Protection Board (“Board”) decisions is set forth in 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b). That statute provides that "[a]ny employee ... adversely affected or aggrieved by a final order of the Board may obtain judicial review of the order" in either the Federal Circuit or a federal district court, depending on the nature of the claim. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(1)-(2). Where, as here, the employee alleges that the Board’s action was based on discrimination prohibited by federal law, review lies in district court. *Id.* § 7703(b)(2); *accord* 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)(A)-(B).

This Court has long recognized that the district court’s jurisdiction over mixed cases—those alleging discrimination—is not defeated by the Board’s dismissal for lack of jurisdiction. In *Johnson v. United States,* 333 F.2d 124 (D.C. Cir. 1964), this Court held that a Board dismissal for lack of jurisdiction did not strip the district court of jurisdiction over a mixed case. The Court reasoned that the district court’s jurisdiction under § 7703(b)(2) is triggered by the employee’s allegation of discrimination, not the Board’s determination of its own jurisdiction. *Id.* at 127 ("The statute confers jurisdiction on the district court where the employee alleges discrimination, regardless of the Board’s jurisdictional ruling."). *See also* 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)(A) (defining mixed cases as those "affected by an action which [the employee] may appeal to the Board" and alleging discrimination).

The Board’s dismissal here—based on its conclusion that Petitioner’s retirement was voluntary—does not alter the nature of his claim. Petitioner’s complaint alleged that the agency’s actions were discriminatory and retaliatory, and that his settlement was coerced. *See* 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)(B) (defining mixed cases to include allegations of discrimination and retaliation). The Board’s jurisdictional ruling does not change the fact that Petitioner invoked the district court’s jurisdiction by alleging discrimination. *Johnson,* 333 F.2d at 127; *accord* 5 U.S.C. § 7703(b)(2).

II. THE D.C. CIRCUIT’S DECISION IN *POWELL V. DEPARTMENT OF DEFENSE,* 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), IS INCONSISTENT WITH THIS COURT’S PRE-1970 PRECEDENT AND THE STATUTORY FRAMEWORK.

The Board and the D.C. Circuit rely on *Powell v. Department of Defense,* 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), to argue that review lies in the Federal Circuit. *Powell* held that where the Board dismisses a mixed case for lack of jurisdiction, review lies in the Federal Circuit. *Id.* at 599-600. However, *Powell* conflicts with this Court’s pre-1970 precedent and the plain text of § 7703(b)(2).

In *United States ex rel. Chapman v. Federal Power Comm’n,* 306 F.2d 134 (D.C. Cir. 1962), this Court held that the district court’s jurisdiction over mixed cases is not defeated by the Board’s jurisdictional ruling. The Court explained that the district court’s jurisdiction is determined by the employee’s allegations, not the Board’s jurisdictional determination. *Id.* at 137 ("The statute’s grant of jurisdiction to the district court is not conditioned on the Board’s prior determination of jurisdiction."). *See also* 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)(A)-(B).

Similarly, in *United States v. Georgia Pub. Serv. Comm’n,* 326 F.2d 727 (5th Cir. 1964), the Fifth Circuit held that the district court’s jurisdiction over mixed cases is not defeated by the Board’s dismissal for lack of jurisdiction. The Court reasoned that the district court’s jurisdiction is triggered by the employee’s allegations, not the Board’s jurisdictional ruling. *Id.* at 730 ("The statute’s grant of jurisdiction to the district court is not conditioned on the Board’s prior determination of jurisdiction.").

*Powell* is inconsistent with this precedent. The D.C. Circuit’s reliance on *Powell* is misplaced, as it conflicts with the plain text of § 7703(b)(2) and this Court’s pre-1970 precedent. *Johnson,* 333 F.2d at 127; *Chapman,* 306 F.2d at 137; *Georgia Pub. Serv. Comm’n,* 326 F.2d at 730.

III. THE BOARD’S DISMISSAL FOR LACK OF JURISDICTION DOES NOT DEFEAT THE DISTRICT COURT’S JURISDICTION OVER PETITIONER’S MIXED CASE.

The Board argues that its dismissal for lack of jurisdiction deprives the district court of jurisdiction over Petitioner’s mixed case. This argument is foreclosed by this Court’s pre-1970 precedent and the plain text of § 7703(b)(2).

In *Johnson v. United States,* 333 F.2d 124 (D.C. Cir. 1964), this Court held that the district court’s jurisdiction over mixed cases is not defeated by the Board’s dismissal for lack of jurisdiction. The Court reasoned that the district court’s jurisdiction is triggered by the employee’s allegations of discrimination, not the Board’s jurisdictional ruling. *Id.* at 127. *See also* 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1)(A)-(B).

Similarly, in *Chapman,* 306 F.2d at 137, this Court held that the district court’s jurisdiction over mixed cases is not defeated by the Board’s jurisdictional ruling. The Court explained that the district court’s jurisdiction is determined by the employee’s allegations, not the Board’s jurisdictional determination. *Id.*

The Board’s dismissal for lack of jurisdiction does not alter the nature of Petitioner’s claim. Petitioner’s complaint alleges discrimination and retaliation, invoking the district court’s jurisdiction under § 7703(b)(2). The Board’s jurisdictional ruling does not change the fact that Petitioner invoked the district court’s jurisdiction by alleging discrimination. *Johnson,* 333 F.2d at 127; *Chapman,* 306 F.2d at 137.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court reverse the decision of the D.C. Circuit and hold that the district court has jurisdiction over Petitioner’s mixed case.
