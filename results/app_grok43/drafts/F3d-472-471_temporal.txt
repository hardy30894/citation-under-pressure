I. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment, as Burnett's Communications Provided Legally Sufficient Notice of His Medical Condition.

Summary judgment is improper where the record reveals the slightest doubt as to material facts. Sartor v. Arkansas Natural Gas Corp., 321 U.S. 620, 627 (1944). The facts, viewed in Burnett's favor, establish that he repeatedly apprised Habitat of his bladder ailment, elevated PSA levels, upcoming biopsy, and related absences beginning in October 2003. These disclosures satisfied any notice obligation, as they were reasonably calculated, under all the circumstances, to apprise the employer of the need for medical leave and to afford an opportunity to respond. Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S. 306, 314 (1950).

Burnett's October statement to Polo concerning his "weak bladder" and planned medical consultation, his December presentation of the blood-test order together with his report of high PSA and cholesterol readings, his explicit reference at the December 16 meeting to his brother's prostate cancer, and his January submission of the biopsy-procedure document naming elevated PSA as an indication, collectively conveyed the requisite information. No greater specificity was demanded under settled principles requiring only that notice fairly inform the recipient of the essential facts. Grannis v. Ordean, 234 U.S. 385, 394 (1914).

The district court's contrary conclusion rests on an unduly rigid construction of the notice requirement that finds no support in these authorities. Reversal is therefore required.
