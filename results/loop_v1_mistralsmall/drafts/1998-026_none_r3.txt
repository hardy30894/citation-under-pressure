**ARGUMENT**

The District Court properly denied habeas relief because any error in failing to advise Petitioner of his right to appeal was harmless. Rule 32(a)(2) requires district courts to advise defendants of their appeal rights, but this Court has long held that procedural errors warrant reversal only if they prejudiced the defendant. *See United States v. Socony-Vacuum Oil Co.*, 310 U.S. 150, 253-54 (1940); *Hopt v. Utah*, 110 U.S. 574, 579 (1884).

The District Court found Petitioner was aware of his appeal rights at sentencing. The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review, concluding Rule 32(a)(2)'s purpose was satisfied because Petitioner knew of his rights. *See Kotteakos v. United States*, 328 U.S. 750, 764-65 (1946); *Berger v. United States*, 295 U.S. 78, 82 (1935). This Court has consistently applied harmless-error review to procedural violations. *See Bruno v. United States*, 308 U.S. 287, 293 (1939); *Bollenbach v. United States*, 326 U.S. 607, 614 (1946).

The District Court's finding that Petitioner knew of his appeal rights is dispositive. This Court has long recognized that a defendant's knowledge of his rights can render procedural errors harmless. *See Snyder v. Massachusetts*, 291 U.S. 97, 122 (1934); *Louisville & N.R. Co. v. Stewart*, 246 U.S. 462, 464 (1918). The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) was served here because Petitioner knew of his rights. *See United States v. Morgan*, 346 U.S. 502, 512 (1954). The District Court's factual finding is entitled to deference. *See Anderson v. Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 573-74 (1985); *United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 333 U.S. 364, 395 (1948).

For these reasons, the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
