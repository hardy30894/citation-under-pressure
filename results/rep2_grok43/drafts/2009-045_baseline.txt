**ARGUMENT**

The lodestar figure may be enhanced under a federal fee-shifting statute when the fee applicant offers specific evidence that the quality of service rendered was superior to that reasonably expected given the hourly rates charged and that the success obtained was exceptional. *Blum v. Stenson*, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984). In *Blum*, the Court held that “quality of representation” and “results obtained” could not serve as an independent basis for increasing the basic fee award only because the attorneys “offered no evidence” of exceptional result or superior performance. *Id.* The Court stated that an upward adjustment “may be justified in the rare case where the fee applicant offers specific evidence to show that the quality of service rendered was superior to that one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged” and resulted in “exceptional” success. *Id.* at 899-900.

*Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air*, 478 U.S. 546 (1986), did not overrule *Blum*. The Court repeatedly cited *Blum* with approval and reaffirmed that “upward adjustments of the lodestar figure are still permissible * * * in certain ‘rare’ and ‘exceptional’ cases, supported by both ‘specific evidence’ on the record and detailed findings by the lower courts.” *Id.* at 565 (quoting *Blum*, 465 U.S. at 898-901). Although the Court observed that “the lodestar figure includes most, if not all, of the relevant factors constituting a ‘reasonable’ attorney’s fee,” it left open the possibility of enhancement “in the rare case” where the lodestar does not fully reflect superior performance. *Id.* at 565-566.

Every circuit that has addressed the question has held that a district court may enhance a lodestar for exceptional performance and results when supported by specific evidence. *See, e.g.*, *Geier v. Sundquist*, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); *Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co.*, 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000); *Hyatt v. Apfel*, 195 F.3d 188, 192 (4th Cir. 1999); *Roe v. Cheyenne Mountain Conference Resort, Inc.*, 124 F.3d 1221, 1233 n.8 (10th Cir. 1997); *Shipes v. Trinity Indus.*, 987 F.2d 311, 320 (5th Cir. 1993).

The district court here made detailed findings, based on its observation of counsel throughout the litigation and its familiarity with prevailing Atlanta rates, that the representation was “stellar” and produced results “far beyond what could have been expected.” Those findings satisfy the “specific evidence” requirement of *Blum* and *Delaware Valley I*. The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that the enhancement was within the district court’s discretion.
