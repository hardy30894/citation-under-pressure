\title{Citation under Pressure: What Deployment Constraints Do to Legal Citation Integrity, and What Deterministic Verification Can Repair}

\author{\fnms{Harsh} \snm{Dubey}%
\thanks{Corresponding Author: Harsh Dubey, New York University; hd2225@nyu.edu.}}
\runningauthor{H. Dubey}
\address{New York University, New York, USA}

\begin{abstract}
Most research on fabricated legal citations scores the errors in finished briefs; this paper measures how they are produced. Seven models from seven vendors, open-weight 30B to frontier, drafted argument sections for 48 U.S. Supreme Court matters under five prompting conditions: baseline, a citation quota, a restriction to pre-1970 authority, a sanctions warning, and all three combined. Every citation in the \nDrafts{} drafts was checked deterministically, with no language-model judge: existence against an 18-million-row reporter index, quotations against a verbatim checker validated on seeded faults and audited by hand on real drafts, and, for quotations on U.S. Reports authority, pinpoint pages against official reporter pagination from public archives. The failure mode moves with capability: under combined pressure the smallest model's citation existence fell from \existQwenBase{} to \existQwenCombo{}, verbatim quotation accuracy fell for four models in the pre-registered fit, three of them under both templates (six of seven in an exploratory fit pooling three generations), the count of accurate quotations per draft fell for \countModelsWithFall{} models, two of them at the top of the range, and Sonnet~5's rate did not fall, most plausibly because it quoted less under pressure; its residual errors are paraphrase presented as quotation, real language from the wrong case, and wrong pages. In a second experiment the checker fed its findings back into revision. Sonnet~5 reached \loopSonnetTrueFinal{} verbatim accuracy over the quotations it kept: of its \trFlagRZeroSonnet{} flagged quotations, \trTrueFlagRepairedSonnet{} were corrected and \trTrueFlagDeletedSonnet{} deleted, and accurate quotations per draft rose for one model of seven. A flag carrying the closest passage of the cited opinion raised corrections fourfold, and removal still exceeded repair in every model. A verifier with 50 percent precision removed \trHalfAccRemovedSonnet{} of Sonnet~5's \trAccRZeroSonnet{} correct quotations, as many as revision with no feedback (\trNoneAccRemovedSonnet) and against \trTrueAccRemovedSonnet{} under a precise one. A second task on 48 federal appellate matters shows lower baseline existence for six of seven models; instruments, drafts, and records are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Since 2023, courts have repeatedly sanctioned lawyers who filed briefs with citations a language model invented, from \textit{Mata v.~Avianca}~\cite{mata2023} to a docket a public tracker now counts in the thousands~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}. Most published work frames the problem as detection: given a brief that already contains errors, how many can a model or agent find~\cite{liu2026whochecks,chen2026legalcitebench}? Detection is the right measure for reviewing a filed brief, not for asking which prompting conditions produce the errors, this paper's question.

This paper asks two production-side questions. First, which prompting conditions cause citation failure, and for which models? Deployed prompts carry constraints of the kind Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} studied for scholarly citations, where a publication-year window lowered citation existence even in capable models; we add a sanctions warning and know of no comparable study for law. Second, when a deterministic checker feeds its findings back into revision, does the model correct them, leave them, or change the draft so that the tests pass without the quotations being corrected?

Both questions need an instrument the field has said does not exist in public: the best published detection agent misses nearly half of incorrect pinpoint citations (52.8 percent recall), which its authors attribute to page-level information living behind commercial databases~\cite{liu2026whochecks}. We built it from public sources: existence against a local index derived from CourtListener bulk data, verbatim quotation checking validated on seeded faults and audited on real drafts, and page-level pinpoint verification for quotations on U.S. Reports authority against the official pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's volume archives~\cite{cap2024}.

Our primary setting is the closed-book use of general-purpose models, the setting of most sanctioned filings documented to date~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}; a grounded arm with ten retrieved, verified authorities in the prompt measures what retrieval changes. Drafts under the four non-baseline conditions are the pressured drafts (\nPressured{} in the Supreme Court task, three refusals). The paper contributes a constraint-sensitive evaluation, in which reliability at a neutral prompt does not predict reliability under drafting constraints and the failure mode moves with capability; deterministic verification of existence, verbatim quotation, and pinpoint page with no language-model judge, released with the drafts; and the finding that verification is not repair, since flags fed back to the model raise measured accuracy by deletion and an imprecise verifier damages correct content.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Legal citation reliability.} Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} build a detection benchmark from real appellate briefs with injected errors and a taxonomy grounded in sanctioned filings; we adopt their error categories and measure the naturally occurring errors their limitations section asks for. LegalCiteBench~\cite{chen2026legalcitebench} shows that models asked closed-book for a named case's citation almost never produce it; our task asks whether the cases a model chooses to cite exist. Dahl et al.~\cite{dahl2024fictions} and Magesh et al.~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree} measure hallucinated authority in chatbots and research tools.

\textbf{Constraints and fabrication.} Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} is the closest design: five prompting regimes over scholarly claims, verified by fuzzy bibliographic matching that left 36 to 61 percent of citations unresolved. We transplant their factorial to law with deterministic verification (\unverifiablePct{} percent unresolved) and a formal statistical model.

\textbf{Revision with execution feedback.} The loop protocol follows execution-feedback work in code generation~\cite{gehring2025rlef}, with its no-feedback control separating feedback content from extra revision; we add controls in which a quarter to all of the feedback is false.

\textbf{AI and law.} Blair-Stanek and Van Durme~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable} found that frontier models flip the winning party across identical zero-temperature runs, which Section~\ref{sec:limits} bounds with repeated generations. Evaluation in this community pairs automated metrics with the checks lawyers perform~\cite{gultekin2025twodim,sanchi2024hybrid}; citation integrity is one with deterministic ground truth. Garneau et al.~\cite{garneau2024pincites} found retrieving the paragraph a proposition rests on far harder than retrieving the case; our verifier checks a generated pincite against the page text. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025overruled} show models miss overruled precedent, ConsRAG~\cite{nguyen2024consrag} constrains retrieval, and Trozze et al.~\cite{trozze2025crypto} and Harasta et al.~\cite{harasta2026cannotberight} study how readers perceive model-written documents. Evaluations of generated legal argument~\cite{gray2025magnitudes,pires2025legalwriting} do not check the authority it cites; citation-network work~\cite{khatri2023citecaselaw,sadeghian2018edgelabeling,dadgostari2021lawsearch,mumford2025citationnetworks} studies the network our checker queries.

\section{Tasks, Conditions, and Instruments}\label{sec:method}

\textbf{Terms.} In 347 U.S. 483, 495, the second page is the \textit{pincite}, for a quotation the page where the quoted language appears. \textit{Citation existence} is the share of adjudicable citations that resolve to a real case; \textit{strict quotation accuracy} the share of scored quotations, those the draft attributes to a case whose text the checker holds, that reproduce the opinion verbatim; a quotation the draft does not attribute is outside the rate.

\textbf{Matters.} The matters are forty-eight Supreme Court cases from 1990 to 2020 drawn from the Supreme Court Database~\cite{spaeth2025scdb}. Each packet holds the question presented, party names, a factual summary, and a lower-court opinion excerpt, the Supreme Court's decision excluded and outcome leakage screened. The model argues an assigned side in an argument section of under 900 words; nothing predicts outcomes~\cite{ortega2025scotusmem}. A second task uses forty-eight published F.3d decisions from the Caselaw Access Project archive (1995 to 2019, four from each of the First through Eleventh and Federal Circuits, no cross-appeals); the packet is the opinion's statement of the case and facts, cut before the analysis and scrubbed of outcome sentences, and the model argues for the appellant under all five conditions.

\textbf{Conditions.} The five conditions are baseline; quota (``cite at least eight distinct case authorities''); temporal (``every authority you cite MUST have been decided before 1970''), a clause no user writes, a checkable stand-in for the authority limits practitioners impose; unlike a jurisdiction limit, which pushes toward the circuit authority where the second task finds fabrication, it moves citations toward well-memorized opinions, the gentler direction; stakes (``this section will be filed with the court; citing authority that does not exist, or misquoting authority, risks sanctions under Rule~11''); and combined, which concatenates the three clauses. Each condition has one fixed template in the primary run, at temperature 0. The stakes clause names Rule~11, the rule invoked in the sanctioned-filing cases~\cite{mata2023}, which governs district-court civil actions~\cite{frcp2024} and neither task's forum, so the clause is a stakes signal only. A second template rephrasing every condition was run on all 48 matters and seven models. A \textit{grounded} arm reran baseline and combined with ten retrieved authorities in the prompt: the U.S. Reports opinions most similar to the question and facts (TF-IDF over \retrievalCorpus{} cached opinions decided before argument and, for the combined condition, before 1970, neither party named), each with citation, year, and its best-matching 250-word passage, stated to be real, quotation restricted to the excerpts.

{\sloppy\textbf{Models.} The models are Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mistral Small, Llama-4 Maverick, DeepSeek V4 Flash, Grok~4.3, GPT-5.4-mini, and Claude Sonnet~5, one per vendor; tables are ordered by baseline strict quotation accuracy, and Sonnet~5, GPT-5.4-mini, and Grok~4.3, with the highest baseline existence, are called the top of the range. The seven were run through OpenRouter in August 2026 with completion limits of 1,400 to 12,000 tokens (identifiers in the repository). An eighth model, GLM-4.7-flash, exhausted a 30,000-token budget in reasoning on \glmMissing{} of 240 drafts and is excluded.\par}

\textbf{Adjudication.} Citations are extracted with eyecite~\cite{cushman2021eyecite} and given one of three labels: exists, not found, or unverifiable (vendor citations and coverage gaps), so that index coverage is never counted as fabrication; \unverifiablePct{} percent of the \nCitationRecords{} citation records in the full run are unverifiable. Citations resolve against an 18-million-row table; quotations are located by balanced quotation marks and attributed, in order, to the citation whose parenthetical holds them, the case named in their own sentence, the citation that follows them in that sentence (not one introduced by \textit{see} or \textit{cf.}), the citation an \textit{Id.} points to, or the last citation in the paragraph; a quotation introduced by a statute, rule, the record, or a lower court, or with no citation in its paragraph, is left unpaired, since the checker has nothing to test it against: \unpairedPctAll{} percent of the \quotesAll{} quotations in the full run, \unpairedNoncasePct{} percent introduced by non-case material, \unpairedNoCitePct{} percent standing in a paragraph that cites nothing, and \unpairedFarPct{} percent too far from any citation, a share that runs from \unpairedPctMin{} to \unpairedPctMax{} percent across models. \textit{Exists} means the volume, reporter, and page resolve to an indexed opinion (\nResolving{} of the \nCitationRecords{} records; \nNotFoundAll{} are not found); for the \nmChecked{} whose draft names the parties the case name is compared separately, and \nmMismatch{} (\nmPct{} percent; \nmPctBase{} at baseline, \nmPctTemporal{} under the temporal clause; \nmPctSonnet{} for Sonnet~5, \nmPctMistral{} for Mistral Small) carry a name the index does not match, abbreviated names included; counting every mismatch as not found keeps the \nmSurvKept{} surviving existence contrasts, adds Mistral Small's temporal fall (OR \nmGainMistralTemporalOr, corrected $p$=\nmGainMistralTemporalHolm), and removes Sonnet~5's uncorrected combined rise (OR \nmSonnetComboOr). A quotation is \textit{near miss} when every fragment reaches token coverage of at least 0.85 without matching exactly, and \textit{inaccurate} otherwise. A lenient rate counting near misses as correct is reported beside the strict rate. Matching is case and punctuation insensitive, splits on ellipses and on bracketed alterations (so that a lawful substitution is read as an omission and the words around it must match), and drops alteration parentheticals, though an internal citation omitted without an ellipsis still fails; the lenient rate is a diagnostic, since 0.85 coverage admits one wrong word in seven. The quotation checker scores 240 of 240 on twenty synthetic drafts planting twelve item kinds, six of them attribution traps, and the existence and pinpoint checks \valPinsPass{} of \valPinsTotal{} on \valPinsCases{} seeded opinions. Pinpoint pages are verified against the official reporter pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's HTML volumes, which mark every page break; a pincite range is read at its first page, a quotation is located by its longest fragment, so one that straddles a page break is placed where that fragment sits, and only quotations whose fragment is somewhere in the cited case enter the page-level rate.

\textbf{Human reference.} The same instrument scored \humanExcerpts{} pre-ChatGPT appellate brief excerpts from Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} (no injected error; OCR-converted, so a floor); human lawyers reach \humanStrict{} strict and \humanLenient{} lenient quotation accuracy (\humanScored{} scored quotations) and \humanExist{} citation existence, a reference for the severity of the verbatim standard only, since human briefs differ in citation mix. The literal matcher the strict standard replaced, which read a bracketed alteration as its contents, puts the human rate at \humanAltStrict{} and moves model cells by at most \altMaxCellDiff{} points, the same \altSurv{} contrasts surviving; \humanFailNearUnaltered{} of the \humanFailTotal{} human strict failures are near misses without any alteration, the signature of a corrupted character.

\textbf{Inference.} The primary analysis is a citation-level logistic generalized estimating equation per model, $\text{logit}\,P(y_{imk}=1)=\beta_0+\beta_k$ for citation $i$ in matter $m$ under condition $k$, with exchangeable working correlation within matters~\cite{liang1986gee}, so that the 48 matters are the independent units. Each model has \nHolmTests{} contrasts (four conditions, two outcomes); we report Holm-corrected $p$ values within model, each model being the family the paper draws conclusions about, and call an effect significant only when it survives correction; counts of models are descriptive, and the effective sample is 48 matters, not thousands of citations. A matter-level cluster bootstrap agreed in sign with every corrected result. Every other fit is exploratory and follows the same model and the same rule, Holm within model over that fit's own tests: the lenient outcome, distinct authorities, the second template, and pooled templates, tasks, and generations (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), each pooling with its fixed effect; the released matrix gives every contrast under every fit. For the revision experiment, pre-registered descriptively, we add matter-paired comparisons of final strict accuracy between arms (Wilcoxon, bootstrap interval). Hypotheses and decision rules were frozen before the full run; every later decision, including two withdrawn findings, is in a dated ledger.

\textbf{Citation existence.} In the single run, only the 30B model's existence moves significantly under pressure: from \existQwenBase{} at baseline to \existQwenCombo{} under the combined condition, with the quota, temporal, and combined contrasts all significant after correction (combined OR \orCiteQwenCombo, 95 percent CI \ciCiteQwenCombo, corrected $p\,\holmCiteQwenCombo$). Mistral Small falls without reaching significance, and the five other models stay between 0.93 and 1.00 in every condition. Each surviving existence contrast rests on at least \nfQwenBase{} citations not found per arm; a record is one occurrence of a full citation (short forms are not counted), and refitting on distinct authorities per draft leaves the same \distinctSurv{} contrasts.

\textbf{Quotation accuracy.} The temporal restriction damages quoting most; after correction, strict quotation accuracy falls under temporal and combined pressure for Llama-4 (\strictLlamaBase{} to \strictLlamaCombo), under the combined condition for Qwen3-30B (\strictQwenBase{} to \strictQwenCombo) and DeepSeek (\strictDeepseekBase{} to \strictDeepseekCombo), and under the temporal clause for Mistral Small; GPT-5.4-mini and Grok~4.3 fall in the same direction without surviving correction in the single run, and Sonnet~5 does not fall (below). \textbf{The strongest model does not degrade; the leading explanation is that it quotes less.} Sonnet~5's strict rate is numerically higher under the temporal, stakes, and combined conditions and highest under the combined one (\strictSonnetBase{} to \strictSonnetCombo; uncorrected $p=\pQuoteSonnetCombo$, corrected $p=\holmQuoteSonnetCombo$), the one pre-registered contrast in that direction and one that reaches no fit. Sonnet~5 wrote \quotesPerDraftSonnetBase{} quotations per draft at baseline and \quotesPerDraftSonnetCombo{} under the combined condition; its accurate quotations per draft held (\accPerDraftSonnetBase{} to \accPerDraftSonnetCombo) while its inaccurate ones fell (\inaccPerDraftSonnetBase{} to \inaccPerDraftSonnetCombo), so the quotations it stopped writing were disproportionately the ones that would have failed. Selection is the leading reading; which quotations were dropped, by length or case age, is not tested, and the temporal restriction also shifts the cited cases toward older, better-memorized opinions (median decision year \sonnetYearCombo{} against \sonnetYearBase). With three generations pooled Sonnet~5's existence rise survives the logistic fit (corrected $p$=\runsHolmCiteSonnet) but rests on twelve citations not found (\sonnetRunsNfBase{} of \sonnetRunsAdjBase{} against \sonnetRunsNfCombo{} of \sonnetRunsAdjCombo), on which an exact test gives $p$=\sonnetRunsFisherP; the quotation rise reaches corrected $p$=\runsHolmQuoteSonnet{} there. In the appellate task the stakes clause alone raises the rate (\aStrictSonnetBase{} to \aStrictSonnetStakes, corrected $p$=\aHolmQuoteSonnetStakes) without moving the cited cases (median year \aSonnetYearBase{} in both); the direction is consistent across tasks.


\section{Experiment 2: The Checker inside the Revision Loop}\label{sec:exp2}

Combined-condition drafts entered a revision loop: each round the checker returned one line per failure, the model revised, and the loop ended on a clean draft or after three revisions. A \textit{false feedback} control gave the same number of identically formatted lines aimed at correct items, \textit{mixed} controls replaced each line with a false one at random for a precision of about 0.25, 0.5, or 0.75, and a \textit{no-feedback} control gave the same rounds with a generic instruction to revise. The loop used the first 24 matters in pre-registered order; loop rates are means over drafts with at least one scored quotation (\lcEmptyTrueTotal{} of the 168 true-arm episodes ended with none, \lcEmptyTrueGrok{} of Grok~4.3's 24, so its final rate rests on \lcGrokTrueDrafts{} drafts), so round-0 figures differ from Table~\ref{tab:pressure}. The loop was rerun after the attribution rule was corrected; the original run, whose true-arm flags had realized precision \rpTrueAll{} under the corrected rule (\rpTrueSonnet{} for Sonnet~5), is released beside it. The rerun's flags are the corrected rule's own verdicts, whose precision on quotation flags is \auditRulePrecisionPct{} percent by the hand reading of Section~\ref{sec:residual} (\auditRulePrecisionCiLow{} to \auditRulePrecisionCiHigh), so the precision axis of Figure~\ref{fig:repair} is nominal and its arm labelled 1 sits nearer 0.8. Every scored round-0 quotation was traced to the final draft (Table~\ref{tab:true}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Where the checker belongs.} Two recommendations follow from the loop results; no arm had a person in it, so they are inferences, not findings. The first is the checker at the gate, returning failures to a person, over a loop in which the model resolves bare flags, and, if a loop is built, the passage in the flag: removing an unverifiable quotation is a defensible response to a flag, but false flags get the same response and a loop-pattern tool reports a clean draft.

\textbf{Verifier precision is a deployment requirement.} Our checker's error is \auditSampleCheckerSidePct{} percent by the hand reading; the best published detection agent reaches 84.4 percent recall at an F1 of 55.0, a precision near 41 percent~\cite{liu2026whochecks}. At precision one half Sonnet~5 lost \trHalfAccRemovedSonnet{} of \trAccRZeroSonnet{} correct quotations, no more than revision with no feedback removed (\trNoneAccRemovedSonnet), and the damage beyond that appears at 0.25 and 0 (Figure~\ref{fig:repair}). A pipeline that feeds a detector's flags to a model should report the detector's precision beside its recall. The second is to report the strict standard as defined here, which admits bracketed alterations and which the OCR-converted human reference passes at \humanStrict, with 0.85 coverage as a diagnostic.

\textbf{What evaluations should include.} The two experiments make one point. A model can raise an observable correctness rate by changing what it exposes to the check, quoting less under pressure (Section~\ref{sec:exp1}) and deleting under feedback (Section~\ref{sec:exp2}), without the underlying quoting improving; a rate should be read beside the count of correct items it is computed over.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

Both tasks are United States federal case law, the stakes clause names a rule that governs neither forum, and the checks stop at existence, wording, and page, since three model panels for relevance, calibrated on expert labels~\cite{liu2026whochecks}, fell short of a \juryBar{} bar, so none was used~\cite{norman2026reliability,zheng2023judging}. The controls, grounded arm and loop, second task, trace, count outcome, and corrected attribution rule postdate the pre-registered analysis and are reported apart from it. Three generations of the baseline and combined cells bound run-to-run variation~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable}: cell standard deviations were at most \repMaxExistSd{} (existence) and \repMaxStrictSd{} (strict), and the combined-versus-baseline quotation contrast kept its sign in every run for all \repSignStable{} models; the between-template spread (\phrMaxStrictDiff{} points) exceeds many single-run effects, so we headline only contrasts that survive across templates. The seven models are one per vendor, so capability, family, and recipe are confounded.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Under a quota, a date restriction, and a sanctions warning the failure mode moves with capability: the 30B model loses existence, the middle of the range loses verbatim quotation and correct quotations per draft, and the strongest model's rate holds, most plausibly because it quotes less. Fed back to the model, the checker's flags are removed far more often than corrected, even with the passage in the flag, and false flags remove correct quotations; we recommend verification at the gate and verifier precision in the evaluation of any system that acts on it. Instruments, drafts, records, and ledger are at \url{https://github.com/hardy30894/citation-under-pressure}.
