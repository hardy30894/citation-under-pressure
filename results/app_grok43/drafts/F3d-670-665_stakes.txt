I. A Credible Showing of Actual Innocence Equitably Tolls AEDPA’s Statute of Limitations Without an Independent Diligence Requirement.

This Court has held that “a petitioner who can make a credible showing of actual innocence may equitably toll the statute of limitations.” Souter v. Jones, 395 F.3d 577, 602 (6th Cir. 2005). That holding rests on the fundamental miscarriage-of-justice exception recognized in Schlup v. Delo, 513 U.S. 298, 324 (1995), which permits review of otherwise barred constitutional claims when “it is more likely than not that no reasonable juror would have convicted” the petitioner. Because the exception is grounded in the “equitable discretion” of the federal courts to prevent manifest injustice, id. at 319, it operates independently of the diligence ordinarily required for equitable tolling. See Pace v. DiGuglielmo, 544 U.S. 408, 418 (2005) (diligence requirement applies to “equitable tolling” generally).

The district court therefore erred in treating Pace as imposing an additional, freestanding diligence prerequisite once a petitioner has satisfied the Schlup gateway. Souter expressly rejected that approach, holding that the actual-innocence exception “does not require a showing of diligence” beyond the credible showing itself. 395 F.3d at 602. Holland v. Florida, 560 U.S. 631, 649 (2010), confirms that equitable principles remain available under AEDPA and must be applied “flexibly” to avoid “gross injustice.” Requiring separate diligence would convert the miscarriage-of-justice exception into a nullity for untimely petitioners—the very class the exception was designed to protect.

The affidavits Perkins proffered, if credited, satisfy Schlup. They place the murder weapon and blood-stained clothing in the hands of the State’s sole eyewitness, directly undermining the only evidence of guilt. The district court’s contrary conclusion—that the evidence was “substantially available” at trial—overlooks that the affidavits were executed after trial and were never presented to any fact-finder. Whether Perkins acted with reasonable diligence in locating and presenting them is relevant only to the credibility of the innocence claim under Schlup, not as an independent bar. Souter, 395 F.3d at 602.

Because Perkins has made a credible showing of actual innocence, AEDPA’s limitations period is equitably tolled and the petition must be addressed on the merits.
