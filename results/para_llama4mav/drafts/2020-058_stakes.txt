ARGUMENT

The Third Circuit erred in holding that a grant of Temporary Protected Status (TPS) under 8 U.S.C. § 1254a does not constitute an "admission" into the United States under 8 U.S.C. § 1255. A straightforward reading of the relevant statutes, informed by the principles of statutory interpretation, reveals that Congress intended for TPS recipients to be considered "admitted" for purposes of adjustment of status.

The text of 8 U.S.C. § 1254a(f)(4) is dispositive. It provides that an alien granted TPS "shall be considered as being in, and maintaining, lawful status as a nonimmigrant" for purposes of adjustment of status under § 1255. The Third Circuit's opinion fails to give effect to the plain meaning of this provision, instead relying on a formalistic distinction between "admission" and "status." However, as the Ninth Circuit correctly observed in Ramirez v. Brown, 852 F.3d 954, 960 (9th Cir. 2017), "by the very nature of obtaining lawful nonimmigrant status, the alien goes through inspection and is deemed admitted." The grant of TPS necessarily implies a lawful admission, as it confers a lawful status on the alien.

Furthermore, the statutory context and structure support the conclusion that a grant of TPS constitutes an admission. Congress created an exception to the admission requirement for TPS recipients by deeming them to be in lawful nonimmigrant status. This provision would be rendered superfluous if TPS recipients were still required to demonstrate a separate admission. See Hibbs v. Winn, 542 U.S. 88, 101 (2004) ("A statute should be construed so that effect is given to all its provisions, so that no part will be inoperative or superfluous, void or insignificant.") (internal quotation marks and citation omitted).

The Sixth and Ninth Circuits have reached the same conclusion, holding that a grant of TPS satisfies the admission requirement under § 1255. See Flores v. Barr, 934 F.3d 910 (9th Cir. 2019); Ramirez v. Brown, 852 F.3d 954 (9th Cir. 2017); Serrano v. U.S. Att'y Gen., 655 F.3d 1260 (11th Cir. 2011) (dicta). While the Third Circuit disagreed with these decisions, its opinion is unpersuasive.

The Government's interpretation would undermine the purpose of the TPS statute, which is to provide a limited, temporary form of relief to aliens who are unable to return to their home countries due to extraordinary conditions. By denying TPS recipients the opportunity to adjust their status, the Government's interpretation would create an anomalous result, where aliens who entered the United States lawfully are eligible for adjustment of status, but those who entered unlawfully are not. See Ramirez, 852 F.3d at 962.

In light of the statutory text, context, and purpose, it is clear that a grant of TPS constitutes an "admission" into the United States under § 1255. The Third Circuit's decision should be reversed, and the petitioners should be deemed eligible for adjustment of status.
