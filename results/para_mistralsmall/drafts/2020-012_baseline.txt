**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s Decision Denying Reopening Is a Final, Reviewable Order

The Railroad Retirement Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 disability application is a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit’s contrary holding in *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), is inconsistent with the Supreme Court’s guidance on finality and the text of § 355(f).

The Supreme Court has instructed that a decision is final when it “conclusively determine[s] the rights of the parties” and “leaves nothing for the agency to do but execute the decision.” *FCC v. Fox Television Stations, Inc.*, 556 U.S. 502, 515 (2009) (quoting *Port of Boston Marine Terminal Ass’n v. Rederiaktiebolaget Transatlantic*, 400 U.S. 62, 71 (1970)). Here, the Board’s denial of reopening definitively resolved Salinas’s entitlement to reconsideration of his 2006 claim. The Board’s regulations limit reopening to cases involving new and material evidence or administrative error within four years, 20 C.F.R. § 261.2, and the hearing officer applied that standard to foreclose reopening. This is not a tentative or interlocutory ruling; it is a definitive resolution of Salinas’s request.

Moreover, § 355(f) grants jurisdiction over “any order” of the Board that finally determines a claim. The statute does not distinguish between denials on the merits and denials of reopening motions. *See Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 107 (1977) (interpreting similar language in the Social Security Act to permit review of reopening denials). The Fifth Circuit’s attempt to cabin § 355(f) to “decisions on the merits” in *Roberts* reads a limitation into the statute that does not exist. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 140. The Supreme Court has cautioned against such judicial narrowing of statutory review provisions. *See Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians*, 471 U.S. 667, 673 (1985) (rejecting an agency’s attempt to preclude judicial review of a statutory interpretation issue).

II. The Board’s Reopening Denial Is Reviewable Under the Railroad Retirement Act

The Board’s refusal to reopen is not merely a procedural dismissal but a substantive denial of relief that implicates Salinas’s statutory rights. The Supreme Court has recognized that denials of reopening motions under analogous statutory schemes are reviewable when they effectively resolve a claimant’s rights. *See Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. at 108 (holding that a denial of reopening under the Social Security Act is reviewable as a “final decision”); *Weinberger v. Salfi*, 422 U.S. 749, 762 (1975) (explaining that finality attaches when an agency’s action “mark[s] the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process”).

The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Roberts* to bar review of reopening denials conflicts with the weight of authority. The D.C. Circuit recently held in *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), that § 355(f) permits review of reopening denials, reasoning that such decisions are “final” and “dispositive” of a claimant’s rights. *Id.* at 504. The Second and Eighth Circuits have reached similar conclusions. *See Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 370 F.2d 253, 254–55 (2d Cir. 1966); *Sones v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991). The majority of circuits that have addressed this issue—including the Fourth, Sixth, Seventh, and Tenth—have done so under the deferential standard of *Califano v. Sanders*, not by barring review altogether. *See Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141 (citing cases). The Fifth Circuit’s categorical bar is an outlier and should be rejected.

III. The Rule of Orderliness Does Not Bar Review

The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on the rule of orderliness to dismiss Salinas’s petition is misplaced. The rule of orderliness “prevents a three-judge panel from overruling a prior panel decision” only when the earlier decision is “clearly wrong.” *Vaughan v. Anderson Reg’l Med. Ctr.*, 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir. 2017) (quoting *United States v. Lipscomb*, 299 F.3d 303, 310 (5th Cir. 2002)). *Roberts* is not “clearly wrong.” It conflicts with Supreme Court precedent and the weight of circuit authority. Moreover, the rule of orderliness does not apply when a panel decision conflicts with intervening Supreme Court authority. *See, e.g., Miller v. Louisiana State Univ. Bd. of Supervisors*, 635 F.3d 1234, 1240 (11th Cir. 2011). Here, *Califano v. Sanders* and *Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians* undermine *Roberts*’s reasoning.

The Fifth Circuit should correct its erroneous precedent and hold that the Board’s denial of reopening is a final, reviewable order under § 355(f).
