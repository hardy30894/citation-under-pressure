ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN REFUSING TO QUESTION THE JURY TO CLARIFY THE VERDICT

The district court's refusal to question the jury to clarify the verdict was reversible error. When a jury returns an ambiguous verdict, the trial court has a duty to clarify the verdict to ensure that it accurately reflects the jury's intentions. See, e.g., Robles v. Exxon Corp., 862 F.2d 1201, 1204 (5th Cir. 1989) ("When a jury returns an inconsistent or ambiguous verdict, the trial court has a duty to clarify the verdict or send it back for further deliberation."). The court's failure to do so in this case resulted in a verdict that was susceptible to multiple interpretations, leading to an incorrect interpretation that denied Unit any damages.

Unit requested that the trial judge question the jury to clarify the damage awards, but the court refused. This refusal was error, as it denied Unit the opportunity to have the jury's intentions clarified. See, e.g., Karl v. Burlington Northern R.R., 880 F.2d 68, 73 (8th Cir. 1989) ("The trial court's failure to clarify the jury's verdict was error, as it left the meaning of the verdict uncertain."). The district court's interpretation of the verdict, which resulted in Unit recovering no damages, was therefore not supported by the jury's actual intentions.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN INTERPRETING THE JURY'S DAMAGE AWARDS

The district court's interpretation of the jury's damage awards was incorrect. The jury's award of $70,768.02 on Form 1 and $86,494.30 on Form 3 was ambiguous, and the court's interpretation of it was not supported by the record. Unit argued that the jury had already subtracted the amount of Enron's set-off from the amount of the unpaid invoices to arrive at the award of $70,768.02. This interpretation is supported by the fact that the difference between $157,262.36 (the amount of the unpaid invoices) and $70,768.02 is $86,494.34, which is nearly equal to the amount of the set-off awarded to Enron on Form 3.

The district court's interpretation, which subtracted the set-off from the damages award, resulted in Unit recovering no damages. However, this interpretation is not supported by the language of the jury instructions or the verdict forms. See, e.g., Aquachem Co. v. Olin Corp., 699 F.2d 516, 521 (11th Cir. 1983) ("The jury's verdict must be construed in light of the jury instructions and the pleadings."). The court's failure to clarify the verdict with the jury led to this incorrect interpretation.

The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings to determine the correct amount of damages owed to Unit.
