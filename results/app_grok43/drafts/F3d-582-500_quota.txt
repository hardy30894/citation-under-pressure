ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ERDMAN FAILED TO ACCUMULATE 1,250 HOURS OF SERVICE.

This Court exercises plenary review over a grant of summary judgment, viewing the record in the light most favorable to the nonmoving party. McTernan v. City of York, 564 F.3d 636, 646 (3d Cir. 2009). Summary judgment is proper only when no genuine issue of material fact exists and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. Id.

An employee is eligible for FMLA leave if she has been employed for at least twelve months and has worked at least 1,250 hours in the preceding year. 29 U.S.C. § 2611(2)(A); Conoshenti v. Pub. Serv. Elec. & Gas Co., 364 F.3d 135, 148 (3d Cir. 2004). Hours of service are measured according to principles under the Fair Labor Standards Act. Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc., 535 U.S. 81, 94-95 (2002). All time an employee is suffered or permitted to work counts, regardless of whether the employer recorded or compensated it as overtime. Anderson v. Mt. Clemens Pottery Co., 328 U.S. 680, 690-91 (1946); IBP, Inc. v. Alvarez, 546 U.S. 21, 29-30 (2005).

Erdman’s supervisor authorized payment or compensatory time for extra hours she regularly performed outside the office. Those hours therefore constitute compensable work time under the FLSA and must be included in the FMLA eligibility calculation. See Callison v. City of Philadelphia, 430 F.3d 546, 549 (3d Cir. 2005) (hours worked control eligibility). Nationwide’s later directive prohibiting further accumulation did not retroactively erase the hours already worked. Sarnowski v. Air Brooke Limousine, Inc., 510 F.3d 398, 403 (3d Cir. 2007).

An employer may not manipulate records to defeat eligibility when the employee has in fact performed the requisite service. See King v. Preferred Tech. Grp., 166 F.3d 887, 891 (7th Cir. 1999). Because the record contains evidence that Erdman’s extra hours brought her total above 1,250, the district court’s contrary finding was error. See also Bachelder v. Am. W. Airlines, Inc., 259 F.3d 1112, 1124 (9th Cir. 2001) (eligibility turns on actual hours worked).

II. ERDMAN’S FMLA INTERFERENCE AND RETALIATION CLAIMS SHOULD HAVE PROCEEDED TO TRIAL.

To prevail on an interference claim, a plaintiff need only show that she was entitled to leave and the employer denied it. Lichtenstein v. Univ. of Pittsburgh Med. Ctr., 691 F.3d 294, 312 (3d Cir. 2012). Retaliation requires proof that the request for leave was a motivating factor in the discharge. Id. at 307-08. Because Erdman satisfied the hours threshold, both claims survive summary judgment.
