**ARGUMENT**

I. The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f).

The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), misreads the jurisdictional scope of § 355(f). That statute confers jurisdiction over “any order or decision of the Board” after exhaustion of administrative remedies. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Board’s refusal to reopen is an “order or decision” within the plain meaning of the statute. *See generally* *Abbruzzese v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995) (holding that a refusal to reopen is an appealable order); *Steebe v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 708 F.2d 250, 254–55 (7th Cir. 1983) (same).

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *Roberts* rests on an unduly narrow construction of § 355(f). The court relied on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), but *Sanders* addressed the Social Security Act, not the Railroad Retirement Act, and did not construe § 355(f). *Sanders* held that refusals to reopen under the Social Security Act are not reviewable, but it expressly declined to address whether other statutes might provide for review. *Id.* at 107–08. The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Sanders* was therefore misplaced. *See also Gutierrez v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990) (distinguishing *Sanders* and holding that § 355(f) permits review of refusals to reopen).

II. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable as a final decision under the Administrative Procedure Act.

The Board’s refusal to reopen is a final agency action subject to judicial review under the APA. 5 U.S.C. § 704. The APA defines “final agency action” as “the whole or a part of an order, rule, or sanction.” 5 U.S.C. § 551(4). The Board’s refusal to reopen is an “order” within this definition. *See* *Szostak v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 370 F.2d 253, 254–55 (2d Cir. 1966) (holding that a refusal to reopen is a final order subject to review under the APA).

The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Roberts* ignored the APA’s broad definition of final agency action. The court erroneously confined § 355(f) to review of “decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits,” *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 140, but the statute’s text contains no such limitation. *See* 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The APA’s definition of final agency action applies to all orders, including refusals to reopen. *See* *Abbruzzese*, 63 F.3d at 974.

III. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable under the Railroad Retirement Act’s remedial scheme.

The Railroad Retirement Act’s remedial scheme contemplates judicial review of all Board orders, including refusals to reopen. *See* 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Roberts* conflicts with the Act’s remedial purpose. *See* *Steebe*, 708 F.2d at 254–55 (holding that § 355(f) permits review of refusals to reopen to ensure the Act’s remedial scheme is not frustrated).

The Fifth Circuit’s reliance on *Sanders* is misplaced. *Sanders* addressed the Social Security Act, which contains no provision analogous to § 355(f). *Sanders*, 430 U.S. at 107–08. The Railroad Retirement Act’s remedial scheme is distinct and provides for review of all Board orders. *See* *Szostak*, 370 F.2d at 254–55.

IV. The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable under the doctrine of judicial review of refusals to reopen.

The Board’s refusal to reopen is reviewable under the doctrine of judicial review of refusals to reopen. *See* *Abbruzzese*, 63 F.3d at 974; *Steebe*, 708 F.2d at 254–55; *Szostak*, 370 F.2d at 254–55. The Fifth Circuit’s decision in *Roberts* conflicts with this well-established doctrine. The court erroneously confined § 355(f) to review of “decisions on the merits of a claim for benefits,” *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 140, but the doctrine of judicial review of refusals to reopen applies to all refusals to reopen, regardless of the merits of the underlying claim.
