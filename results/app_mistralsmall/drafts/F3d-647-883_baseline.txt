**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Concluding That Buckles’ § 2255 Motion Was Untimely.**

A. The Filing of Buckles’ Motion for Appointment of Certiorari Counsel Restarted the 90-Day Period for Filing a Petition for Certiorari.

Under Supreme Court Rule 13(3), the 90-day period for filing a petition for certiorari runs from “the date of the denial of rehearing” unless the lower court “appropriately entertains an untimely petition for rehearing.” *Supreme Court Rule 13(3)*. Here, Buckles filed a motion for appointment of certiorari counsel pursuant to Ninth Circuit Rule 4-1(e), which this Court construed and denied on June 12, 2007. *See supra* Part I. This Court’s order denying Buckles’ motion for appointment of counsel constituted an “appropriate[] entertain[ment]” of an untimely motion, thereby restarting the 90-day period for filing a petition for certiorari. *See id.*; *see also Lawrence v. Florida*, 549 U.S. 327, 336 (2007) (holding that the 90-day period runs from the date of the denial of rehearing unless the lower court entertains an untimely petition for rehearing).

B. Buckles’ Petition for Certiorari Was Timely Filed Within 90 Days of the June 12, 2007 Order.

Buckles filed his petition for certiorari on September 5, 2007—90 days after the June 12, 2007 order denying his motion for appointment of counsel. *See* Sup. Ct. R. 13(1) (90-day deadline for certiorari review). The Supreme Court Clerk’s notation that the petition was untimely was erroneous, as the 90-day period did not begin to run until June 12, 2007. *See Lawrence*, 549 U.S. at 336.

C. Buckles’ § 2255 Motion Was Timely Filed Within One Year of the Supreme Court’s Denial of Certiorari.

Under 28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)(1), a § 2255 motion must be filed “within one year of the latest of” several specified dates, including “the date on which the judgment of conviction becomes final.” Here, Buckles’ conviction became final on June 12, 2007, when this Court denied his motion for appointment of counsel and reissued the mandate. *See Clay v. United States*, 537 U.S. 522, 527 (2003) (holding that a judgment of conviction becomes final when the time for filing a petition for certiorari expires). Buckles filed his § 2255 motion on September 5, 2008—well within one year of June 12, 2007. *See* 28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)(1).

**II. Buckles Is Entitled to Equitable Tolling of the Statute of Limitations.**

A. Buckles Demonstrates Extraordinary Circumstances Warranting Equitable Tolling.

Equitable tolling is available where a petitioner “has been prevented from timely filing his claim by circumstances beyond his control.” *Pace v. DiGuglielmo*, 544 U.S. 408, 418 (2005). Here, Buckles was misled by the Ninth Circuit Clerk’s erroneous advice that the 90-day period for filing a petition for certiorari would be stayed until a ruling was made on his motion for appointment of counsel. *See supra* Part I. This misinformation constitutes an “extraordinary circumstance” warranting equitable tolling. *See Irwin v. Dep’t of Veterans Affairs*, 498 U.S. 89, 96 (1990) (holding that equitable tolling may be appropriate where a petitioner is misled by court officials).

B. Buckles Acted with Reasonable Diligence in Pursuing His Claims.

Buckles acted diligently in pursuing his claims, filing his motion for appointment of counsel within 21 days of the denial of rehearing and filing his petition for certiorari within 90 days of the June 12, 2007 order. *See supra* Part I. His subsequent § 2255 motion was filed within one year of the Supreme Court’s denial of certiorari. *See* 28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)(1). Under these circumstances, equitable tolling is appropriate. *See Pace*, 544 U.S. at 418.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in concluding that Buckles’ § 2255 motion was untimely. Buckles’ motion was timely filed, and he is entitled to equitable tolling. The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
