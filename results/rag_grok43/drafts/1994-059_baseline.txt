ARGUMENT

The Qualifications Clauses of Article I fix the exclusive requirements for service in Congress. U.S. Const. art. I, § 2, cl. 2; art. I, § 3, cl. 3. States therefore lack authority to impose additional eligibility restrictions, including term limits.

This Court has long recognized that the Clauses establish uniform, national standards that the States may not vary. In *Wiley v. Sinkler*, 179 U.S. 58 (1900), the Court explained that the Constitution “declares how [the House] shall be filled, namely, by election,” and that a State prescribing voter qualifications for its legislature “do[es] not do this with reference to the election for members of Congress.” *Id.* at 62. The same principle governs candidate qualifications: the federal document alone supplies them.

The history of the Clauses confirms their exclusivity. The Framers rejected proposals that would have permitted States to add requirements, opting instead for fixed standards to ensure a national legislature free from parochial barriers. *See Newberry v. United States*, 256 U.S. 232, 250-51 (1921) (discussing the Seventeenth Amendment’s preservation of uniform senatorial qualifications). Allowing Arkansas to disqualify otherwise eligible candidates after a fixed number of terms would recreate the “hodge-podge of qualifications” the Framers sought to avoid. *See Hawke v. Smith (No. 1)*, 253 U.S. 221, 227 (1920) (emphasizing that Article V procedures, not unilateral state action, govern alterations to federal constitutional rules).

The ballot-access mechanism of Amendment 73 does not evade this prohibition. As the Arkansas Supreme Court correctly observed, the practical effect is to render long-serving members ineligible, a direct addition to the constitutional qualifications. *See also Barry v. United States ex rel. Cunningham*, 279 U.S. 597, 614 (1929) (Senate’s power to judge elections and qualifications does not authorize external bodies to enlarge those qualifications). Because the federal qualifications are exclusive, Section 3 of Amendment 73 is void.
