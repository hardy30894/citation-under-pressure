The issue presented in this case is whether a District Court's failure to advise a defendant of his right to appeal, as required by the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, provides ground for habeas relief when the defendant was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The respondent respectfully submits that it does not.

The Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, specifically Rule 32(a)(2), require that the court advise a defendant of his right to appeal after sentencing. However, the failure to comply with this rule does not automatically entitle a defendant to habeas relief. The Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit correctly applied the harmless-error doctrine, as established in Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750, 764-65 (1946), to hold that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review. (See also United States v. Daddato, 160 F.2d 695, 697 (7th Cir. 1947); United States v. Paglia, 190 F.2d 445, 448 (2d Cir. 1951)).

The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that a defendant is aware of his right to appeal. (United States v. Bazzano, 146 F.2d 923, 925 (3d Cir. 1945)). In this case, the District Court found that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing, despite the court's failure to advise him. This finding is consistent with the principle that a defendant's knowledge of his rights is crucial. (Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938); Coppedge v. United States, 369 U.S. 438, 442 (1962)).

When a defendant is already aware of his right to appeal, as Peguero was, the rule's purpose is served. The District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal did not prejudice him, as he was already aware of this right. (Glasser v. United States, 315 U.S. 60, 83 (1942)). The absence of prejudice, combined with Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal, supports the conclusion that the District Court's error was harmless.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that not all procedural errors require reversal. (Snyder v. Massachusetts, 291 U.S. 97, 116-17 (1934)). In this case, the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was a technical error that did not affect the outcome of the case. The Court of Appeals correctly applied the harmless-error doctrine to affirm the District Court's denial of habeas relief.

In conclusion, the respondent submits that the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal does not provide ground for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals' decision affirming the District Court's denial of habeas relief should be upheld, as it is consistent with the harmless-error doctrine and the principle that a defendant's knowledge of his rights can cure a procedural error.
