The plain language of 28 USC section 1407(a) mandates that the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation "shall" remand any action transferred for pretrial proceedings to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The statute's clear directive is reinforced by a long line of cases emphasizing the importance of adhering to the statutory text. See, e.g., United States v. American Trucking Ass'ns, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940) ("There is, of course, no more persuasive evidence of the purpose of a statute than the words by which the legislature undertook to give expression to its wishes.").

The District of Arizona's invocation of section 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the statutory scheme established by section 1407(a). As the court noted in Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501 (1947), the transfer provisions of section 1404(a) must be applied in a manner consistent with the overall statutory framework governing the transfer of cases. In this case, the specific mandate of section 1407(a) takes precedence over the general transfer authority granted by section 1404(a), as is consistent with principles of statutory construction. See, e.g., D. Ginsberg & Sons v. Popkin, 285 U.S. 204 (1932).

Furthermore, the practice of allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial under section 1404(a) undermines the policy choice reflected in section 1407(a), which is to limit the transfer of cases to pretrial proceedings and preserve the plaintiff's traditional right to select the forum for trial. See, e.g., Norwood v. Kirkpatrick, 349 U.S. 29 (1955) (discussing the importance of the plaintiff's choice of forum in the context of section 1404(a)).

The Supreme Court has consistently held that statutory provisions should be interpreted in a manner that gives effect to their plain meaning. In Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470 (1917), the Court stated that the meaning of a statute must be sought in the language in which the act is framed. The Court emphasized the importance of adhering to the statutory text. 

Other cases have also emphasized the importance of adhering to the statutory text and respecting the policy choices reflected in the statute. See, e.g., United States v. Wiltberger, 18 U.S. 76, 95-96 (1820) (Marshall, C.J.) ("The intention of the legislature is to be collected from the words they employ."); United States v. Freight Ass'n, 166 U.S. 290 (1897) (noting the importance of statutory language).

In light of these authorities, it is clear that the District of Arizona erred in invoking section 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial, and the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of that decision was incorrect. The plain language of section 1407(a) and the principles of statutory construction require that the case be remanded to the original district for trial.
