**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred by Dismissing Bryant’s Claim to Enforce the Arbitration Award.**

Bryant has standing to seek judicial enforcement of the arbitration award issued pursuant to the collective bargaining agreement. Under § 301 of the Labor Management Relations Act, 29 U.S.C. § 185, an individual employee may bring suit to enforce a final arbitration award without joining the union. *United Steelworkers v. Enterprise Wheel & Car Corp.*, 363 U.S. 593, 597 (1960) (federal courts have jurisdiction to enforce arbitration awards under § 301). The award runs to Bryant’s benefit, and Bell Atlantic’s refusal to comply gives him a direct stake in the controversy. *See Textile Workers Union v. Lincoln Mills*, 353 U.S. 448, 456–57 (1957). The district court’s contrary conclusion conflicts with settled law.

**II. The District Court Applied an Incorrect Burden-Shifting Standard Under Title VII and § 1981.**

The district court failed to apply the Supreme Court’s clarification of the McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting framework announced in *Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc.*, 530 U.S. 133 (2000). Under *Reeves*, once a plaintiff establishes a prima facie case and produces evidence that the employer’s proffered reason is pretextual, the trier of fact may—and often should—infer discrimination. *Id.* at 147. The district court erroneously required Bryant to produce “additional evidence” of discrimination beyond pretext, in direct contravention of *Reeves*. *See St. Mary’s Honor Center v. Hicks*, 509 U.S. 502, 511 (1993) (discrimination may be inferred from pretext combined with prima facie case). Bryant presented ample evidence that Bell Atlantic’s stated reason—failure to work overtime—was pretextual, including evidence that similarly situated non-African American employees were treated more leniently and that Bryant’s child-care obligations were consistently ignored.

**III. Genuine Issues of Material Fact Preclude Summary Judgment on Bryant’s Discrimination and Retaliation Claims.**

Summary judgment is proper only when no genuine dispute of material fact exists and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986). The district court ignored multiple factual disputes:

- Whether Bryant’s child-care responsibilities were a factor in Bell Atlantic’s disciplinary decisions, which directly bears on gender discrimination under Title VII. *See Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins*, 490 U.S. 228, 251 (1989) (sex stereotyping based on family roles may support discrimination claim).
- Whether Bell Atlantic’s refusal to accept co-worker offers to perform Bryant’s overtime shows disparate treatment compared to non-African American employees with similar conflicts. *See McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792, 804 (1973) (plaintiff may show discrimination through evidence of differential treatment of comparators).
- Whether Bryant engaged in protected activity—filing grievances and opposing the Red Letter Day policy—and was subjected to escalating discipline in retaliation. *See Burlington N. & Santa Fe Ry. v. White*, 548 U.S. 53, 67 (2006) (retaliation includes any action that would dissuade reasonable worker from making or supporting a charge).
- Whether the company’s shifting explanations for Bryant’s termination reveal pretext. *See Reeves*, 530 U.S. at 147.

The district court improperly weighed evidence and resolved credibility disputes at summary judgment. *See Anderson*, 477 U.S. at 255 (trial judge must avoid making credibility determinations at summary judgment). Bryant’s testimony that his child-care needs were known and that he was singled out for discipline creates triable issues. *See Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 327 (1986) (summary judgment inappropriate where facts are disputed). Additionally, the timing of discipline—escalating immediately after Bryant protested—supports an inference of retaliation. *See Clark County Sch. Dist. v. Breeden*, 532 U.S. 268, 273 (2001) (temporal proximity may establish causal link).

**Conclusion**

The district court erred in dismissing Bryant’s arbitration enforcement claim and in granting summary judgment on his Title VII and § 1981 claims. This Court should reverse and remand for trial.
