**ARGUMENT**

The District violated the First Amendment when it denied Lamb’s Chapel access to school premises because the proposed films were “church related.” Once the District opened its facilities for after-hours community use, it could not exclude a speaker based on the religious viewpoint of speech otherwise within the permitted subject matter. *Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 45-46 (1983); *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 276 (1981).

A public school may create a limited public forum by opening its property for certain expressive activity. *Perry*, 460 U.S. at 46. In such a forum, the government may reserve access to particular classes of speakers or subjects, but it may not discriminate against speech because of its viewpoint. *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Def. & Educ. Fund, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 788, 802-06 (1985); *Rosenberger v. Rector & Visitors of Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829-31 (1995). Here, the District allowed after-hours use for community purposes, including film presentations and other meetings, but barred only speech with a religious perspective. That is viewpoint discrimination.

This Court’s precedents make the point. In *Widmar*, a university that had made its facilities generally available to student groups could not exclude a religious group from using the facilities for religious speech. 454 U.S. at 267-70, 273-77. The Court held that once the forum was opened, excluding worship and discussion “simply because” the speech was religious was unconstitutional. Id. at 269-70, 274. The same logic governs here. Lamb’s Chapel sought to show films on family values and child rearing—subjects plainly within the District’s community-use policy—but from a Christian perspective. Refusing access because the films would express religious views on those subjects is the very sort of discrimination forbidden by the First Amendment. *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 828-30.

The Second Circuit erred in treating the District’s exclusion as merely a subject-matter restriction. The Court has long distinguished permissible limits on forum subject from impermissible discrimination among speakers or viewpoints. *Perry*, 460 U.S. at 49; *Cornelius*, 473 U.S. at 806. A restriction that singles out speech on a subject only when expressed from a religious viewpoint is viewpoint discrimination, not neutral subject regulation. *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 831-32. The District did not bar all films about family values or all discussions of child rearing; it barred only a “family” film series because it was “church related.” That is content-based discrimination at minimum, and viewpoint discrimination more precisely. *Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist.*, 959 F.2d 381, 389-90 (2d Cir. 1992).

The District cannot justify its exclusion by invoking the Establishment Clause. The Constitution does not require government to suppress private religious expression in an otherwise open forum. *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 271-75; *Board of Educ. of the Westside Cmty. Schs. v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226, 248-53 (1990). In *Widmar*, the Court rejected the very argument made here: that allowing religious use would amount to endorsement. 454 U.S. at 274-75. The Court explained that equal access, not exclusion, is the proper constitutional course. Id. at 274. Likewise, *Mergens* held that permitting a religious student group to meet on school premises did not violate the Establishment Clause. 496 U.S. at 247-53. If anything, excluding Lamb’s Chapel because it is religious creates the constitutional problem by denying equal access on the basis of faith. *Good News Club v. Milford Cent. Sch.*, 533 U.S. 98, 112-19 (2001).

Nor can the District defend its policy as a reasonable nonpublic-forum restriction. Once a school has opened its property for a category of expressive activity, it may not invoke forum doctrine to suppress religious viewpoints within that category. *Perry*, 460 U.S. at 46; *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 829. The denial here was not based on scheduling, capacity, or disruption. It rested on the District’s judgment that religious speech was disfavored. That is precisely what the First Amendment forbids. *Lamb’s Chapel*, 959 F.2d at 390-91.

The judgment should be reversed. The First Amendment entitles Lamb’s Chapel to equal access to the District’s facilities on the same terms as other community speakers. *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 277; *Mergens*, 496 U.S. at 247-53; *Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 845-46.
