**ARGUMENT**

The district court erred in entering summary judgment for Rand because the summary-judgment record, viewed in the Mouas’ favor, permits a finding that Rand’s violations of TILA and HOEPA extended the right to rescind to three years. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1635(a), a borrower may rescind a covered transaction for three business days after consummation, but if the creditor fails to make required material disclosures, the right continues for three years. *Beach v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, 523 U.S. 410, 411-12 (1998); Jesinoski v. Countrywide Home Loans, Inc., 574 U.S. 259, 261-62 (2015).* Regulation Z likewise provides that the rescission period extends to three years if the creditor fails to deliver the required notice of the right to rescind or the required material disclosures. *12 C.F.R. § 226.23(a)(3); see also Mars v. Spartanburg Chrysler Plymouth, Inc., 713 F.2d 65, 67-68 (4th Cir. 1983).*  

I. **Rand’s HOEPA and TILA disclosure failures created a triable issue that the rescission period was extended.**

HOEPA imposes enhanced disclosure duties in high-cost mortgage transactions, including advance notice and other material disclosures. *15 U.S.C. § 1639(a), (b); 12 C.F.R. § 226.32(c), (d); Jackson v. Grant, 890 F.2d 118, 121-22 (9th Cir. 1989).* The record shows a genuine dispute whether Rand delivered the required three-day advance HOEPA disclosures before closing. The Mouas testified they did not receive them. That alone defeats summary judgment because a creditor’s failure to provide required disclosures extends the rescission right. *See Semar v. Platte Valley Fed. Sav. & Loan Ass’n, 791 F.2d 699, 701-02 (9th Cir. 1986); Rodash v. AIB Mortgage Co., 16 F.3d 1142, 1146-47 (11th Cir. 1994), superseded on other grounds by statute as recognized in Veale v. Citibank, F.S.B., 85 F.3d 577, 579 (11th Cir. 1996).*

Rand’s argument that the Mouas “waived” the advance HOEPA disclosure period also fails as a matter of law. TILA permits waiver of the rescission waiting period only in limited circumstances and only by a “bona fide personal financial emergency.” *15 U.S.C. § 1635(d); 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(e); In re Ramsey, 176 B.R. 183, 186-87 (B.A.P. 9th Cir. 1994).* Whether such an emergency existed is ordinarily a fact-intensive inquiry. *See Nigh v. Koons Buick Pontiac GMC, Inc., 319 F.3d 119, 124-25 (4th Cir. 2003), aff’d on other grounds, 543 U.S. 50 (2004).* Here, the Mouas’ handwritten statement was obtained at the closing, allegedly on the instruction of the closer, without informed understanding of what they were relinquishing. A jury could find that this was not the knowing and voluntary waiver TILA requires. *See In re Porter, 961 F.2d 1066, 1076-77 (3d Cir. 1992); cf. Williams v. Homestake Mortg. Co., 968 F.2d 1137, 1140-41 (11th Cir. 1992).*

II. **The Notice of Right to Cancel was not “clear and conspicuous,” and the premature “Confirmation” did not extinguish the rescission right.**

TILA requires the creditor to deliver notice of the right to cancel in a form that is “clear and conspicuous.” *15 U.S.C. § 1635(a); 12 C.F.R. § 226.23(b)(1).* The notice must inform the borrower of the retention of the right to rescind until midnight of the third business day after consummation or delivery of the required disclosures, whichever is later. *12 C.F.R. § 226.23(b)(1), (c); Handy v. Anchor Mortg. Corp., 464 F.3d 760, 764-65 (7th Cir. 2006).* The Mouas contend they were presented with, and signed, a “Confirmation” stating that more than three business days had elapsed even though the rescission period had not expired. A creditor may not use a form that misstates the existence or expiration of rescission rights. *See Porter, 961 F.2d at 1077-78; Rodash, 16 F.3d at 1146-47.*  

Rand’s claim that Excel Title, not Rand, directed the premature signature does not defeat liability. A creditor is responsible for the acts of its closing agent when the agent performs the creditor’s disclosure obligations. *See, e.g., Krenisky v. Rollins Protective Servs. Co., 728 F.2d 64, 68 (2d Cir. 1984); Baker v. Sunny Chevrolet, Inc., 349 F.3d 862, 869-70 (6th Cir. 2003).* At minimum, the evidence creates a genuine dispute whether Rand, through its closing arrangements, caused the premature execution of a form that could reasonably mislead borrowers into believing rescission was no longer available. *See Handy, 464 F.3d at 765-66; Semar, 791 F.2d at 701-02.*

III. **The inclusion of a prohibited prepayment penalty independently supported rescission.**

HOEPA prohibits certain prepayment penalties in covered high-cost loans. *15 U.S.C. § 1639(c); 12 C.F.R. § 226.32(d)(7).* The Mouas’ loan documents included terms reflecting a prepayment penalty, a feature expressly regulated by HOEPA. If Rand imposed a prohibited penalty, that constituted an independent HOEPA violation and a failure to provide accurate material disclosures. *See Barber v. Beneficial Loan Corp., 844 F.2d 806, 808-09 (11th Cir. 1988); In re Stanley, 441 B.R. 372, 381-82 (Bankr. D. Mass. 2010).* Such a violation is material because it affects the borrower’s cost and repayment rights. *See In re Brown, 538 B.R. 714, 719-20 (Bankr. E.D. Va. 2015).*

Because the record supports findings that Rand failed to provide required HOEPA disclosures, failed to deliver a clear and conspicuous rescission notice, and included an impermissible prepayment penalty, the Mouas’ rescission period extended to three years. The judgment should be reversed.
