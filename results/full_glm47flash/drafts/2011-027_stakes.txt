**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE STATE ACQUIRED TITLE TO THE RIVERBEDS UNDER THE EQUAL FOOTING DOCTRINE.**

The State of Montana holds title to the riverbeds underlying PPL’s hydroelectric facilities because those rivers were navigable at the time of statehood. Under the Equal Footing Doctrine, a state acquires title to lands beneath navigable waters upon its admission to the Union. *United States v. Utah*, 283 U.S. 64, 73 (1931). This doctrine ensures that new states enter the Union with the same rights to public lands as the original states. *Id.* Consequently, the State’s ownership of the riverbeds is not dependent on the construction of dams or the subsequent alteration of the rivers, but rather on the condition of the rivers at the moment Montana became a state in 1889.

**II. THE TEST FOR NAVIGABILITY FOR TITLE PURPOSES IS HISTORICAL AND LIMITED TO COMMERCIAL NAVIGATION.**

The constitutional test for determining navigability for title purposes requires a historical inquiry into the condition of the river at the time of statehood. This test is distinct from the modern test used for federal regulatory purposes, which considers current recreational use. *United States v. Oregon*, 295 U.S. 1, 18 (1935). For title purposes, navigability is limited to the capacity of the waters to support commerce. *Id.* The Supreme Court has held that the "test of navigability for title purposes is whether the waters are navigable in fact at the time of the acquisition of the title by the state." *Id.* This requires a determination of whether the river was used for commercial transport in its ordinary condition at the time of statehood.

**III. THE MISSOURI, MADISON, AND CLARK FORK RIVERS WERE NAVIGABLE AT STATEHOOD.**

The record demonstrates that the Missouri, Madison, and Clark Fork Rivers were capable of supporting commercial navigation at the time of statehood. *United States v. Oregon*, 295 U.S. at 18. These rivers served as vital arteries for the transport of goods and people in the late 19th century. Because the rivers were navigable in fact at the time of statehood, the State acquired title to the beds and banks of these waterways. *United States v. Utah*, 283 U.S. at 73. PPL’s construction of dams on these rivers occurred long after the State acquired title to the riverbeds, and does not alter the historical fact of navigability that established the State’s ownership. Therefore, the trial court correctly applied the historical navigability test to determine that the State holds title to the riverbeds underlying PPL’s facilities.
