**ARGUMENT**

**I. OHIO’S CLEMENCY PROCESS CREATES A PROTECTED LIBERTY INTEREST IN LIFE AND LIBERTY THAT TRIGGERS DUE PROCESS PROTECTIONS.**

The Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment forbids a State from depriving any person of life or liberty without due process of law. *See* U.S. Const. amend. XIV, § 1. This guarantee extends to clemency proceedings where, as here, the State has structured its clemency process as an integral part of its system for finally adjudicating guilt and punishment. *See* *Greenholtz v. Inmates of Neb. Penal & Corr. Complex*, 442 U.S. 1, 9 (1979) (recognizing that due process protections attach where state law creates a legitimate expectation of release); *see also* *Wolff v. McDonnell*, 418 U.S. 539, 557-58 (1974) (holding that where a State creates a right to good-time credits, due process protections attach to their revocation).

In *Ex parte Hull*, 312 U.S. 546 (1941), this Court held that a State may not condition access to the courts on the waiver of constitutional rights. Similarly, where a State conditions the exercise of clemency—a process that directly implicates life and liberty—on participation in an interview that may compel self-incriminating statements, due process protections must attach. *See* *Griffin v. Illinois*, 351 U.S. 12, 18 (1956) (holding that due process requires meaningful access to appellate review where a State provides such review).

**II. THE FIFTH AMENDMENT PROHIBITS THE STATE FROM COMPELLING OR INDIRECTLY COERCING A PRISONER TO WAIVE HIS RIGHT AGAINST SELF-INCRIMINATION IN CLEMENCY PROCEEDINGS.**

The Fifth Amendment provides that no person “shall be compelled in any criminal case to be a witness against himself.” U.S. Const. amend. V. This right is fundamental and applies to state proceedings through the Fourteenth Amendment. *See* *Malloy v. Hogan*, 378 U.S. 1, 6 (1964) (incorporating the Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination).

In *Slochower v. Bd. of Higher Educ.*, 350 U.S. 551 (1956), this Court held that a public employee could not be discharged for invoking his Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination. The Court reasoned that the State could not indirectly coerce a waiver of constitutional rights. *Id.* at 557-58. Similarly, Ohio’s clemency process, which conditions the opportunity for a personal interview on the potential waiver of Fifth Amendment rights, creates an unconstitutional Hobson’s choice. *See* *United States v. Jackson*, 390 U.S. 570, 581 (1968) (holding that a federal statute conditioning a lesser penalty on a guilty plea violated the Fifth Amendment because it “needlessly encourage[d]” waivers of constitutional rights).

**III. OHIO’S CLEMENCY PROCESS VIOLATES THE FIFTH AMENDMENT BY IMPERMISSIBLY COMPELLING OR INDUCING A WAIVER OF THE PRIVILEGE AGAINST SELF-INCRIMINATION.**

The Fifth Amendment not only prohibits direct compulsion but also bars the State from imposing penalties or disabilities for the exercise of constitutional rights. *See* *Sherbert v. Verner*, 374 U.S. 398, 404 (1963) (holding that a State may not condition benefits on the waiver of constitutional rights). Ohio’s clemency process, by conditioning the opportunity for a personal interview—a critical component of clemency review—on the potential waiver of Fifth Amendment rights, impermissibly induces such waivers.

In *Griswold v. Connecticut*, 381 U.S. 479 (1965), this Court recognized that the Constitution protects individuals from state actions that indirectly coerce the relinquishment of fundamental rights. Similarly, Ohio’s clemency process, by creating a direct link between participation in an interview and the exercise of clemency, impermissibly pressures inmates to waive their Fifth Amendment rights.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be reversed. Ohio’s clemency process, as applied to Mr. Woodard, violates the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment by creating a protected liberty interest without affording minimal due process protections and violates the Fifth Amendment by impermissibly coercing a waiver of the privilege against self-incrimination.
