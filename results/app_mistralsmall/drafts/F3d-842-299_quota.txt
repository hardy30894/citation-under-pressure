**ARGUMENT**

**I. The District Court Erred in Denying Remand Because This Case Does Not Arise Under Federal Law.**

The Pressls’ complaint sought only a declaration of their rights under state property law—specifically, the scope of APCO’s flowage easement and their own rights to build a dock. A case arises under federal law only when the plaintiff’s well-pleaded complaint necessarily raises a federal issue. *Caterpillar Inc. v. Williams*, 482 U.S. 386, 392 (1987). Here, the Pressls’ claims are grounded in state law and do not implicate any federal issue on their face.

APCO’s removal argument—that the case arises under federal law because the property lies within a FERC-licensed project—fails. The mere presence of a federal regulatory scheme does not confer federal jurisdiction over state-law claims. *Gulf Offshore Co. v. Mobil Oil Corp.*, 453 U.S. 473, 478 (1981). The Pressls’ complaint does not challenge APCO’s FERC license or seek review of any FERC action. *See id.* at 479 (finding no federal jurisdiction where the plaintiff’s claims did not require interpretation of federal regulations). Thus, the district court lacked subject matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331.

**II. The District Court Erred in Concluding That It Had Jurisdiction Under 16 U.S.C. § 825p.**

APCO alternatively argued—and the district court agreed—that jurisdiction existed under 16 U.S.C. § 825p, which grants federal courts jurisdiction over civil actions arising under the Federal Power Act. However, § 825p applies only to actions that “arise under” the Federal Power Act itself. *See First Iowa Hydro-Electric Co-op. v. Fed. Power Comm’n*, 328 U.S. 152, 166 (1946) (limiting § 825p jurisdiction to cases involving the Federal Power Act’s provisions). The Pressls’ complaint does not allege any violation of the Federal Power Act or seek relief under its terms. *See id.* at 167 (holding that § 825p does not confer jurisdiction over state-law claims). Therefore, § 825p does not provide an independent basis for federal jurisdiction here.

**III. The District Court Erred in Dismissing the Pressls’ Claims on the Merits.**

Even assuming, arguendo, that federal jurisdiction existed, the district court erred in dismissing the Pressls’ claims. The flowage easement grants APCO the right to overflow the land below the 800-foot contour but does not expressly authorize APCO to regulate the Pressls’ use of their property above that contour. *See Restatement (Third) of Property (Servitudes) § 1.2 cmt. a* (2000) (explaining that easements are strictly construed against the dominant estate). The easement’s grant of APCO’s right to “possess and use” the land is limited to the portion below 800 feet. *See id.* § 1.5 cmt. b (noting that easement rights are confined to the scope of the grant).

The district court’s reliance on Virginia law to uphold APCO’s demand for an Occupancy and Use Permit is misplaced. Virginia law recognizes that easements are limited to their express terms. *See, e.g., Meeks v. Johnson*, 233 Va. 556, 560 (1987) (holding that an easement’s scope is determined by its express terms). The Pressls’ right to use their property for recreational purposes, including building a dock, is not inconsistent with APCO’s flowage easement. *See id.* at 561 (finding that use must not materially interfere with the easement’s purpose).

**IV. The District Court Erred in Requiring the Pressls to Exhaust Administrative Remedies Before Filing Suit.**

The district court held that the Pressls must raise their complaints with FERC before filing suit. However, exhaustion of administrative remedies is not required where the plaintiff seeks purely state-law relief and does not challenge a FERC order or regulation. *See, e.g., City of Tacoma v. Taxpayers of Tacoma*, 357 U.S. 320, 340 (1958) (holding that exhaustion is not required where the plaintiff’s claims do not involve a federal regulatory scheme). The Pressls’ complaint seeks only a declaration of their rights under state property law, not review of FERC’s actions. Thus, exhaustion was not required.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Pressls respectfully request that this Court vacate the district court’s judgment and remand the case to the district court with instructions to remand the case to state court.
