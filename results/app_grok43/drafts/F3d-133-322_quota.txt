ARGUMENT

Mrs. Parker’s convictions rest on an erroneous construction of “official act” under 18 U.S.C. § 201(a)(3) and (b)(2)(C). An official act is “any decision or action on any question, matter, cause, suit, proceeding or controversy, which may at any time be pending, or which may by law be brought before any public official, in such official’s official capacity.” 18 U.S.C. § 201(a)(3). This definition requires that the act be one the official is authorized to perform. United States v. Birdsall, 233 U.S. 223, 231 (1914) (duties “established by settled practice” define the scope of official action). Mrs. Parker’s duties were limited to assisting ALJ Aronson, recording hearings, and typing decisions; she possessed no authority to approve benefits or sign his name. Acts outside that authority fall beyond the statute. See United States v. Muntain, 610 F.2d 964, 969 (D.C. Cir. 1979) (no official act where employee lacked power to influence the matter).

The same limitation appears in Fifth Circuit precedent. United States v. Tomblin, 46 F.3d 1369, 1380 (5th Cir. 1995) (bribery requires “a specific official act” within the official’s power). Accord United States v. Dobbs, 506 F.2d 445, 447 (5th Cir. 1975) (conviction reversed where defendant had no official capacity to perform the promised act). Mrs. Parker’s alteration of letters and forgery of the ALJ’s signature were not decisions “brought before” her “in [her] official capacity.” 18 U.S.C. § 201(a)(3). They were instead private crimes.

Other circuits have reached identical conclusions. United States v. Carson, 464 F.2d 424, 433 (2d Cir. 1972) (official act must involve “a matter that is already pending or may by law be brought before a public official”); United States v. Biaggi, 853 F.2d 89, 98 (2d Cir. 1988) (same); United States v. Rabbitt, 583 F.2d 1014, 1020 (8th Cir. 1978) (no official act where legislator lacked authority over the transaction). The government’s theory—that any use of government resources or access constitutes an official act—would erase the statutory boundary. Dixson v. United States, 465 U.S. 482, 496 (1984) (official act requires “a question, matter, cause, suit, proceeding or controversy” pending before the official).

Because the indictment charged only acts Mrs. Parker had no authority to perform, it failed to allege an essential element. The convictions must be reversed.
