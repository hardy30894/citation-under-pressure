The Center Moriches Union Free School District's denial of Lamb's Chapel's request to use school facilities for a religious-oriented film series constitutes a content-based exclusion of speech in a limited public forum, violating the First Amendment's freedom of speech. The District's facilities, although not traditional public forums, were opened for use by the general public for certain purposes, thereby creating a limited public forum.

As established in Hague v. CIO, 307 U.S. 496, 515 (1939), the government's power to regulate expression in a public forum is limited. The exclusion of speech from a public forum based on content is permissible only if it serves a compelling state interest and is narrowly drawn to achieve that interest, as required by Cantwell v. Connecticut, 310 U.S. 296 (1940). In Cantwell, the Court emphasized that the state may not unduly suppress free communication of views. The District's exclusion of Lamb's Chapel's film series was based on the religious content of the speech.

The District's policy, as stated in Local Rule No. 7, prohibited the use of school premises by any group for religious purposes. Lamb's Chapel's application described the film series as a family-oriented movie from a Christian perspective. This rule, as applied to Lamb's Chapel, constitutes a content-based exclusion.

The Supreme Court has held that a state may not exclude speech from a public forum based on its content unless the exclusion is necessary to serve a compelling state interest. In Lovell v. City of Griffin, 303 U.S. 444, 451 (1938), the Court emphasized the importance of protecting freedom of speech. Similarly, in Niemotko v. Maryland, 340 U.S. 268, 272 (1951), the Court held that a regulation that grants or denies a permit based on the content of the speech is not viewpoint-neutral.

Furthermore, the District's policy and practice of excluding religious groups from using school facilities while allowing other groups to use the facilities for various purposes is not viewpoint-neutral. As the Court held in Kunz v. New York, 340 U.S. 290, 294 (1951), a prior restraint on speech is not justified simply because there have been previous instances of disorder or unrest. The District's exclusion of Lamb's Chapel's film series is not justified by a history of prior religious uses, as there is no evidence that the District had previously allowed such uses.

In conclusion, the District's denial of Lamb's Chapel's request to use school facilities for a religious-oriented film series constitutes a content-based exclusion of speech in a limited public forum, violating the First Amendment's freedom of speech. The District's policy and practice of excluding religious groups from using school facilities while allowing other groups to use the facilities for various purposes is not viewpoint-neutral and does not serve a compelling state interest, as supported by the principles in Zorach v. Clauson, 343 U.S. 306, 314 (1952).
