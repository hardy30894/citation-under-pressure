**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON APPELLANT’S ADA AND MHRA CLAIMS BECAUSE PINE LAWN REGARDED HIM AS DISABLED.**

The district court erred in granting summary judgment on Appellant’s claims under the Americans with Disabilities Act, 42 U.S.C. § 12101 *et seq.* (“ADA”), and the Missouri Human Rights Act, Mo. Rev. Stat. § 213.010 *et seq.* (“MHRA”), because Pine Lawn regarded Appellant as disabled within the meaning of these statutes. Under the ADA and MHRA, an individual is “regarded as” disabled if he or she is subjected to an action prohibited under these statutes because of an actual or perceived impairment that is not transitory and minor. *Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc.*, 527 U.S. 471, 489 (1999); *Taylor v. Principal Financial Group, Inc.*, 93 S.W.3d 349, 355 (Mo. Ct. App. 2002).

Pine Lawn terminated Appellant based on its perception that his degenerative disk disease rendered him unable to perform the essential functions of a police officer. Chief Hardy’s memoranda to the Mayor and Board of Aldermen explicitly relied on Dr. Mirkin’s assessment that Appellant would “very likely need to seek a different type of occupation” and that he would “never be able to function as a regular officer.” These statements demonstrate that Pine Lawn regarded Appellant as substantially limited in the major life activity of working. *See* 29 C.F.R. § 1630.2(j)(3)(i) (2002) (defining “substantially limited” as “significantly restricted” in the ability to perform a class of jobs or a broad range of jobs in various classes).

Pine Lawn’s reliance on the number of workers’ compensation claims and sick days is further evidence of its discriminatory motive. *See* *Daubert v. City of W. Frankfort*, 139 F.3d 1057, 1060 (7th Cir. 1998) (holding that an employer’s reliance on excessive workers’ compensation claims and sick days may support an inference of discrimination under the ADA). The district court improperly discounted this evidence, instead focusing on the lack of a formal “regarded as” disability determination by Pine Lawn. However, the ADA and MHRA do not require a formal label; they prohibit discrimination based on an employer’s perception of disability. *See* *Murphy v. United Parcel Serv., Inc.*, 527 U.S. 516, 521 (1999) (holding that the ADA protects individuals regarded as disabled, regardless of whether the employer formally labeled them as such).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON APPELLANT’S WORKERS’ COMPENSATION RETALIATION CLAIM BECAUSE PINE LAWN’S ACTIONS WERE NOT SHIELDED BY SOVEREIGN IMMUNITY.**

The district court erred in granting summary judgment on Appellant’s workers’ compensation retaliation claim under Mo. Rev. Stat. § 287.780 because Pine Lawn is not shielded by sovereign immunity. Section 287.780 prohibits employers from discharging or discriminating against employees for exercising their rights under the Missouri Workers’ Compensation Law. *See* *Foster v. Cardinal Glennon Hosp.*, 994 S.W.2d 593, 597 (Mo. Ct. App. 1999).

Pine Lawn, as a municipal corporation, is not entitled to sovereign immunity for claims arising under § 287.780. *See* *State ex rel. City of Kansas City v. Ross*, 51 S.W.3d 269, 273 (Mo. 2001) (holding that municipal corporations are not entitled to sovereign immunity for claims under the Missouri Human Rights Act). The district court incorrectly applied sovereign immunity to bar Appellant’s retaliation claim, as § 287.780 does not contain an immunity provision. *See* *Huffman v. City of Kansas City*, 989 S.W.2d 190, 193 (Mo. Ct. App. 1999) (holding that sovereign immunity does not bar claims under § 287.780).

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON APPELLANT’S INTENTIONAL INFLICTION OF EMOTIONAL DISTRESS CLAIM BECAUSE PINE LAWN’S CONDUCT WAS EXTREME AND OUTRAGEOUS.**

The district court erred in granting summary judgment on Appellant’s intentional infliction of emotional distress claim because Pine Lawn’s conduct was extreme and outrageous. To prevail on such a claim under Missouri law, Appellant must show that Pine Lawn’s conduct was “so outrageous in character, and so extreme in degree, as to go beyond all possible bounds of decency, and to be regarded as atrocious, and utterly intolerable in a civilized community.” *Farrow v. Health Care Servs. Corp.*, 92 S.W.3d 430, 436 (Mo. Ct. App. 2002) (quoting *Restatement (Second) of Torts* § 46 cmt. d (1965)).

Pine Lawn’s termination of Appellant, following Chief Hardy’s repeated reliance on his workers’ compensation history and perceived disability, was sufficiently extreme and outrageous to support a claim for intentional infliction of emotional distress. *See* *Jones v. City of Kansas City*, 761 S.W.2d 722, 726 (Mo. Ct. App. 1988) (holding that an employer’s conduct in terminating an employee based on a disability may be extreme and outrageous). The district court improperly dismissed this claim, as Appellant presented sufficient evidence to create a genuine issue of material fact.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court’s summary judgment in favor of Pine Lawn should be reversed, and this matter should be remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
