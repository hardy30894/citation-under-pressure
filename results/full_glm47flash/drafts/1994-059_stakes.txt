**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE QUALIFICATIONS CLAUSES OF ARTICLE I, SECTION 5 OF THE UNITED STATES CONSTITUTION ARE EXCLUSIVE AND PRECLUDE STATES FROM IMPOSING ADDITIONAL QUALIFICATIONS, SUCH AS TERM LIMITS, ON MEMBERS OF CONGRESS.**

The Constitution enumerates the qualifications for members of Congress in Article I, Section 5. This list is not merely a floor for eligibility; it is an exclusive set of requirements. In *Powell v. McCormack*, 395 U.S. 486 (1969), the Supreme Court held that the House of Representatives could not exclude a member based on conduct unrelated to the enumerated qualifications. The Court reasoned that the Constitution’s qualifications are "exclusive" and that Congress lacks the power to add to them. This principle extends to the states. The Qualifications Clauses are "minimum" qualifications, not "maximum" ones. *U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton*, 514 U.S. 779, 788 (1995). Therefore, a state cannot add a term limit to the qualifications for a member of the House or Senate.

**II. THE CONSTITUTION REQUIRES UNIFORMITY IN THE QUALIFICATIONS OF MEMBERS OF CONGRESS TO PREVENT A "HODGEPODGE" OF STATE REQUIREMENTS.**

The Founding Fathers designed the Qualifications Clauses to ensure uniformity across the nation. In *Hollingsworth v. Virginia*, 3 U.S. (3 Dall.) 1 (1798), the Supreme Court noted that the Constitution is a compact among the states, and uniformity is essential to the national character of the legislature. If states were free to add qualifications, the result would be a "hodgepodge" of varying requirements. *Thornton*, 514 U.S. at 789. This would undermine the national character of Congress and the uniformity of the federal system. The Arkansas Term Limitation Amendment attempts to do exactly this by imposing a residency-based term limit on Arkansas representatives, which is not authorized by the Constitution.

**III. AMENDMENT 73 VIOLATES ARTICLE I, SECTION 5 OF THE UNITED STATES CONSTITUTION.**

Because the Qualifications Clauses are exclusive, Amendment 73, which attempts to limit the terms of Arkansas’s congressional delegation, is unconstitutional. The people of Arkansas have no power to alter the qualifications for members of Congress. *Thornton*, 514 U.S. at 790. The Amendment is therefore void.
