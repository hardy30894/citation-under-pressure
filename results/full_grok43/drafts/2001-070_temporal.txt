ARGUMENT

The Eighth Amendment forbids only those punishments that are both cruel and unusual in the sense of involving torture or lingering death. See Wilkerson v. Utah, 99 U.S. 130, 136 (1879) (approving execution by firing squad because it did not “involve torture or a lingering death”); In re Kemmler, 136 U.S. 436, 447 (1890) (same for electrocution). The Amendment’s prohibition is “directed against” punishments “manifestly cruel and unusual, as burning at the stake, crucifixion, breaking on the wheel, or the like.” O’Neil v. Vermont, 144 U.S. 323, 339 (1892) (Field, J., dissenting, adopted in Weems v. United States, 217 U.S. 349, 368 (1910)).

Execution of a person with mental retardation does not inflict any such prohibited method. The sentence remains death by lawful means long approved by this Court. See Louisiana ex rel. Francis v. Resweber, 329 U.S. 459, 464 (1947) (second attempt at electrocution after first failure held constitutional because the State sought “merely to enforce the original sentence”). Mental capacity bears on culpability, yet the Amendment has never required a particular degree of intelligence as a prerequisite to capital punishment. See Trop v. Dulles, 356 U.S. 86, 99 (1958) (plurality) (Eighth Amendment draws its meaning from “the evolving standards of decency that mark the progress of a maturing society,” but still permits death for the most serious offenses).

The common-law tradition likewise recognized no categorical exemption for the mentally deficient. At the time of the Bill of Rights, English and colonial law permitted execution of those found guilty of capital crimes even when their understanding was limited, provided they were competent to stand trial. That historical practice confirms the punishment is not “unusual.” See Weems, 217 U.S. at 366–67 (Eighth Amendment incorporates the “cruel and unusual punishments” known to the Framers).

Because the sentence imposed here employs an accepted method and rests on a conviction obtained under established procedures, it does not violate the Eighth Amendment.
