ARGUMENT

THE ELEVENTH CIRCUIT CORRECTLY AFFIRMED DISMISSAL BECAUSE THE REVISED POLICIES RENDERED THE CASE MOOT AND NOMINAL DAMAGES CANNOT PRESERVE A LIVE CONTROVERSY.

A case becomes moot when the issues presented are no longer live or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome. The district court found that Georgia Gwinnett College “unambiguously terminated the Prior Policies and there is no reasonable basis to expect that it will return to them.” Appellants do not contest that their claims for declaratory and injunctive relief are moot.

Under binding precedent, “a prayer for nominal damages generally will not save an otherwise moot challenge to an allegedly unconstitutional policy or law.” Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs, 868 F.3d 1248, 1263 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc). The First Amended Complaint requested only nominal damages. It alleged no actual injury—monetary loss, physical pain, emotional distress, or reputational harm—required for compensatory damages in a § 1983 action. Slicker v. Jackson, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230-31 (11th Cir. 2000). References to “monetary damages” and “damages in an amount to be determined by the evidence” were properly construed, in context, as seeking only nominal relief. Chaparro v. Carnival Corp., 693 F.3d 1333, 1335 (11th Cir. 2012).

This case is not distinguishable from Flanigan’s on the ground that it involves an as-applied challenge. The prior-precedent rule binds this Court to follow Flanigan’s “unless and until it is overruled by this Court en banc or by the Supreme Court.” United States v. Vega-Castillo, 540 F.3d 1235, 1236 (11th Cir. 2008) (quoting United States v. Brown, 342 F.3d 1245, 1246 (11th Cir. 2003)).

The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying leave to amend. A request for leave embedded in an opposition memorandum is not properly raised. Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018) (quoting Rosenberg v. Gould, 554 F.3d 962, 967 (11th Cir. 2009)). Appellants failed to attach or describe the substance of any proposed amendment. Newton v. Duke Energy Fla., LLC, 895 F.3d 1270, 1277 (11th Cir. 2018). After dismissal without prejudice, any further request could have been made under Rule 59(e) or 60(b), yet none was pursued. Czeremcha v. Int’l Ass’n of Machinists & Aerospace Workers, AFL-CIO, 724 F.2d 1552, 1556 (11th Cir. 1984).

The judgment should be affirmed.
