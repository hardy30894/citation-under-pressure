The Railroad Retirement Act authorizes judicial review of “any final decision of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). That language encompasses a Board order denying reopening. Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 826 F.3d 500, 502 (D.C. Cir. 2016) (holding that “the Railroad Retirement Act grants the Court jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations”); Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 933 F.2d 636, 638 (8th Cir. 1991); Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254–55 (2d Cir. 1966).

A decision refusing to reopen is final because it terminates the administrative process and leaves the claimant with “no further recourse within the agency.” Abbott Labs. v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136, 149 (1967). The same principle governs analogous reopening provisions under the Social Security Act. Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99, 108 (1977) (recognizing that reopening decisions may be reviewed when they rest on legal error); Bowen v. City of New York, 476 U.S. 467, 483 (1986).

Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003), erred in holding otherwise. Its reliance on the Fourth, Sixth, Seventh, and Tenth Circuits is unpersuasive because those decisions misread § 355(f) as limited to “decisions on the merits.” Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999); Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990); Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 708 F.2d 250, 254–55 (7th Cir. 1983); Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995). Section 355(f) contains no such textual restriction.

Because the Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application was a final decision, the court of appeals possessed jurisdiction under § 355(f). The petition should be granted and the judgment reversed.
