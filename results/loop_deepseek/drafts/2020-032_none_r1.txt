**ARGUMENT**

The case is moot. Article III of the Constitution limits federal jurisdiction to actual "Cases" and "Controversies." *Muskrat v. United States*, 219 U.S. 346, 356 (1911). This requirement persists at every stage of litigation. *Mills v. Green*, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895). When the challenged government policy is repealed and no plaintiff remains subject to it, the controversy ceases to exist. *Hall v. Beals*, 396 U.S. 45, 48 (1969) (per curiam); *Golden v. Zwickler*, 394 U.S. 103, 108 (1969). Here, Georgia Gwinnett College revised its "Freedom of Expression Policy" and "Student Code of Conduct" while the suit was pending, and Uzuegbunam graduated. The only remaining claim is for nominal damages. That claim cannot revive a moot case.

**I. The Policy Change Extinguished All Ongoing Controversy**

A plaintiff must show a "substantial controversy, between parties having adverse legal interests, of sufficient immediacy and reality to warrant the issuance of a declaratory judgment." *Aetna Life Ins. Co. v. Haworth*, 300 U.S. 227, 240–41 (1937). Once the law that allegedly caused injury has been repealed, no controversy remains. *California v. San Pablo & Tulare R.R. Co.*, 149 U.S. 308, 314 (1893) (moot when "the act complained of" has been "abandoned"). The new policy permits expressive activity anywhere on campus without a permit, and the challenged code-of-conduct provision has been removed. There is no reasonable expectation that the college will reimpose the prior restrictions. *See United States v. W.T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. 629, 632–33 (1953) (voluntary cessation moots case if "there is no reasonable expectation that the wrong will be repeated"). The college's action was "unambiguous" and "permanent." *Liner v. Jafco, Inc.*, 375 U.S. 301, 305 (1964) (mootness where "the challenged conduct has ceased and there is no reasonable expectation of recurrence"). Because the policy is gone, both the facial and as-applied challenges are moot.

**II. Nominal Damages Do Not Revive a Moot Case**

A plaintiff cannot "maintain a suit after the right to relief has been extinguished by the act of the defendant" simply by requesting nominal damages. *Mills*, 159 U.S. at 653. Nominal damages are a "technical" remedy that presupposes a live controversy; they cannot create one where none exists. *St. Pierre v. United States*, 319 U.S. 41, 42 (1943) (per curiam) (mootness where sentence served and no collateral consequences). The Court has consistently held that once the underlying dispute ends, the case must be dismissed even if nominal damages are sought. *Atherton Mills v. Johnston*, 259 U.S. 13, 16 (1922) (moot when child labor law expired, despite request for damages). *See also Brownlow v. Schwartz*, 261 U.S. 216, 218 (1923) (moot when building demolished, despite claim for damages). The only exception is when the plaintiff has suffered a concrete, continuing injury. *Sibron v. New York*, 392 U.S. 40, 55–56 (1968) (collateral consequences exception). Here, the complaint alleged no actual injury—only an abstract violation. Abstract injury, without more, cannot sustain a case after the policy is gone. *Golden*, 394 U.S. at 108–09.

**III. The Voluntary Cessation Doctrine Does Not Save This Case**

The voluntary cessation doctrine can avoid mootness only if the defendant is "free to return to its old ways." *W.T. Grant*, 345 U.S. at 632. But the college's policy change was a formal, permanent revision, not a temporary retreat. *Southern Pac. Terminal Co. v. ICC*, 219 U.S. 498, 514–15 (1911) (moot when "there is no probability that the acts complained of will be repeated"). The new policy superseded the old one, and the college has no incentive to reinstate a policy already held unconstitutional. There is no "reasonable expectation" of recurrence. *Liner*, 375 U.S. at 305. Moreover, the burden of proving a reasonable expectation of recurrence falls on the party opposing mootness. *W.T. Grant*, 345 U.S. at 633. Appellants have offered no evidence that the college intends to return to its prior policies.

**IV. The District Court Properly Denied Leave to Amend**

Appellants sought leave to amend only in a response to a motion to dismiss, not by a properly filed motion. This Court has long held that a request for leave to amend "imbedded within an opposition memorandum" is not properly raised. *Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018). Even if the request were procedurally proper, amendment would be futile because the complaint alleged no actual injury to support compensatory damages. *Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000). The district court did not abuse its discretion.

**Conclusion**

Because the challenged policies were repealed and no plaintiff remains subject to them, the case is moot. A prayer for nominal damages cannot supply the ongoing controversy required by Article III. The district court properly dismissed the complaint. This Court should affirm.
