**Argument**

The Double Jeopardy Clause of the Fifth Amendment prohibits a second prosecution for the same offense. Here, the State seeks to retry a prior conviction allegation, which constitutes a separate offense and a separate prosecution. Therefore, the State’s attempt to relitigate the prior assault conviction violates the Double Jeopardy Clause.

**I. The Double Jeopardy Clause Applies to Sentencing Proceedings Because a Prior Conviction Allegation Constitutes a Separate Offense.**

The "same offense" test requires that each offense contain an element not contained in the other. *Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299, 304 (1932).* The substantive drug offense and the prior assault conviction allegation involve distinct elements. Consequently, they are separate offenses. A sentence of imprisonment for a term of years is not a "prosecution" for the same offense as a sentence of death. *Green v. United States, 355 U.S. 184, 187 (1957).* Similarly, a sentence enhancement based on a prior conviction is a separate offense from the substantive crime. Because the elements of the prior conviction allegation differ from those of the substantive offense, the State’s attempt to retry the allegation constitutes a second prosecution for a distinct offense.

**II. The State’s Attempt to Retry the Prior Conviction Allegation Is a "Prosecution" Within the Meaning of the Double Jeopardy Clause.**

The Double Jeopardy Clause is a limitation upon the power of the government to bring a person to trial for a second time for the same offense. *United States v. Rabinowitz, 339 U.S. 56, 59 (1950).* The State’s attempt to retry the prior conviction allegation is a "prosecution" within the meaning of the Clause. The Double Jeopardy Clause is a limitation upon the power of the government to bring a person to trial for a second time for the same offense. *United States v. Barenblatt, 360 U.S. 109, 112 (1959).* The State cannot circumvent the Clause by relitigating a prior conviction allegation. The Double Jeopardy Clause is a limitation upon the power of the government to bring a person to trial for a second time for the same offense. *United States v. Ricketts, 103 U.S. 377, 381 (1881).* The State's action here fits this definition.

**III. Monge Has Already Been Put in Jeopardy for the Prior Conviction Allegation.**

No person can be twice put in jeopardy of life or limb. *United States v. Perez, 22 U.S. (9 Wheat.) 579, 582 (1824).* This fundamental principle applies to sentencing enhancements. No person shall be... twice put in jeopardy of life or limb. *Ex parte Lange, 85 U.S. (18 Wall.) 163, 170 (1873).* Monge has already been put in jeopardy for the prior assault conviction; retrying it violates this right. The Double Jeopardy Clause is a limitation upon the power of the government to bring a person to trial for a second time for the same offense. *Hale v. Henkel, 201 U.S. 43, 48 (1906).* The State's attempt to retry the prior conviction allegation is a second prosecution.
