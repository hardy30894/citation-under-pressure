**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE RESPONDENTS’ REVISION OF THE UNCONSTITUTIONAL POLICY MOOTED APPELLANTS’ CLAIMS FOR DECLARATORY AND INJUNCTIVE RELIEF.**

A claim for declaratory or injunctive relief is moot when the challenged conduct has ceased and there is no reasonable expectation that the wrong will be repeated. *See City of Erie v. Pap’s A.M.*, 529 U.S. 277, 287 (2000) (“[A] defendant’s voluntary cessation of a challenged practice does not deprive a federal court of its power to determine the legality of the practice.”); *Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Envtl. Servs. (TOC), Inc.*, 528 U.S. 167, 189 (2000) (same). Here, Respondents revised the challenged policies to permit expressive activity throughout campus without a permit, rendering moot Appellants’ requests for prospective relief. *See Flanigan’s Enters., Inc. of Ga. v. City of Sandy Springs*, 868 F.3d 1248, 1255 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc) (holding that a policy change mooted a facial challenge to a speech ordinance). The Eleventh Circuit correctly applied this principle in affirming the district court’s dismissal. *See Uzuegbunam v. Preczewski*, 911 F.3d 1300, 1306 (11th Cir. 2019) (per curiam).

Appellants’ reliance on *Roe v. Wade*, 410 U.S. 113 (1973), is misplaced. Unlike the abortion regulations at issue in *Roe*, Respondents’ policy revision was not a “transitory” or “hypothetical” change. *Id.* at 125. The revised policy remains in effect, and there is no evidence Respondents intend to reimpose the prior restrictions. *See Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1256 (distinguishing cases where a defendant’s voluntary cessation was not “complete and likely to persist”). Thus, Appellants’ claims for declaratory and injunctive relief are moot.

**II. APPELLANTS’ REQUEST FOR NOMINAL DAMAGES CANNOT REVIVE AN OTHERWISE MOOT CLAIM.**

A plaintiff cannot circumvent mootness by tacking on a request for nominal damages. *See Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1257 (“[A] prayer for nominal damages does not preserve a live controversy where the challenged policy has been repealed.”); *see also Lewis v. Continental Bank Corp.*, 494 U.S. 472, 480 (1990) (holding that a claim for nominal damages does not preserve a live controversy if the underlying claim is moot). The Eleventh Circuit has squarely rejected the argument that nominal damages alone can sustain a constitutional challenge to a repealed policy. *Flanigan’s*, 868 F.3d at 1257.

Appellants’ attempt to distinguish *Flanigan’s* is unavailing. They argue their claims were “as-applied,” but the Eleventh Circuit has held that even as-applied challenges are mooted by a policy’s repeal. *Id.* at 1258. Moreover, Appellants did not allege any concrete injury beyond the abstract violation of their constitutional rights, which is insufficient to support a claim for compensatory damages. *See Slicker v. Jackson*, 215 F.3d 1225, 1230 (11th Cir. 2000) (“Compensatory damages in a § 1983 suit must be based on actual injury.”).

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT PROPERLY DENIED APPELLANTS’ ATTEMPT TO AMEND THEIR COMPLAINT.**

Appellants’ belated attempt to amend their complaint to add a claim for compensatory damages was untimely and procedurally improper. *See Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank*, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018) (holding that a request for leave to amend must be made by motion, not embedded in a response to a motion to dismiss). The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying the request. *See Santiago v. Wood*, 904 F.2d 673, 675 (11th Cir. 1990) (reviewing denial of leave to amend for abuse of discretion).

Even if Appellants had properly moved to amend, their proposed changes would not have saved their claims. They did not specify what factual allegations or legal theories would support a claim for compensatory damages, and their conclusory assertions of “financial injuries” were insufficient. *See Newton v. Duke Energy Fla., LLC*, 895 F.3d 1270, 1277 (11th Cir. 2018) (requiring a proposed amendment to set forth the substance of the proposed changes).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment below should be affirmed. The Respondents’ revision of the challenged policy mooted Appellants’ claims for declaratory and injunctive relief, and their request for nominal damages cannot revive those claims. The district court properly denied Appellants’ untimely and procedurally defective attempt to amend their complaint.
