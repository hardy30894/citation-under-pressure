**ARGUMENT**

**I. Lucero Retained a Personal Stake in Class Certification Notwithstanding BCR's Rule 68 Offer, and the District Court Therefore Erred in Dismissing for Mootness.**

The district court's mootness analysis cannot be reconciled with the Supreme Court's class-action mootness trilogy. In *Sosna v. Iowa*, 419 U.S. 393 (1975), the Court held that once a class action is filed, the interests of the class "acquired a legal status separate from the interest asserted" by the individual plaintiff, *id.* at 401, such that satisfaction of the named plaintiff's individual claim does not automatically extinguish the controversy. Five years later, in *U.S. Parole Commission v. Geraghty*, 445 U.S. 388 (1980), the Court extended this principle to the pre-certification context, holding that a class representative retains a "personal stake" in the resolution of the class certification question even after his individual claim has become moot. *Id.* at 404. The same day, in *Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper*, 445 U.S. 326 (1980), the Court held that named plaintiffs tendered full individual relief nonetheless retained a sufficient stake in the litigation to pursue class certification on appeal, because a rule permitting a defendant unilaterally to extinguish a class action by tendering relief to the named plaintiff "would put at defendant's mercy the ability of the class representative to litigate the claims of the class." *Id.* at 339.

Read together, *Sosna*, *Geraghty*, and *Roper* establish that Article III's case-or-controversy requirement does not evaporate simply because the defendant offers to satisfy the named plaintiff's individual demand before a certification ruling issues. Lucero filed his class complaint on April 20, 2009, and diligently pursued certification within the schedule the parties themselves jointly proposed and the district court adopted—a schedule that allotted the first six months exclusively to class-certification discovery. Aplt.App. 58-59. Lucero's certification motion was filed on February 22, 2010, within the court-ordered deadline. Aplt. Br. 4. Nothing in this record suggests undue delay of the kind that might justify treating the class claims as abandoned. Under *Roper* and *Geraghty*, Lucero's personal stake in obtaining a ruling on certification survived BCR's June 2009 offer, and the district court's Rule 12(b)(1) dismissal for mootness therefore rested on an erroneous legal premise.

**II. This Court Should Apply the Relation-Back Doctrine to Prevent BCR from Using a Rule 68 Offer to "Buy Off" the Class Representative Before Certification.**

Even if this Court concludes that *Sosna*'s "separate legal status" rationale applies only post-certification, the Third and Fifth Circuits have recognized that a defendant's tactical use of Rule 68 to extinguish a class representative's individual claim before certification threatens to defeat the very purpose of Rule 23. In *Weiss v. Regal Collections*, 385 F.3d 337 (3d Cir. 2004), the court held that an unaccepted Rule 68 offer for the full amount of the named plaintiff's individual FDCPA claim, tendered before the plaintiff had a fair opportunity to move for certification, does not moot the action; instead, the plaintiff's timely certification motion "relates back" to the filing of the class complaint. *Id.* at 344-45. The Fifth Circuit reached the same conclusion earlier in *Zeidman v. J.C. Penney Co.*, 686 F.2d 385 (5th Cir. 1982), explaining that a certification motion filed within a reasonable time relates back to avoid mootness where the defendant's tender occurs before the plaintiff has had that opportunity.

That reasoning applies with particular force here, where BCR served its Rule 68 offer on the very same day it answered the complaint—before any discovery had occurred and before the parties had even proposed a certification schedule. Aplt.App. 2, 64. To permit BCR's offer to moot the action under these circumstances would sanction exactly the kind of "buy-off" strategy that *Roper* and its progeny forbid, allowing any well-resourced debt collector to escape FDCPA class liability by tendering individual relief to whichever consumer first files suit. Adopting the relation-back rule preserves the class's day in court while still respecting the ordinary operation of Rule 68.

**III. *Clark v. State Farm* Does Not Compel a Different Result.**

The district court felt "bound" by this Court's statement in *Clark v. State Farm Mutual Auto Insurance*, 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir. 2009), that "a suit brought as a class action must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified." But *Clark*'s general statement did not confront a plaintiff who, as here, was diligently pursuing certification on a court-ordered schedule when the defendant's offer issued. *Clark*'s formulation cannot be read to override the Supreme Court's holdings in *Sosna*, *Geraghty*, and *Roper*, which this Court is bound to follow. Because *Clark* did not analyze—much less abrogate—the personal-stake and relation-back principles at issue here, it does not control.

**CONCLUSION**

For these reasons, this Court should reverse the district court's dismissal for lack of subject matter jurisdiction and remand for consideration of Lucero's timely-filed motion for class certification.
