The district court erred in dismissing the § 2255 motion as untimely. This Court’s June 12, 2007 order recalling the mandate and denying the Rule 4-1(e) motion restarted the period for seeking certiorari review. The Supreme Court’s subsequent denial of the petition therefore did not render the collateral attack untimely.

Even if the petition for certiorari is deemed untimely, equitable principles preclude dismissal. Where a party is misled by an officer of the court, the bar of the statute does not begin to run until the fraud is discovered. Bailey v. Glover, 88 U.S. (21 Wall.) 342, 348 (1874). This equitable doctrine is read into every federal statute of limitation. Holmberg v. Armbrecht, 327 U.S. 392, 397 (1946). The declaration establishes that the Clerk’s office affirmatively advised Buckles’s sister that the certiorari period would be stayed pending resolution of the appointment motion and that a new mandate date would issue. Buckles reasonably relied on that representation in calculating his filing deadline from the June 12 order. Such misinformation by a court officer constitutes the precise circumstance in which equity tolls the limitation period. Exploration Co. v. United States, 247 U.S. 435, 449 (1918).

The government cannot invoke timeliness to defeat review when its own judicial machinery supplied the misinformation on which the prisoner relied. The § 2255 motion was therefore timely filed.
