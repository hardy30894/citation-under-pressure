The Tax Court erred in concluding that the Amendment fails to create a qualifying income interest for life under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). That provision requires only that the surviving spouse “be entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals.” The Amendment satisfies this standard on its face: after the decedent’s death, “the trustee shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse, in quarter annual or more frequent installments, all of the net income from the trust estate” for purposes that include the spouse’s “health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare . . . in accordance with the surviving spouse’s accustomed manner of living.”

This Court has long held that the marital deduction must be construed to effectuate its purpose of eliminating the burden of successive taxation on property passing between spouses. See Commissioner v. Estate of Bosch, 387 U.S. 456, 464 (1967) (quoting the legislative history of the 1948 marital-deduction provisions). The QTIP rules extend that policy by permitting deduction when the spouse receives the economic benefit of all income for life, even if remainder interests are fixed. See Estate of Clayton v. Commissioner, 976 F.2d 1486, 1490-91 (5th Cir. 1992) (holding that the statute “focuses on the spouse’s entitlement to income, not on the absence of trustee discretion that might affect timing or amount within the spouse’s lifetime”).

The Amendment’s reference to the trustee’s “reasonable discretion” does not defeat the spouse’s entitlement. The regulation implementing the statute confirms that a trust satisfies the income-interest requirement when the trustee is obligated to distribute all income currently, even if the precise amount distributed in any interval is determined by reference to the beneficiary’s needs. Treas. Reg. § 20.2056(b)-7(d)(2). The Ninth Circuit has applied analogous reasoning to uphold deductions where trust instruments directed distribution of income for the spouse’s “support and maintenance” measured by her accustomed standard of living. See Estate of McCoy v. Commissioner, 764 F.2d 657, 660 (9th Cir. 1985).

Because the Amendment directs that all net income be applied for the surviving spouse’s benefit at least quarterly and no person holds a power to appoint trust property to anyone other than the spouse, the trust meets every statutory prerequisite for QTIP treatment. The estate is therefore entitled to the marital deduction claimed under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7).
