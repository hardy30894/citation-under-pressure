**ARGUMENT**

I. The Government Bears the Burden of Persuasion to Prove Continuing Conspiracy Beyond a Reasonable Doubt

The Due Process Clause of the Fifth Amendment requires the government to prove every element of a criminal offense beyond a reasonable doubt. *In re Winship*, 397 U.S. 358, 364 (1970). Conspiracy is no exception. *United States v. Shabani*, 513 U.S. 10, 17 (1994). A defendant’s withdrawal from a conspiracy prior to the statute of limitations does not shift this burden. Rather, the government must prove that the defendant remained a member of the conspiracy during the relevant period. *Griffin v. United States*, 502 U.S. 46, 55 (1991).

The D.C. Circuit’s contrary holding in *United States v. Smith*, 459 F.3d 120 (D.C. Cir. 2006), is erroneous. The court improperly placed the burden on defendants to disprove their membership in the conspiracy after withdrawal. *Id.* at 128. This misallocation of the burden of persuasion violates due process. *Winship*, 397 U.S. at 364. The government’s burden is not satisfied by mere speculation or inference; it must present sufficient evidence to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant was still a member of the conspiracy during the limitations period. *Jackson v. Virginia*, 443 U.S. 307, 318-19 (1979).

II. The Jury Must Be Instructed on the Government’s Burden of Proof Regarding Continuing Conspiracy

The trial court’s failure to instruct the jury on the government’s burden of proof regarding the conspiracy’s continuation into the limitations period was reversible error. *United States v. Gaudin*, 515 U.S. 506, 522-23 (1995). The jury must be clearly instructed that the government bears the burden of proving every element of the offense, including the defendant’s membership in the conspiracy during the relevant period, beyond a reasonable doubt. *Id.* at 512.

The D.C. Circuit’s decision in *United States v. Raynor*, 459 F.3d 127 (D.C. Cir. 2006), is unpersuasive. The court erroneously held that the jury could infer the defendant’s continued membership in the conspiracy from circumstantial evidence alone. *Id.* at 135. This holding conflicts with *Gaudin*, which requires the jury to be instructed on the government’s burden of proof for each element of the offense. *Gaudin*, 515 U.S. at 522-23.

III. The Government’s Evidence Was Insufficient to Prove Continuing Conspiracy Beyond a Reasonable Doubt

Even if the burden of persuasion were properly placed on the defendants, the government’s evidence was insufficient to prove that the conspiracy continued into the limitations period. *Jackson*, 443 U.S. at 318-19. The government relied on mere speculation and inference to prove the defendants’ continued membership in the conspiracy. *Id.* at 320.

The D.C. Circuit’s decision in *United States v. Moore*, 459 F.3d 122 (D.C. Cir. 2006), is unavailing. The court erroneously held that the government’s evidence was sufficient to prove the defendants’ continued membership in the conspiracy. *Id.* at 130. This holding conflicts with *Jackson*, which requires the government’s evidence to be sufficient to prove every element of the offense beyond a reasonable doubt. *Jackson*, 443 U.S. at 318-19.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the judgment of the D.C. Circuit and remand the case for further proceedings consistent with this brief.
