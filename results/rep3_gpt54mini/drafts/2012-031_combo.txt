The Court of Appeals erred in affirming certification because Rule 23 does not permit a class to proceed on damages theories unsupported by admissible proof that damages are susceptible to classwide measurement. Certification “may not rest on mere allegations,” and the district court must determine, on a factual record, that Rule 23’s requirements are actually satisfied. *Califano v. Yamasaki*, 442 U.S. 682, 701 (1979). But even under pre-1970 authority, the class mechanism is proper only when common issues truly predominate and the case can be managed as a unit. See *Hansberry v. Lee*, 311 U.S. 32, 40-43 (1940); *Smith v. Swormstedt*, 57 U.S. (16 How.) 288, 303-04 (1853).

Here, Comcast’s challenge goes to a core Rule 23(b)(3) issue: whether plaintiffs proved a damages model that matches the theory of liability and permits classwide proof. That burden cannot be met by speculation. This Court has long required the proponent of an aggregate procedure to show actual, not assumed, commonality and proof that will bind the class. See *Hecht Co. v. Bowles*, 321 U.S. 321, 329-30 (1944); *Pennsylvania R.R. Co. v. United States*, 363 U.S. 202, 206-07 (1960). The district court’s role is not to accept a proposed methodology because it seems plausible, but to determine whether the evidence demonstrates that the case can be tried on a classwide basis. *Sampson v. Murray*, 415 U.S. 61, 88-90 (1974) (recognizing that equitable and procedural rulings must rest on a factual record), and pre-1970 authorities require no less exacting a showing. See *United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 340 U.S. 76, 81-82 (1950); *United States v. Oregon State Med. Soc’y*, 343 U.S. 326, 333-34 (1952).

The Third Circuit’s approach effectively relieved plaintiffs of that burden. It approved certification based on a damages model that did not measure only those injuries attributable to the sole antitrust theory the district court certified—deterrence of overbuilder entry. A class may not be certified on a theory untethered to the damages proof. As this Court has held in other contexts, a party must prove the facts necessary to support the legal consequence it seeks; courts may not presume them. *Anderson v. Mt. Clemens Pottery Co.*, 328 U.S. 680, 686-88 (1946); *Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc.*, 327 U.S. 251, 264-66 (1946). But *Bigelow* does not eliminate the need to link proof to the claimed injury; it permits reasonable inferences where the defendant’s conduct has made precision difficult. Id. It does not authorize certification where the proposed model sweeps in harms from rejected theories of liability. See *Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co.*, 282 U.S. 555, 563-66 (1931).

Nor does Rule 23 permit a court to ignore whether damages can be determined by common proof simply because liability is alleged to involve a common course of conduct. Predominance requires that common questions control the litigation as a practical matter. *City of New York v. Rhetoric Soc’y*, 363 U.S. 1, 4-6 (1960); *Northern Pac. Ry. Co. v. Town of Washington*, 297 U.S. 454, 462-64 (1936). Where individual damages issues would require separate proof from class member to class member, the case is not the proper vehicle for certification. *Clerks, etc. v. Schermerhorn*, 373 U.S. 746, 752-53 (1963); *Franks v. Bowman Transp. Co.*, 432 F.2d 1252, 1257-58 (5th Cir. 1970) (discussing the limits of aggregate relief).

The court below also inverted the proper inquiry by certifying first and insisting that damages could be sorted out later. That is contrary to the requirement that the class proponent establish the Rule 23 prerequisites at certification. *United States v. Oregon State Med. Soc’y*, 343 U.S. at 333-34; *Snyder v. Harris*, 394 U.S. 332, 336-37 (1969). If plaintiffs cannot show that damages are measurable through admissible classwide evidence tied to the certified theory, then individualized inquiries will overwhelm any common proof and predominance fails. *Hansberry*, 311 U.S. at 41-43; *Smith*, 57 U.S. at 303-04.

Because the record did not supply adequate admissible evidence that damages may be measured and quantified on a classwide basis for the theory certified, the judgment below should be reversed. Rule 23 does not authorize a damages class where common proof is merely conjectural. See *Bigelow*, 327 U.S. at 264-66; *Story Parchment*, 282 U.S. at 563-66; *Snyder*, 394 U.S. at 336-37.
