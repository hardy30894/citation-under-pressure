**ARGUMENT**

I. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT ESQD ARC MAPS ARE “PREDOMINANTLY INTERNAL” AND RISK CIRCUMVENTION OF AGENCY REGULATION UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 2.

The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” standard for Exemption 2 is contrary to the plain text of the statute and this Court’s precedent. Exemption 2 shields only “matters that are related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The statute does not authorize courts to withhold documents merely because they are “predominantly internal” or could “risk circumvention of agency regulation.” *Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 369 (1976) (Exemption 2 applies only to “trivial administrative matters” such as parking and lunch-hour rules). The Ninth Circuit’s expansion of Exemption 2 to cover ESQD arc maps—documents used to assess public safety risks—distorts the statute’s narrow construction. *U.S. Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989) (FOIA exemptions are “explicitly exclusive” and must be narrowly construed).

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 670 F.2d 1051 (D.C. Cir. 1981), is misplaced. *Crooker* applied a “predominantly internal” test to law enforcement manuals, but this Court has never adopted such a broad interpretation of Exemption 2. *Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms*, 631 F.2d 653, 656 (9th Cir. 1980) (Exemption 2 applies only to “law enforcement materials” that risk circumvention). The Ninth Circuit’s later decision in *Maricopa Audubon Soc’y v. U.S. Forest Serv.*, 108 F.3d 1082 (9th Cir. 1997), did not endorse the “predominantly internal” standard but instead rejected it. *Id.* at 1085 (holding that Exemption 2 does not apply to goshawk nesting site data because it is not “predominantly internal”).

The ESQD arc maps at issue here are not “internal personnel rules and practices.” They are safety planning tools used to assess risks to the public. *See* Joint Appendix at 45 (Commander Whitbred’s affidavit stating that ESQD arcs are used to “design, array, and construct ammunition storage facilities”). The Ninth Circuit’s holding that these maps are exempt under Exemption 2 because they are “predominantly internal” is unsupported by the statute or precedent.

II. THE NINTH CIRCUIT ERRED IN HOLDING THAT THE ESQD ARC MAPS COULD REASONABLY ENDANGER INDIVIDUALS UNDER FOIA EXEMPTION 7(F).

Exemption 7(F) permits withholding of records “compiled for law enforcement purposes” if disclosure “could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of any individual.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(F). The Ninth Circuit erred in applying this exemption to ESQD arc maps, which are not compiled for law enforcement purposes. *Church of Scientology v. U.S. Dep’t of Defense*, 611 F.2d 738, 748 (D.C. Cir. 1979) (Exemption 7(F) applies only to records compiled for “adjudicative or enforcement purposes”).

Commander Whitbred’s affidavit states that the ESQD arcs are used for “risk mitigation and enhanced safety,” not law enforcement. Joint Appendix at 45. The maps are safety planning tools, not records compiled to investigate or prosecute crimes. The Ninth Circuit’s holding that these maps could endanger individuals if disclosed is speculative and unsupported by the record.

III. THE NINTH CIRCUIT’S HOLDING THREATENS TO UNRAVEL FOIA’S PROMISE OF PUBLIC ACCESS TO GOVERNMENT INFORMATION.

FOIA’s exemptions are “narrowly construed” and “explicitly exclusive.” *Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. at 151. The Ninth Circuit’s expansion of Exemption 2 to cover ESQD arc maps—documents that assess public safety risks—undermines FOIA’s purpose of promoting transparency. *Rose*, 425 U.S. at 360-61 (FOIA reflects a “philosophy of full agency disclosure”).

The Ninth Circuit’s holding also conflicts with this Court’s instruction that FOIA exemptions must be applied narrowly. *Id.* at 361. The ESQD arc maps are not “internal personnel rules and practices,” nor are they compiled for law enforcement purposes. The Ninth Circuit’s decision to withhold them under Exemptions 2 and 7(F) is contrary to the statute and precedent.

**CONCLUSION**

The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that ESQD arc maps are exempt from disclosure under FOIA Exemptions 2 and 7(F). The judgment below should be reversed.
