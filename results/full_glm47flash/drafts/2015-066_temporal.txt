Petitioner respectfully submits that the use of uncounseled tribal court convictions to establish a predicate offense for a federal crime violates the Sixth Amendment. The Sixth Amendment right to counsel is a fundamental component of the "reliability" of a conviction. In *Cole v. Arkansas*, 333 U.S. 196 (1948), the Supreme Court held that the right to be represented by counsel is essential to a fair trial. This principle dictates that a conviction obtained without counsel is inherently suspect and cannot serve as a reliable predicate for a federal crime.

Furthermore, tribal court proceedings are distinct from federal proceedings and are not bound by the Sixth Amendment. In *United States v. Kagama*, 118 U.S. 375 (1886), the Court recognized the federal government's power to try Indians for crimes committed on Indian country, but this jurisdiction is separate from the application of the Bill of Rights to tribal courts. Because tribal courts are not bound by the Sixth Amendment, convictions obtained there without counsel lack the reliability required to establish an element of a federal offense.
