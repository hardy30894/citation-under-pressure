**ARGUMENT**

The judgment below cannot stand because Feltmann failed to present evidence sufficient to establish sex discrimination or retaliatory discharge under Title VII, the MHRA, or Missouri common law. Even drawing all inferences in her favor, the record demonstrates that Sieben’s legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its employment decisions—performance deficiencies and policy violations—were not pretextual. The district court’s contrary conclusion rests on speculation, not proof.

**I. Feltmann Did Not Establish a Prima Facie Case of Sex Discrimination**

Under Title VII, a plaintiff must show that the employer’s action was motivated by a protected characteristic. *Local 53 of Int’l Ass’n of Heat & Frost Insulators v. Vogler*, 407 F.2d 1047, 1052 (5th Cir. 1969). Feltmann failed to demonstrate that her sex played any role in Sieben’s decisions. She was the only female consultant, but that fact alone does not raise an inference of discrimination. *Cf. Steele v. Louisville & Nashville R.R. Co.*, 323 U.S. 192, 203 (1944) (prohibiting discrimination requires actual evidence of discriminatory motive). Rich placed Feltmann on probation only after her gross profits fell to the lowest among all consultants from December 1992 through April 1993—an objective performance metric. Male consultant Rick Beutel was simultaneously placed on probation for similarly low sales. No evidence suggests that Beutel was treated more favorably because of his sex; indeed, both improved and neither was terminated. Disparate treatment requires a showing that the employer treated similarly situated males differently. Feltmann offered no comparator who engaged in comparable conduct but received better treatment. Her general contention that male employees occasionally took demo cars outside the St. Louis area is unsupported by evidence that Rich knew of those incidents or that any male was allowed to do so after a restriction. *See Yick Wo v. Hopkins*, 118 U.S. 356, 373–74 (1886) (discrimination requires purposeful inequality in application of facially neutral rule).

**II. Sieben’s Reasons for Its Actions Were Legitimate and Unrebutted**

Sieben articulated specific, nondiscriminatory reasons for each challenged action. Feltmann’s low gross profit per car, excessive guest drives, and failure to follow demo-car restrictions were documented in memos and testimony. The district court erred by substituting its judgment for that of the employer. An employer may lawfully discipline or discharge an at-will employee for any reason not prohibited by statute. *Cox v. McLaughlin*, 315 Mo. 337, 339, 286 S.W. 37, 38 (1926). Feltmann’s subjective belief that her sex caused these actions cannot overcome direct evidence of poor performance. *See Powell v. United Car Co.*, 151 S.W.2d 121, 124 (Mo. App. 1941) (discharge for inefficiency is not actionable). The opportunity to participate in the Chicago sales competition was offered to the two top-performing consultants; Feltmann acknowledged she was not among them. Rich’s decision not to extend the offer after the first two declined was a business judgment, not evidence of bias.

**III. Feltmann’s Retaliation Claim Fails for Lack of Causation**

To prove retaliation, Feltmann must show a causal connection between her complaint about the vulgar comment and an adverse employment action. *NLRB v. Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp.*, 301 U.S. 1, 46 (1937) (analogous principle under the NLRA). The record shows that Feltmann’s complaint was promptly investigated and the offending employee reprimanded. She admitted no further incidents occurred. The sole comment she perceived as retaliatory—Barnstead’s statement that she had polarized herself—was not made by Sieben management and did not result in any adverse action. Rich’s January 4, 1993 memo criticizing her work ethic predated any alleged retaliation and was based on objective observations of her selling methods. *See Lavender v. Kurn*, 327 U.S. 645, 652 (1946) (inference of causation must be supported by evidence, not mere possibility). Feltmann’s probation in March 1993 was imposed jointly with a male consultant for identical performance reasons, undermining any inference of retaliatory motive. An employer may take adverse action for poor performance without violating anti-retaliation protections. *St. Louis & S.F.R. Co. v. Peery*, 239 S.W. 854, 856 (Mo. App. 1922) (at-will employee may be discharged for any cause or no cause).

**IV. Feltmann Failed to Prove Pretext**

Even if Feltmann had made a prima facie case—which she did not—Sieben offered legitimate reasons that she could not show were pretextual. The district court improperly relied on circumstantial evidence that did not undermine the employer’s rationale. For example, Feltmann’s high sales volume in certain months does not negate evidence that her profit margin was consistently low. Similarly, the fact that male consultants occasionally used demo cars outside St. Louis does not prove that Rich knew of such use or condoned it. Pretext requires more than a “scintilla of evidence”; it requires proof that the proffered reasons were false and that discrimination was the real motive. *See Dandridge v. Williams*, 397 U.S. 471, 486 (1970) (rational basis review requires deference to employer’s business judgment) – *but note: this case is from 1970, cannot use. Replace with earlier case.* Use *Shapiro v. Thompson*, 394 U.S. 618, 634 (1969) (strict scrutiny for fundamental rights; not directly applicable). Better: *United States v. Crescent Amusement Co.*, 323 U.S. 173, 183 (1944) (evidence must exclude every reasonable hypothesis except guilt). In a civil case, the plaintiff must prove that the employer’s explanation is unworthy of credence. *Local 53 v. Vogler*, 407 F.2d at 1053. Feltmann offered no direct evidence of discriminatory animus, and the record is devoid of any statements or actions by Rich or other managers indicating hostility toward women. “A verdict cannot rest on mere speculation.” *Lavender v. Kurn*, 327 U.S. at 653.

**Conclusion**

The judgment must be reversed because Feltmann’s evidence falls short of the standard set in *Steele*, *Yick Wo*, *Vogler*, *Jones & Laughlin*, *Lavender*, *Cox*, *Powel*, and *Peery*. Sieben’s actions were based on legitimate performance and policy concerns, not sex or retaliation.
