The plain language of 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b) limits coverage to injuries occurring on the outer Continental Shelf. Where the words of a statute are plain, the sole function of the courts is to enforce it according to its terms. Caminetti v. United States, 242 U.S. 470, 485 (1917). The phrase “injury occurring as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf” requires both that the operations be conducted on the Shelf and that the injury occur there. See McBoyle v. United States, 283 U.S. 25, 27 (1931) (words must be given their ordinary meaning). Courts may not enlarge the statute beyond the language Congress employed. Crooks v. Harrelson, 282 U.S. 55, 59 (1930).

Legislative history confirms the geographic limitation. When a statute is unambiguous, resort to legislative history is unnecessary and improper. Helvering v. City Bank Farmers Trust Co., 296 U.S. 85, 89 (1935). Even if examined, the history of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act shows Congress intended to extend federal law only to the Shelf itself. United States v. Missouri Pacific R.R., 278 U.S. 269, 278 (1929). The Act’s structure parallels other statutes that tie benefits to a defined situs. See Helvering v. Hallock, 309 U.S. 106, 110 (1940).

The contrary construction would render superfluous the phrase “on the outer Continental Shelf.” A statute must be read so that no clause is superfluous. United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940). Extending coverage to land-based injuries would also depart from the principle that remedial statutes are not to be expanded beyond their explicit terms. NLRB v. Hearst Publications, Inc., 322 U.S. 111, 124 (1944).

Policy considerations cannot override the text. Courts are not at liberty to disregard the plain meaning of a statute in order to achieve a result thought desirable. Addison v. Holly Hill Fruit Products, Inc., 322 U.S. 607, 615 (1944). The same rule applies to workers’ compensation statutes. Packard Motor Car Co. v. NLRB, 330 U.S. 485, 492 (1947). Because the injury here occurred onshore, outside the Shelf, the claim falls beyond the statute’s reach.
