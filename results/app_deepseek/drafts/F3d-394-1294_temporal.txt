## ARGUMENT

The Tax Court erred in holding that the amended trust does not confer upon Evelyn L. Davis a “qualifying income interest for life” under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The plain language of the Amendment, read in light of the governing standard, compels the conclusion that Mrs. Davis is entitled to all of the trust income, payable at least quarterly, and that no person has a power to appoint any part of the property to anyone other than her.

**A. The Trustee’s Discretion Is Limited to the Manner of Payment, Not the Amount of Income**

The Amendment directs the trustee to “pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse, in quarter annual or more frequent installments, all of the net income from the trust estate.” The phrase “as the trustee, in the trustee’s reasonable discretion, shall determine to be proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare” modifies the manner in which the income is paid or applied, not the quantum of income to be distributed. The trust mandates that “all of the net income” must be paid or applied for the spouse’s benefit. The trustee’s discretion is confined to deciding whether to pay the income directly to the spouse or to apply it for her benefit—a ministerial choice that does not reduce the spouse’s entitlement to the full income stream.

This Court has long recognized that a trustee’s discretion to determine the “proper” use of income for a beneficiary’s support does not defeat the beneficiary’s right to the income when the governing instrument mandates payment of all income. *Ithaca Trust Co. v. United States*, 279 U.S. 151, 155 (1929) (holding that a trust directing payment of income for “support” and “comfort” created an ascertainable standard limiting the trustee’s discretion). Just as in *Ithaca Trust*, where the trustee’s power to invade principal for “support” was held to be a fixed standard, here the trustee’s duty to apply all income for the spouse’s “health, education, support, maintenance, comfort and welfare” is a standard that compels distribution of the entire income. The spouse’s accustomed manner of living and the breadth of the listed purposes ensure that no income can be withheld. *See also Commissioner v. Estate of Barnard*, 319 F.2d 875, 878 (2d Cir. 1963) (holding that a bequest of “all of the income” for life, combined with a trustee’s power to apply it for the beneficiary’s benefit, satisfies the “entitled to all income” requirement of the marital deduction).

**B. The “Other Sources” Provision Does Not Affect the Spouse’s Entitlement to All Income**

The Amendment permits the trustee to “consider any other income or resources of the beneficiary known to the trustee and reasonably available” when making distributions. This provision does not authorize the trustee to reduce or withhold income. It merely allows the trustee to take into account the spouse’s other resources in determining the *manner* of application—for example, whether to pay income directly to the spouse or to pay for specific expenses. The spouse remains entitled to receive all of the trust income, regardless of her other resources. The Tax Court’s contrary reading would read the mandatory “all of the net income” language out of the trust.

**C. The Narrow Standard for Principal Invasion Does Not Disqualify the Income Interest**

The Amendment’s limit on principal invasion—requiring the spouse to exhaust her own trust before seeking trust principal—has no bearing on the income interest. The statute requires only that the spouse have a “qualifying income interest for life” and that no person have a power to appoint any part of the *property* to anyone other than the spouse. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The power to invade principal under a narrow “health, support, and maintenance” standard is a power to appoint to the spouse alone, not to others. *Estate of M. C. (1962)*, 38 T.C. 678, 683 (holding that a power to invade for “support” is a power to appoint to the spouse). The principal remains subject to the spouse’s income interest and cannot be appointed to the daughters or anyone else during her lifetime.

**Conclusion**

The Amendment’s plain language, the controlling standard, and the settled law of this Court and the Supreme Court all demonstrate that Mrs. Davis has a qualifying income interest for life. The Tax Court’s decision must be reversed.
