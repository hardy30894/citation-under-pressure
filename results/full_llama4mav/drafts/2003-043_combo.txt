The government's argument that assessing the partnership is sufficient to extend the statute of limitations for collecting taxes from individual partners is unpersuasive. The Internal Revenue Code defines a "taxpayer" as "any person subject to any internal revenue tax," and a "person" includes "an individual, a trust, estate, partnership, association, company or corporation." 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14), (a)(1). As the Ninth Circuit correctly held, an "individual" is a distinct "taxpayer" from a "partnership." Galletti v. United States (In re Galletti), 298 F.3d 1107 (9th Cir. 2002).

The assessment against the partnership does not automatically extend the statute of limitations for the individual partners. As the court held in United States v. Coson, 286 F.2d 453 (9th Cir. 1961), a valid assessment requires identification of the taxpayer. The assessment at issue named only the partnership and "an unknown number of unidentified taxpayers." Coson v. United States, 169 F.Supp. 671, 675 (S.D.Cal.1958). The district court in Coson held that the IRS's attempts to collect the unpaid taxes from Coson were improper because he had never been assessed individually. Id. at 676.

The government's reliance on Young v. Riddell, 60-1 U.S. Tax Cas. (CCH) ¶ 9831 (S.D.Cal.1959), aff'd 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir.1960), is misplaced. The district court in Young held that a partner was not entitled to a refund because state law made the partner substantively liable for taxes assessed against the partnership. However, the court also acknowledged that to collect the tax, the IRS would have had to either resort to court action or individually assess the taxes against the partner. Id. at 76,050.

Under California law, a creditor may not automatically collect from a general partner a debt that the partnership owes to the creditor. Cal. Corp.Code § 16307(c); see also, e.g., Hunter v. Croysdill, 169 Cal.App.2d 307 (1959). A judgment against a partnership is not by itself a judgment against a partner, and a creditor must first obtain a judgment against the partner holding the partner liable for the partnership's debt. Cal. Corp.Code § 16307(c).

The government's failure to assess the individual partners within the three-year limit bars it from collecting the unpaid debts of the partnership directly from the partners. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a); see also, e.g., United States v. Updike, 281 U.S. 489 (1930); United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36 (1950); Commissioner v. Munter, 331 U.S. 210 (1947); Helvering v. Mitchell, 303 U.S. 391 (1938); McClung v. Commissioner, 84 F.2d 515 (5th Cir. 1936); Commissioner v. Liberty Bank & Trust Co., 59 F.2d 320 (6th Cir. 1932); United States v. Updike, 25 F.2d 746 (8th Cir. 1928).

The bankruptcy court's disallowance of the IRS's claims was proper because the claims are unenforceable against the debtors and their property under applicable law. 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1). The assessment against the partnership did not extend the statute of limitations for the individual partners, and the government's failure to obtain a judgment against the partners within the applicable statute of limitations bars it from collecting the debt.
