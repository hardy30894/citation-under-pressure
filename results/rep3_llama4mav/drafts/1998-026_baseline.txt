The District Court's failure to advise Manuel Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2), does not provide a basis for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals correctly held that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review.

As this Court has recognized, not every error in the administration of criminal justice warrants relief. See United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725, 731 (1993) (citing, inter alia, Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(a)). In particular, the harmless-error doctrine applies to errors that occur during the sentencing process. See, e.g., Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193, 203 (1992) (applying harmless-error analysis to a sentencing court's consideration of an improper factor).

The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that a defendant is aware of his right to appeal. See United States v. Deans, 436 F.2d 596, 599 (3d Cir. 1971). When, as here, a defendant is aware of his right to appeal, the rule's purpose has been served, and a failure to advise the defendant of that right is harmless. See, e.g., United States v. Drummond, 903 F.2d 1171, 1173-74 (8th Cir. 1990) (holding that a Rule 32 violation was harmless when the defendant was aware of his right to appeal).

The District Court's finding that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing is supported by the record. Given this finding, the Court of Appeals correctly concluded that the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was harmless error. Peguero's claim for habeas relief should be denied.

The judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
