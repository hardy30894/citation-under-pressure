## ARGUMENT

### I. The complaint states a claim that Wells Fargo breached the settlement agreement’s servicing commitments.

A complaint survives dismissal if it alleges facts that, accepted as true, plausibly entitle the plaintiff to relief. *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009); *Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544, 555–56 (2007). Under Massachusetts law, a third party may enforce a contract when the contract manifests an intent to give that party the benefit of its performance. *Levenson v. D’Agostino*, 58 Mass. App. Ct. 747, 752, 792 N.E.2d 319 (2003); *Parent v. Stone & Webster Eng’g Corp.*, 408 Mass. 108, 113–14, 556 N.E.2d 1009 (1990).

Foley alleged that he was a member of Settlement Class B, that the settlement required Wells Fargo to consider him first for HAMP and then for MAP2R, and that Wells Fargo never did so before proceeding toward foreclosure. Those allegations state a straightforward breach of the settlement’s express servicing commitments. A contract must be construed according to its plain language, giving effect to every term. *Citation Ins. Co. v. Gomez*, 426 Mass. 379, 381–82, 688 N.E.2d 951 (1998); *Mardirossian & Assocs., Inc. v. City of Springfield*, 68 Mass. App. Ct. 715, 719, 864 N.E.2d 1231 (2007). The settlement here did not merely create a promise to evaluate borrowers someday; it required that class members “first shall be considered for a HAMP modification” and, if ineligible, be considered for MAP2R. Where a contract imposes a sequence of mandatory steps, failure to follow that sequence is breach. *Drs. Inc. ex rel. Bharucha v. Blue Cross of Mass., Inc.*, 62 Mass. App. Ct. 172, 176–77, 815 N.E.2d 417 (2004).

Wells Fargo’s contrary view—that the settlement created no enforceable duty to consider Foley in good faith—cannot be reconciled with the pleadings. The complaint alleges that Wells Fargo told Foley for months that HAMP was his only option, delayed sending an application for seven months, ignored follow-up calls, and then steered him toward short sale and deed-in-lieu alternatives while foreclosure loomed. At minimum, those facts permit a reasonable inference that Wells Fargo failed to perform the contractual consideration process the settlement required. Massachusetts recognizes that every contract carries an implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. *Anthony’s Pier Four, Inc. v. HBC Assocs.*, 411 Mass. 451, 471–72, 583 N.E.2d 806 (1991); *Uno Rests., Inc. v. Bos. Kenmore Realty Corp.*, 441 Mass. 376, 385, 805 N.E.2d 957 (2004). The complaint plausibly alleges a violation of that covenant.

### II. Foley adequately pleaded his Massachusetts statutory claims.

Foley also alleged violations of Massachusetts consumer-protection and foreclosure-related statutes. Massachusetts courts construe remedial consumer statutes broadly to effectuate their purpose. *Commonwealth v. Fremont Inv. & Loan*, 452 Mass. 733, 741–42, 897 N.E.2d 548 (2008); *Mass. Eye & Ear Infirmary v. QLT Phototherapeutics, Inc.*, 412 Mass. 487, 490, 590 N.E.2d 1249 (1992).

As to deceptive conduct, chapter 93A reaches “unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce.” Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A, §§ 2, 9; *Tyler v. Michaels Stores, Inc.*, 464 Mass. 492, 503, 984 N.E.2d 737 (2013). A practice is deceptive if it could reasonably be found to have caused a person to act differently from how he otherwise would have acted. *Aspen Aerospace & Def. Sys., Inc. v. Prime Med. Eng’g, LLC*, 95 Mass. App. Ct. 343, 349, 124 N.E.3d 427 (2019). Foley alleged that Wells Fargo repeatedly represented that it would provide a HAMP application and evaluate him for modification, withheld that application for months, ignored his inquiries, and pushed foreclosure while concealing his rights under the settlement. Those allegations readily satisfy chapter 93A’s deceptiveness standard. See *PMP Assocs., Inc. v. Globe Newspaper Co.*, 366 Mass. 593, 596, 321 N.E.2d 915 (1975).

The complaint likewise states a claim under chapter 244. Massachusetts foreclosure statutes impose mandatory notice and procedural requirements before foreclosure may proceed. Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 244, §§ 14, 35A; *U.S. Bank Nat’l Ass’n v. Ibanez*, 458 Mass. 637, 646–48, 941 N.E.2d 40 (2011); *Pinti v. Emigrant Mortg. Co.*, 472 Mass. 226, 233–35, 33 N.E.3d 1213 (2015). Foley alleged that the settlement obligated Wells Fargo to notify him that he remained under review for modification, yet the bank instead sent foreclosure-related communications while failing to honor the modification process. Those allegations plausibly plead violation of the statutory foreclosure scheme and, at a minimum, present factual questions unsuitable for dismissal. *Ibanez*, 458 Mass. at 647–48.

### III. The state-law claims are not preempted.

Wells Fargo’s preemption argument fails. Federal law preempts state law only where Congress clearly manifests that intent. *English v. Gen. Elec. Co.*, 496 U.S. 72, 78–79 (1990). The Home Owners’ Loan Act does not categorically displace all state laws affecting mortgage servicing; state contract, consumer-protection, and foreclosure laws survive unless they significantly interfere with federal powers. *Fidelity Fed. Sav. & Loan Ass’n v. de la Cuesta*, 458 U.S. 141, 153–54 (1982); *Watters v. Wachovia Bank, N.A.*, 550 U.S. 1, 11–12 (2007).

Here, Foley seeks to enforce a settlement agreement and state statutes regulating foreclosure conduct. That is classic state-law territory. The claims do not seek to impose a conflicting underwriting regime or to regulate the substantive terms of mortgage products; they seek to hold Wells Fargo to promised modification review procedures and foreclosure notice obligations. Courts routinely reject preemption where state law merely requires honesty, fairness, or contractual compliance. *SPGGC, LLC v. Ayotte*, 488 F.3d 525, 532–33 (1st Cir. 2007); *In re Ocwen Loan Servicing, LLC Mortg. Servicing Litig.*, 491 F.3d 638, 643–44 (7th Cir. 2007). Foley’s claims therefore are not preempted.

### IV. Dismissal was improper.

Taking the allegations and incorporated settlement documents in the light most favorable to Foley, the complaint plausibly alleges breach, deceptive conduct, and statutory foreclosure violations. The judgment should be reversed and the case remanded for further proceedings. See *Watterson v. Page*, 987 F.2d 1, 3 (1st Cir. 1993); *Medina-Velázquez v. Hernández-Gregorat*, 767 F.3d 103, 108 (1st Cir. 2014).
