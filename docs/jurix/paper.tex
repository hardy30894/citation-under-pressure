\title{Citation under Pressure: What Deployment Constraints Do to Legal Citation Integrity, and What Deterministic Verification Can Repair}

\author{\fnms{Harsh} \snm{Dubey}%
\thanks{Corresponding Author: Harsh Dubey, New York University; harshdubeyjp@gmail.com.}}
\runningauthor{H. Dubey}
\address{New York University, New York, USA}

\begin{abstract}
Sanctions over fabricated legal citations have made language-model drafting a problem for courts, and most research on the problem has produced detection benchmarks that score errors in finished briefs. This paper measures how the errors are produced. Seven models from seven vendors, from a 30B open-weight model to frontier systems, drafted argument sections for 48 U.S. Supreme Court matters under five prompting conditions: baseline, a citation quota, a restriction to pre-1970 authority, a sanctions warning, and all three combined. Every citation in the \nDrafts{} drafts was checked deterministically, with no language-model judge in any primary measurement: existence against an 18-million-row reporter index, quotations against a verbatim checker validated on seeded faults, and pinpoint pages against official reporter pagination from public archives. Under combined pressure the smallest model's citation existence fell from \existQwenBase{} to \existQwenCombo{}, and verbatim quotation accuracy fell for six of seven models, a fall that survives correction once three generations or two prompt templates are pooled; Sonnet~5 was the exception. In a second experiment the checker fed its findings back into revision. Sonnet~5 reached \loopSonnetTrueFinal{} verbatim accuracy, and a per-quotation trace shows why. Of its \trFlagRZeroSonnet{} flagged quotations, \trTrueFlagToAccSonnet{} were corrected; the rest were deleted or stripped of their quotation marks. False feedback produced the same deletions aimed at correct quotations, five of seven models ended less accurate than they started, and a verifier right half the time cost Sonnet~5 more than twice the correct quotations a precise one did. We release the instruments, including a page-level pinpoint verifier built from public data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Since 2023, courts have repeatedly sanctioned lawyers who filed briefs containing citations that a language model invented, beginning with \textit{Mata v.~Avianca}~\cite{mata2023} and continuing in a growing docket that a public tracker now counts in the hundreds~\cite{charlotin2025tracker}. Most published work frames the problem as detection: given a brief that already contains errors, how many can a model or agent find~\cite{liu2026whochecks,chen2026legalcitebench}? Detection accuracy is the right measure for reviewing a filed brief; it does not tell a deployer which prompting conditions produce the errors, which is the question this paper addresses.

This paper asks two production-side questions. First, which prompting conditions cause citation failure, and does the answer depend on the model? Deployed prompts can carry constraints of the kind Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} studied for scholarly citations, where a minimum count of authorities, a restriction on which may be cited, and a warning about consequences collapsed citation existence even in capable models; we are not aware of a comparable study for law. Second, when a checker that can verify citations deterministically feeds its findings back into revision, does the model correct its drafts, fail to, or change the draft so that the checker's tests pass without the quotations being corrected?

Both questions need an instrument the field has said does not exist in public: the best published detection agent misses nearly half of incorrect pinpoint citations (52.8 percent recall; open-weight detectors 19 to 51 percent), and its authors conclude that ``no models can reliably detect wrong pincites'' because page-level information lives behind commercial databases~\cite{liu2026whochecks}. We built it from public sources: existence and name pairing against a local index derived from CourtListener bulk data, verbatim quotation checking validated on seeded faults, and page-level pinpoint verification against the official reporter pagination in the Caselaw Access Project's volume archives~\cite{cap2024}. Every primary number reported here is produced by this instrument; no language-model judge is used in any primary measurement.

Our scope is the closed-book use of general-purpose models, the setting of most sanctioned filings documented to date~\cite{charlotin2025tracker,dahl2024fictions}; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} says what we expect to transfer to retrieval-grounded tools. The paper reports three results. First, pressure degrades citation integrity beginning with the smallest model, and the degradation is rarely accompanied by refusal; in the \nPressured{} drafts under the four non-baseline conditions (the pressured drafts) there were three refusals. Second, feedback from a deterministic checker leads capable models to delete flagged quotations far more often than to correct them, and false feedback leads them to delete correct ones. Third, at the top of the range the remaining errors are mostly paraphrase presented as verbatim quotation, genuine language from a different case, or the wrong page, all of which the released instruments detect.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Legal citation reliability.} Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} build a detection benchmark from real appellate briefs with injected errors and a taxonomy grounded in sanctioned filings; we adopt their error categories as outcome coding and measure the naturally occurring errors their limitations section asks for. LegalCiteBench~\cite{chen2026legalcitebench} shows that models asked closed-book for the citation of a named case almost never produce the right one and almost never abstain; our task asks the easier question of whether the cases a model chooses to cite exist, and the low abstention transfers. Dahl et al.~\cite{dahl2024fictions} measured hallucinated authority in general-purpose chatbots and Magesh et al.~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree} in commercial legal research tools.

\textbf{Constraints and fabrication.} Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2026deployment} is the closest design: five prompting regimes over scholarly claims, verified by fuzzy bibliographic matching that left 36 to 61 percent of citations unresolved. We transplant their factorial to law with deterministic verification (\unverifiablePct{} percent unresolved) and a formal statistical model; their mechanism, compliance with the temporal window filled with fabrications, transfers to law for some models only (Section~\ref{sec:exp1}).

\textbf{Revision with execution feedback.} The loop protocol follows execution-feedback work in code generation~\cite{gehring2025rlef}, including a no-feedback control that separates feedback content from extra revision attempts; we add controls in which half or all of the feedback is false.

\textbf{AI and law.} Blair-Stanek and Van Durme~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable} found at ICAIL that frontier models flip the winning party across identical zero-temperature runs; the primary run generated each cell once, and Section~\ref{sec:limits} treats that instability as a limitation of this design. Evaluation methodology in this community has moved toward separating factual truth from reasoning soundness~\cite{gultekin2025twodim} and pairing automated metrics with the checks lawyers perform~\cite{sanchi2024hybrid}; citation integrity is a check for which deterministic ground truth is available, and this paper automates it. Closest to our pinpoint instrument, Garneau et al.~\cite{garneau2024pincites} found that retrieving the paragraph a proposition rests on is far harder than retrieving the case; our verifier approaches the same gap from the other side, checking a generated pincite against the page text. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025overruled} show models miss overruled precedent, a failure adjacent to the wrong-authority errors we measure, and ConsRAG~\cite{nguyen2024consrag} constrains retrieval to prevent hallucination, the strategy complementary to the post-hoc feedback our loop tests. On trust in model-drafted documents, Trozze et al.~\cite{trozze2025crypto} found mock jurors' decisions unrelated to whether a complaint was written by ChatGPT or a lawyer, and Harasta et al.~\cite{harasta2026cannotberight} found lawyers rate identical documents lower when told a model wrote them; machine verification of citation integrity offers a basis for trust that depends on neither perception. Gray et al.~\cite{gray2025magnitudes} and Pires et al.~\cite{pires2025legalwriting} evaluate generated legal argument and legal writing without checking the authority the text cites, the layer this paper adds; citation-worthiness detection~\cite{khatri2023citecaselaw} asks where a citation is needed, and the citation index behind our checker is the kind of network studied in~\cite{sadeghian2018edgelabeling,dadgostari2021lawsearch,mumford2025citationnetworks}, used here to test whether a cited case exists.

\section{Tasks, Conditions, and Instruments}\label{sec:method}

\textbf{Terms.} In a citation such as \textit{Brown v.~Board of Education}, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954), the volume, reporter, and first page identify the case and the optional second page (495) is the \textit{pincite}, which for a quotation must be the page where the quoted language appears. Throughout, \textit{citation existence} is the share of adjudicable citations that resolve to a real case, and \textit{strict quotation accuracy} is the share of scored quotations that reproduce the opinion verbatim.

\textbf{Matters.} The matters are forty-eight Supreme Court cases from 1990 to 2020, drawn from a 500-case pool built from the Supreme Court Database~\cite{spaeth2025scdb}. Each packet holds the question presented, party names, a factual summary, and a lower-court opinion excerpt, with the Supreme Court's decision excluded and every packet screened for outcome leakage. The model argues an assigned side (petitioner for even-numbered matters, respondent for odd) in an argument section of under 900 words; nothing in the design predicts outcomes, so the memorization confound of Supreme Court prediction tasks~\cite{ortega2025scotusmem} is out of scope.

\textbf{Conditions.} The five conditions are baseline; quota (``cite at least eight distinct case authorities''); temporal (``every authority you cite MUST have been decided before 1970''); stakes (``this section will be filed with the court; citing authority that does not exist, or misquoting authority, risks sanctions under Rule~11''); and combined, which concatenates the three clauses. Each condition has one fixed template, run at temperature 0. The stakes clause names Rule~11 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure because it is the sanctions rule invoked in the sanctioned-filing cases~\cite{mata2023}; Rule~11 governs civil actions in the district courts~\cite{frcp2024}, so the clause serves only as a stakes signal for a Supreme Court filing. Each condition is a single clause so that its effect can be isolated. A second template, an alternative phrasing of every condition, was run on all 48 matters and all seven models.

\textbf{Models.} The models are Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mistral Small, Llama-4 Maverick, DeepSeek V4 Flash, Grok~4.3, GPT-5.4-mini, and Claude Sonnet~5, one per vendor, all accessed through one API gateway. The set spans open-weight 30B to frontier with no vendor contributing two models; we do not assume a total ordering, tables are ordered by baseline strict quotation accuracy, and Sonnet~5, GPT-5.4-mini, and Grok~4.3, with the highest baseline existence, are called the top of the range. The gateway identifiers, run through OpenRouter in August 2026 with completion limits of 1,400 to 12,000 tokens, are \texttt{qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507}, \texttt{mistral-small-2603}, \texttt{llama-4-maverick}, \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash}, \texttt{grok-4.3}, \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini}, and \texttt{claude-sonnet-5}. An eighth model, GLM-4.7-flash, completed \glmDrafts{} of 240 drafts and is reported in Section~\ref{sec:exp1}.

\textbf{Adjudication.} Citations are extracted with eyecite~\cite{cushman2021eyecite} and given one of three labels: exists, not found, or unverifiable (vendor citations and coverage gaps), so that index coverage is never counted as fabrication; \unverifiablePct{} percent of the \nCitationRecords{} citation records in the full run are unverifiable. Existence and name pairing resolve against an 18-million-row citation table. Quotations are located by balanced quotation marks and attributed to the case named in the same sentence, else to the nearest citation within 260 characters after the quotation, else to the nearest preceding one. Each is checked verbatim against the cited opinion's text from public sources; a quotation is \textit{near miss} when every fragment reaches token coverage of at least 0.85 without matching exactly, and \textit{inaccurate} otherwise. The strict rate for a draft $d$ is $S_d = n_{\text{acc}} / (n_{\text{acc}} + n_{\text{near}} + n_{\text{inacc}})$, and a lenient rate counting near misses as correct is reported alongside it. Before any measurement the quotation checker scored 100 of 100 on twenty synthetic drafts with planted genuine quotations, wrong attributions, single-word corruptions, fabrications, and formatting artifacts; Section~\ref{sec:residual} also audits its verdicts on real drafts. Pinpoint pages are verified against the official reporter pagination parsed from the Caselaw Access Project's HTML volumes, which mark every page break; a quotation counts as on the cited page when its longest fragment lies within that page's text, and adjacent-page matches are reported separately.

\textbf{Human reference.} The same instrument scored \humanExcerpts{} clean, pre-ChatGPT federal appellate brief excerpts from Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks}; human lawyers reach \humanStrict{} strict and \humanLenient{} lenient quotation accuracy (\humanScored{} scored quotations) and \humanExist{} citation existence (the shortfall is index coverage of state reporters). Human briefs cite more non-federal authority and quote statutes and records more often than the models, so the human figures are a reference for the severity of the verbatim standard only.

\textbf{Inference.} The primary analysis is a citation-level logistic generalized estimating equation per model, $\text{logit}\,P(y_{imk}=1)=\beta_0+\beta_k$ for citation $i$ in matter $m$ under condition $k$, with exchangeable working correlation within matters~\cite{liang1986gee}, so that the 48 matters rather than the citations are the independent units. Each model has \nHolmTests{} contrasts (four conditions, two outcomes); we report Holm-corrected $p$ values within model and call an effect significant only when it survives correction, so statements that count models (``three of seven'') are descriptive. Odds ratios, 95 percent intervals, and raw and corrected $p$ values for all 56 contrasts are in the released results, and a matter-level cluster bootstrap over draft rates agreed in sign with every corrected result. Two robustness fits follow the same model: one pools two further generations of the baseline and combined drafts (Section~\ref{sec:limits}) with a run effect, Holm-corrected over its 14 tests; the other pools both templates with a template effect, corrected within model as above. For the revision experiment, which was pre-registered descriptively, we add matter-paired comparisons of final strict accuracy between arms (Wilcoxon signed-rank test and a bootstrap interval on the mean difference), chosen after the data were seen. Hypotheses and decision rules were frozen before the full run; every later analysis decision, including two withdrawn findings, is recorded in a dated ledger released with the code.

\textbf{Quotation accuracy.} The temporal restriction is the condition that damages quoting. After correction, strict quotation accuracy falls significantly under temporal and combined pressure for Qwen3-30B (\strictQwenBase{} to \strictQwenCombo) and Llama-4 (\strictLlamaBase{} to \strictLlamaCombo), and under the combined condition for DeepSeek (\strictDeepseekBase{} to \strictDeepseekCombo); GPT-5.4-mini, Mistral Small, and Grok~4.3 fall in the same direction without surviving correction in the single run, and Sonnet~5 does not fall at all. \section{Experiment 2: The Checker inside the Revision Loop}\label{sec:exp2}

Combined-condition drafts entered a revision loop. Each round, the checker returned one line per failure (a citation that does not resolve; a quotation that does not appear verbatim in the cited opinion), the model revised, and the loop ended on a clean draft or after three revisions. A control with \textit{false feedback} gave the same number of identically formatted lines pointed at items that were correct; \textit{mixed} controls kept each line or replaced it with a false one at random, so that the verifier's precision was about 0.25, 0.5, or 0.75; and a \textit{no-feedback} control gave the same number of revision rounds with a generic instruction to revise and no list of failures. The loop used the first 24 matters in the pre-registered order (twelve per side); loop rates are means over drafts on 24 matters, so round-0 figures differ from the pooled citation-level rates over 48 matters in Table~\ref{tab:pressure}, and citation counts in this section include unverifiable citations. Every scored round-0 quotation was traced to the final draft as \textit{corrected} (the same or an edited quotation on the same citation is now accurate), \textit{dequoted} (its words remain without quotation marks), or \textit{deleted}.

\section{Residual Errors at the Top of the Range}\label{sec:residual}

Three error classes remain when fabrication is rare, each measured deterministically.

\textbf{Paraphrase inside quotation marks.} Decomposing every inaccurate quotation against the cited opinion's own text, \decompParaPctSonnet{} percent of Sonnet~5's inaccurate quotations (\decompParaSonnet{} of them) are recognizable paraphrases of the correct case presented as verbatim quotation, \decompFabSonnet{} match nothing substantial in the cited opinion, and \decompOutSonnet{} cite authority outside the text store's scope; GPT-5.4-mini's split is \decompParaPctGpt{} percent paraphrase.

\textbf{Real language, wrong case.} Searching every inaccurate quotation against a segmented corpus of 8,392 Supreme Court opinions locates \misattrTotal{} quotations across the seven models (\misattrSonnet{} for Sonnet~5, \misattrQwen{} for Qwen3-30B) whose language appears verbatim in a different opinion than the one cited (matched probes are at least six words, so stock legal phrases do not count); quotations found nowhere in the corpus (\notInCorpusSonnet{} for Sonnet~5) include the paraphrases of the preceding paragraph and quotations from other courts. The same search audits the checker: it classified \falseAlarmSonnet{} of Sonnet~5's inaccurate verdicts and \falseAlarmQwen{} of Qwen3-30B's as the checker's own error, the language being present in the attributed case. A screen for models reproducing the argued case's own later opinion from memory found 41 candidates, all record text from the prompt or doctrinal formulas the opinions were themselves quoting; the finding was withdrawn.

\textbf{Right case, wrong page.} Where a pinpoint and a quotation co-occur on a U.S. Reports authority, the quotation lies on the cited page \pinAtSonnet{} percent of the time for Sonnet~5, between \pinAtMistral{} and \pinAtLlama{} percent for the other six, against \pinAtHuman{} percent for the human briefs of Section~\ref{sec:method} (\pinHumanN{} quotations), and on a different page of the cited case \pinWrongSonnet{} to \pinWrongMistral{} percent of the time (adjacent-page matches make up the remainder); pins outside the cited case are rare at every tier.

\textbf{Relevance judging: three failed calibrations.} Whether a real, correctly quoted case supports the proposition has no deterministic test; three language-model panels calibrated against the expert-labeled content-misrepresentation items of Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks}, with a pre-declared bar of 0.75 accuracy, scored 0.500, 0.700, and 0.594 (misrepresentation recall at best 4 of 17); no verdict from a failed panel was interpreted, and the result joins evidence that agreement statistics overstate judge validity~\cite{norman2026reliability,zheng2023judging}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Where the checker belongs.} The revision experiment argues for placing the checker at the gate, where it holds a draft and returns the failures to a person, and against placing it in the loop, where the model resolves them; the two positions use the same instrument and differ only in who acts on its output. Table~\ref{tab:true} records Sonnet~5's revisions: \trFlagRZeroSonnet{} flagged quotations, \trTrueFlagToAccSonnet{} corrected, the rest removed. Removing an unverifiable quotation is a defensible response to a flag, and three facts keep it from being a repair. The words usually survive as paraphrase the checker cannot test and the reader cannot tell from a claim that was never quoted; false flags get the same response, so correct quotations go too (Figure~\ref{fig:repair}); and a tool built on the loop pattern reports a clean draft, so the supervising attorney never sees the list. With the checker at the gate that list reaches the attorney (\cleanRZeroTotal{} of the 168 combined-condition drafts entering the loop would have passed unrevised).

\textbf{Verifier precision is a deployment requirement.} The mixed and false-feedback arms measure what an imprecise verifier costs. Our checker's own false-alarm rate on Sonnet~5's drafts was \falseAlarmPctSonnet{} percent. Published detection agents are less precise: Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026whochecks} report that weaker detectors flag a substantial share of correct citations, reading a coverage gap in a public index as a fabrication. Figure~\ref{fig:repair} shows what such flags do once a capable model acts on them: the correct quotations lost rise at every step as precision falls from 1 to 0, and at a precision of one half Sonnet~5 lost more than twice what it lost under a precise verifier and ended within seven points of where revision without feedback left it. A pipeline that feeds a probabilistic detector's flags to a model should therefore report the detector's precision alongside its recall, since precision sets how many correct quotations the model will remove. For existence, verbatim quotation, and pinpoint page no probabilistic detector is needed, because the check is exact and the released instrument performs it from public data.

\textbf{What evaluations should include.} A single fabrication rate at a neutral prompt misses both effects reported here: the 30B model's baseline existence of \existQwenBase{} overstates what it delivers under constraints a user is likely to add, and quotation accuracy, which existence benchmarks omit, fell for six of seven models. The temporal restriction was the most damaging single clause and the one to include first, since a date or jurisdiction restriction is a constraint a drafting prompt can carry. The residual classes at the top of the range are invisible to existence checks, and we expect retrieval-grounded tools to leave them in place, since they arise when the model composes the quotation.

\textbf{For courts.} Court orders that require disclosure of AI assistance~\cite{charlotin2025tracker} ask filers to state whether a model was used; the three checks reported here can be run by the court itself on any filing and cover the failures named in the sanctions decisions~\cite{mata2023}. Whether a real, correctly quoted case supports the proposition remains a human judgment (Section~\ref{sec:residual}).

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

The study covers one domain and task, Supreme Court merits argument in one packet format with two templates per condition, closed-book, and the stakes clause names a sanctions rule that does not govern the forum. The no-feedback and mixed controls and the per-quotation trace were added after the pre-registered analysis, and the tests in Section~\ref{sec:exp2} were chosen after the data were seen. Each cell was generated once at temperature 0. Two further independent generations of the baseline and combined drafts on all seven models (\repDrafts{} drafts per run) produced no draft identical to another run's except \repMistralIdentical{} of Mistral Small's, the instability reported by Blair-Stanek and Van Durme~\cite{blairstanek2025unstable}; across the three runs the standard deviation of a cell's existence rate was at most \repMaxExistSd{} and of its strict quotation rate at most \repMaxStrictSd, and the combined-versus-baseline quotation contrast kept its sign in every run for all \repSignStable{} models. Single-run cells therefore carry noise of a few points, and the pooled three-run fit reported in Section~\ref{sec:exp1} is the more precise estimate of the combined-condition effect. Two templates per condition do not span the phrasings users write; the quotation rates in Table~\ref{tab:pressure} are specific to the template, and the claims we rely on are the within-model contrasts that survive correction when both templates are pooled. Inference is per model at 48 matters, so effects of a few points are not detectable, and the seven models are one per vendor rather than a sampled population, so capability, family, and training recipe are confounded.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Under a citation quota, a date restriction, and a sanctions warning, citation integrity degrades from the smallest model upward, with nonexistent citations at 30B, misquotation through the middle of the range, and paraphrase in quotation marks, misattribution, and wrong pages at the top, with three refusals in \nPressured{} drafts. A checker built from public data detects each class, including the page-level errors thought to require commercial databases. Returned to the model, its findings repair citation existence; flagged quotations are removed far more often than corrected, and false flags remove correct ones. We therefore recommend placing verification at the gate and reporting verifier precision for any system that acts on its output.
