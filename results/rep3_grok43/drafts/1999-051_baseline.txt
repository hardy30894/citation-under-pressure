**ARGUMENT**

A federal habeas petitioner may not use a procedurally defaulted claim of ineffective assistance of appellate counsel to establish cause for the default of another claim. The Sixth Circuit’s contrary holding conflicts with settled precedent requiring that any asserted cause itself be free of unexcused procedural default.

To obtain federal review of a claim defaulted in state court, a petitioner must show both “cause for the default and actual prejudice as a result of the alleged violation of federal law.” Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722, 750 (1991). Cause exists only when some objective factor external to the defense impeded compliance with the state procedural rule. Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478, 488 (1986). Although ineffective assistance of counsel may constitute cause, that assistance must itself have been raised and exhausted in state court; otherwise the ineffective-assistance claim is itself defaulted and cannot serve as cause. Id. at 488-489; see also Edwards v. Carpenter, 529 U.S. 446, 451-453 (2000) (holding that a defaulted ineffective-assistance claim requires a separate showing of cause and prejudice before it may excuse another default).

Here, Carpenter’s application to reopen his direct appeal on the ground of appellate counsel’s ineffectiveness was dismissed as untimely under Ohio App. R. 26(B). That dismissal rested on an independent and adequate state procedural ground. Coleman, 501 U.S. at 729-732. Because Carpenter never established cause and prejudice for that separate default, the ineffective-assistance claim remained defaulted and could not excuse the default of his sufficiency-of-the-evidence claim. Carrier, 477 U.S. at 489.

The Sixth Circuit’s rule—that a defaulted ineffective-assistance claim may automatically constitute cause—would eviscerate the exhaustion requirement and permit federal courts to review claims never fairly presented to the state courts. Wainwright v. Sykes, 433 U.S. 72, 87-88 (1977). It would also undermine federalism by allowing prisoners to circumvent state procedural rules through successive layers of defaulted claims. Coleman, 501 U.S. at 731.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
