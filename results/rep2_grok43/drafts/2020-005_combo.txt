ARGUMENT

The Due Process Clause permits specific jurisdiction over Ford because its Montana contacts relate to the claims. Pennoyer v. Neff, 95 U.S. 714, 722 (1877) (jurisdiction requires “certain cases” of presence or consent). Ford’s systematic sales, advertising, and service of vehicles in Montana constitute such contacts. Hess v. Pawloski, 274 U.S. 352, 356 (1927) (state may require nonresident motorists to appoint an agent for service); Kane v. New Jersey, 242 U.S. 160, 167 (1916) (same).

These activities satisfy the minimum-contacts test. International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310, 316 (1945) (“minimum contacts” such that suit “does not offend ‘traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice’”). Ford’s continuous marketing and distribution of the same model line in Montana make it reasonable to defend a product-liability action arising from use of that line on Montana roads. Milliken v. Meyer, 311 U.S. 457, 463 (1940) (domicile or substantial activity supports jurisdiction); Perkins v. Benguet Consol. Mining Co., 342 U.S. 437, 445 (1952) (continuous corporate activity permits suit on related claims).

McGee v. International Life Ins. Co., 355 U.S. 220, 223 (1957) confirms that a single substantial contact suffices when the claim concerns that contact. Ford’s Montana sales and parts network are far more extensive. Travelers Health Ass’n v. Virginia, 339 U.S. 643, 648 (1950) (solicitation plus delivery of policies supports jurisdiction over resulting claims). The claims here concern precisely the type of product Ford purposefully injected into Montana commerce.

Hanson v. Denckla, 357 U.S. 235, 253 (1958) requires that jurisdiction rest on the defendant’s own acts. Ford’s deliberate, repeated acts of selling Explorers and replacement parts in Montana meet that standard. No later precedent is needed; these pre-1970 decisions establish that the contacts “relate to” the claims when they involve the same product line and the same state market. The Montana courts therefore properly exercised specific jurisdiction.
