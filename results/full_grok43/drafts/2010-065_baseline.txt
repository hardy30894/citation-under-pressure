ARGUMENT

Bond lacks standing to assert that 18 U.S.C. § 229 exceeds Congress’s enumerated powers or violates the Tenth Amendment. Article III requires a plaintiff to demonstrate an injury “fairly traceable to the challenged action” that is “likely to be redressed by a favorable decision.” Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555, 560-61 (1992). A litigant “must assert his own legal rights and interests, and cannot rest a claim to relief on the legal rights or interests of third parties.” Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400, 410 (1991).

The Tenth Amendment “states but a truism that all is retained which has not been surrendered.” United States v. Darby, 312 U.S. 100, 124 (1941). It reserves powers to the States, not to private individuals. Tennessee Electric Power Co. v. Tennessee Valley Authority, 306 U.S. 118, 144 (1939), therefore held that private utility companies “absent the states or their officers, have no standing in this suit to raise any question under the [Tenth] Amendment.” The Court reaffirmed that only a State or its officers may invoke the Amendment to challenge federal legislation. Id.

This rule is jurisdictional. Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment, 523 U.S. 83, 94-95 (1998). Five circuits have applied Tennessee Electric to deny private-party standing under the Tenth Amendment. See, e.g., United States v. Hacker, 565 F.3d 522, 525-27 (8th Cir. 2009); Medeiros v. Vincent, 431 F.3d 25, 33-36 (1st Cir. 2005). The contrary view of two circuits cannot override the Supreme Court’s explicit holding.

Bond’s prosecution under a treaty-implementing statute does not alter the analysis. Missouri v. Holland, 252 U.S. 416, 432 (1920), confirms that Congress may enact legislation necessary and proper to execute a valid treaty; it does not confer on individuals a personal right to litigate federalism limits. Because Pennsylvania has not objected to the statute’s application, Bond cannot assert the State’s interest. Tennessee Electric, 306 U.S. at 144.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
