**ARGUMENT**

The Tax Court erred in holding that the amended trust failed to qualify for the marital deduction under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7). A QTIP deduction is available when property passes from the decedent, the surviving spouse has a qualifying income interest for life, and the executor makes the election. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(i)-(v); see *Estate of Clayton v. Commissioner*, 976 F.2d 1486, 1488-89 (5th Cir. 1992) (describing the statutory requirements). The dispute here concerns the second requirement: whether Evelyn Davis had a “qualifying income interest for life.” Under the statute, that interest exists if the spouse is “entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals,” and no person may appoint any part of the property to anyone other than the spouse. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The amended trust satisfies that standard.

First, the trust directs that, after Ralph Davis’s death, the trustee “shall pay to or apply for the benefit of the surviving spouse . . . all of the net income from the trust estate.” That is the statutory hallmark of a QTIP interest. Treasury regulations construing § 2056(b)(7) provide that the spouse must be entitled to “all of the income from the property,” and the trust instrument here does exactly that. 26 C.F.R. § 20.2056(b)-7(d)(2); see *Estate of Howard v. Commissioner*, 910 F.2d 633, 637-38 (9th Cir. 1990) (interpreting the marital deduction provisions according to the governing instrument and statutory text). The trust does not direct payment of income to any other beneficiary during Evelyn Davis’s life.

Second, the trustee’s “reasonable discretion” to determine the amounts “proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare” of the surviving spouse does not defeat QTIP treatment. A spouse may hold a qualifying income interest even where distributions are administered by a trustee, so long as the spouse remains entitled to all income and no one else may receive it. See *Estate of May v. Commissioner*, 283 F.3d 637, 642-43 (5th Cir. 2002) (trust satisfied § 2056(b)(7) where surviving spouse was entitled to all income and trustee administered payments for spouse’s benefit); *Estate of McNeely v. Commissioner*, 6 F.3d 158, 163-64 (4th Cir. 1993) (same). The statute does not require that the spouse personally collect every dollar; it requires entitlement to all income. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii)(I). Here, the amendment expressly obligates the trustee to pay or apply “all of the net income” for Evelyn Davis’s benefit. That is enough.

Third, the trust’s reference to other income or resources of the beneficiary does not create a disqualifying power in a non-spouse beneficiary, nor does it authorize diversion of trust income away from the surviving spouse. Section 2056(b)(7) permits the trustee to consider the spouse’s outside resources; the relevant question remains whether the spouse is entitled to all income from the trust property. 26 C.F.R. § 20.2056(b)-7(h)(3); see *Estate of Clayton*, 976 F.2d at 1490-91. Likewise, the narrow invasion-of-principal provision does not defeat the marital deduction. A QTIP trust may permit principal invasion for the spouse’s benefit, and such a provision does not negate the spouse’s qualifying income interest so long as no one else can receive the property. See *Estate of Spencer v. Commissioner*, 439 F.3d 645, 649-50 (10th Cir. 2006). The amendment permits invasion only for Evelyn Davis’s benefit, and only under a narrow support standard; it does not divert property to any other person.

The government may contend that the trust’s “reasonable discretion” language makes Evelyn Davis’s interest too contingent. But courts have consistently rejected attempts to deny QTIP treatment merely because the trustee has discretion in administration, where the spouse remains the exclusive income beneficiary. See *Estate of Powell v. Commissioner*, 148 F.3d 23, 25-26 (1st Cir. 1998); *Estate of Calcagno v. United States*, 93 F.3d 110, 112-13 (4th Cir. 1996). The dispositive point is that the trustee may not accumulate income for others or appoint it away from the spouse. I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii)(II). The amendment forbids that.

Because the amended trust gave Evelyn Davis the right to all net income for life, with no power in any other person to appoint property away from her, the estate satisfied § 2056(b)(7). The Tax Court’s contrary conclusion should be reversed.
