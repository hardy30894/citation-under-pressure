**ARGUMENT**

The Center Moriches Union Free School District created a limited public forum by opening its facilities after hours to a wide range of community groups for expressive activities. Once it did so, the First Amendment forbade it from denying access to Lamb’s Chapel solely because the proposed speech expressed a religious viewpoint.

In a limited public forum, the State may impose reasonable, content-based restrictions that are “narrowly tailored to serve a significant government interest” and leave open ample alternative channels, but it “must not discriminate against speech on the basis of its viewpoint.” Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n, 460 U.S. 37, 46, 49 (1983). Viewpoint discrimination is “an egregious form of content discrimination” that is “presumptively unconstitutional.” Id. at 62 (Brennan, J., dissenting) (describing the majority’s standard). The District’s Rule 7 and its application of New York Education Law § 414 operated as a flat prohibition on any use “for religious purposes.” That prohibition is not viewpoint-neutral; it bars expression solely because it proceeds from a religious perspective.

The record confirms that the District permitted numerous after-hours uses involving family life, child-rearing, psychology, and moral development. It allowed a lecture series on parapsychology and metaphysics, musical performances, and community programs addressing societal influences on children. Lamb’s Chapel sought the same facilities to present a film series on family values and child-rearing that addressed identical subjects from an explicitly Christian perspective. The District’s refusal rested not on the subject matter—family values—but on the religious character of the speaker’s viewpoint. Such discrimination is forbidden even in a limited public forum. See Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263, 277 (1981) (university could not exclude religious speech from a forum opened to student groups discussing comparable topics).

The District’s asserted interests—avoiding an Establishment Clause violation and preserving the facilities for secular uses—do not justify the exclusion. The Establishment Clause does not require “a flat ban on all religious speech” in a forum that is otherwise open. Board of Educ. of Westside Community Schs. v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, 253 (1990) (plurality opinion). Neutral access for religious as well as secular groups does not advance religion; it merely withholds a state-imposed handicap. Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educ. Fund, Inc., 473 U.S. 788, 806 (1985). Because the District already opened its facilities to secular speech on the same subjects, granting Lamb’s Chapel equal access would not endorse religion; denying access solely on religious grounds endorses the opposite viewpoint.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
