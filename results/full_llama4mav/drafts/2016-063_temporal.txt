## Argument

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit erred in transferring Anthony Perry's case to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit. The proper forum for judicial review of the Merit Systems Protection Board's (MSPB) dismissal of Perry's mixed case is a federal district court.

The Civil Service Reform Act of 1978, as codified in 5 U.S.C. § 7702(a)(1), defines a mixed case as one in which an employee "has been affected by an action which [she] may appeal to the Merit Systems Protection Board, and alleges that a basis for the action was discrimination prohibited by" certain federal laws. Although the MSPB dismissed Perry's appeal for lack of jurisdiction, the underlying action was still a mixed case. The statute does not explicitly distinguish between procedural and jurisdictional dismissals.

In Brown v. United States, 126 F. Supp. 654 (Ct. Cl. 1954), the court held that the jurisdiction of the Court of Claims was not limited to cases where the plaintiff had a valid claim on the merits, but also extended to cases where the court had to determine its own jurisdiction. Similarly, in this case, the district court should have jurisdiction to review the MSPB's jurisdictional dismissal.

Furthermore, in United States v. ICC, 337 U.S. 426 (1949), the Supreme Court held that the statutory scheme for judicial review should be interpreted to allow for review in a single forum, rather than fragmenting review across multiple courts. Allowing district courts to review MSPB jurisdictional dismissals in mixed cases would be consistent with this principle.

The D.C. Circuit's reliance on Powell v. Department of Defense, 158 F.3d 597 (D.C. Cir. 1998), is misplaced. Powell was decided after the Supreme Court's decision in United States v. Interstate Commerce Commission, but it cited and relied upon the reasoning in United States v. ICC, which held that the statutory scheme should be interpreted to allow for review in a single forum.

In Ex parte Peru, 318 U.S. 578 (1943), the Supreme Court emphasized the importance of a clear and consistent statutory scheme for judicial review. In this case, allowing district courts to review MSPB jurisdictional dismissals would provide a consistent and coherent approach to judicial review.

Therefore, we urge the Court to reverse the D.C. Circuit's decision and hold that the proper forum for judicial review of the MSPB's dismissal of Perry's mixed case is a federal district court.
