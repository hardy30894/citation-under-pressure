**Argument**

The district court correctly held that Santander was a creditor, not a “debt collector,” when it collected the petitioners’ debts. Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), a “debt collector” is defined as a person who “regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed or due or asserted to be owed or due another.” 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). This definition is mutually exclusive with that of a “creditor,” who collects debts for his own account. See Ruth v. Triumph P’ships, 577 F.3d 790, 796 (7th Cir. 2009); Bridge v. Ocwen Fed. Bank, FSB, 681 F.3d 355, 359 (6th Cir. 2012). The critical inquiry is whether Santander was collecting debts “owed or due another” at the time of the challenged conduct. See Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A., 797 F.3d 1309, 1315 (11th Cir. 2015). The statute is not satisfied by looking at the original ownership of the debt, but rather the ownership at the time of collection. See Schlegel v. Wells Fargo Bank, NA, 720 F.3d 1204, 1209 (9th Cir. 2013).

Here, Santander purchased the defaulted loans from CitiFinancial. At that point, Santander became the creditor. It then collected the debts for its own account, not for CitiFinancial. See S. Rep. No. 95-382, at 3 (1977) (“The Committee intends the term ‘debt collector,’ subject to the exclusions discussed below, to cover all third persons who regularly collect, debts for others”). Because Santander collected for its own account, it was not a “debt collector” under the Act. See Heintz v. Jenkins, 514 U.S. 291, 293 (1995). The petitioners argue that the default status of the debt determines the classification. However, the exclusion in § 1692a(6)(F)(iii) applies only to persons collecting non-defaulted debts for others. See FTC v. Check Investors, Inc., 502 F.3d 159, 173 (3d Cir. 2007). It does not apply to a purchaser of defaulted debt who collects for its own account. The petitioners’ interpretation would create a loophole, allowing a debt collector to become a creditor simply by purchasing the debt it was collecting for another. See id. at 172. Congress did not intend this result. See 15 U.S.C. § 1692e (purpose of the Act).

Finally, the statutory language supports the respondent’s position. The phrase “debts owed or due another” must be read to apply to the debt at the time of the collection activity. See Paroline v. United States, 572 U.S. 834, 1721 (2014) (quoting Porto Rico Ry., Light & Power Co. v. Mor, 253 U.S. 345, 348 (1920)). Because Santander collected debts it owned, it was a creditor, not a debt collector, and is not subject to the FDCPA.
