# ARGUMENT

## I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING LEADER'S MOTION FOR JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW BECAUSE FACEBOOK FAILED TO PROVE BY CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE THAT THE LEADER2LEADER® PRODUCT PRACTICED EVERY LIMITATION OF THE ASSERTED CLAIMS

### A. Standard of Review

This Court reviews the denial of a motion for judgment as a matter of law without deference, applying the law of the regional circuit—here, the Third Circuit—to determine whether substantial evidence supports the jury's verdict. *Applied Med. Res. Corp. v. U.S. Surgical Corp.*, 448 F.3d 1324, 1332 (Fed. Cir. 2006). Because invalidity must be proven by clear and convincing evidence, this heightened standard governs the substantial-evidence inquiry. *Microsoft Corp. v. i4i Ltd. P'ship*, 131 S. Ct. 2238, 2242 (2011) (confirming that § 282's presumption of validity requires invalidity defenses to be proved by clear and convincing evidence). A patent is invalid under § 102(b) only if the accused prior-art product embodies *every* limitation of the asserted claims. *Trintec Indus., Inc. v. Top-U.S.A. Corp.*, 295 F.3d 1292, 1295 (Fed. Cir. 2002) ("It is well settled that an invalidity defense premised on the on-sale bar or public use bar must show that the claimed invention was in public use or on sale, embodying each and every limitation of the claim.").

### B. Facebook Failed to Prove that Leader2Leader® Practiced the "Dynamically Associating Metadata" Limitation

Claim 9 requires "dynamically associating metadata with the data," where "the metadata includes information related to the user, the data, the application, and the user environment." '761 patent, col.21 ll.44-47. This limitation is the heart of the invention: it is what allows data to be tagged to "capture the association between the data and its context," '761 patent, col.9 ll.53-56, such that "[a]s users create and change their contexts, the data ... and applications automatically follow," *id.* col.7 ll.46-49.

Facebook's proof at trial rested almost entirely on the January 2002 white paper presented at Wright Patterson Air Force Base. That document, however, describes the *problem* Leader's invention solves—the communications "glass ceiling" caused by data "silos," J.A. 27202—without disclosing the specific dynamic metadata architecture the claims require. General assertions that a product has been "[f]ully developed," J.A. 27204, or is "operational now," J.A. 27207, are marketing statements, not technical proof that the accused system practiced the specific metadata-tagging limitation recited in claim 9. This Court has cautioned that invalidity may not rest on "conclusory statements" untethered to the specific claim language. *ActiveVideo Networks, Inc. v. Verizon Commc'ns, Inc.*, 694 F.3d 1312, 1327 (Fed. Cir. 2012) (rejecting expert testimony that failed to map prior art to specific claim limitations). Facebook's evidence suffers the same defect: it never demonstrates that the pre-critical-date Leader2Leader® system dynamically updated metadata to include information about "the user, the data, the application, and the user environment" in the manner claimed.

### C. Facebook Failed to Prove that Leader2Leader® Practiced the "Tracking Movement" and "Dynamically Updating" Limitations

Claim 9 further requires "tracking movement of the user from the user environment ... to a second user environment" and "dynamically updating the stored metadata with an association of the data, the application, and the second user environment." '761 patent, col.21 ll.48-55. Nothing in the white paper, or in the "Big Board" attachment depicting "analyst collaboration and information flow," J.A. 27210, establishes that the pre-2002 product tracked a user's movement between environments and dynamically updated metadata accordingly. The Big Board's reference to a "Browser" as an "Input & Display Collaboration Device[]," J.A. 27210, shows only that the system was web-accessible—not that it performed the specific tracking-and-updating function the claims require.

This Court has repeatedly held that testimony describing a product in general terms, without correlating specific product features to specific claim limitations, cannot sustain a clear-and-convincing-evidence finding of invalidity. *See Finjan, Inc. v. Secure Computing Corp.*, 626 F.3d 1197, 1206 (Fed. Cir. 2010) (vacating invalidity verdict where testimony failed to address every limitation with particularity). Mr. McKibben's testimony that the patent covered the "underlying engine," J.A. 25585-86, does not concede that the *commercial* Leader2Leader® product—as sold before the critical date—practiced each limitation; it speaks only to the relationship between the patent and Leader's internal technology generally.

### D. The Jury's Verdict Cannot Stand Where Facebook Relied on Inference and Generalized Marketing Materials Rather than Claim-by-Claim Proof

Public-use and on-sale invalidity defenses demand rigorous, limitation-by-limitation proof precisely because they upset the statutory presumption of validity. *See Microsoft*, 131 S. Ct. at 2245-46. Facebook's case was built on inference from promotional language rather than technical evidence mapping the accused product to the claims. A reasonable jury could not have found, by clear and convincing evidence, that a document describing a system as "operational" and touting fixes to unspecified "flaws," J.A. 27202, 27207, necessarily practiced the specific dynamic-metadata, tracking, and updating limitations of claim 9 and its dependent claims. Because "substantial evidence does not support the jury's findings," *Applied Med.*, 448 F.3d at 1332, the district court erred in denying Leader's motion for judgment as a matter of law.

### E. In the Alternative, Leader Is Entitled to a New Trial

Even if this Court concludes that the evidence was not so one-sided as to warrant judgment as a matter of law, the verdict is against the weight of the evidence and a new trial is warranted. *See Lightning Lube, Inc. v. Witco Corp.*, 4 F.3d 1153, 1166 (3d Cir. 1993) (new trial appropriate where verdict is against the weight of the evidence). The jury's reliance on generalized marketing statements, divorced from specific claim analysis, created a serious risk of miscarriage of justice that only retrial can cure.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the judgment of invalidity, or, in the alternative, vacate the judgment and remand for a new trial.
