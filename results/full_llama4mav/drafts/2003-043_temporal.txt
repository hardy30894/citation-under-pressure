The government's argument that it can collect a partnership's tax debt from individual partners without assessing the partners separately is unpersuasive. The Internal Revenue Code requires that a tax be assessed against a "taxpayer" within three years after the filing of the taxpayer's return. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a). The Code defines a "taxpayer" as "any person subject to any internal revenue tax," and a "person" includes "an individual, a trust, estate, partnership, association, company or corporation." 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14), (a)(1). As the Ninth Circuit correctly held, both the partnership and the individual partners are separate "taxpayers" under the Code. 

The government's assessment against the partnership does not extend the statute of limitations for collecting the tax from the individual partners. As the Ninth Circuit noted, the regulation interpreting § 6203 requires that a valid assessment "shall provide identification of the taxpayer." 26 C.F.R. § 301.6203-1. The assessment against the partnership identifies the partnership as the taxpayer, not the individual partners.

The government's reliance on Young v. Riddell is misplaced. In Young, the district court held that a partner was not entitled to a refund of taxes paid because he was liable for the partnership's tax debt under state law. 60-1 U.S. Tax Cas. (CCH) ¶ 9831, at 76,054 (S.D.Cal.1959). However, the court also acknowledged that the government would have had to resort to court action or individually assess the taxes against the partner to collect the tax. Id. at 76,050. The Ninth Circuit's affirmance of the district court's decision in Young did not reject this interpretation. Young v. Riddell, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir.1960).

Furthermore, the Ninth Circuit's decision in United States v. Coson supports the conclusion that an assessment against a partnership is not sufficient to collect the tax from individual partners. 286 F.2d 453 (9th Cir.1961). In Coson, the court held that a tax lien against a partner's property was invalid because the partner had not been individually assessed. Id. at 458. Although the court ultimately affirmed on different grounds, the district court's holding that an assessment against the partnership was insufficient to create a lien against the partner's property is relevant here. Coson v. United States, 169 F.Supp. 671, 676 (S.D.Cal.1958).

Under California law, a creditor may not collect a partnership debt from an individual partner without first obtaining a judgment against the partner. Cal. Corp.Code § 16307(c). The government has not obtained a judgment against the individual partners, and the statute of limitations for doing so has expired. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a). Therefore, the government's claim against the individual partners is barred, and the bankruptcy court's disallowance of the claim was proper.
