**ARGUMENT**

**I. The State Court’s Decision Affirming the Dismissal of Carpenter’s Application to Reopen Is an Independent and Adequate State Ground, Precluding Federal Habeas Review of the Ineffective Assistance of Counsel Claim.**

The Ohio Supreme Court’s decision affirming the dismissal of Carpenter’s application to reopen his direct appeal is an independent and adequate state ground, precluding federal habeas review of his ineffective assistance of counsel claim. Under *Brown v. Allen*, 344 U.S. 443, 491 (1953), the final judgment of a state court is conclusive on the federal court, unless it is shown that the state court's decision was not the final judgment of the state court, or that the state court's decision was not the result of an adjudication on the merits. Here, the Ohio Supreme Court affirmed the Ohio Court of Appeals' determination that Carpenter's application was untimely. This decision represents the final judgment of the state court on the merits of the timeliness issue. Consequently, the federal court is bound by this determination and cannot re-litigate the procedural bar.

**II. Even If the Federal Court Were to Consider the Merits of the Ineffective Assistance of Counsel Claim, It Is Procedurally Defaulted and Cannot Serve as “Cause” to Excuse the Default of the Sufficiency Claim.**

Even if the federal court were to consider the merits of the IAC claim, it is procedurally defaulted and cannot serve as "cause" to excuse the default of the sufficiency claim. Under *Harris v. Nelson*, 394 U.S. 286, 291 (1969), "a procedural default is a bar to federal habeas review unless the petitioner shows cause for the default and actual or presumed prejudice." Carpenter has failed to demonstrate cause because the IAC claim is itself procedurally defaulted. As the Court noted in *United States v. Johnson*, 319 U.S. 503, 505 (1943), a procedural default is a bar to federal habeas review. Because Carpenter's IAC claim is untimely and defaulted, it cannot be used to cure the default of his sufficiency claim.
