### Argument

The petition should be dismissed because a Board refusal to reopen a prior benefits claim is not a “final decision” subject to judicial review under the Railroad Retirement Act. The Fifth Circuit’s dismissal accords with this Court’s precedent and with the governing Supreme Court framework.

Section 355(f) authorizes review only of “final decision[s]” of the Railroad Retirement Board. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). But this Court has repeatedly explained that the statute reaches only merits determinations made after administrative remedies are exhausted. In *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139, 140-41 (5th Cir. 2003), the Fifth Circuit held that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board’s refusal to reopen a prior claim for benefits. Relying on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), the court concluded that reopening decisions are not final merits decisions within the meaning of the statute. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141. That rule controlled below and correctly required dismissal here.

This Court’s decision in *Califano* is dispositive. There, the Court held that the Social Security Act’s judicial-review provision does not permit review of the agency’s refusal to reopen a previously denied claim. 430 U.S. at 107-08. The Court reasoned that allowing judicial review of reopening denials would undercut the statutory scheme by enabling claimants to obtain review of stale claims after the time for direct review had passed. Id. at 108-09. Although *Califano* construed the Social Security Act, courts of appeals have consistently applied its reasoning to analogous benefits-review provisions, including the Railroad Retirement Act. See *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141; *Harris v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999); *Abbruzzese v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995); *Gutierrez v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990); *Steebe v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 708 F.2d 250, 254-55 (7th Cir. 1983).

Salinas contends that the Board’s refusal to reopen his 2006 claim is itself a reviewable final decision. But that argument was expressly rejected in *Roberts*. The Fifth Circuit acknowledged the circuit split and still held that “we have no jurisdiction” to review a decision “not to reopen a prior claim for benefits.” 346 F.3d at 140-41. That holding remains binding unless and until overruled by the court en banc or this Court. See *Agostini v. Felton*, 521 U.S. 203, 237 (1997) (lower courts should follow controlling precedent unless and until it is overruled by this Court); *Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc.*, 490 U.S. 477, 484 (1989). The Fifth Circuit therefore correctly applied its precedent and dismissed the petition.

The D.C. Circuit’s contrary decision in *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), does not aid Salinas. Whatever the merits of that court’s interpretation, it cannot displace binding circuit precedent in the Fifth Circuit. See *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141; *Vaughan v. Anderson Regional Medical Center*, 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir. 2017). And in any event, *Stovic* directly conflicts with the better-reasoned majority view, which follows *Califano* in holding that reopening denials are not judicially reviewable final decisions. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141.

Nor is there any basis to treat Salinas’s petition as presenting a reviewable merits challenge in disguise. The Board’s refusal to reopen his 2006 application did not alter the original denial on the merits; it simply declined to revisit a closed case after the regulatory reopening period had expired. As *Califano* explains, a request to reopen is a collateral plea for administrative grace, not a fresh final decision on entitlement. 430 U.S. at 107-09. The statute supplies no jurisdiction to second-guess that discretionary choice.

Because the Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s prior claim is not a final decision within 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), and because *Roberts* squarely forecloses jurisdiction, the petition for review was properly dismissed.
