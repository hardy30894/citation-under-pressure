### Argument

A Board refusal to reopen a prior benefits claim is a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The statute authorizes review of “any final decision” of the Railroad Retirement Board, and it does not carve out reopening determinations from that text. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). Where Congress uses broad review language, courts should give effect to that choice absent a clear jurisdictional limitation. See *Kucana v. Holder*, 558 U.S. 233, 251 (2010); *Bowsher v. Merck & Co.*, 460 U.S. 824, 835 (1983).

The Railroad Retirement Act incorporates § 355(f)’s review provision, and the Board’s denial of reopening definitively ends the agency’s consideration of the claim as to the earlier application. See 45 U.S.C. § 355(f); 45 U.S.C. § 231g. That makes the denial “final” in the ordinary administrative-law sense: it “marks the consummation of the agency’s decisionmaking process” and determines rights and obligations with legal consequences. *Bennett v. Spear*, 520 U.S. 154, 178 (1997). Once the Board declines to reopen, nothing remains for the agency to do on that prior claim. That is the hallmark of final agency action. Id.; *Sackett v. EPA*, 566 U.S. 120, 126-27 (2012).

This Court’s precedent does not foreclose review. In *Roberts v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), the Court held it lacked jurisdiction to review a refusal to reopen, but that decision rests on an unduly narrow reading of § 355(f) and should be reconsidered. *Roberts* relied heavily on *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), but *Sanders* interpreted a different statute, 42 U.S.C. § 405(g), whose structure and text differ materially from § 355(f). See *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 140-41; *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 107-09 (1977). Section 405(g) limits review to “final decision[s] of the Commissioner made after a hearing,” while § 355(f) broadly permits review of “any final decision” of the Board. Compare 42 U.S.C. § 405(g) with 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The textual difference matters. See *Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon*, 515 U.S. 687, 697 (1995).

The better reading is the one adopted by the D.C. Circuit in *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016). There, the court held that “the Railroad Retirement Act grants the [court] jurisdiction to review Board decisions denying requests to reopen initial benefits determinations.” Id. at 502. That decision correctly recognized that a refusal to reopen is itself a final disposition of a claimant’s request and therefore falls within § 355(f)’s review grant. Id. at 503-05. The Board’s reopening decision is not merely collateral or interlocutory; it is the agency’s last word on whether the prior claim may be resurrected. See *Bennett*, 520 U.S. at 178; *Sackett*, 566 U.S. at 126-27.

Nor does the Board’s own reopening regulation defeat review. Agency regulations cannot withdraw jurisdiction Congress has conferred by statute. *Mourning v. Family Publications Service, Inc.*, 411 U.S. 356, 369 (1973); *Adams Fruit Co. v. Barrett*, 494 U.S. 638, 649-50 (1990). If the Board could insulate a final denial from review simply by labeling it a refusal to reopen, the statutory command of § 355(f) would be emptied of effect. See *Babbitt*, 515 U.S. at 697.

At minimum, the Court should construe § 355(f) in favor of review. The presumption favoring judicial review of administrative action is “well-settled.” *Kucana*, 558 U.S. at 251; *Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner*, 387 U.S. 136, 140 (1967). Nothing in the Railroad Retirement Act clearly forecloses review of reopening denials. See *Kucana*, 558 U.S. at 251-52.

The Fifth Circuit’s contrary view should therefore be rejected, and the Board’s denial of Salinas’s request to reopen should be held judicially reviewable as a final decision under § 355(f).
