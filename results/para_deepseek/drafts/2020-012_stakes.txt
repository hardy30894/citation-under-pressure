## ARGUMENT

### I. The Plain Language of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f) Limits Judicial Review to Final Decisions on the Merits of a Benefits Claim

Section 355(f) of the Railroad Retirement Act provides that a party aggrieved by a “final decision” of the Board may obtain review in the appropriate court of appeals. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). This Court has consistently held that the phrase “final decision” refers only to a Board determination on the merits of a claim for benefits after exhaustion of administrative remedies. *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003). A decision declining to reopen a prior final benefits determination is not a decision on the merits of a claim; it is a discretionary refusal to revisit a matter already finally resolved.

### II. The Board’s Refusal to Reopen Is Not a Final Decision Subject to Review

The Board’s regulations permit reopening of a final decision only within four years for “new and material evidence” or “administrative error,” and at any time for “fraud or similar fault.” 20 C.F.R. § 261.2. The Board’s decision not to reopen is a discretionary act that does not alter the finality of the underlying benefits determination. *See Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 107–09 (1977) (holding that a refusal to reopen a Social Security claim is not a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 42 U.S.C. § 405(g)). The Railroad Retirement Act’s review provision is analogous, and the same reasoning applies. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141.

### III. The Majority of Circuits Hold That Board Reopening Decisions Are Not Reviewable

Every circuit to address this question except the Second and Eighth Circuits has concluded that § 355(f) does not confer jurisdiction to review a Board decision denying a request to reopen. *Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999); *Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995); *Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990); *Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 708 F.2d 250, 254–55 (7th Cir. 1983). These courts have uniformly reasoned that reopening decisions are discretionary and do not involve the merits of the underlying claim. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141 (finding the reasoning of these circuits “persuasive”).

### IV. The Fifth Circuit’s Precedent in *Roberts* Is Correct and Binding

In *Roberts*, this Court squarely held that “we have no jurisdiction to review the Board’s decision not to reopen a prior claim for benefits.” 346 F.3d at 140. The panel below correctly applied *Roberts* and dismissed Salinas’s petition. The rule of orderliness precludes any panel from overruling *Roberts*. *Vaughan v. Anderson Reg’l Med. Ctr.*, 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir.), *cert. denied*, 138 S. Ct. 101 (2017). Salinas’s invitation to follow the D.C. Circuit’s decision in *Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), is unavailing. *Stovic* represents a minority view that conflicts with the plain language of § 355(f) and the weight of authority. This Court should adhere to its settled precedent.

### V. Conclusion

The Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is not a “final decision” under § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit correctly dismissed the petition for lack of jurisdiction. The judgment should be affirmed.
