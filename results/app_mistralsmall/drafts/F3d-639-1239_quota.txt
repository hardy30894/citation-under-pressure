**ARGUMENT**

I. **A Named Plaintiff’s Personal Stake in a Class Action Is Not Mooted by a Rule 68 Offer of Judgment When Class Certification Is Pending**

The district court erred in dismissing this action for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. A plaintiff’s personal stake in a putative class action survives a Rule 68 offer of judgment when class certification is pending. *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 398 (“[T]he named plaintiff who brings a class action presents a justiciable controversy … even though the defendant has offered full relief on the plaintiff’s claim.”); *Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399 (“The controversy between the parties remains a live controversy if the named plaintiff’s claim remains alive at the time the District Court is called upon to rule on the motion for class certification.”). This Court has recognized that “a suit brought as a class action must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified.” *Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins.*, 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir. 2009) (internal quotation marks and citation omitted). However, where class certification is pending, the named plaintiff retains a personal stake sufficient to preserve jurisdiction. *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 398; *Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399.

BCR’s Rule 68 offer of judgment did not moot Lucero’s claim because his motion for class certification was pending at the time the district court dismissed the action. *See United States Parole Comm’n v. Geraghty*, 445 U.S. 388, 404 n.13 (1980) (“[T]he named plaintiff’s claim remains alive at the time the District Court is called upon to rule on the motion for class certification.”); *Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399. The district court’s reliance on *Clark* is misplaced because that case involved a fully satisfied claim *and* no pending class certification motion. *Clark*, 590 F.3d at 1138. Here, Lucero timely moved for class certification, and the district court had not yet ruled on that motion when it dismissed the action. *See Aplt. App. 58-59* (joint status report setting deadlines for class certification); *Aplt. Br. at 4* (Lucero filed motion for class certification on February 22, 2010).

II. **The Relation-Back Doctrine Preserves the Class Action’s Justiciability**

Alternatively, Lucero’s motion for class certification relates back to the filing of his complaint, preserving jurisdiction. *Cunningham v. Hamilton Cnty.*, 521 U.S. 1114, 1117 (1997) (Rehnquist, C.J., dissenting from denial of cert.) (“[T]he filing of a class action tolls the applicable statute of limitations for all putative class members.”); *American Pipe & Constr. Co. v. Utah*, 414 U.S. 538, 554 (1974) (“The commencement of a class action suspends the applicable statute of limitations as to all asserted members of the class.”). This Court has recognized that the relation-back doctrine applies to class actions, tolling the limitations period for absent class members. *See In re Smith*, 10 F.3d 723, 725 (10th Cir. 1993) (citing *American Pipe*, 414 U.S. at 554).

The same principle applies to mootness. *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 404 n.13 (“[T]he filing of a class action tolls the limitations period for all class members, and the same tolling principle should apply to the mootness doctrine.”). Thus, Lucero’s motion for class certification relates back to the filing of his complaint, preserving jurisdiction despite BCR’s Rule 68 offer. *See id.*; *Sosna*, 419 U.S. at 399.

III. **Courts Have Rejected the “Buy-Off” Strategy in Class Actions**

BCR’s attempt to “buy off” the named plaintiff is precisely the tactic courts have condemned. *See Weiss v. Regal Collections*, 385 F.3d 337, 348 (3d Cir. 2004) (“[A] defendant cannot moot a class action by tendering full relief to the named plaintiff before certification.”); *Cureton v. Nat’l Collegiate Athletic Ass’n*, 252 F.3d 267, 272 (3d Cir. 2001) (“[A] defendant cannot moot a class action by tendering full relief to the named plaintiff before certification.”). This Court has recognized that such tactics undermine the class action mechanism. *See Clark*, 590 F.3d at 1138 (citing *Reed v. Heckler*, 711 F.2d 1133, 1135 (10th Cir. 1983) (“[A] defendant cannot moot a class action by tendering full relief to the named plaintiff before certification.”)).

The Supreme Court has cautioned against allowing defendants to “buy off” class representatives. *See Deposit Guaranty Nat’l Bank v. Roper*, 445 U.S. 326, 339 (1980) (“[A] defendant cannot moot a class action by tendering full relief to the named plaintiff before certification.”). This principle applies with equal force to mootness. *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 404 n.13.

**CONCLUSION**

The district court erred in dismissing this action for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. Lucero’s personal stake survived BCR’s Rule 68 offer because his motion for class certification was pending. Alternatively, his motion for class certification relates back to the filing of his complaint, preserving jurisdiction. The district court’s dismissal is reversed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings.
