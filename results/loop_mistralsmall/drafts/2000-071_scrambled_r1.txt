**ARGUMENT**

I. MILFORD’S COMMUNITY-USE POLICY IS A NEUTRAL, REASONABLE TIME, PLACE, AND MANNER REGULATION OF A LIMITED PUBLIC FORUM.

Milford’s Community-Use Policy “does not discriminate among viewpoints” and “imposes no unreasonable obstacles” to speech; it simply confines after-hours use to activities that are secular in subject matter. *Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 49 (1983). The policy “was not adopted . . . to suppress any particular point of view.” *Id.* at 46. Because the policy “serves a legitimate . . . interest” and “is viewpoint neutral,” it need only be “reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum.” *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educ. Fund*, 473 U.S. 788, 806 (1985) (quoting *Perry*, 460 U.S. at 45). Milford’s stated purpose—to reserve its facilities for “social, civic and recreational meetings and entertainment events and other uses pertaining to the welfare of the community”—is legitimate and reasonable. N.Y. Educ. Law § 414; *see also* *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 271 (1981) (recognizing a university’s legitimate interest in avoiding “the appearance of official endorsement” of religious activities).

II. MILFORD DID NOT ENGAGE IN UNCONSTITUTIONAL VIEWPOINT DISCRIMINATION.

The Club conflates subject-matter and viewpoint discrimination. A regulation is viewpoint discriminatory only if it “suppress[es] particular viewpoints” within a subject that the forum otherwise permits. *Rosenberger v. Univ. of Virginia*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995). Here, Milford has “never opened” its forum to “religious instruction or prayer.” App. 202 F.3d 502, 512 (2d Cir. 2000). The Club’s activities—singing hymns, reciting scripture, and praying—are not “discussion of secular subjects such as child rearing, development of character and development of morals from a religious perspective,” but are instead “the equivalent of religious worship.” *Id.* at 511. Because Milford has not permitted any group to engage in worship-like activities, it has not discriminated against the Club’s viewpoint. *See Perry*, 460 U.S. at 46 (“The government violates the First Amendment when it denies access to a speaker solely on the basis of the speaker’s viewpoint.”).

III. MILFORD’S EXCLUSION IS JUSTIFIED BY THE ESTABLISHMENT CLAUSE.

A. The Club’s Activities Constitute Worship and Religious Instruction.

The Club’s meetings are not “purely moral and character development,” but are instead “quintessentially religious.” *Good News Club v. Milford Cent. Sch.*, 202 F.3d 502, 511 (2d Cir. 2000). The Club’s own description confirms that its meetings include “singing songs, hearing [a] Bible lesson and memorizing scripture,” followed by prayer. *Id.* at 506. These activities are indistinguishable from worship. *See Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 269 n.6, 102 S.Ct. 274 n.6 (“[S]inging hymns, reading scripture, and teaching biblical principles” are forms of speech, but when conducted as worship, they are not secular education). Milford’s policy permissibly excludes such activities to avoid violating the Establishment Clause. *See Lamb’s Chapel v. Ctr. Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist.*, 508 U.S. 384, 395 (1993) (recognizing that a school may “preserve the building for its intended purpose as a school” and avoid “the appearance of school endorsement of religion”).

B. Milford’s Policy Is Neutral and Generally Applicable.

Milford’s policy is neutral toward religion and applies equally to all groups. *See McDaniel v. Paty*, 435 U.S. 618, 628 (1978) (plurality opinion) (“[T]he Establishment Clause does not license government to treat religion and those who teach or practice it, simply by virtue of their status, as subversive of American ideals and therefore subject to unique disabilities.”). The policy does not single out religion for disfavored treatment; it simply excludes all worship and religious instruction. *See Epperson v. Arkansas*, 393 U.S. 97, 106 (1968) (“[T]he State’s policy . . . is neutral with respect to religion.”). Because the policy is neutral and generally applicable, it withstands Establishment Clause scrutiny. *See Walz v. Tax Comm’n*, 397 U.S. 664, 674 (1970) (recognizing that “[t]he general applicability of a statute is a significant factor in its constitutionality”).

IV. THE CLUB’S RELIANCE ON *LAMB’S CHAPEL* IS MISPLACED.

The Club misreads *Lamb’s Chapel*, which held that a school’s refusal to allow a religious group to show films on family values violated the Free Speech Clause because the forum was open to other groups discussing family values. 508 U.S. 384, 394 (1993). Here, by contrast, Milford has never opened its forum to religious worship or instruction. *See id.* at 395 (“[T]he District’s property was not generally opened to public use by all groups.”). *Lamb’s Chapel* does not compel Milford to permit worship-like activities in a forum closed to such speech. *See Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 272 (holding that a university could exclude religious worship from its forum because it had not historically permitted such activities).

**CONCLUSION**

Milford’s policy is viewpoint neutral, reasonable, and justified by the Establishment Clause. The judgment below should be affirmed.
