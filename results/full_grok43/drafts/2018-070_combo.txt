ARGUMENT

The Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act, 43 U.S.C. § 1333(a)(2)(A), incorporates the civil and criminal laws of adjacent States only “to the extent that they are applicable and not inconsistent with this subchapter or with other Federal laws.” This language limits assimilation to circumstances in which federal law leaves a gap. See Rodrigue v. Aetna Cas. & Sur. Co., 395 U.S. 352, 355 (1969) (federal law governs the outer Continental Shelf “to the same extent as if” it were an area of exclusive federal jurisdiction).

The same principle governs federal enclaves. State law is assimilated only where Congress has not supplied a complete rule. Chicago, R.I. & P. Ry. Co. v. McGlinn, 114 U.S. 542, 546 (1885) (“the laws of the State . . . cease to be operative . . . so far as they affect the rights of the United States”); James Stewart & Co. v. Sadrakula, 309 U.S. 94, 100 (1940) (state law applies “only to the extent that Congress has not enacted a different rule”); Murray v. Joe Gerrick & Co., 291 U.S. 315, 318 (1934) (state workmen’s compensation statute inapplicable where federal statute supplied the remedy).

Congress intended the same result on the outer Continental Shelf. United States v. Sharpnack, 355 U.S. 286, 294 (1958) (assimilation statutes “adopt state law . . . only in the absence of contrary federal legislation”). The Fair Labor Standards Act supplies comprehensive wage-and-hour regulation, including an express savings clause for more protective state laws that Congress chose not to extend to the Shelf. Because the federal statute is complete, California’s additional requirements are not assimilated. Howard v. Commissioners of Sinking Fund, 344 U.S. 624, 627 (1953) (state law may not “interfere with the operations of the federal government”).

The same rule prevents incorporation of later-enacted or inconsistent state measures. Pacific Coast Dairy, Inc. v. Dep’t of Agric., 318 U.S. 285, 294 (1943) (“the state may not . . . impose its own requirements” where federal law occupies the field); Arlington Hotel Co. v. Fant, 278 U.S. 439, 454 (1929) (state statute enacted after cession “has no application” within the federal enclave). The Ninth Circuit’s contrary holding—that state law applies whenever it is “not inconsistent”—contradicts this settled limitation.

Accordingly, the judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
