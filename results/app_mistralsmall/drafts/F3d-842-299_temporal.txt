**ARGUMENT**

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN CONCLUDING THAT IT HAD SUBJECT-MATTER JURISDICTION UNDER 28 U.S.C. § 1331.

The district court’s exercise of federal question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 was erroneous because the Pressls’ complaint does not present a federal question. “A suit arises under the Constitution and laws of the United States only when the plaintiff’s statement of his own cause of action shows that it is based upon those laws or that Constitution.” *Franchise Tax Bd. of Cal. v. Constr. Laborers Vacation Trust*, 463 U.S. 1, 9 (1983). Here, the Pressls’ complaint rests solely on state-law rights arising from the flowage easement and Virginia property law. The mere fact that APCO operates under a FERC license does not convert the dispute into a federal question. *See Gully v. First Nat’l Bank*, 299 U.S. 109, 116 (1936) (“It is not enough that the defendant asserts some federal question in his answer, or that the plaintiff replies to such assertion.”).

Moreover, the presence of a federal regulatory scheme does not confer federal jurisdiction where the plaintiff’s claim does not implicate the federal interest. *See Shoshone Mining Co. v. Rutter*, 177 U.S. 505, 519 (1900) (“The mere fact that the controversy relates to a matter which may be the subject of federal regulation does not necessarily bring it within the jurisdiction of the federal courts.”). The Pressls’ claim arises from the interpretation of a private easement and does not challenge APCO’s compliance with its FERC license. Thus, the district court lacked jurisdiction under § 1331.

II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN CONCLUDING THAT IT HAD SUBJECT-MATTER JURISDICTION UNDER 16 U.S.C. § 825p.

APCO argued, and the district court agreed, that § 825p confers jurisdiction because the Pressls’ property lies within the project boundary of APCO’s FERC-licensed hydroelectric project. However, § 825p does not grant federal jurisdiction over all disputes arising within a FERC project boundary. Instead, it provides federal jurisdiction only over “any civil action or proceeding arising under [the Federal Power Act].” 16 U.S.C. § 825p (1964). The Pressls’ complaint does not allege a violation of the Federal Power Act or any other federal statute; it merely seeks a declaration of rights under a state-law easement. *See Alabama Power Co. v. Ickes*, 302 U.S. 464, 479 (1938) (“The mere fact that a controversy relates to a matter which may be the subject of federal regulation does not necessarily bring it within the jurisdiction of the federal courts.”).

Furthermore, § 825p does not confer jurisdiction over state-law claims merely because they arise in the shadow of a federally licensed project. *See First Nat’l Bank v. Board of County Comm’rs*, 264 U.S. 250, 254 (1924) (“The jurisdiction of the federal courts is limited to cases and controversies arising under the Constitution and laws of the United States.”). The Pressls’ claim is a state-law dispute about property rights, not a federal regulatory dispute. Thus, the district court lacked jurisdiction under § 825p.

III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING THE PRESSLS’ MOTION TO REMAND.

Because the district court lacked subject-matter jurisdiction under either § 1331 or § 825p, it erred in denying the Pressls’ motion to remand. “If at any time before final judgment it appears that the district court lacks subject matter jurisdiction, the case shall be remanded.” 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c) (1964). The Supreme Court has made clear that remand is mandatory where jurisdiction is lacking. *See Thermtron Prods., Inc. v. Hermansdorfer*, 423 U.S. 336, 343 (1976) (citing prior cases). Here, the district court improperly retained jurisdiction despite the absence of a federal question or a federal cause of action. Accordingly, the denial of the motion to remand was erroneous.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court lacked subject-matter jurisdiction, and the Pressls’ motion to remand should have been granted. The judgment of the district court should be vacated, and the case remanded with instructions to remand to the state court.
