ARGUMENT

A district court's failure to comply with the advice requirement of former Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2) does not furnish a basis for collateral relief when the defendant already knew of his right to appeal and elected not to exercise it. Collateral attack is available only when the asserted error "resulted in a complete miscarriage of justice" or is "inconsistent with the rudimentary demands of fair procedure." Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424, 428 (1962). A Rule 32 violation that leaves the defendant fully informed of his appellate rights produces neither consequence.

This Court has repeatedly declined to permit habeas or § 2255 relief for technical sentencing errors that cause no prejudice. In United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780, 784 (1979), the Court held that a judge's failure to mention a mandatory special parole term during a Rule 11 plea colloquy did not justify collateral relief because the defendant "was not actually prejudiced." The same principle controls here. Once the district court found that Peguero "knew of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing," the omission of the Rule 32 advice caused no prejudice and therefore supplies no ground for vacating the sentence.

The harmless-error doctrine reinforces this conclusion. A constitutional error is harmless on collateral review unless it "had substantial and injurious effect or influence in determining the jury's verdict." Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619, 637 (1993) (quoting Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750, 776 (1946)). An error that leaves the defendant aware of and able to exercise his appellate rights cannot have such an effect. See also United States v. Addonizio, 442 U.S. 178, 184-85 (1979) (limiting collateral review to errors that undermine the fundamental fairness of the proceeding).

The court of appeals correctly applied these standards. Because the record established Peguero's actual knowledge, the Rule 32 violation was harmless and afforded no basis for habeas relief. The judgment should be affirmed.
