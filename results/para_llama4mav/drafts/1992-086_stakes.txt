The Center Moriches Union Free School District's denial of Lamb's Chapel's request to use school facilities for a religious-oriented film series on family values and child rearing constitutes a content-based exclusion of speech in a limited public forum, violating the First Amendment's freedom of speech.

The Supreme Court has established that the extent of permissible governmental regulation of expressive activity on publicly owned property depends on the character of the public property in question. Perry Education Ass'n v. Perry Local Educators' Ass'n, 460 U.S. 37, 44 (1983). The Court has identified three categories of publicly owned property: traditional public forums, limited or designated public forums, and nonpublic forums. Id. at 45-46. The Center Moriches School District facilities fall within the category of limited public forums, as they have been opened for use by certain groups or for the discussion of certain subjects.

In a limited public forum, the government's power to regulate expression is limited, and content-based exclusions are subject to strict scrutiny. See Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund, Inc., 473 U.S. 788, 802 (1985). The School District's policy of excluding religious groups from using its facilities is a content-based exclusion, as it discriminates against speech based on its religious content. The District Court and the Second Circuit erred in holding that the School District's facilities were not open to religious groups by policy or practice.

The Supreme Court's decisions in Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263 (1981), and Board of Education of the Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226 (1990), support Lamb's Chapel's claim. In Widmar, the Court held that a state university could not deny access to university facilities to students who wished to conduct religious meetings on campus, as the university had created a forum generally open for use by student groups. 454 U.S. at 273. Similarly, in Mergens, the Court held that a high school had created a limited open forum by allowing noncurriculum-related student groups to use school facilities, and therefore could not deny access to a Christian club. 496 U.S. at 247.

The School District's exclusion of Lamb's Chapel from using its facilities is not viewpoint-neutral, as it discriminates against religious speech. The film series, "Turn Your Heart Toward Home," is a family-oriented presentation that discusses family values and child rearing from a Christian perspective. The School District's denial of access to Lamb's Chapel is based on the religious content of the speech, rather than any legitimate concern about the use of the facilities.

The Establishment Clause does not require the School District to exclude Lamb's Chapel from using its facilities. In fact, denying access to Lamb's Chapel while allowing other groups to use the facilities could be seen as discriminatory. The School District's policy of excluding religious groups from using its facilities is not necessary to avoid an Establishment Clause violation.

In conclusion, the Center Moriches Union Free School District's denial of Lamb's Chapel's request to use school facilities for a religious-oriented film series constitutes a content-based exclusion of speech in a limited public forum, violating the First Amendment's freedom of speech. The Court should reverse the Second Circuit's decision and hold that the School District's policy of excluding religious groups from using its facilities is unconstitutional.
