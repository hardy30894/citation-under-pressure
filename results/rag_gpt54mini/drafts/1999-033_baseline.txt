**Argument**

The Equal Protection Clause permits a “class of one” claim where the plaintiff alleges that she was intentionally treated differently from others similarly situated and that there was no rational basis for the difference in treatment. *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. 562, 564 (2000) (per curiam). That rule flows from the basic command that equal protection is violated when government draws an irrational distinction between persons who are alike in all relevant respects. See *Railway Express Agency, Inc. v. New York*, 336 U.S. 106, 110 (1949); *Allegheny Pittsburgh Coal Co. v. County Comm’n of Webster County*, 488 U.S. 336, 343-46 (1989).

Petitioner alleged exactly that. The Village conditioned connection to the municipal water supply on her granting a 33-foot easement, even though it required only a 15-foot easement from other property owners. App. __. Those allegations fit squarely within settled equal-protection doctrine. When government “intentionally treats one differently from others similarly situated” without a rational basis, the Constitution is offended. *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. at 564; *Allegheny Pittsburgh*, 488 U.S. at 345.

The court of appeals erred by treating animus or “spite” as a required element. The Equal Protection Clause does not confine its protection to cases of class-based discrimination or to cases where plaintiff can prove subjective ill will. In *Yick Wo v. Hopkins*, 118 U.S. 356, 373-74 (1886), this Court held that a facially neutral law administered with “an evil eye and an unequal hand” violates equal protection. And in *Sioux City Bridge Co. v. Dakota County*, 260 U.S. 441, 445-47 (1923), the Court held that systematic unequal assessment of a single taxpayer, even without class membership, contravened equal protection. These cases show that the Constitution reaches deliberate, irrational singling out.

The Seventh Circuit’s approach also conflicts with *Olech* itself, which rejected the premise that a “class of one” plaintiff must plead membership in a group. 528 U.S. at 564. The Court held that petitioner’s allegation of differential treatment from similarly situated owners, coupled with the absence of any rational basis, stated an equal-protection claim. Id. The Court did not require proof that the Village’s conduct was driven by personal spite. Id. To the contrary, the Court grounded the claim in the absence of a rational justification, not in a showing of animus. Id.; see also *Rinaldi v. Yeager*, 384 U.S. 305, 308-09 (1966) (equal protection forbids irrational discrimination).

The Village may argue that land-use and utility decisions warrant special deference. But rational-basis review still forbids arbitrary distinctions. *New Orleans v. Dukes*, 427 U.S. 297, 303 (1976) (per curiam); *FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc.*, 508 U.S. 307, 313 (1993). Where a plaintiff alleges that the government imposed a uniquely burdensome easement requirement on her alone, while granting others comparable access on less onerous terms, the complaint is not about ordinary discretion; it is about unequal treatment without a rational basis. *Allegheny Pittsburgh*, 488 U.S. at 346; *Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 432, 439-42 (1985).

That principle is especially apt here because petitioner seeks only parity with her neighbors. Equal protection “is essentially a direction that all persons similarly situated should be treated alike.” *Cleburne*, 473 U.S. at 439. The Village did not identify any legitimate reason for demanding an additional 18 feet from petitioner alone. App. __. On a motion to dismiss, that allegation must be accepted as true, and petitioner was entitled to proceed. *Conley v. Gibson*, 355 U.S. 41, 45-46 (1957); *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544, 555-56 (2007).

The judgment below should be reversed. The Equal Protection Clause protects individuals against irrational, differential treatment even when they are not members of a class or group. *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. at 564; *Yick Wo*, 118 U.S. at 373-74; *Allegheny Pittsburgh*, 488 U.S. at 343-46. Petitioner stated a valid claim.
