ARGUMENT

I. THE BOARD ERRED IN HOLDING THAT THE '838 AND '768 APPLICATIONS SUFFICIENTLY INCORPORATED THE '579 APPLICATION BY REFERENCE

The Board's determination that the '838 and '768 applications sufficiently incorporated the '579 application by reference is contrary to law. The incorporation by reference doctrine requires that the incorporating application clearly identify the incorporated material. See Advanced Display Sys., Inc. v. Kent State Univ., 212 F.3d 1272, 1282 (Fed. Cir. 2000) ("To incorporate material by reference, the host document must identify with detailed particularity what specific material it incorporates and clearly indicate where that material is found in the various documents.").

The incorporation statement in the '566 application, which was copied into the subsequent applications, including the '838 and '768 applications, refers to a copending application "filed on the same day as the present application" by Sanjay Mehrotra and Dr. Eliyahou Harari. J.A. 1204. At the time the '566 application was filed, the "present application" referred to the '566 application, and the copending application was the '579 application, which was indeed filed on the same day. However, when the '838 and '768 applications were filed, the "present application" referred to those applications, and the '579 application was no longer "filed on the same day."

This Court's decision in Harari I does not control the outcome here. In Harari I, this Court applied a narrow rule for reviewing incorporation by reference statements at the "initial filing stage." Harari I, 602 F.3d at 1352-53. The Court held that the reasonable examiner, with the benefit of the transmittal sheet and the preliminary amendment, would understand that the copied language from the original '566 application in the '880 application referred to the '579 application. Id. at 1353-54. However, the issue in Harari I was whether the preliminary amendment was new matter, not whether the '838 and '768 applications sufficiently incorporated the '579 application by reference.

The '838 and '768 applications did not provide sufficient information to identify the '579 application. See In re Fouche, 439 F.2d 1227, 1230 (C.C.P.A. 1971) (holding that an incorporation by reference must "clearly identify" the incorporated material). The incorporation statement in those applications was ambiguous, as it referred to an application "filed on the same day as the present application," which was no longer true. Therefore, the Board erred in holding that the '838 and '768 applications sufficiently incorporated the '579 application by reference.

II. THE '880 APPLICATION IS NOT ENTITLED TO THE BENEFIT OF THE '566 APPLICATION'S FILING DATE

Because the '838 and '768 applications did not sufficiently incorporate the '579 application by reference, the '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date under 35 U.S.C. § 120. The '880 application's earliest possible filing date is December 20, 1996, the filing date of the '708 application, which undisputedly discloses the '579 application. As a result, Harari's '880 application does not precede the date of conception for Hollmer's '601 patent, and the Board's judgment against Hollmer should be reversed.

The decision in Lockwood v. American Airlines, Inc., 107 F.3d 1565 (Fed. Cir. 1997), supports this conclusion. In Lockwood, this Court held that an application is not entitled to the benefit of an earlier filing date if the intervening applications do not comply with the continuity requirements of 35 U.S.C. § 120. Id. at 1571. Here, the '838 and '768 applications did not comply with those requirements because they did not sufficiently incorporate the '579 application by reference.

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the Board's judgment and hold that Harari's '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date.
