ARGUMENT

Petitioner lacks standing to challenge her conviction on Tenth Amendment grounds. In Tennessee Electric Power Co. v. Tennessee Valley Authority, 306 U.S. 118 (1939), private utilities sought to enjoin federal operations as violative of the Tenth Amendment. The Court held that, absent objection by the States, the utilities “have no standing in this suit to raise any question under the [Tenth] Amendment.” Id. at 144.

The same rule governs here. The Tenth Amendment “states but a truism that all is retained which has not been surrendered.” United States v. Darby, 312 U.S. 100, 124 (1941). It “deals with the distribution of power between the States and the national government,” and “is not a source of private rights.” Ibid. Only the States or their officers may invoke it to protect their sovereign interests. See Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447, 485-486 (1923) (Frothingham v. Mellon).

Petitioner is a private citizen prosecuted under a federal criminal statute. She asserts no personal right or immunity; she claims only that Congress exceeded its enumerated powers. Such a claim “is not a cause of action that a private person may assert.” See Carter v. Carter Coal Co., 298 U.S. 238, 289-290 (1936); United States v. Butler, 297 U.S. 1, 68-69 (1936). The injury she alleges is “not to [her]self as an individual but to the sovereignty of the state of which [she] is a citizen.” See Helvering v. Davis, 301 U.S. 619, 640 (1937).

Missouri v. Holland, 252 U.S. 416 (1920), confirms that treaty-implementing legislation rests on the Treaty Power and the Necessary and Proper Clause, U.S. Const. art. I, § 8, cl. 18; art. II, § 2, cl. 2. When Congress acts pursuant to a valid treaty, “there can be no dispute about the validity of the statute.” Id. at 432. Petitioner may not litigate the federal-state balance that the States themselves have not contested. See Ashwander v. Tennessee Valley Authority, 297 U.S. 288, 347-348 (1936) (Brandeis, J., concurring); Screws v. United States, 325 U.S. 91, 109 (1945).

Because petitioner has no standing to press the Tenth Amendment objection, the judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
