ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING THE ASSERTED CLAIMS THE BENEFIT OF THE FILING DATE OF THE ORIGINAL APPLICATION

The district court concluded that the asserted claims of the '658 and '400 patents were not entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the Original Application because the earlier application did not provide a written description of the invention claimed in the asserted patents, as required by 35 U.S.C. § 112. However, this conclusion is not supported by the law.

The written description requirement is satisfied if the original application "reasonably conveys to one skilled in the art that the inventor had possession of the claimed subject matter as of the filing date." In re Ruschig, 379 F.2d 990, 995 (C.C.P.A. 1967). The district court erred in focusing solely on the new language added to the 2000 CIP Application, rather than considering whether the Original Application provided a sufficient written description of the invention.

The Original Application disclosed a vending machine for vending telecommunications channel access to a customer, which is the same invention claimed in the asserted patents. See United States v. Adams, 383 U.S. 39, 49 (1966) (holding that an invention is adequately described if the specification "discloses the means for carrying out the invention"). The fact that the Original Application did not contain the exact language of the asserted claims is not dispositive. See Crown Cork & Seal Co. v. Aluminum Stopper Co., 108 F. 845, 850 (4th Cir. 1901) (holding that an applicant is not required to "describe his invention in the exact language of his claims").

Therefore, the district court erred in denying the asserted claims the benefit of the filing date of the Original Application.

II. THE ASSERTED CLAIMS ARE ENTITLED TO THE BENEFIT OF THE FILING DATE OF THE ORIGINAL APPLICATION, AND THUS ARE NOT ANTICIPATED BY THE MOBILSTAR NETWORK

As established above, the asserted claims are entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the Original Application, which is February 6, 1997. The MobileStar Network was not publicly used, sold, or offered for sale until after June 15, 1999, more than two years after the Original Application filing date. Therefore, the MobileStar Network does not constitute § 102(b) prior art, and the asserted claims are not anticipated.

The district court's grant of summary judgment was based on its incorrect conclusion that the asserted claims were not entitled to the benefit of the Original Application filing date. As a result, the grant of summary judgment should be reversed.

In Hazeltine Corp. v. General Electric Co., 95 F.2d 881, 883 (2d Cir. 1938), the court held that a patent is not invalid as anticipated if the prior art reference was not publicly available before the patent's priority date. Similarly, in this case, the MobileStar Network was not publicly available before the Original Application filing date, and therefore does not anticipate the asserted claims.

The judgment of the district court should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
