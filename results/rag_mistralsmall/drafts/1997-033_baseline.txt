**ARGUMENT**

I. **Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial; Section 1404(a) Cannot Be Invoked to Defeat That Command**

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation (the “Panel”) is expressly directed by §1407(a) to “remand” any transferred action “at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. §1407(a). This Court has repeatedly recognized that §1407(a) is a “specific and detailed statute” that “sets the outer limits of the Panel’s authority.” *Van Dusen v. Barrack*, 376 U.S. 612, 638 (1964). The statute’s remand command is categorical: “[e]ach action so transferred shall be remanded by the panel.” *Id.* at 639 (quoting §1407(a)). The Panel’s rule purporting to authorize a transferee court to “transfer” a case to itself for trial under §1404(a) directly contravenes this statutory mandate. *See* Rule 14(b), Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation.

This Court has never sanctioned such a circumvention of §1407(a). To the contrary, it has emphasized that §1404(a) “is not a mechanism for circumventing the venue and transfer provisions of §1407.” *Stewart Org., Inc. v. Ricoh Corp.*, 487 U.S. 22, 33 (1988). Section 1404(a) permits transfer “to any other district or division where [the action] might have been brought,” but it does not empower a transferee court to retain a case for trial after the Panel’s pretrial proceedings have concluded. *See id.* at 27-28. The text of §1407(a) is unambiguous: once pretrial proceedings are complete, the Panel “shall” remand the case to the transferor district. *SeeVan Dusen*, 376 U.S. at 639.

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary conclusion—that a transferee court may invoke §1404(a) to “transfer” a case to itself for trial—rests on a misreading of *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), and its progeny. *See* 61 F.3d at 915. But those cases ignore the plain text of §1407(a). As Judge Kozinski observed in dissent, the courts have “simply disregarded the statutory mandate” by allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial. *Id.* at 916 (Kozinski, J., dissenting). This Court should reject that practice as inconsistent with §1407(a)’s clear command.

II. **Permitting Transferee Courts to Retain Cases for Trial Undermines the Statutory Scheme and Violates the Right to Forum Choice**

The statutory scheme of §1407 reflects Congress’s judgment that multidistrict litigation should be limited to pretrial coordination. *SeeVan Dusen*, 376 U.S. at 638-39. Once common factual issues are resolved, the case must be remanded to the transferor district for trial. *Id.* This preserves plaintiffs’ traditional right to choose their forum, a right this Court has repeatedly recognized. *See id.* at 636-37; *Ferens v. John Deere Co.*, 494 U.S. 516, 529 (1990).

Allowing a transferee court to invoke §1404(a) to retain a case for trial subverts this scheme. It permits a court with no connection to the underlying dispute—other than its role in pretrial coordination—to arrogate the plaintiff’s forum choice. *See* 61 F.3d at 917 (Kozinski, J., dissenting). This is particularly problematic where, as here, the transferee court lacks familiarity with the case and the parties have already been forced to litigate in a remote forum for years. *Id.* at 918.

The Panel’s rule and the lower courts’ practice also invite forum shopping by transferee courts. *See id.* at 916. This undermines the neutrality of the judicial process and frustrates the purpose of §1407. This Court should reject such an unseemly practice. *Id.*

III. **Section 1404(a) Does Not Authorize the Transferee Court’s “Transfer” to Itself**

Section 1404(a) permits transfer “to any other district or division where [the action] might have been brought.” 28 U.S.C. §1404(a). It does not authorize a transferee court to retain a case for trial. *SeeStewart Org.*, 487 U.S. at 27-28. The statute’s text is clear: it applies only to actions “brought” in a district, not to actions retained for trial after pretrial proceedings are complete. *See id.*

The lower courts’ reliance on *Pfizer* and its progeny is misplaced. Those cases erroneously conflate the Panel’s authority under §1407(a) with a transferee court’s authority under §1404(a). *SeePfizer*, 447 F.2d at 124-25. But §1407(a) and §1404(a) serve distinct purposes. Section 1407(a) authorizes the Panel to transfer cases for pretrial coordination; §1404(a) permits transfer to a more convenient forum for trial. *SeeVan Dusen*, 376 U.S. at 638-39. The Panel’s rule purporting to authorize a transferee court to “transfer” a case to itself for trial conflates these distinct authorities. This Court should reject that conflation as inconsistent with the statutory scheme. *SeeStewart Org.*, 487 U.S. at 33.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should reverse the judgment below and hold that §1407(a) mandates remand to the transferor district for trial and that §1404(a) cannot be invoked to defeat that command.
