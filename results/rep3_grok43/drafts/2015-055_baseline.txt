**ARGUMENT**

**I. Johnson v. United States, 135 S. Ct. 2551 (2015), announced a substantive rule that applies retroactively on collateral review.**

A new rule applies retroactively to cases on collateral review if it is substantive. Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, 310 (1989) (plurality opinion). Substantive rules “alter[] the range of conduct or the class of persons that the law punishes.” Schriro v. Summerlin, 542 U.S. 348, 353 (2004). They “necessarily carry a significant risk that a defendant stands convicted of an act that the law does not make criminal or faces a punishment that the law cannot impose upon him.” Id. (internal quotation marks omitted). Procedural rules, by contrast, “regulate only the manner of determining the defendant’s culpability.” Id. at 353.

Johnson held the ACCA residual clause unconstitutionally vague because it “denie[d] fair notice” and “invite[d] arbitrary enforcement.” 135 S. Ct. at 2557. The clause required courts to imagine the “ordinary case” of a crime and assess its “potential risk,” an inquiry that “tie[d] the judicial assessment of risk to a judicially imagined abstraction.” Id. at 2557-58. After Johnson, a prior conviction qualifies as an ACCA predicate only if it satisfies the elements clause or the enumerated-offense clause. The rule therefore “narrow[s] the class of people who are eligible” for ACCA’s fifteen-year mandatory minimum. Schriro, 542 U.S. at 352.

Because Johnson “place[s] particular conduct or persons . . . beyond the State’s power to punish,” it is substantive. Id. at 353. It is not a procedural rule governing “the manner of determining” whether a defendant’s conduct falls within the statute. Id. Accordingly, the rule applies retroactively to cases on collateral review. Bousley v. United States, 523 U.S. 614, 620-21 (1998).

**II. Welch’s Florida robbery conviction is no longer a violent felony under the ACCA.**

Welch’s 1996 conviction was counted solely under the residual clause. After Johnson, that clause is invalid. The conviction therefore cannot serve as an ACCA predicate. Welch is entitled to resentencing without the statutory minimum.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
