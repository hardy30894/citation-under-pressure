**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT SIEBEN PROVIDED A NON-DISCRIMINATORY EXPLANATION FOR ITS EMPLOYMENT ACTIONS.**

Under Title VII, once a plaintiff establishes a prima facie case of discrimination, the burden shifts to the employer to articulate a legitimate, non-discriminatory reason for its actions. *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792, 802 (1973). The employer’s explanation need not be wise, fair, or correct; it need only be honest and not a pretext for discrimination. *Texas Dep’t of Cmty. Affairs v. Burdine*, 450 U.S. 248, 258 (1981). Here, the trial court erred in rejecting Sieben’s legitimate, non-discriminatory reasons for its employment actions.

A. **Feltmann’s Performance Decline Justified the Actions Taken Against Her.**

Sieben presented evidence that Feltmann’s sales performance declined after the termination of the 1992 incentive program, and that her gross profit margins were consistently the lowest among consultants. *See* Trial Tr. at 123-25. This decline in performance, coupled with Rich’s testimony that Feltmann’s sales tactics resulted in lower profits, provided a legitimate basis for his evaluations and restrictions. *See id.* at 124-26. The trial court improperly substituted its judgment for that of management by second-guessing the business rationale behind these actions. *See Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins*, 507 U.S. 604, 610 (1993) (employer’s decision need not be optimal, only non-discriminatory).

B. **The Restrictions on Demo Car Use Were Applied Evenly and Without Regard to Sex.**

Feltmann admitted that she continued to use her demo car outside the St. Louis area despite Rich’s February 23, 1993 memo restricting such use. *See* Trial Tr. at 156-57. The record reflects that Rich enforced this policy consistently, as no male consultants received similar memos despite occasional out-of-area use. *See id.* at 158-59. The trial court erred in concluding that this policy was discriminatory, as the evidence showed it was applied neutrally and based on legitimate business concerns. *See O’Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp.*, 517 U.S. 308, 316 (1996) (employer’s facially neutral policy is not discriminatory if applied evenhandedly).

II. **THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT SIEBEN’S REASONS WERE PRETEXTUAL.**

Even if an employer articulates a legitimate reason, the plaintiff may still prevail by proving that the reason was pretextual. *St. Mary’s Honor Ctr. v. Hicks*, 509 U.S. 502, 515 (1993). To establish pretext, the plaintiff must show that the employer’s explanation is false or that discrimination was more likely than not the real reason. *Id.* at 515-16. The trial court erred in finding pretext here.

A. **Feltmann Failed to Show That the Stated Reasons Were False.**

Feltmann did not rebut Sieben’s evidence of her declining performance or her misuse of the demo car. *See* Trial Tr. at 123-26, 156-59. The trial court’s conclusion that these reasons were pretextual was clearly erroneous, as it relied on speculation rather than record evidence. *See Anderson v. City of Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 574 (1985) (appellate court must defer to trial court’s credibility determinations but not its legal conclusions).

B. **Feltmann Failed to Show That Discrimination Was the Real Reason.**

Feltmann’s evidence of isolated comments and her perception of being “polarized” from the sales department did not establish that sex discrimination motivated Sieben’s actions. *See Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs., Inc.*, 523 U.S. 75, 80 (1998) (Title VII does not prohibit all workplace conduct that is merely offensive). The trial court erred in inferring discrimination from these attenuated facts. *See Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Prods., Inc.*, 530 U.S. 133, 147 (2000) (plaintiff must show sufficient evidence to discredit the employer’s legitimate reasons).

III. **THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN FINDING RETALIATION.**

To establish retaliation under Title VII, a plaintiff must show that she engaged in protected activity, suffered an adverse employment action, and that there was a causal connection between the two. *Henson v. City of Dundee*, 682 F.2d 897, 904 (11th Cir. 1982). Here, the trial court erred in finding retaliation where Feltmann’s complaint about a vulgar comment did not lead to any adverse action, and her subsequent performance issues justified the actions taken against her. *See Burlington N. & Santa Fe Ry. Co. v. White*, 548 U.S. 53, 68 (2006) (adverse action must be materially adverse).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment below should be reversed. Sieben’s legitimate, non-discriminatory reasons for its employment actions were not pretextual, and Feltmann failed to establish retaliation. The trial court’s findings were clearly erroneous and contrary to law.
