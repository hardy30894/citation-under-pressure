**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE RELEASE DOES NOT BAR APPELLANT’S CLAIMS BECAUSE ITS LANGUAGE DOES NOT EXTEND TO THEM.**

Appellant’s claims are not barred by the 1988 release, which unambiguously covers only claims “arising from or with respect to the decision of the [OCC] to close the Bank on June 21, 1985.” *See FDIC v. Meyer*, 510 U.S. 471, 476 (1994) (release construed “as written, without resort to parol evidence”); *United States v. Seckinger*, 397 U.S. 203, 210 (1970) (release interpreted “to mean what it says”).

The release’s scope is limited to claims tied to the OCC’s closure decision. Appellant’s claims—unjust enrichment for post-insolvency interest, breach of fiduciary duty in asset sales, corporate waste for improper expense allocations, and inadequate accounting—arise from the FDIC’s post-closure administration of the receivership, not the OCC’s decision to close the Bank. *See FDIC v. McSweeney*, 976 F.2d 53, 56 (2d Cir. 1992) (release did not bar claims arising from post-closure conduct). The release’s plain text does not encompass these distinct claims, and courts “will not read into a release language that is not there.” *Meyer*, 510 U.S. at 476.

**II. THE STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS DOES NOT BAR APPELLANT’S CLAIMS BECAUSE IT DID NOT ACCRUE UNTIL THE RECEIVERSHIP TERMINATED.**

New York’s six-year limitations period for unjust enrichment, breach of fiduciary duty, and corporate waste under N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 213(7) did not begin to run until the FDIC’s receivership terminated on November 1, 1995. *See In re World Trade Ctr. Disaster Site Litig.*, 54 N.Y.3d 404, 413 (2020) (claim accrues when plaintiff “knows or reasonably should know of the injury”); *Simcuski v. Saeli*, 44 N.Y.2d 442, 448-49 (1978) (fiduciary duty claim accrues when “the wrongdoing is sufficiently brought to [plaintiff’s] attention”).

Appellant’s claims stem from the FDIC’s post-insolvency conduct—post-receivership interest payments, asset sales, expense allocations, and accounting—none of which were ascertainable until the receivership ended. *See FDIC v. Keating*, 28 F.3d 1315, 1320 (9th Cir. 1994) (claims accrued when receivership closed). The FDIC’s argument that the limitations period began earlier is foreclosed by New York law, which ties accrual to the plaintiff’s ability to assert the claim. *See World Trade Ctr. Disaster Site Litig.*, 54 N.Y.3d at 413.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court erred in granting summary judgment to the FDIC. The release does not bar Appellant’s claims, and the statute of limitations did not begin to run until the receivership terminated. The judgment below should be reversed.
