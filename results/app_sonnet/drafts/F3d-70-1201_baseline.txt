# ARGUMENT

## I. RULE 407 DOES NOT BAR EVIDENCE OF SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES IN STRICT PRODUCTS LIABILITY ACTIONS BROUGHT UNDER FLORIDA LAW.

Federal Rule of Evidence 407 provides that evidence of subsequent remedial measures is inadmissible "to prove negligence or culpable conduct in connection with the event." Fed. R. Evid. 407. By its terms, Rule 407 addresses only proof of "negligence or culpable conduct"—a standard rooted in fault-based liability theories. Strict products liability, by contrast, does not turn on the defendant's conduct at all, but on whether the product itself was defective and unreasonably dangerous when it left the manufacturer's control. Under Florida law, "the plaintiff in a product liability case need not prove specific acts of negligence" because "the doctrine of strict liability applies where the article is dangerously defective." *West v. Caterpillar Tractor Co.*, 336 So. 2d 80, 87 (Fla. 1976). Because strict liability focuses on the condition of the product rather than the fault of the manufacturer, evidence of post-accident design changes bears directly on whether the product's design was defective, not on whether Morbark was "negligent" or engaged in "culpable conduct" within the meaning of Rule 407.

This distinction has been recognized by other courts confronting the question. In *Ault v. International Harvester Co.*, 528 P.2d 1148 (Cal. 1975), the California Supreme Court held that the rationale for excluding subsequent remedial measures in negligence actions—encouraging manufacturers to make improvements without fear that doing so will be used against them—does not translate to the strict liability context, because "the fact that a manufacturer, either by adopting a new design or by a modification of an old one, has improved a product... is not particularly probative of whether the product... was negligently designed." *Id.* at 1153. The court reasoned that manufacturers, driven by market forces and potential liability exposure regardless of the evidentiary rule, will continue to improve their products, and thus the exclusionary policy loses its force in strict liability. *Id.*

While the Eleventh Circuit has not yet resolved this question, the Advisory Committee Note to Rule 407 itself signals that the Rule's exclusionary policy was drafted with negligence actions in mind, and courts applying Rule 407 in diversity cases governed by state substantive law should not mechanically extend a negligence-based evidentiary bar to a distinct theory of liability that Florida has deliberately structured to avoid inquiry into manufacturer conduct. *See West*, 336 So. 2d at 87. This Court should hold, consistent with *Ault*, that Rule 407 does not exclude evidence of subsequent remedial measures when offered to prove that a product was defectively designed under a strict liability theory.

## II. EVEN IF RULE 407 APPLIES, THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN INSTRUCTING THE JURY TO DISREGARD EVIDENCE OF SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES THAT WAS PROPERLY ADMITTED TO IMPEACH MORBARK'S COUNSEL'S MISLEADING SUGGESTIONS.

Rule 407 excludes evidence of subsequent remedial measures only when offered to prove negligence or culpable conduct; the Rule does not bar such evidence when offered for another purpose, including impeachment. Fed. R. Evid. 407 (evidence of subsequent measures "does not require the exclusion of the evidence when offered for another purpose, such as proving ownership, control, or feasibility of precautionary measures, if controverted, or impeachment"). This Court has recognized that a party who "opens the door" to a subject may not later invoke an evidentiary shield to prevent the opposing party from correcting a misleading impression created by that party's own conduct. *Cf. United States v. Winston*, 447 F.2d 1236, 1240 (D.C. Cir. 1971) (party who introduces misleading evidence cannot object when opponent introduces evidence to rebut the misleading inference).

Here, Morbark's counsel affirmatively and repeatedly suggested to the jury—through opening statement and through cross-examination of Infinger—that the seventeen-inch infeed chute remained unchanged and continued in widespread governmental use, including by the Army Corps of Engineers following Hurricane Andrew. R4-141-22. Counsel then elicited testimony from Infinger that the City of DeFuniak Springs "still" used "that Morbark chipper machine." R4-141-44. This testimony created a false and misleading impression that the machine's design, including the chute length at issue, remained in unaltered, continued, and implicitly safe use.

The district court correctly recognized that Morbark had "opened the door" to this issue and that "it's certainly fair for the other side to point out that there have been modifications to that chipper." R4-141-56. The court permitted Wood's counsel to elicit testimony that the chute had, in fact, been lengthened—testimony offered not to prove negligence or defect, but solely to impeach the false impression left by Morbark's own examination. This is precisely the "impeachment" purpose contemplated by Rule 407's plain text. Having properly admitted this evidence for that limited purpose, the district court's subsequent instruction directing the jury to disregard the testimony entirely was error, because it deprived Wood of the corrective effect the evidence was designed to achieve and left the jury with the false impression Morbark's counsel had created. See *Fed. R. Evid. 407* (evidence "when offered for another purpose... impeachment" is not excluded).

## III. THE DISTRICT COURT'S ERROR WAS NOT HARMLESS AND REQUIRES A NEW TRIAL.

The erroneous curative instruction directly undermined Wood's ability to rebut Morbark's central defense theme—that the seventeen-inch chute was safe, adequate, and endorsed by continued governmental use. Because this misleading impression went to the heart of the design defect claim, the error affected Wood's substantial rights and was not harmless. A new trial is required.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the judgment of the district court and remand for a new trial.
