**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE IRS MUST INDIVIDUALLY ASSESS EACH PARTNER TO EXTEND THE STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS FOR COLLECTION**

The Ninth Circuit correctly held that the IRS’s assessment against the partnership did not extend the limitations period for collecting from the individual partners. The plain text of the Internal Revenue Code requires an assessment against a specific taxpayer to trigger the 10-year collection period under § 6502(a). *See* 26 U.S.C. § 6203 (requiring an assessment to be recorded against the "taxpayer"); *United States v. Coson*, 286 F.2d 453, 458 (9th Cir. 1961) (holding that a lien cannot attach to an individual’s property absent an assessment against that individual). The IRS’s argument that a partnership assessment suffices for partners ignores the statutory definition of "taxpayer," which includes individuals separately. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14) (defining "taxpayer" to include "an individual").

This Court has long recognized that assessments must identify the taxpayer. *Coson*, 286 F.2d at 458. The IRS’s failure to assess the partners individually within the three-year window under § 6501(a) bars collection. *Id.* The IRS’s reliance on *United States v. Wright*, 57 F.3d 561 (7th Cir. 1995), is misplaced because Wright involved assessments against both the partnership *and* the partners. *Wright*, 57 F.3d at 562. Here, no such individualized assessments occurred.

**II. STATE PARTNERSHIP LAW DOES NOT CREATE AN ENFORCEABLE CLAIM WITHOUT A JUDGMENT**

The IRS alternatively argues that California law’s joint and several liability of partners obviates the need for an assessment. However, California law requires a judgment against a partner before their assets may be reached. *Cal. Corp. Code § 16307(c)*; *Witkin, Summary of California Law* § 60V. The IRS obtained no such judgment, and the limitations period expired. *See* 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a). Thus, the IRS lacks an enforceable claim under § 502(b)(1) of the Bankruptcy Code. *See United States v. LTV Corp.*, 30 F.3d 303, 306 (9th Cir. 1994) (disallowing untimely claims).

**III. THE NINTH CIRCUIT’S DECISION DOES NOT CONFLICT WITH OTHER CIRCUITS**

The IRS cites cases from other circuits allowing collection from partners without individual assessments. *See, e.g., United States v. Western Products, Ltd.*, 168 F.Supp.2d 84 (S.D.N.Y. 2001). However, those cases involved partnerships governed by laws permitting direct collection from partners, unlike California’s requirement of a judgment. *Id.* at 91. The Ninth Circuit’s decision is consistent with its precedent and the Code’s text.

**CONCLUSION**

The IRS’s failure to assess the partners individually and its lack of a judgment against them render its claims unenforceable. The Ninth Circuit’s decision should be affirmed.
