**ARGUMENT**

I. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DISMISSING LUCERO’S CLAIMS AS MOOT WHERE HIS INDIVIDUAL CLAIMS WERE NOT MOOT AND CLASS CERTIFICATION WAS PENDING.**

The district court erred in dismissing Lucero’s claims for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. A case is not moot where the named plaintiff retains a personal stake in the litigation, even if the defendant tenders an offer of judgment satisfying the plaintiff’s individual claim. *United States Parole Comm’n v. Geraghty*, 445 U.S. 388, 401-02 (1980) (holding that a named plaintiff in a class action retains a personal stake sufficient to maintain an appeal even after the named plaintiff’s individual claim becomes moot, so long as the class certification question remains live). The Supreme Court has long recognized that a plaintiff’s personal stake in a controversy is not extinguished merely because the defendant offers to satisfy the plaintiff’s claim. *See id.* at 397 (explaining that a controversy remains justiciable where the defendant’s voluntary cessation does not guarantee against recurrence). The same principle applies with equal force where, as here, the defendant tenders an offer of judgment under Rule 68 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure. *See id.* at 402-03 (distinguishing between mootness resulting from a defendant’s voluntary cessation and mootness resulting from a plaintiff’s acceptance of a settlement offer).

The district court’s reliance on *Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co.*, 590 F.3d 1134 (10th Cir. 2009), is misplaced. In *Clark*, the Tenth Circuit dismissed the named plaintiff’s claims as moot after the defendant tendered an offer of judgment for the full amount of the plaintiff’s individual claim and the plaintiff failed to move for class certification before the offer was accepted. *Id.* at 1136-38. Unlike the plaintiff in *Clark*, Lucero timely filed his motion for class certification and supported it with a detailed memorandum. *See* Aplt. App. 61-85. The district court’s dismissal therefore contravenes the principle that a named plaintiff’s personal stake persists where class certification is pending. *See Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 402-03.

II. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO RECOGNIZE THAT LUCERO’S MOTION FOR CLASS CERTIFICATION RELATES BACK TO THE FILING OF HIS COMPLAINT.**

The district court further erred in failing to recognize that Lucero’s motion for class certification relates back to the filing of his complaint. The Supreme Court has held that a motion for class certification relates back to the filing of the complaint for purposes of tolling the statute of limitations. *See Crown, Cork & Seal Co. v. Parker*, 462 U.S. 345, 354 (1983) (explaining that the relation-back doctrine ensures that the commencement of a class action tolls the applicable limitations period for all putative class members). The same principle applies to mootness analysis in the class action context. *See Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 402-03 (recognizing that the relation-back doctrine preserves the named plaintiff’s personal stake in the litigation where class certification is pending).

The district court’s refusal to apply the relation-back doctrine here frustrates the remedial purposes of the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (“FDCPA”) and the New Mexico Collection Agency Regulatory Act. *See 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)* (authorizing private enforcement of the FDCPA); *N.M. Stat. Ann. § 61-1-1 et seq.* (establishing a private right of action for violations of the New Mexico Collection Agency Regulatory Act). Permitting defendants to moot class actions by tendering nominal offers to named plaintiffs would eviscerate these statutory schemes. *See Deposit Guar. Nat’l Bank, Jackson v. Roper*, 445 U.S. 326, 339 (1980) (recognizing that the relation-back doctrine prevents defendants from defeating class actions through premature settlement offers).

III. **THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSIDER THE IMPACT OF LUCERO’S PENDING CLASS CERTIFICATION MOTION ON HIS PERSONAL STAKE IN THE LITIGATION.**

Finally, the district court erred in failing to consider the impact of Lucero’s pending class certification motion on his personal stake in the litigation. The Supreme Court has recognized that a named plaintiff in a class action retains a personal stake sufficient to maintain an appeal even after the named plaintiff’s individual claim becomes moot, so long as the class certification question remains live. *Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 401-02. The same principle applies where, as here, the named plaintiff’s individual claim is satisfied but the class certification motion is pending. *See id.* at 402-03.

The district court’s dismissal of Lucero’s claims therefore contravenes the principle that a named plaintiff’s personal stake in a class action persists where class certification is pending. *See id.* The district court’s order should be reversed, and the case should be remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.
