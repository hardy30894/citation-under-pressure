The school’s exclusion of the Good News Club’s proposed meetings did not abridge the Club’s free-speech rights. Milford’s Community Use Policy opens its facilities to a limited public forum for “social, civic and recreational meetings” and other uses “pertaining to the welfare of the community,” but expressly excludes “religious purposes.” 202 F.3d at 504-05. In a limited public forum, the State may impose restrictions that are “reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum” and that do not discriminate on the basis of viewpoint. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of Univ. of Va., 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995); Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist., 508 U.S. 384, 392-93 (1993).

The Club’s activities—singing songs whose lyrics refer to Jesus Christ, memorizing scripture, hearing Bible lessons, receiving invitations to accept Christ as personal Savior, and praying—constitute “religious instruction and prayer” rather than the discussion of “secular subject matter from a religious viewpoint.” 202 F.3d at 507, 510. Milford has permitted other organizations to address moral and character development without religious worship or instruction. The distinction drawn by the policy is therefore one of subject matter, not viewpoint. See Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263, 269 n.6 (1981) (recognizing that “religious worship” may be treated differently from “religious speech” discussing otherwise permissible topics).

Even if the exclusion implicated viewpoint, it was justified by the Establishment Clause. Permitting the Club’s meetings would have required Milford to sponsor “quintessentially religious” activity on school premises immediately after the school day, creating at least the appearance of official endorsement of a particular faith. See Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, 612-13 (1971). The district court and court of appeals correctly concluded that the school’s interest in avoiding that endorsement supplied an adequate justification for the content-based restriction. 202 F.3d at 512-13.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
