The district court's grant of summary judgment to Defendants United Transportation Union ("UTU") and CSX Transportation, Inc. ("CSX") on Plaintiff Kenneth Smoot's claims of conspiracy in violation of the Railway Labor Act ("RLA"), 45 U.S.C. § 153, was improper. The RLA requires that a union fairly represent its members in grievance proceedings. Steele v. Louisville & Nashville R. Co., 323 U.S. 192, 204 (1944). A union breaches its duty of fair representation when its actions are "arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith." Vaca v. Sipes, 386 U.S. 171, 190 (1967). 

Here, the record shows that UTU representative Robert Earley may have acted in bad faith during the executive session of Public Law Board No. 3882 ("PLB"), potentially breaching UTU's duty of fair representation. The taping of the executive session by Smoot revealed that Earley may have argued against Smoot's position, contrary to UTU's official stance. This raises a genuine issue of material fact regarding UTU's representation of Smoot.

Furthermore, the district court's award of damages and attorneys' fees to Defendants under the Federal Wiretap Act (the "Act"), 18 U.S.C. § 2510 et seq., was also erroneous. The Act prohibits the interception of oral communications, but exempts situations where one party to the communication has given prior consent. 18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d). As established in Rathbun v. United States, 355 U.S. 107, 111 (1957), one-party consent is sufficient to render the interception lawful. Smoot, as a party to the communication, arguably had the right to record the executive session.

Additionally, the Act's prohibition on disclosure of intercepted communications does not apply when the disclosure is made in the course of a "lawful" activity. 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(c). Smoot's use of the tape recording in internal union proceedings against Earley was a lawful activity, as it was related to a legitimate grievance against a union representative. See United States v. Polakoff, 112 F.2d 888, 889 (2d Cir. 1940) (holding that a communication is not "unlawfully intercepted" if one party consents). Moreover, the use of the recording in a judicial proceeding is also permissible, as seen in Goldman v. United States, 316 U.S. 129, 132 (1942), where the Court noted that evidence obtained through a recording could be used in a court of law.

The district court's finding that Smoot intentionally recorded the executive session in violation of the Act was not supported by the evidence. As seen in Benanti v. United States, 355 U.S. 96, 100 (1957), the Act is not intended to punish innocent or accidental interceptions. Smoot's actions, leaving the recorders in the room during the executive session, may have been negligent, but not necessarily intentional.

Moreover, the damages awarded to Defendants were excessive. The Supreme Court has held that damages under a similar statute should be "proportional to the actual damages suffered." See United States v. Harriss, 347 U.S. 612, 623 (1954). Here, Defendants failed to demonstrate actual damages resulting from Smoot's actions.

In light of the above, the district court's grant of summary judgment to Defendants and the award of damages and attorneys' fees should be reversed. The case law supports Smoot's position that UTU may have breached its duty of fair representation and that Smoot's actions may not have been in violation of the Act. See Oliphant v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Firemen and Enginemen, 262 F.2d 359, 363 (6th Cir. 1958) (reversing summary judgment where a genuine issue of material fact existed regarding a union's duty of fair representation); NLRB v. Local 294, International Brotherhood of Teamsters, 298 F.2d 105, 108 (2d Cir. 1961) (holding that a union's breach of its duty of fair representation can be grounds for a claim under the NLRA).

In conclusion, the district court's decisions were not supported by the evidence and relevant case law, including Nardone v. United States, 308 U.S. 338 (1939), and Weiss v. United States, 308 U.S. 321 (1939). The grant of summary judgment and the award of damages and attorneys' fees should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
