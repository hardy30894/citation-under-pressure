**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING PETITIONER’S REQUEST FOR AN EVIDENTIARY HEARING ON HIS DUE PROCESS CLAIM ARISING FROM THE PROSECUTOR’S IMPROPER COMMENT ON PETITIONER’S POST-ARRAIGNMENT STATEMENT.**

Petitioner contends that the prosecutor’s post-arraignment statement to him—“he was going to do his damnest to make sure [he] got the death penalty”—and the subsequent use of that statement to impeach him at trial violated his Fourteenth Amendment right to due process. *See Donnelly v. DeChristoforo*, 416 U.S. 637, 643 (1974) (holding that due process is violated when prosecutorial misconduct “so infected the trial with unfairness as to make the resulting conviction a denial of due process”). The district court erred in denying an evidentiary hearing on this claim without first determining whether the claim was exhausted or whether an evidentiary hearing was otherwise warranted under *Townsend v. Sain*, 372 U.S. 293, 312–13 (1963).

The Fifth Circuit has repeatedly emphasized that when a habeas petitioner alleges prosecutorial misconduct that implicates due process, an evidentiary hearing is required if the petitioner presents “factual allegations that, if true, would entitle him to relief.” *See, e.g., Martinez v. Dretke*, 350 F.3d 851, 857 (5th Cir. 2003) (citing *Townsend*, 372 U.S. at 312–13). Here, Petitioner’s allegations—if proven—would demonstrate that the prosecutor’s conduct was not merely improper but fundamentally unfair, as it directly influenced the jury’s assessment of his credibility. *See Napue v. Illinois*, 360 U.S. 264, 269 (1959) (holding that a prosecutor’s knowing use of false evidence violates due process). The district court’s refusal to hold an evidentiary hearing on this claim was therefore reversible error.

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT ON PETITIONER’S INEFFECTIVE ASSISTANCE OF COUNSEL CLAIM WITHOUT HOLDING AN EVIDENTIARY HEARING.**

Petitioner’s ineffective assistance of counsel claim arises from trial counsel’s failure to object to the prosecutor’s improper impeachment of Petitioner with the post-arraignment statement. *See Strickland v. Washington*, 466 U.S. 668, 687 (1984) (establishing the two-prong test for ineffective assistance claims). The district court erred in resolving this claim on summary judgment without affording Petitioner an evidentiary hearing, as factual disputes remain regarding counsel’s performance and prejudice. *See, e.g., United States v. Gonzalez-Lopez*, 548 U.S. 140, 148 (2006) (recognizing that evidentiary hearings are necessary when credibility determinations are required).

The Fifth Circuit has held that summary judgment is inappropriate in habeas cases where “the facts material to the petitioner’s claim were not adequately developed in the state court proceedings.” *See, e.g., Fisher v. Texas*, 169 F.3d 295, 302 (5th Cir. 1999) (citing *Townsend*, 372 U.S. at 313). Here, the record does not conclusively establish whether trial counsel’s failure to object was a strategic decision or a constitutionally deficient omission. An evidentiary hearing is therefore necessary to resolve this claim.

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING PETITIONER’S REQUEST FOR AN EVIDENTIARY HEARING ON HIS CLAIM OF PROSECUTORIAL MISCONDUCT DURING CLOSING ARGUMENT.**

During closing argument, the assistant prosecutor argued that Petitioner should be viewed as “incredulous” because the district attorney had shown “that [Petitioner] was a liar.” This argument improperly invited the jury to infer guilt from Petitioner’s post-arraignment statement rather than the evidence. *See Berger v. United States*, 295 U.S. 78, 88 (1935) (holding that prosecutorial misconduct in closing argument violates due process if it “infects the trial with unfairness”). The district court erred in denying an evidentiary hearing on this claim without first determining whether the claim was properly exhausted or whether an evidentiary hearing was otherwise required under *Townsend*.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court reverse the district court’s denial of habeas relief and remand the case with instructions to hold an evidentiary hearing on Petitioner’s due process and ineffective assistance claims.
