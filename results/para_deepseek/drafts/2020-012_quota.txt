**ARGUMENT**

The Railroad Retirement Board’s decision declining to reopen Salinas’s 2006 benefits claim is not a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit correctly dismissed the petition for lack of jurisdiction, consistent with the overwhelming weight of circuit authority and the plain text of the statute.

**I. Section 355(f) Limits Review to Final Decisions on the Merits After Exhaustion of Administrative Appeals**

Section 355(f) provides that a petitioner “may obtain a review of any final decision of the Board” in the courts of appeals. This language, by its plain terms, restricts jurisdiction to decisions that resolve the merits of a benefits claim after all administrative remedies have been exhausted. *Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 346 F.3d 139, 140 (5th Cir. 2003). A decision not to reopen a prior claim is not a merits determination; it is a discretionary refusal to revisit a final, unappealed decision. *Id.* at 141. The Fifth Circuit in *Roberts* joined the majority of circuits in holding that such reopening decisions are unreviewable. *Id.* (citing *Harris v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 198 F.3d 139, 142 (4th Cir. 1999); *Abbruzzese v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 63 F.3d 972, 974 (10th Cir. 1995); *Gutierrez v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 918 F.2d 567, 570 (6th Cir. 1990); *Steebe v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 708 F.2d 250, 254–55 (7th Cir. 1983)).

**II. The Majority Rule Is Anchored in *Califano v. Sanders* and the Distinction Between Final Decisions and Reopening Decisions**

The Supreme Court’s decision in *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), provides the analytical foundation for the majority rule. In *Sanders*, the Court held that a refusal to reopen a prior Social Security benefits determination is not a “final decision” reviewable under 42 U.S.C. § 405(g). *Id.* at 107–09. The Court reasoned that reopening is a discretionary, administrative act that does not alter the underlying finality of the original decision. *Id.* at 108. The Railroad Retirement Act’s review provision, § 355(f), is modeled on the Social Security Act and should be interpreted consistently. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141 (finding *Sanders* “persuasive” in the Railroad Retirement context). Every circuit that has adopted the majority rule has relied on *Sanders* to conclude that reopening decisions are not final decisions under § 355(f). *See, e.g., Harris*, 198 F.3d at 142; *Abbruzzese*, 63 F.3d at 974; *Gutierrez*, 918 F.2d at 570; *Steebe*, 708 F.2d at 254–55.

**III. The Minority View Is Inconsistent with the Statutory Text and the Weight of Authority**

Salinas urges this Court to follow the D.C. Circuit’s decision in *Stovic v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), which held that reopening decisions are reviewable. *Stovic* is an outlier. It conflicts with the plain language of § 355(f), which speaks only to “final decision[s]” on the merits, not to discretionary administrative actions. *Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 140. Moreover, *Stovic* misapplies *Sanders* by treating a reopening decision as a new final decision when the Board reconsiders the merits. *Stovic*, 826 F.3d at 504. But here, the Board did not reconsider the merits; it simply declined to reopen based on the regulatory time bar. That is precisely the type of discretionary decision *Sanders* held unreviewable. *Sanders*, 430 U.S. at 108. The Eighth Circuit’s decision in *Sones v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 933 F.2d 636 (8th Cir. 1991), also adopted a minority view, but it has been criticized and not followed by the majority. *See Roberts*, 346 F.3d at 141 (declining to follow *Sones*). The Second Circuit’s early decision in *Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd.*, 370 F.2d 253 (2d Cir. 1966), likewise predates *Sanders* and is no longer persuasive.

**IV. The Fifth Circuit’s Rule of Orderliness Precludes Reconsideration of *Roberts***

The Fifth Circuit correctly applied its rule of orderliness, which binds panels to prior circuit precedent. *Vaughan v. Anderson Reg’l Med. Ctr.*, 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir.), *cert. denied*, 138 S. Ct. 101 (2017). *Roberts* squarely controls this case. Salinas’s invitation to overrule *Roberts* must be directed to the en banc court or the Supreme Court, not to this panel.

**CONCLUSION**

The Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 claim is not a final decision under § 355(f). The petition for review should be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction.
