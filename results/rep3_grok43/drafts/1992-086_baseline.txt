ARGUMENT

The School District violated the First Amendment by denying Lamb’s Chapel access to its facilities solely because the proposed films addressed family values from a religious perspective. The District created a limited public forum by opening its premises after hours to a wide array of community groups for expressive activities. See Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983) (“[W]hen the government has intentionally designated its property as a place for expressive activity, the government may not exclude speech on the basis of its content unless the exclusion is necessary to serve a compelling state interest and is narrowly drawn.”). Once such a forum exists, “the First Amendment forbids the government to regulate speech in ways that favor some viewpoints or ideas at the expense of others.” Id. at 46.

The District permitted numerous after-hours uses, including lectures on parapsychology, musical performances, and community meetings. It nevertheless refused Lamb’s Chapel’s applications because the films presented “Christian values.” This distinction turns on the speaker’s viewpoint, not on any subject-matter limitation the District consistently enforced. Viewpoint discrimination is “an egregious form of content discrimination” that is “presumptively unconstitutional.” Rosenberger v. Rector & Visitors of Univ. of Va., 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995). The District’s policy therefore fails even the limited scrutiny applicable to a designated forum.

Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263 (1981), confirms the constitutional infirmity. There the Court held that a university could not close its facilities to student religious groups when it had opened them to other student organizations. Id. at 277. The same principle applies here: having opened its facilities to secular discussions of family life and child-rearing, the District could not bar the identical discussion when presented from a religious viewpoint. See also Board of Educ. of Westside Cmty. Schs. v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, 250 (1990) (plurality opinion) (Equal Access Act codifies the prohibition on content-based exclusion once a limited forum is created).

The District’s asserted interest in avoiding an Establishment Clause violation does not justify the exclusion. Permitting religious speech on equal terms with secular speech in a forum already open to the public does not constitute an establishment of religion. Widmar, 454 U.S. at 271–73. Because the denial rested on viewpoint discrimination rather than any neutral, narrowly tailored restriction, it violated the First Amendment’s guarantee of freedom of speech.
