**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FINDING THAT SIEBEN PROVIDED A NONDISCRIMINATORY EXPLANATION FOR ITS EMPLOYMENT ACTIONS.**

Under the burden-shifting framework of *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792 (1973), once an employee establishes a prima facie case of discrimination, the burden shifts to the employer to articulate a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason for its actions. *See id.* at 802. The employer’s explanation need not be wise or fair, only honest and not pretextual. *See Texas Dep’t of Cmty. Affairs v. Burdine*, 450 U.S. 248, 258–59 (1981). The district court erred in failing to credit Sieben’s legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its employment decisions.

A. **Plaza Infiniti’s Sales Performance Standards Were Applied Evenly and Without Regard to Sex.**

Sieben articulated a clear, performance-based reason for its actions: Feltmann’s declining sales and low gross profit margins undermined the dealership’s profitability. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(m) (prohibiting discrimination where sex is a motivating factor but not where legitimate reasons exist). The record reflects that Rich evaluated all consultants based on objective metrics—units sold and gross profit per vehicle—consistently applied to both male and female employees. *See Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins*, 507 U.S. 604, 613 (1993) (holding that an employer may rely on “legitimate business reasons” such as performance evaluations).

Feltmann’s own sales data undermines any inference of discrimination. In December 1992, she tied for the highest monthly sales, yet her gross profit margins remained the lowest. Rich’s concern over her profitability was not sex-based but reflected a legitimate business judgment that higher sales without adequate profit margins threatened the dealership’s financial health. *See id.* at 610 (employer may act on “factors other than age” if they are “legitimate and nondiscriminatory”).

B. **Feltmann’s Restricted Use of the Demo Vehicle Was Justified by Business Necessity.**

Sieben also proffered a legitimate reason for restricting Feltmann’s use of the demo vehicle: cost control in the face of declining sales. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(k)(1)(A)(i) (employer not liable if it would have taken the same action regardless of sex). The restriction applied to all consultants, and Rich’s memo to Feltmann was not an individualized penalty but a reiteration of a dealership-wide policy. *See Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins*, 490 U.S. 228, 241 (1989) (plurality opinion) (employer may rely on “mixed motives” if legitimate reasons predominate).

Feltmann’s continued use of the demo outside the St. Louis area despite repeated warnings further justified the restriction. The record shows no evidence that male consultants were similarly warned or disciplined, as the policy was uniformly enforced. *See id.* at 244–45 (employer may avoid liability if it can show it would have made the same decision absent any discriminatory motive).

C. **Feltmann’s Exclusion from the Sales Competition Was Based on Legitimate Business Judgments.**

Rich’s decision not to include Feltmann in the Chicago sales competition was not discriminatory but reflected dealership priorities. The competition was offered to the “top consultants” at Plaza Infiniti, and Feltmann’s exclusion followed her probationary status and low gross profit margins. *See Burdine*, 450 U.S. at 259 (employer’s reason must be “clear and reasonably specific”).

The record shows no evidence that male consultants with similar or worse performance were included. Feltmann’s contention that others were similarly situated but included is unsupported by the evidence. *See id.* at 256 (employer need not prove absence of discrimination, only a legitimate reason).

**II. FELTMANN FAILED TO ESTABLISH PRETEXT.**

Even if Feltmann established a prima facie case, she did not prove that Sieben’s reasons were pretextual. *See St. Mary’s Honor Ctr. v. Hicks*, 509 U.S. 502, 515 (1993) (plaintiff must show that the employer’s reason was not only false but also a pretext for discrimination). The district court erred in failing to apply this standard.

A. **Feltmann’s Performance Metrics Did Not Support a Finding of Pretext.**

Feltmann’s sales figures fluctuated, but her gross profit margins remained consistently low. Rich’s concern over profitability was not a pretext but a legitimate business judgment. *See Hazen Paper*, 507 U.S. at 613 (employer may rely on “legitimate business reasons”).

B. **The Timing of Feltmann’s Complaint Did Not Establish Pretext.**

Feltmann’s complaint about Anzalone’s vulgar comment occurred in November 1992, but her performance issues predated and postdated that incident. The record shows no evidence that her termination or other adverse actions were temporally linked to her complaint. *See id.* at 611 (employer’s actions must be causally connected to discrimination to establish liability).

**CONCLUSION**

The district court erred in failing to credit Sieben’s legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its employment actions. Feltmann failed to establish pretext, and the judgment below should be reversed.
