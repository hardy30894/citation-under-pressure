**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Ninth Circuit Erred by Adopting the “Predominantly Internal” Standard, Which Is Not Supported by the Statute or Supreme Court Precedent.**

The Ninth Circuit erred by adopting the “predominantly internal” standard from the D.C. Circuit, a standard that is not authorized by the text of the statute or Supreme Court precedent. Exemption 2 exempts from disclosure matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Supreme Court has held that an agency may withhold such material only if disclosure “may risk circumvention of agency regulation.” *Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352, 369 (1976). The Court has also instructed that FOIA exemptions are “explicitly exclusive” and must be “narrowly construed.” *U.S. Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts*, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989). The “predominantly internal” standard adds a new requirement to the statute that is not found in the text or the Court’s interpretation. By requiring that information be “predominantly” internal, the Ninth Circuit effectively expands the scope of Exemption 2 beyond the “internal personnel rules and practices” identified by Congress.

**II. The Documents at Issue Do Not Relate to “Internal Personnel Rules and Practices.”**

Even under the correct standard, the documents at issue do not relate to “internal personnel rules and practices.” The Navy’s Explosive Safety Quantity Distance (ESQD) arcs are technical specifications used to design and construct ammunition storage facilities, not rules governing the conduct of Navy personnel. The Ninth Circuit’s own precedent supports the view that Exemption 2 is limited to specific types of internal rules. In *Maricopa Audubon Society v. United States Forest Service*, the court held that data regarding goshawk nesting sites did not “relate ‘solely,’ or even predominantly, ‘to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.’” 108 F.3d 1082, 1085 (9th Cir. 1997). Similarly, the ESQD arcs are not rules for personnel; they are safety data used for facility planning. The Navy has not shown that these technical documents are “internal personnel rules and practices” subject to Exemption 2.
