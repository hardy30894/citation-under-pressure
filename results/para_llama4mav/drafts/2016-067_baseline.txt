The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) regulates the conduct of "debt collectors," not "creditors." 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). The distinction between the two is critical in determining whether Santander Consumer USA, Inc. (Santander) is subject to the FDCPA. As the Fourth Circuit correctly held, a company that purchases defaulted debt and then attempts to collect on it is not necessarily a "debt collector" under the FDCPA.

The FDCPA defines a "debt collector" in 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6) as a person who (1) has a principal business purpose of collecting debts, (2) regularly collects debts owed to another, or (3) collects its own debts using a name other than its own. The petitioners argue that Santander is a "debt collector" because it acquired defaulted debt from CitiFinancial Auto. However, this argument ignores the statutory language and the legislative intent behind the FDCPA.

The Fourth Circuit correctly interpreted the statute, holding that the default status of a debt has no bearing on whether a person qualifies as a debt collector under the threshold definition set forth in 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). Henson v. Santander Consumer USA, Inc., 817 F.3d 131, 136 (4th Cir. 2016). The court noted that the material distinction between a debt collector and a creditor is whether a person regularly collects debt for others or for itself. Id. at 137.

As the Fourth Circuit observed, when Santander collected on the debts it had purchased from CitiFinancial Auto, it was collecting debts owed to itself, not to another. Id. at 139. Therefore, Santander does not meet the definition of a "debt collector" under 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). See Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A., 797 F.3d 1309, 1315-16 (11th Cir. 2015) (holding that a person must regularly collect or attempt to collect debts for others to qualify as a "debt collector").

The petitioners' argument that Santander's status as a debt collector before purchasing the debt somehow continues after the purchase is unpersuasive. The FDCPA regulates debt collectors when they are acting as debt collectors, not when they are collecting their own debts. See 15 U.S.C. § 1692(e). Santander's collection activities after purchasing the debt were on its own behalf, not on behalf of CitiFinancial Auto.

In conclusion, the Fourth Circuit correctly held that Santander is not a "debt collector" under the FDCPA when it collects on debts it has purchased. The FDCPA's definitions and legislative history support this interpretation. See Heintz v. Jenkins, 514 U.S. 291, 293 (1995); S. Rep. No. 95-382, at 3 (1977). Therefore, the judgment of the Fourth Circuit should be affirmed.
