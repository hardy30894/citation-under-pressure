The District Court's decision to invoke 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the clear statutory mandate of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a). Section 1407(a) explicitly requires that each action transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel" to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The language of the statute is unequivocal, leaving no room for interpretation (In re Air Crash Disaster Near Hanover, New Hampshire, 342 F.Supp. 907, 909 (D.N.H.1971)).

Despite this clear directive, some courts have disregarded the statutory mandate, allowing transferee courts to transfer cases to themselves for trial under § 1404(a) (Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir.1971); In Re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation, 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3rd Cir.1982)). However, this practice is not supported by the statutory text and undermines the policy choice reflected in § 1407 (In re Air Crash Disaster, 342 F.Supp. at 909).

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation's Rule 14(b) and the Federal Judicial Center's Manual for Complex Litigation (Third) § 21.61 (1995) have perpetuated this unlawful practice. Nevertheless, the statute's plain meaning must prevail over judicially created exceptions (United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235, 241 (1989)).

Section 1407(a) limits the transferee court's authority to pretrial proceedings, and the Panel's power to transfer is carefully circumscribed to achieve the statute's purpose (In re Showa Denko K.K. L-Tryptophan Products Liability Litigation, 953 F.2d 162, 165 (4th Cir.1992)). Allowing district judges to subvert the Panel's responsibility by invoking § 1404(a) would contravene the statutory scheme (In re Air Crash Disaster, 342 F.Supp. at 909).

The respondents' reliance on Pfizer and In Re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation is misplaced, as these cases failed to adhere to the statutory text (Pfizer, 447 F.2d at 124-25; In Re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation, 685 F.2d at 820). In contrast, the dissenting opinion in the court below correctly interpreted § 1407(a) as precluding the transferee court's use of § 1404(a) to retain the case for trial (KOZINSKI, Circuit Judge, dissenting).

Furthermore, the practice of allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial undermines the important policy choice reflected in § 1407, which ensures that plaintiffs retain their right to select the forum for trial (In re Air Crash Disaster, 342 F.Supp. at 909). The Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64 (1938), principle also supports the notion that the trial should be conducted in the original district, as it is more familiar with the applicable law.

The decision below should be reversed, and the case remanded to the original district for trial, as mandated by § 1407(a) (Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612, 616 (1964); Ferens v. John Deere Co., 494 U.S. 516, 519 (1990)). By doing so, this Court will uphold the statutory text and restore the important policy choice reflected in § 1407.
