ARGUMENT

The district court erred in denying the asserted claims the benefit of the February 6, 1997 filing date. Under settled authority, a continuation application is entitled to that date whenever the original specification contains a description of the invention sufficient to show that the inventor was in possession of it. Chapman v. Wintroath, 252 U.S. 126, 137 (1920) (holding that a continuation relates back if it “discloses the same invention” described in the parent). The original application meets this standard.

The specification need not employ the precise words of the later claims; it is enough that the invention be “described in such full, clear, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to make and use the same.” Evans v. Eaton, 20 U.S. (7 Wheat.) 356, 434 (1822). The original disclosure here identifies a vending machine that supplies telecommunications access through a connector, receives payment, and controls the transaction by electronic circuit. These elements, taken together, necessarily comprehend an interface by which the customer interacts with the machine to initiate and monitor the transaction. No additional language is required to convey possession of that subject matter.

Where, as here, the claims are supported by the original disclosure, later continuations or continuations-in-part do not forfeit the earlier date merely because they add clarifying language. The priority date is fixed by the first application that adequately describes the invention, not by subsequent refinements. Chapman, 252 U.S. at 137. Because the asserted claims find written-description support in the 1997 application, they antedate the MobileStar Network and are not anticipated under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b).

The judgment of invalidity should therefore be reversed.
