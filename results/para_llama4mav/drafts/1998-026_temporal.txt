The District Court's failure to advise Manuel D. Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, does not provide a basis for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review to the Rule 32 violation and determined that the rule's purpose was served since Peguero knew of his right to appeal.

The Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure were designed to ensure fairness and efficiency in the administration of justice. However, not every technical violation of the rules warrants relief. As the Court held in McCandless v. United States, 298 U.S. 342 (1936), a court's failure to follow a procedural rule does not automatically entitle a defendant to relief. The Court emphasized that the rule's purpose must be considered in determining whether a violation is grounds for relief.

In this case, the purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that a defendant is aware of his right to appeal. Since Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing, as found by the District Court after an evidentiary hearing, the rule's purpose was fulfilled. The District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was, therefore, harmless error.

The principle that a court's error is harmless when it does not prejudice the defendant is well-established. In Snyder v. Massachusetts, 291 U.S. 97 (1934), the Court held that a defendant's right to be present at trial was not violated when he was excluded from a view of the crime scene, as his presence would not have aided his defense. Similarly, in this case, the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal did not prejudice him, as he was already aware of his right.

Furthermore, the Court has long recognized that a defendant's knowledge and understanding of his rights are crucial in determining whether a procedural error is prejudicial. In Adams v. United States ex rel. McCann, 317 U.S. 269 (1942), the Court emphasized that a defendant's informed decision is essential in ensuring that his rights are protected. Here, Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal and his decision not to exercise it demonstrate that he made an informed decision, unaffected by the District Court's failure to advise him.

In light of these precedents, it is clear that the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal does not provide a basis for habeas relief. The Court of Appeals correctly applied harmless-error review and determined that the Rule 32 violation did not prejudice Peguero. Therefore, the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be affirmed.
