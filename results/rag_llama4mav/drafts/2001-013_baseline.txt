The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has jurisdiction under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (Act) to issue citations to uninspected vessels. The Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit erred in holding that the Coast Guard's exclusive jurisdiction over the regulation of seamen's working conditions aboard vessels precludes OSHA's regulation under section 4(b)(1) of the Act, and that this pre-emption encompasses both inspected and uninspected vessels.

Section 4(b)(1) of the Act provides that the Act does not apply to "working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health." 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). However, the Coast Guard's authority to regulate uninspected vessels is limited, and it has not exercised its authority to promulgate comprehensive regulations for uninspected vessels.

The Fifth Circuit's reliance on Clary v. Ocean Drilling and Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), and Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), is misplaced. While these cases establish that the Coast Guard has exclusive authority over the working conditions of seamen on vessels, they do not address the specific issue of uninspected vessels. The Coast Guard's regulations for uninspected vessels are limited to specific areas, such as fire extinguishers, life preservers, and ventilation systems. 46 U.S.C. § 4102; 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01 et seq; 46 C.F.R. §§ 26.01 et seq.

The Fifth Circuit's broad language in Clary and Donovan should not be read to preclude OSHA's jurisdiction over uninspected vessels. The Supreme Court has recognized that the Act is intended to provide a comprehensive regulatory scheme for occupational safety and health, and that OSHA has broad authority to regulate workplace hazards. See American Textile Manufacturers Institute, Inc. v. Donovan, 452 U.S. 490 (1981); Industrial Union Dept., AFL-CIO v. American Petroleum Institute, 448 U.S. 607 (1980).

In Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Assn., 505 U.S. 88 (1992), the Supreme Court held that the Act pre-empts state laws that establish occupational safety and health standards on issues for which OSHA has already promulgated a standard. However, the Court also recognized that the Act does not pre-empt state laws or regulations that address specific hazards or working conditions not addressed by OSHA standards. Similarly, OSHA's jurisdiction over uninspected vessels is not pre-empted by the Coast Guard's limited authority to regulate these vessels.

In conclusion, OSHA has jurisdiction under the Act to issue citations to uninspected vessels. The Coast Guard's limited authority to regulate uninspected vessels does not pre-empt OSHA's jurisdiction, and the Fifth Circuit's holding to the contrary was in error. The citations issued by OSHA to Mallard Bay Drilling, Inc. should be upheld.
