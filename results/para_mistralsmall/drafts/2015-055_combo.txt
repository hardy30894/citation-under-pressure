**ARGUMENT**

Petitioner Gregory Welch respectfully submits that the rule announced in *Johnson v. United States*, 359 U.S. 14 (1959), regarding the definition of a violent offense under the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA) applies retroactively to his case. The Eleventh Circuit erred in affirming his sentence under the ACCA’s residual clause, which this Court has since declared unconstitutionally vague. The rule announced in *Johnson* is a procedural rule that applies retroactively to cases on collateral review.

This Court has long held that procedural rules that regulate the manner of determining guilt or innocence apply retroactively. In *Mackey v. United States*, 401 U.S. 667 (1971) (plurality opinion), the Court explained that such rules apply retroactively because they do not alter the underlying substantive law but merely change the process by which guilt is determined. *See also Linkletter v. Walker*, 381 U.S. 618 (1965) (holding that procedural rules apply retroactively unless there is a compelling justification for non-retroactivity).

The rule announced in *Johnson* is a procedural rule. It clarifies the standard for determining whether a prior conviction qualifies as a violent felony under the ACCA’s residual clause. As this Court explained in *Teague v. Lane*, 489 U.S. 288 (1989) (plurality opinion), a procedural rule applies retroactively if it "places 'certain kinds of primary, private individual conduct beyond the power of the criminal law-making authority to proscribe.'" *Id.* at 307 (quoting *Mackey*, 401 U.S. at 692). The rule in *Johnson* does not alter the substantive law of what constitutes a violent felony; it merely clarifies the standard for determining whether a prior conviction qualifies as such. *See also United States v. Peltier*, 422 U.S. 531 (1975) (holding that a rule clarifying the standard for determining guilt applies retroactively).

Moreover, the rule in *Johnson* is a "watershed" rule of criminal procedure that implicates the fundamental fairness and accuracy of the criminal proceeding. *See Teague*, 489 U.S. at 311. The residual clause of the ACCA was unconstitutionally vague, and its application led to arbitrary and unpredictable results. *See Johnson*, 359 U.S. at 25. The rule announced in *Johnson* ensures that the ACCA is applied in a manner consistent with due process, thereby enhancing the fairness and accuracy of the criminal proceeding. *See Mackey*, 401 U.S. at 681-82.

The Eleventh Circuit erred in affirming Petitioner’s sentence under the residual clause. The residual clause was unconstitutionally vague, and its application to Petitioner’s prior conviction for strong-arm robbery violated due process. *See Johnson*, 359 U.S. at 25. The rule announced in *Johnson* applies retroactively, and Petitioner is entitled to relief under that rule.

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court reverse the judgment of the Eleventh Circuit and remand this case for resentencing consistent with *Johnson v. United States*, 359 U.S. 14 (1959).
