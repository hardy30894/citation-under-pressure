**ARGUMENT**

**I. The School District Violated the First Amendment by Excluding Lamb’s Chapel’s Religious Speech From a Limited Public Forum Based on Its Viewpoint.**

The Court of Appeals erred in holding that the School District’s facilities were not open to religious uses. The District had opened its facilities to a wide array of community groups, including the Southern Harmonizers (a choral group that performed religious music) and a lecture series on parapsychology and metaphysics. *See Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist.*, 959 F.2d 381, 384 (2d Cir. 1992). By permitting these groups to use school property, the District created a “limited public forum” for expressive activity. *See Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983). Once a government entity opens a forum for expressive activity, it may not discriminate against speech based on its viewpoint. *See Rosenberger v. Rector & Visitors of Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995).

The District’s exclusion of Lamb’s Chapel was viewpoint discrimination. The District permitted secular discussions of family values and child rearing but excluded Lamb’s Chapel solely because its film series presented those topics from a religious perspective. This Court has repeatedly held that the government may not “discriminate against religious speech when the speech is otherwise within the forum’s limitations.” *Good News Club v. Milford Cent. Sch.*, 533 U.S. 98, 112 (2001). As *Rosenberger* makes clear, “the government may not regulate speech in ways that favor some viewpoints or ideas at the expense of others.” 515 U.S. at 829. The District’s policy of excluding “religious purposes” while permitting secular groups to discuss identical subjects constitutes precisely the kind of viewpoint discrimination the First Amendment forbids.

**II. The District’s Denial of Access Cannot Be Justified by the Establishment Clause.**

The District’s asserted interest in avoiding an Establishment Clause violation does not justify its discriminatory policy. This Court has consistently held that permitting private religious speech in a limited public forum on the same terms as secular speech does not violate the Establishment Clause. *See Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 271 (1981) (holding that an “open forum” policy does not “confer any imprimatur of state approval on religious sects or practices”). Indeed, “there is a crucial difference between *government speech* endorsing religion, which the Establishment Clause forbids, and *private speech* endorsing religion, which the Free Speech and Free Exercise Clauses protect.” *Santa Fe Indep. Sch. Dist. v. Doe*, 530 U.S. 290, 302 (2000). The District’s fear of an Establishment Clause violation is unfounded, as this Court has repeatedly upheld equal access policies that treat religious speech neutrally. *See, e.g., Board of Educ. v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226, 248 (1990) (plurality opinion) (holding that the Equal Access Act’s requirement of equal access for religious student groups does not violate the Establishment Clause).

**III. The Court of Appeals Erred in Distinguishing *Widmar* and *Mergens*.**

The Court of Appeals attempted to distinguish *Widmar* and *Mergens* on the ground that those cases involved student groups rather than outside organizations. But this Court has never held that the First Amendment’s protections against viewpoint discrimination apply only to students. In *Good News Club*, this Court applied the same principles to a private Christian organization seeking access to school facilities after hours. 533 U.S. at 115. The Court held that “the Club’s activities were not religious worship, but the teaching of morals and character from a religious viewpoint,” and that excluding the Club while permitting other groups to teach morals and character constituted viewpoint discrimination. *Id.* at 108-09. Similarly, Lamb’s Chapel sought to show a film series on family values from a Christian perspective—a subject the District permitted other groups to discuss. The District’s exclusion of Lamb’s Chapel cannot be reconciled with *Good News Club*.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment of the Court of Appeals should be reversed.
