## Argument

The district court's judgment that the asserted claims of the '761 patent are invalid under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b) should be reversed because the jury's finding that the Leader2Leader product was publicly used and on sale prior to the critical date is not supported by substantial evidence that it embodied the claimed invention.

The '761 patent claims a data management system that enables users to collaborate and communicate through a web-based platform. The system achieves this by associating data with metadata that captures the context in which the data is created and used. As the Supreme Court held in United States v. Adams, 383 U.S. 39, 48 (1966), a claimed invention is not anticipated by a prior art reference unless the reference discloses all the elements of the claimed invention. The Leader2Leader product, as described in the white paper presented to the Wright Patterson Air Force Base, does not disclose all the elements of the claimed invention.

The white paper describes the Leader2Leader product as a platform that is "operational now with low user volumes" and that has been "[f]ully developed." J.A. 27207, 27204. However, it does not disclose the specific metadata association and tracking features claimed in the '761 patent. As the court held in Corona Cord Tire Co. v. Dovan Chemical Corp., 276 U.S. 358, 382-83 (1928), a prior art reference must disclose the claimed invention in sufficient detail to enable one skilled in the art to practice the invention. The white paper does not provide such detail.

Furthermore, the testimony of Leader's founder, Michael McKibben, and Jeffrey Lamb, one of the inventors, establishes that the Digital Leaderboard software, which is the underlying engine of the Leader2Leader product, was still under development in 2002. J.A. 24829. As the court held in Coffin v. Ogden, 85 U.S. 120, 124 (1873), a prior use or sale is not anticipatory if the invention is not yet complete. The evidence shows that the Leader2Leader product was not a complete embodiment of the claimed invention prior to the critical date.

Therefore, the jury's finding that the Leader2Leader product was publicly used and on sale prior to the critical date is not supported by substantial evidence, and the district court's judgment should be reversed. The asserted claims of the '761 patent are not invalid under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b).
