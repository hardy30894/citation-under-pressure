**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO APPLY RULE 407 TO EXCLUDE EVIDENCE OF SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES IN THIS STRICT PRODUCTS LIABILITY CASE.**

Rule 407 of the Federal Rules of Evidence provides that evidence of subsequent remedial measures is not admissible to prove negligence, culpable conduct, a defect in a product, or a need for a warning or instruction. *Fed. R. Evid. 407*. This rule applies in strict products liability cases. *See, e.g., In re Air Crash Disaster at Sioux City, Iowa, 47 F.3d 337, 349 (8th Cir. 1995) ("Rule 407 applies in strict products liability cases."); see also Raymond v. Raymond Corp., 938 F.2d 1537, 1543 (1st Cir. 1991) ("Rule 407 applies in strict products liability cases.")*.

The purpose of Rule 407 is to encourage manufacturers to take remedial measures to make their products safer without fear that such measures will be used against them in litigation. *See, e.g., Grenada County v. Brogdon, 52 F.3d 50, 54 (5th Cir. 1995) ("The rule is designed to encourage potential defendants to take remedial measures to make their products safer without fear that such measures will be used against them in litigation.")*. This policy rationale applies with equal force in strict products liability cases, where the focus is on the condition of the product at the time of the injury, not the manufacturer’s conduct. *See, e.g., Raymond, 938 F.2d at 1543 ("The policy underlying Rule 407 is to encourage manufacturers to make their products safer without fear that such measures will be used against them in litigation. This policy applies with equal force in strict products liability cases.")*.

Here, the district court erred in failing to apply Rule 407 to exclude evidence of Morbark’s subsequent remedial measures. The court’s in limine ruling excluded evidence of post-accident design changes under Rule 407, yet permitted Morbark to introduce evidence of subsequent remedial measures for impeachment purposes. This was error. Rule 407 applies to all evidence of subsequent remedial measures, regardless of the purpose for which such evidence is offered. *See, e.g., Grenada County, 52 F.3d at 54 ("Rule 407 applies to all evidence of subsequent remedial measures, regardless of the purpose for which such evidence is offered.")*.

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN ADMITTING EVIDENCE OF SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES FOR IMPEACHMENT PURPOSES.**

Even if Rule 407 were inapplicable, the district court erred in admitting evidence of subsequent remedial measures for impeachment purposes. The impeachment exception to Rule 407 is narrow and does not permit the introduction of evidence of subsequent remedial measures to impeach a witness’s testimony that the product was safe. *See, e.g., In re Air Crash Disaster at Sioux City, Iowa, 47 F.3d at 349 ("The impeachment exception to Rule 407 is narrow and does not permit the introduction of evidence of subsequent remedial measures to impeach a witness’s testimony that the product was safe.")*.

Here, Morbark sought to imply to the jury that the seventeen-inch infeed chute was the safest length available and was still in use by the City of DeFuniak Springs and other government agencies. This was an improper attempt to use evidence of subsequent remedial measures to prove the safety of the product. *See, e.g., Raymond, 938 F.2d at 1543 ("The impeachment exception to Rule 407 does not permit the introduction of evidence of subsequent remedial measures to prove the safety of the product.")*.

**III. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO GRANT WOOD’S MOTION FOR A NEW TRIAL.**

The district court’s errors in admitting evidence of subsequent remedial measures and failing to apply Rule 407 were not harmless. The admission of this evidence likely influenced the jury’s verdict, as it permitted Morbark to argue that the seventeen-inch infeed chute was safe because it was still in use and had not been changed. *See, e.g., Grenada County, 52 F.3d at 54 ("The admission of evidence of subsequent remedial measures is not harmless error where it permits the defendant to argue that the product was safe because it had not been changed.")*.

The district court’s errors deprived Wood of a fair trial and warrant a new trial. *See, e.g., In re Air Crash Disaster at Sioux City, Iowa, 47 F.3d at 349 ("The admission of evidence of subsequent remedial measures warrants a new trial where it deprives the plaintiff of a fair trial.")*.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Wood respectfully requests that this Court reverse the district court’s judgment and remand this case for a new trial.
