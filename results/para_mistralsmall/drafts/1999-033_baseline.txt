**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Seventh Circuit Correctly Recognized a "Class of One" Equal Protection Claim.**

The Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment prohibits a state from denying any person within its jurisdiction the equal protection of the laws. *Yick Wo v. Hopkins*, 118 U.S. 356, 369 (1886). While equal protection jurisprudence typically focuses on classifications that burden or benefit groups, this Court has recognized that the Clause also prohibits arbitrary and irrational governmental action directed at an individual. *See City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Ctr.*, 473 U.S. 432, 439 (1985) ("The general rule is that legislation is presumed to be valid and will be sustained if the classification drawn by the statute is rationally related to a legitimate state interest."); *see also Nordlinger v. Hahn*, 505 U.S. 1, 10 (1992) ("The Equal Protection Clause is essentially a direction that all persons similarly situated should be treated alike.").

The Seventh Circuit correctly held that a plaintiff may bring a "class of one" equal protection claim where the plaintiff alleges that state action was motivated by "a spiteful effort to 'get' him for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective." *Olech v. Village of Willowbrook*, 160 F.3d 386, 387 (7th Cir. 1998) (Posner, J.). This Court has long recognized that the Equal Protection Clause prohibits arbitrary and irrational governmental action, even when directed at an individual. *See, e.g., Sioux City Bridge Co. v. Dakota County*, 260 U.S. 441, 445 (1923) ("The equal protection clause does not prohibit legislation merely because it is special or limited in its application."); *see also Romer v. Evans*, 517 U.S. 620, 627 (1996) ("The Constitution’s guarantee of equal protection is most honored when it is held to have a rational basis.").

The Seventh Circuit’s decision is consistent with this Court’s recognition that the Equal Protection Clause prohibits arbitrary and irrational governmental action, even when directed at an individual. *See, e.g., Sioux City Bridge Co.*, 260 U.S. at 445; *Romer*, 517 U.S. at 627. The Seventh Circuit correctly held that a plaintiff may bring a "class of one" equal protection claim where the plaintiff alleges that state action was motivated by "a spiteful effort to 'get' him for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective." *Olech*, 160 F.3d at 387 (Posner, J.).

**II. The Village’s Action Violated the Equal Protection Clause.**

The Village’s demand for an 18-foot easement, in excess of the 15-foot easement required from similarly situated property owners, was arbitrary and irrational. The Village offered no legitimate justification for its disparate treatment of Olech. *See Nordlinger*, 505 U.S. at 10 ("The Equal Protection Clause is essentially a direction that all persons similarly situated should be treated alike."). The Village’s action was motivated by "a spiteful effort to 'get' [Olech] for reasons wholly unrelated to any legitimate state objective." *Olech*, 160 F.3d at 387 (Posner, J.).

This Court has recognized that the Equal Protection Clause prohibits arbitrary and irrational governmental action, even when directed at an individual. *See, e.g., Sioux City Bridge Co.*, 260 U.S. at 445; *Romer*, 517 U.S. at 627. The Village’s action violated the Equal Protection Clause by treating Olech differently from similarly situated property owners without any rational basis.
