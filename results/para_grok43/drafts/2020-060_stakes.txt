**ARGUMENT**

Section 113(f)(3)(B) authorizes contribution only when a party “has resolved its liability to the United States or a State for some or all of a response action or for some or all of the costs of such action in an administrative or judicially approved settlement.” 42 U.S.C. § 9613(f)(3)(B). The 2004 consent decree resolved none of Guam’s CERCLA liability. It therefore left intact Guam’s independent right to recover its response costs under section 107(a).

CERCLA distinguishes sharply between cost-recovery and contribution. As originally enacted, section 107(a) permitted “any other person” to recover “necessary costs of response.” Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Aviall Services, Inc., 543 U.S. 157, 161 (2004). Congress added section 113(f) in 1986 to supply an express contribution remedy “during or following” a section 107 action or after settlement of CERCLA liability. Id. at 162–63. The statute of limitations for contribution is three years; for cost recovery it is six. 42 U.S.C. §§ 9613(g)(2)(B), (g)(3).

A settlement triggers section 113(f)(3)(B) only if it resolves liability “for some or all of a response action.” The 2004 decree was entered in a Clean Water Act enforcement action that alleged unpermitted discharges of leachate. It required Guam to pay a civil penalty, close the landfill, and install a cover system. The decree expressly states it was entered “without any finding or admission of liability against or by the Government of Guam.” Consent Decree 3. Because the decree neither invoked CERCLA nor resolved any CERCLA liability, it did not satisfy the statutory predicate for contribution.

The D.C. Circuit’s contrary reading would force every party that settles non-CERCLA claims with the United States to elect between an immediate, and possibly premature, contribution action or forfeiture of its longer section 107 remedy. That result is irreconcilable with the text of section 113(f)(3)(B) and with the structure Congress created in the Superfund Amendments and Reauthorization Act. See Burlington Northern & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. United States, 556 U.S. 599, 613 (2009) (noting CERCLA’s “carefully designed” allocation of liability and remedies).

Guam therefore retains its timely section 107(a) claim against the United States for costs incurred in remediating the Ordot Dump. The judgment of the court of appeals should be reversed.
