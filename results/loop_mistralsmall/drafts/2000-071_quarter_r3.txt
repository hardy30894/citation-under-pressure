**ARGUMENT**

I. MILFORD’S COMMUNITY-USE POLICY IS A NEUTRAL, REASONABLE TIME, PLACE, AND MANNER REGULATION OF A LIMITED PUBLIC FORUM.

Milford’s Community-Use Policy is viewpoint neutral. *Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n*, 460 U.S. 37, 49 (1983). The policy “was not adopted . . . to suppress any particular point of view.” *Id.* at 46. Because the policy “serves a legitimate . . . interest” and “is viewpoint neutral,” it need only be “reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum.” *Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educ. Fund*, 473 U.S. 788, 806 (1985). Milford’s stated purpose—to reserve its facilities for community use—is legitimate and reasonable. N.Y. Educ. Law § 414; *see also* *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263, 271 (1981) (recognizing a university’s legitimate interest in avoiding “the appearance of official endorsement” of religious activities).

II. MILFORD DID NOT ENGAGE IN UNCONSTITUTIONAL VIEWPOINT DISCRIMINATION.

The Club conflates subject-matter and viewpoint discrimination. A regulation is viewpoint discriminatory only if it “suppress[es] particular viewpoints” within a subject that the forum otherwise permits. *Rosenberger v. Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995). Here, Milford has not opened its forum to religious instruction or prayer. *Good News Club v. Milford Cent. Sch.*, 202 F.3d 502, 512 (2d Cir. 2000). The Club’s activities—singing, Bible lessons, and prayer—are religious in nature. *Id.* at 511. Because Milford has not permitted any group to engage in worship-like activities, it has not discriminated against the Club’s viewpoint. *Perry*, 460 U.S. at 46 (“The government violates the First Amendment when it denies access to a speaker solely on the basis of the speaker’s viewpoint.”).

III. MILFORD’S EXCLUSION IS JUSTIFIED BY THE ESTABLISHMENT CLAUSE.

A. The Club’s Activities Constitute Worship and Religious Instruction.

The Club’s meetings are religious in nature. *Good News Club*, 202 F.3d at 511. The Club’s own description confirms that its meetings include singing, Bible lessons, and prayer. *Id.* at 506. These activities are indistinguishable from worship. *Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 269 n.6, 102 S.Ct. at 274 n.6 (“[S]inging hymns, reading scripture, and teaching biblical principles” are forms of speech, but when conducted as worship, they are not secular education). Milford’s policy permissibly excludes such activities to avoid violating the Establishment Clause. *Board of Educ. v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226, 249 (1990) (recognizing that a school may “preserve the building for its intended purpose as a school” and avoid “the appearance of school endorsement of religion”).

B. Milford’s Policy Is Neutral and Generally Applicable.

Milford’s policy is neutral toward religion and applies equally to all groups. *Everson v. Board of Education*, 330 U.S. 1, 15 (1947). The policy does not single out religion for disfavored treatment; it simply excludes all worship and religious instruction. *Epperson v. Arkansas*, 393 U.S. 97, 106 (1968). Because the policy is neutral and generally applicable, it withstands Establishment Clause scrutiny. *Abington School District v. Schempp*, 374 U.S. 203, 226 (1963).
