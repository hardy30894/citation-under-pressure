The Ninth Circuit erred in denying Lexecon's petition for a writ of mandamus. The District Court's decision to invoke 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial contravenes the clear statutory mandate of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a).

Section 1407(a) explicitly requires that each action transferred for pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The statute's language is unequivocal, leaving no room for interpretation that would permit a transferee court to retain a case for trial. As Judge Kozinski noted in his dissent, "It's hard to imagine a clearer statutory command, or one more widely ignored." (61 F.3d 911, KOZINSKI, Circuit Judge, dissenting).

Despite the statutory clarity, the Ninth Circuit and other courts have condoned the practice of allowing transferee courts to transfer cases to themselves for trial under § 1404(a). This practice is not supported by the statute. Section 1407(a) limits the transferee court's authority to pretrial proceedings, and the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation's authority to remand is not subject to the transferee court's discretion.

The cases relied upon by the Ninth Circuit, such as In Re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation, 685 F.2d 810 (3rd Cir.1982), and Pfizer v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir.1971), are not persuasive. These decisions disregard the statutory mandate and justify their departure on grounds of policy or convenience. However, as Judge Kozinski observed, "federal judges are bound to respect" the policy choice reflected in § 1407, "regardless of their views of efficiency or their fondness for particular cases." (61 F.3d 911, KOZINSKI, Circuit Judge, dissenting).

The District Court's order was clearly erroneous as a matter of law. The Ninth Circuit should have granted the writ of mandamus to correct this error and ensure compliance with the statutory requirements. By denying the petition, the Ninth Circuit perpetuated an "illicit practice that has developed willy-nilly during the last quarter century." (61 F.3d 911, KOZINSKI, Circuit Judge, dissenting).

This Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision and hold that a federal district court conducting "pretrial proceedings" under 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) may not invoke § 1404(a) to assign a transferred case to itself for trial. The plain language of § 1407(a) and the policy choices it reflects require that cases be remanded to their original district for trial, ensuring that plaintiffs retain their right to select the forum for dispositive matters.
