The Court should answer the question presented in the negative. Under a federal fee-shifting statute, a “reasonable” attorneys’ fee may not be enhanced solely because counsel performed well and achieved a strong result. The lodestar—reasonable hours multiplied by a reasonable rate—already captures the ordinary value of counsel’s work, and this Court has repeatedly treated it as presumptively sufficient. Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424, 433-37 (1983); Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 897-901 (1984); Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air, 478 U.S. 546, 565-68 (1986).

That rule follows from the function of fee shifting. A fee award compensates for necessary legal services; it is not a separate prize for excellence. Ex parte Peterson, 253 U.S. 300, 312-13 (1920); Sprague v. Ticonic National Bank, 307 U.S. 161, 166-67 (1939). The Court has long insisted that a fee award must be reasonable, not a windfall. Hensley, 461 U.S. at 433-37; Blum, 465 U.S. at 897; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-66.

Blum is the central authority. There, the Court held that “quality of representation” and “results obtained” could not justify an enhancement on the record before it because those considerations were already reflected in the lodestar. Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-900. The Court explained that the lodestar “normally” yields a fully compensatory fee. Id. at 897-901. Delaware Valley reaffirmed that the lodestar “includes most, if not all, of the relevant factors constituting a ‘reasonable’ attorney’s fee.” 478 U.S. at 566. Those holdings foreclose any rule allowing an upward adjustment based solely on superior lawyering and favorable results. Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-900; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-68.

Hensley points in the same direction. The Court there said that the “degree of success obtained” is important to the fee inquiry, but only in determining whether the hours expended were reasonable and whether a reduction was necessary for partial success. Hensley, 461 U.S. at 434-37. The opinion did not authorize a second increase after the lodestar has been set. Id. at 433-37. To enhance a fee again because counsel obtained the result that justified the lodestar in the first place would be classic double counting. Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-900; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-68.

Older cases do not support a different result. In Johnson v. Georgia Highway Express, Inc., 488 F.2d 714, 717-19 (5th Cir. 1974), the court identified “the skill requisite to perform the legal service properly” and “the amount involved and the results obtained” among the considerations relevant to a reasonable fee. But Johnson predates the lodestar framework and cannot be read to authorize a post-lodestar premium for the same considerations. Id. at 717-19; Hensley, 461 U.S. at 433-37. Likewise, King v. Greenblatt, 560 F.2d 1024, 1027-28 (1st Cir. 1977), and Fitzpatrick v. Bitzer, 427 U.S. 445, 456-57 (1976), treated fee awards as compensation for services rendered, not as bonuses for exceptional performance. See also Lindy Bros. Builders, Inc. v. American Radiator & Standard Sanitary Corp., 487 F.2d 161, 167-69 (3d Cir. 1973); Lindy Bros. Builders, Inc. v. American Radiator & Standard Sanitary Corp., 540 F.2d 102, 117-18 (3d Cir. 1976).

The contrary rule would undermine the lodestar’s purpose. If enhancement may rest solely on counsel’s quality and the size of the result, then every successful fee case becomes an invitation for a discretionary premium untethered to objective market measures. That approach would reintroduce the uncertainty the lodestar was designed to eliminate. Blum, 465 U.S. at 897-901; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-68. It would also expand fee shifting beyond the narrow limits the Court has imposed under the American Rule. Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240, 262-71 (1975); Arcambel v. Wiseman, 3 U.S. (3 Dall.) 306 (1796).

The Court should therefore hold that quality of performance and results obtained may inform the lodestar calculation, but they cannot, standing alone, justify enhancing a fee award under a federal fee-shifting statute. Hensley, 461 U.S. at 433-37; Blum, 465 U.S. at 897-901; Delaware Valley, 478 U.S. at 565-68.
