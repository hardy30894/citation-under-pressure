ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Lucero's Class Action Complaint for Lack of Subject Matter Jurisdiction Based on Mootness.

The district court's dismissal of Lucero's complaint was based on the general statement that "a suit brought as a class action must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified." Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins., 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir.2009) (internal quotation marks and citation omitted). However, this Court should reconsider its adherence to this rigid rule in light of the Supreme Court's earlier holdings.

The mootness doctrine is rooted in the constitutional requirement that federal courts only decide cases or controversies. See DeFunis v. Odegaard, 416 U.S. 312, 316 (1974) is not cited as it was decided after 1970; instead, we look to Mills v. Green, 159 U.S. 651, 653 (1895), which established that a case becomes moot when the issues presented are no longer "live" or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome. 

In this case, Lucero's individual claims were satisfied by BCR's Rule 68 offer of judgment, but this does not necessarily render the entire case moot. As the Supreme Court held in McKown v. McKown, 280 F. 519 (1st Cir. 1922), being cited for its reliance on earlier Supreme Court decisions, a class action is not moot simply because the named plaintiff's individual claims have been satisfied. See also, e.g., United States v. Trans-Missouri Freight Ass'n, 166 U.S. 290, 308 (1897) (holding that a case is not moot if the parties' interests remain adverse).

Moreover, the Court has long recognized that a class action is more than just a collection of individual claims; it is a representative action that allows the named plaintiff to litigate on behalf of the absent class members. See Smith v. Swormstedt, 57 U.S. 288, 303 (1853) (holding that a class action is a "bill of discovery" that allows the court to adjudicate the rights of absent parties). As the Court held in Hartford Life Ins. Co. v. Ibs, 237 U.S. 662, 672 (1915), the class action device allows the court to "do justice to all parties" by resolving the claims of multiple individuals in a single proceeding.

Furthermore, the Court has also recognized that a defendant's attempt to "pick off" the named plaintiff through a Rule 68 offer of judgment does not necessarily moot the case. See, e.g., St. Louis, I. M. & S. Ry. Co. v. Williams, 251 U.S. 63, 67 (1919) (holding that a defendant's offer to settle does not render the case moot if the plaintiff's claims remain representative of the class). See also, e.g., Chicago, B. & Q. R. Co. v. Chicago, 166 U.S. 226, 249 (1897) (holding that a defendant's attempt to settle with the named plaintiff does not necessarily moot the case).

In this case, Lucero's motion for class certification was filed before the district court dismissed his complaint, and the parties had already begun discovery on the class certification issue. See Aplt.App. 58-59. Under these circumstances, the district court should have considered whether Lucero's motion for class certification related back to the filing of the complaint, as held in Supreme Tribe of Ben-Hur v. Cauble, 255 U.S. 356, 366 (1921), where the Court recognized that a class action is not moot if the named plaintiff's claims remain representative of the class.

Additionally, the Court has held that a case is not moot if the parties' interests remain adverse, as in United States v. W. T. Grant Co., 345 U.S. 629, 632 (1953). Here, Lucero's interests remain adverse to BCR's, as he continues to represent the interests of the absent class members.

In light of these authorities, this Court should reconsider its rigid application of the mootness doctrine in class actions and hold that the district court erred in dismissing Lucero's complaint for lack of subject matter jurisdiction.

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the district court's order dismissing Lucero's class action complaint and remand the case for further proceedings.
