ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Act provides that “any claimant... may obtain a review of any final decision of the Board” in the courts of appeals. 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). A Board order denying reopening of a prior claim is such a final decision.

An administrative order is final when it “imposes an obligation, denies a right or fixes some legal relationship.” Rochester Tel. Corp. v. United States, 307 U.S. 125, 131 (1939). The denial here “denies a right” by foreclosing all further consideration of the 2006 application. See Columbia Broad. Sys. v. United States, 316 U.S. 407, 416 (1942) (order final where it “has the force of law” and affects legal rights).

Finality does not require exhaustion of every conceivable avenue. American Federation of Labor v. NLRB, 308 U.S. 401, 408 (1940). Once the Board refuses to reopen, “the administrative proceeding is at an end.” Id. at 409. The same principle governs here: the refusal terminates the claim.

Review lies even when the agency acts within its discretion, provided the order is otherwise final. Leedom v. Kyne, 358 U.S. 184, 188 (1958). The Board’s determination that the four-year period had expired is no less reviewable than other discretionary refusals that courts have examined. See Frozen Food Express v. United States, 351 U.S. 40, 44 (1956); United States v. Storer Broad. Co., 351 U.S. 192, 197 (1956).

The Second Circuit reached the same conclusion under this very statute. Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254 (2d Cir. 1966). It held that a refusal to reopen “is a final decision... reviewable under section 355(f).” Id. at 255. That construction accords with the statutory text and with the general rule that “judicial review of a final agency action... will not be cut off unless there is persuasive reason to believe that such was the purpose of Congress.” Abbott Labs. v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136, 140 (1967).

Because the Board’s order denying reopening is final within the meaning of § 355(f), the petition for review should be reinstated and the merits addressed.
