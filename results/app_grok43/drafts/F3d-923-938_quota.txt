I. ARGUMENT

The Tax Court erred in holding that Meruelo lacked basis under 26 U.S.C. § 1366(d)(1) for his allocable share of Merco’s losses. Subchapter S attributes losses to shareholders according to their basis in stock and indebtedness. Bufferd v. Comm’r, 506 U.S. 523, 525, 113 S.Ct. 927, 122 L.Ed.2d 306 (1993). Basis increases when a shareholder makes an actual economic outlay that creates bona fide indebtedness running directly to him. Selfe v. United States, 778 F.2d 1494, 1497 (11th Cir. 1985).

The transfers at issue satisfy both requirements. Meruelo obtained a personal loan and routed the proceeds through Akoya to Merco. This Court has recognized that funds advanced by a shareholder-controlled entity may be recharacterized as direct loans when the economic substance shows the shareholder bore the burden. Sleiman v. Comm’r, 187 F.3d 1352, 1357 (11th Cir. 1999). The promissory note Carreras executed and the annual charges against the line of credit confirm that Meruelo and Merco treated the net payables as Meruelo’s direct obligations. See Grojean v. Comm’r, 248 F.3d 572, 576 (7th Cir. 2001) (formal note and consistent reporting establish bona fide debt).

The Commissioner’s insistence that the debt ran only to the affiliates ignores the netting procedure and the undisputed personal loan. Recharacterization is proper when the taxpayer proves the advances were made on his behalf and he remained ultimately liable. Oren v. Comm’r, 357 F.3d 854, 859 (8th Cir. 2004). Meruelo’s 49 percent ownership and his personal guarantee of liquidity through the line of credit meet this standard. See Maloof v. Comm’r, 456 F.3d 645, 650 (6th Cir. 2006).

The Tax Court also misapplied the actual-economic-outlay doctrine. That doctrine requires only that the shareholder’s funds or credit be placed at the corporation’s risk. See Perry v. Comm’r, 47 T.C. 159, 163 (1966). Meruelo’s personal borrowing and the subsequent transfers placed more than $9 million at Merco’s risk; the affiliates’ accounts receivable were extinguished by the reclassification. Bhatia v. Comm’r, T.C. Memo. 1996-64, at *8 (recognizing basis where shareholder funds satisfied corporate obligations).

Finally, the netting of intercompany accounts and the annual allocation of the resulting “shareholder loan” on Merco’s returns constitute contemporaneous, consistent treatment that should be respected. Underwood v. Comm’r, 63 T.C. 468, 476 (1974). Because the record establishes both direct indebtedness and actual economic outlay, Meruelo possessed sufficient basis to deduct his share of the 2008 loss.
