The withdrawal of a conspirator from the combination before the limitations period commences requires the government to prove the accused's membership in the conspiracy during the period for which prosecution is sought. United States v. Kissel, 218 U.S. 601 (1910). Once a defendant demonstrates severance, continued participation cannot be presumed. Hyde v. United States, 225 U.S. 347 (1912). The government therefore bears the affirmative obligation to establish that the accused remained a member within the statutory window. Brown v. Elliott, 225 U.S. 392 (1912).

This allocation follows from the fundamental rule that the prosecution must prove every element of the offense beyond a reasonable doubt. Davis v. United States, 160 U.S. 469 (1895). Membership in a conspiracy during the limitations period is an essential element when the indictment charges a continuing agreement. Fiswick v. United States, 329 U.S. 211 (1946). The government cannot shift that burden to the defendant by requiring proof of withdrawal as a defense. Krulewitch v. United States, 336 U.S. 440 (1949).

The same principle governs multi-member conspiracies. When the evidence shows that leaders divided operations before the limitations date, the prosecution must connect each accused to the single continuing enterprise during the actionable period. Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946). Absent such proof, separate agreements cannot be treated as one. Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 (1946).

Nor may the government rely on earlier acts alone once withdrawal has occurred. Grunewald v. United States, 353 U.S. 391 (1957). The statute begins to run from the date the combination ends as to the defendant, and the prosecution must demonstrate an overt act or continued membership thereafter. Hyde, 225 U.S. at 369. Requiring the accused to disprove ongoing participation would invert the presumption of innocence. Davis, 160 U.S. at 488.

Accordingly, the court of appeals erred in relieving the government of its burden. The convictions cannot stand without proof beyond a reasonable doubt that each petitioner remained a member of the charged conspiracy within the limitations period.
