ARGUMENT

The reinstatement provision of the Immigration and Nationality Act, 8 U.S.C. § 1251(a)(5) (Supp. II 1997), does not reach an alien whose reentry occurred before its effective date. Statutes operate only prospectively unless Congress has unmistakably declared otherwise. See United States v. Heth, 3 Cranch 399, 413 (1806) (“words of a statute, if of doubtful meaning, ought not to be so construed as to have a retrospective operation”); Winfree v. Northern Pac. Ry., 227 U.S. 296, 301 (1913) (same). The text supplies no such declaration. Compare Galvan v. Press, 347 U.S. 522, 526 (1954) (construing 1952 Act to reach only post-enactment conduct).

Even if the provision were read to apply, its operation would be impermissibly retroactive. A statute that “takes away or impairs vested rights acquired under existing laws” is void. Society for the Propagation of the Gospel v. Wheeler, 22 F. Cas. 756, 767 (C.C.D.N.H. 1814) (Story, J.). Petitioner’s long residence and marriage created settled expectations that the new statute destroys. See Harisiades v. Shaughnessy, 342 U.S. 580, 593 (1952) (recognizing that deportation may rest on past acts only when the statute so provides in terms); Lehmann v. United States ex rel. Carson, 353 U.S. 685, 690 (1957) (same).

The Tenth Circuit’s reliance on the absence of a “protectable expectation” contradicts settled doctrine. Rights are not measured by the alien’s subjective hope but by the legal consequences the statute attaches to completed acts. See Bridges v. Wixon, 326 U.S. 135, 157 (1945) (deportation proceedings must respect “the safeguards of procedural due process”); Ng Fung Ho v. White, 259 U.S. 276, 284 (1922) (same). Retroactive application here imposes a new disability on conduct lawful when undertaken. See Calder v. Bull, 3 Dall. 386, 390 (1798).

Congress knows how to speak expressly when it intends retroactive effect. See Marcello v. Bonds, 349 U.S. 302, 306-07 (1955) (1952 Act applied only because Congress so stated). No comparable language appears here. The statute therefore cannot be applied to reentries completed before April 1, 1997.

The judgment should be reversed.
