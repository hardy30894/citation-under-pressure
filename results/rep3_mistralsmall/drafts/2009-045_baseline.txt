**ARGUMENT**

I. The District Court Did Not Abuse Its Discretion in Enhancing the Lodestar Based on Exceptional Performance and Results.

The Eleventh Circuit correctly affirmed the district court’s award of attorneys’ fees, including the enhancement for superior performance and exceptional results. This Court has long recognized that “[t]he lodestar figure includes most, if not all, of the relevant factors constituting a ‘reasonable’ attorney’s fee,” but it has never held that enhancement is categorically prohibited. *Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air (“Delaware Valley I”)*, 478 U.S. 546, 566 (1986). Rather, the Court has repeatedly acknowledged that in “rare” and “exceptional” cases, an upward adjustment may be appropriate where the lodestar fails to reflect the quality of representation or the results achieved. *Id.* at 565; *Blum v. Stenson*, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984).

The district court here made detailed findings—supported by the record—that this case warranted enhancement. It explained that plaintiffs’ counsel achieved “exceptional success” in securing comprehensive injunctive relief for a vulnerable class of foster children, and that their performance was “stellar” throughout the litigation. *Kenny A. ex rel. Winn v. Perdue*, 454 F. Supp. 2d 1260, 1286, 1290 (N.D. Ga. 2006). The court’s 58 years of experience as a practitioner and jurist provided a sound basis for its assessment. *Id.* at 1286. Such findings are entitled to deference. *See Delaware Valley I*, 478 U.S. at 564 (noting that district courts are “uniquely qualified” to evaluate attorney performance).

The Eleventh Circuit properly deferred to these findings. It held that the district court did not abuse its discretion in enhancing the lodestar by $4.5 million, given the “exceptional nature of the results” and the “superior quality of counsel’s representation.” *Kenny A. ex rel. Winn v. Perdue*, 532 F.3d 1209, 1242 (11th Cir. 2008). This conclusion is consistent with binding Supreme Court precedent. *See Delaware Valley I*, 478 U.S. at 565-66; *Blum*, 465 U.S. at 899.

II. The Lodestar Enhancement Was Properly Tethered to the Statutory Purpose of Fee-Shifting Provisions.

Fee-shifting statutes like 42 U.S.C. § 1988 are designed to ensure that plaintiffs can obtain competent counsel to vindicate important rights. *Delaware Valley I*, 478 U.S. at 561. The lodestar method—hours reasonably expended multiplied by a reasonable hourly rate—is a “presumptively reasonable” starting point. *Blum*, 465 U.S. at 897. But the Court has never suggested that the lodestar is the *exclusive* measure of a reasonable fee. *See id.* at 901 (noting that “an upward adjustment to an attorney’s fee is never appropriate” was rejected). Where, as here, the lodestar fails to account for the exceptional results obtained or the extraordinary skill deployed, enhancement is permissible. *Delaware Valley I*, 478 U.S. at 565-66.

The district court’s enhancement was not arbitrary. It relied on specific evidence in the record—including the scope of relief, the complexity of the litigation, and the court’s firsthand observation of counsel’s performance. *Kenny A. III*, 454 F. Supp. 2d at 1286, 1290. This is precisely the kind of “specific evidence” the Supreme Court required in *Blum*. 465 U.S. at 899. The Eleventh Circuit correctly upheld the award. *Kenny A. IV*, 532 F.3d at 1242.

III. The Concerns Raised by the Dissent Are Unfounded.

The dissent’s suggestion that the district court improperly “testified” ex parte is meritless. *See* 532 F.3d at 1251 (Tjoflat, J., dissenting). The court’s observations were part of its reasoned analysis, not impermissible testimony. *Kenny A. III*, 454 F. Supp. 2d at 1286. Moreover, the Eleventh Circuit’s decision is consistent with every other circuit to have considered this issue. *See, e.g.*, *Geier v. Sundquist*, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); *Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mut. Life Co.*, 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000). There is no circuit split.

**Conclusion**

The district court’s fee award, including the enhancement, was fully supported by the record and consistent with governing precedent. This Court should affirm the Eleventh Circuit’s decision.
