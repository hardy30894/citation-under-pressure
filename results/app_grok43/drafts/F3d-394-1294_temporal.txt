The marital deduction under I.R.C. § 2056(a) is unavailable for terminable interests unless the surviving spouse receives an unqualified right to all trust income for life. Jackson v. United States, 376 U.S. 503, 506 (1964). The Amendment confers no such right. Its trustee may distribute net income only “as the trustee, in the trustee’s reasonable discretion, shall determine to be proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare” of the spouse. This language limits distributions to an ascertainable standard rather than granting an absolute entitlement to every dollar of income.

Section 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii) requires that the spouse be “entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals.” The Amendment’s discretionary clause violates that command. See United States v. Stapf, 375 U.S. 118, 125 (1963) (deduction denied where surviving spouse’s interest was subject to conditions that could divert income or principal). The trustee’s power to consider the spouse’s other resources before making distributions further erodes any claim of unrestricted entitlement. Northeastern Pa. Nat’l Bank & Trust Co. v. United States, 387 U.S. 213, 219 (1967).

Because the surviving spouse lacks a qualifying income interest for life, the trust property cannot constitute qualified terminable interest property. The Tax Court therefore correctly disallowed the deduction.
