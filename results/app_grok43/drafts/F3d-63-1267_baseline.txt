ARGUMENT

I. Stelwagon Failed to Prove Actual Antitrust Damages Recoverable Under Section 4 of the Clayton Act.

A plaintiff seeking treble damages under section 4 of the Clayton Act, 15 U.S.C. § 15(a), “must prove actual damages.” J. Truett Payne Co. v. Chrysler Motors Corp., 451 U.S. 557, 562 (1981). This requirement flows from the statute’s text, which authorizes recovery only of “the damages by him sustained.” Id. at 561 (quoting 15 U.S.C. § 15(a)). Proof of a Robinson-Patman violation alone does not satisfy the damages element. See id. at 562 (“[T]he fact that a violation . . . may have occurred does not necessarily mean that the plaintiff has suffered antitrust injury.”).

To recover, a plaintiff must demonstrate “some evidence of actual injury attributable to the violation.” Id. at 563. Lost profits must be shown “as a matter of fact and with reasonable certainty.” Story Parchment Co. v. Paterson Parchment Paper Co., 282 U.S. 555, 563 (1931). Speculation or conjecture is insufficient. Bigelow v. RKO Radio Pictures, Inc., 327 U.S. 251, 264 (1946).

Stelwagon’s evidence consisted solely of a comparison between prices paid by its competitors and its own prices, without any showing that the differential caused measurable lost sales or profits. Such a showing is inadequate under J. Truett Payne. See 451 U.S. at 565 (mere overcharge evidence does not establish injury when the plaintiff may have passed on the higher cost). The district court’s remittitur order itself recognized that the jury’s award rested on “unsupported” evidence. Stelwagon Mfg. Co. v. Tarmac Roofing Sys., Inc., 862 F. Supp. 1361, 1369 (E.D. Pa. 1994).

Because Stelwagon presented no competent proof of actual antitrust injury, the treble-damages judgment cannot stand. The Robinson-Patman verdict should be vacated insofar as it awards damages under section 4.
