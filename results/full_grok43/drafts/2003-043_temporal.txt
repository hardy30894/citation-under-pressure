ARGUMENT

The Internal Revenue Code requires that an assessment identify the taxpayer against whom it is made. 26 U.S.C. § 6203. An individual partner is a distinct taxpayer from the partnership. 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(1), (14). Consequently, an assessment against the partnership alone does not extend the ten-year collection period of 26 U.S.C. § 6502(a) to the partners.

This Court has long recognized that an assessment operates only against the person or entity named in it. In United States v. Coson, 286 F.2d 453 (9th Cir. 1961), the court held that a tax lien could not attach to an individual’s property unless the underlying obligation had been assessed against him under § 6203. The assessment there named only the partnership and “an unknown number of unidentified taxpayers,” rendering collection efforts against the partner procedurally defective. Id. at 458.

The same principle controls here. The timely assessments against Marina Cabrillo Partners extended the limitations period only for that taxpayer. Because no assessment was made against Debtors within the three-year period of 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a), the ordinary limitations period governs and bars collection.

State-law joint-and-several liability does not alter this result. In Young v. Riddell, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir. 1960), this Court affirmed that a partner remains substantively liable for partnership tax debts under state law, yet the opinion expressly noted that collection without an individual assessment or judgment requires resort to court action. Id. at 910. The district court in the same case had observed that naming the partner was necessary “to enable collection of the tax without resorting to court action.” Young v. Riddell, 60-1 U.S. Tax Cas. (CCH) ¶ 9831, at 76,050 (S.D. Cal. 1959). No such judgment was obtained here, and the time to do so has expired.

The bankruptcy court therefore properly disallowed the claims under 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1) as unenforceable against Debtors.
