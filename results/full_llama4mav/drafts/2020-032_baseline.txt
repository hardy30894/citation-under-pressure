The Eleventh Circuit correctly held that a constitutional challenge to a school policy seeking nominal damages is rendered moot when the unconstitutional policy is revised during litigation. As this Court held in Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc., 568 U.S. 85 (2013), a case becomes moot when the parties no longer have a legally cognizable interest in the outcome. Id. at 91. Here, Georgia Gwinnett College (GGC) revised its "Freedom of Expression Policy" and "Student Code of Conduct" to address the concerns raised by Uzuegbunam and Bradford, rendering their claims for declaratory and injunctive relief moot.

The Eleventh Circuit's decision in Flanigan's Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs, 868 F.3d 1248 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc), controls this case. In Flanigan's, the court held that a prayer for nominal damages generally will not save an otherwise moot challenge to an allegedly unconstitutional policy or law. Id. at 1265-66. Uzuegbunam and Bradford's complaint, which sought only nominal damages in addition to declaratory and injunctive relief, is precisely the type of case that Flanigan's addressed.

Uzuegbunam and Bradford argue that their case is distinguishable from Flanigan's because it involves an as-applied challenge to GGC's policies. However, this argument was not presented to the district court, and therefore, it is not properly before this Court. See Access Now, Inc. v. Southwest Airlines Co., 385 F.3d 1324, 1331 (11th Cir. 2004) ("This Court has repeatedly held that an issue not raised in the district court and raised for the first time in an appeal will not be considered.").

Furthermore, the district court did not abuse its discretion in denying Uzuegbunam and Bradford's request to amend their complaint. As the Eleventh Circuit has held, a request to amend a complaint must be made through a properly filed motion, not through a response to a motion to dismiss. Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018). Uzuegbunam and Bradford failed to follow this procedure, and the district court's dismissal of their complaint without prejudice did not prevent them from filing a new lawsuit with a properly amended complaint.

In conclusion, the Eleventh Circuit correctly applied Flanigan's to hold that Uzuegbunam and Bradford's constitutional challenge to GGC's policies is moot. The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying their request to amend their complaint, and Uzuegbunam and Bradford's new arguments on appeal are not properly before this Court. Therefore, the judgment of the Eleventh Circuit should be affirmed.
