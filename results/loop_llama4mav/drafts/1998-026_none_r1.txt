The issue presented in this case is whether a District Court's failure to advise a defendant of his right to appeal, as required by the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, provides ground for habeas relief when the defendant was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The respondent respectfully submits that it does not.

The Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, specifically Rule 32(a)(2), mandate that the court advise a defendant of his right to appeal after sentencing. However, the failure to comply with this rule does not automatically entitle a defendant to habeas relief. As the Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit correctly held, a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review, a principle supported by precedent. (See United States v. Daddato, 160 F.2d 695, 697 (7th Cir. 1947); United States v. Paglia, 190 F.2d 445, 448 (2d Cir. 1951)).

The District Court's finding that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing, despite the court's failure to advise him, is consistent with the principle that a defendant's knowledge of his rights can cure a procedural error. (See Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 464 (1938)). The purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that a defendant is aware of his right to appeal (United States v. Bazzano, 146 F.2d 923, 925 (3rd Cir. 1945)), and when a defendant is already aware of this right, as Peguero was, the rule's purpose is served.

The harmless-error doctrine, as established in Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750, 764-65 (1946), holds that an error is harmless if it does not affect the outcome of the case. In this instance, Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal and his decision not to exercise it render the District Court's failure to advise him harmless. The Supreme Court has long recognized that not all procedural errors require reversal (Snyder v. Massachusetts, 291 U.S. 97, 116-17 (1934)), and a defendant's failure to show prejudice resulting from an error is a significant factor in determining whether the error is harmless (Glasser v. United States, 315 U.S. 60, 83 (1942)). Peguero has not demonstrated any prejudice resulting from the District Court's failure to advise him of his right to appeal.

Furthermore, the Court has emphasized the importance of a defendant's knowledge and understanding of his rights in determining the validity of a waiver or the impact of a procedural error. (See Adams v. United States ex rel. McCann, 317 U.S. 269, 278 (1942); Von Moltke v. Gillies, 332 U.S. 708, 723-24 (1948)). In this case, Peguero's awareness of his right to appeal and his decision not to appeal demonstrate that the District Court's error did not affect his substantial rights.

In conclusion, the respondent submits that the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal does not provide ground for habeas relief when Peguero was aware of his right and elected not to appeal. The Court of Appeals' decision affirming the District Court's denial of habeas relief should be upheld, as it is consistent with the principles established in the cited authorities, including the harmless-error doctrine and the importance of a defendant's knowledge and understanding of his rights.
