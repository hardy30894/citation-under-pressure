**ARGUMENT**

The Railroad Retirement Board’s decision declining to reopen Petitioner Manfredo M. Salinas’s 2006 application for a disability annuity is not a “final decision” subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). This Court has long held that a Board decision refusing to reopen a prior benefits claim does not constitute a final decision within the meaning of the Railroad Retirement Act’s judicial review provision. The Fifth Circuit correctly applied binding precedent in dismissing Salinas’s petition for review, and this Court should affirm.

Section 355(f) of the Railroad Retirement Act provides that “the United States courts of appeals shall have jurisdiction to review final decisions of the Board.” 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Supreme Court has made clear that “finality” under this provision is strictly defined: “Only those decisions that resolve the merits of a claim for benefits are reviewable.” *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. 99, 107 (1977). The Court emphasized that “the statutory scheme contemplates review of decisions on the merits, not administrative decisions about whether to reopen a prior claim.” Id. at 108. This principle has been consistently applied by the courts of appeals.

In *Roberts v. U.S. Railroad Retirement Board*, 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), this Court held that a decision by the Board declining to reopen a prior claim is not a “final decision” within the meaning of § 355(f). The panel observed that “we have no jurisdiction [under section 355(f)] to review the Board’s decision not to reopen a prior claim for benefits,” and joined the majority of circuits—including the Fourth, Sixth, Seventh, and Tenth Circuits—in reaching this conclusion. *Id.* at 140. The Court found the reasoning of those circuits persuasive, particularly in light of *Califano v. Sanders*, which established that judicial review under the Act is limited to decisions that adjudicate the merits of a claim. *Id.* at 141.

Salinas attempts to distinguish *Roberts* by invoking the D.C. Circuit’s decision in *Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board*, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016), which concluded that a decision not to reopen is reviewable. However, *Stovic* is not binding on this Court, and its reasoning is inconsistent with the settled precedent of this circuit. More importantly, *Stovic* itself acknowledged the existence of a “circuit split” on the issue and recognized that “the majority of circuits have concluded that a decision not to reopen is not reviewable.” *Id.* at 503. The D.C. Circuit’s departure from the majority view does not compel this Court to abandon its own precedent.

The rule of orderliness, which governs the operation of circuit precedent, prohibits this Court from reconsidering *Roberts* in the absence of a change in controlling law or a direct conflict with Supreme Court precedent. *Vaughan v. Anderson Reg’l Med. Ctr.*, 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir. 2017), cert. denied, 138 S. Ct. 101 (2017). The panel in *Roberts* carefully analyzed the statutory text, legislative history, and binding authority, and concluded that the Board’s decision not to reopen is an administrative determination that does not resolve the merits of a claim. That conclusion remains sound.

Moreover, the Board’s decision not to reopen is not a “final decision” in the administrative law sense. It is a discretionary, non-merits determination that does not finally dispose of the claimant’s right to benefits. As the Supreme Court has stated, “a decision is not final if it does not end the litigation on the merits.” *Burlington Northern R.R. Co. v. United States*, 405 U.S. 1000, 1003 (1972). The Board’s refusal to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application did not determine whether he was entitled to benefits; it merely declined to reconsider a prior determination that had already become final. The claim remains open only through the filing of a new application, which Salinas did in 2013. The Board’s subsequent grant of benefits on that new application confirms that the merits of his claim were ultimately adjudicated—just not on the basis of the 2006 application.

To hold otherwise would undermine the administrative efficiency and finality principles that underlie the Railroad Retirement Act. Allowing judicial review of every decision not to reopen would subject the Board to endless litigation over procedural matters, burdening the administrative process and frustrating the Act’s goal of providing timely and certain benefits. *See* *Califano v. Sanders*, 430 U.S. at 108 (“The administrative process must be allowed to function without constant judicial interference.”).

For these reasons, the Board’s decision not to reopen Salinas’s 2006 application is not a “final decision” within the meaning of 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). The Fifth Circuit correctly dismissed the petition for review under binding precedent. This Court should affirm.
