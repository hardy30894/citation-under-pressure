ARGUMENT

I. The IRS Must Separately Assess Each Partner to Extend the Ten-Year Collection Period Under 26 U.S.C. § 6502(a).

Section 6203 requires that an assessment “be made by recording the liability of the taxpayer.” 26 U.S.C. § 6203. A “taxpayer” is “any person subject to any internal revenue tax,” and “person” expressly includes both “an individual” and a “partnership.” 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(14), (a)(1). Because each partner is a distinct taxpayer, an assessment naming only the partnership cannot extend the limitations period for collection from the partners. United States v. Coson, 286 F.2d 453, 458 (9th Cir. 1961) (lien invalid where assessment failed to identify the individual taxpayer as required by § 6203 and its implementing regulation).

The regulation confirms that a valid assessment “shall provide identification of the taxpayer.” 26 C.F.R. § 301.6203-1. Section 6502(a) likewise extends the collection period only “after the assessment of the tax” against the assessed taxpayer. The Ninth Circuit correctly held that the assessment against Marina Cabrillo Partners extended the ten-year period solely with respect to the partnership. Galletti v. United States (In re Galletti), 314 F.3d 336, 340-42 (9th Cir. 2002).

II. California Partnership Law Does Not Authorize Collection Absent a Separate Assessment or Judgment.

Under California law, “[a] judgment against a partnership is not by itself a judgment against a partner. A judgment against a partnership may not be satisfied from a partner’s assets unless there is also a judgment against the partner.” Cal. Corp. Code § 16307(c). The IRS obtained neither an assessment nor a judgment against respondents. The three-year period for obtaining such a judgment has expired. 26 U.S.C. § 6501(a). Therefore, the claims are “unenforceable against the debtor” and were properly disallowed. 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(1).

Young v. Riddell, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir. 1960), does not alter this result. There the partner had already paid the tax; the court merely refused a refund. Id. at 910. The opinion expressly recognized that collection without an individual assessment or judgment would require “resort[] to court action.” Young v. Riddell, 60-1 U.S. Tax Cas. (CCH) ¶ 9831, at 76,050 (S.D. Cal. 1959), aff’d, 283 F.2d 909 (9th Cir. 1960). No such action was timely brought here.

The Seventh Circuit’s decision in United States v. Wright, 57 F.3d 561 (7th Cir. 1995), is inapposite because the IRS had assessed both the partnership and the individual partners. Id. at 562; United States v. Wright, 868 F. Supp. 1070, 1071 & n.1 (S.D. Ind. 1994). No published Ninth Circuit authority supports the IRS’s contrary position.

III. Conclusion

Because the IRS assessed only the partnership, the ten-year extension of § 6502(a) does not apply to respondents. The bankruptcy court correctly disallowed the claims.
