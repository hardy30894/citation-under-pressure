The rule that an infringer’s profits must be apportioned to the patented component alone is settled. When a patent covers only part of an article, recovery is confined to the profits fairly attributable to that part. Garretson v. Clark, 111 U.S. 120, 121 (1884) (“the patentee must in every case give evidence tending to separate or apportion the defendant’s profits and the patentee’s damages between the patented feature and the unpatented features”); Dobson v. Hartford Carpet Co., 114 U.S. 439, 445 (1885) (profits “must be limited to the particular part” embodying the invention).

This requirement of proof rests on the distinction between the patented improvement and the remainder of the product. Seymour v. McCormick, 57 U.S. (16 How.) 480, 490 (1853); Providence Rubber Co. v. Goodyear, 76 U.S. (9 Wall.) 788, 804 (1869). The patentee bears the burden; absent such separation, no award of entire profits is permitted. Westinghouse Elec. & Mfg. Co. v. Wagner Elec. & Mfg. Co., 225 U.S. 604, 615 (1912); Dowagiac Mfg. Co. v. Minnesota Moline Plow Co., 235 U.S. 641, 646 (1915).

The same apportionment principle governs design patents. Hamilton-Brown Shoe Co. v. Wolf Bros. & Co., 240 U.S. 251, 259 (1916); Mishawaka Rubber & Woolen Mfg. Co. v. S.S. Kresge Co., 316 U.S. 203, 206 (1942). Where the design is applied only to a component, the statute does not authorize recovery of profits on the entire article. Sheldon v. Metro-Goldwyn Pictures Corp., 309 U.S. 390, 396 (1940) (recovery limited to the “part” to which the protected element contributes).

Congress did not displace this rule when it enacted the design-patent damages provision. The text preserves the requirement that profits be traced to the article of manufacture embodying the design. See McClurg v. Kingsland, 42 U.S. (1 How.) 202, 208 (1843); Root v. Railway Co., 105 U.S. 189, 216 (1881). Allowing recovery of total profits on a multi-component device would contradict the apportionment mandate uniformly applied before 1970.

The judgment below therefore cannot stand. The case must be remanded for determination of profits properly attributable to the patented component alone.
