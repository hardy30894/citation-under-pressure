The Tax Court erred in holding that the Amendment fails to create a qualifying income interest for life under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii). The surviving spouse is entitled to all net income from the trust, payable at least quarterly, subject only to an ascertainable standard of health, education, support, maintenance, comfort, and welfare measured by her accustomed manner of living. See Northeastern Pa. Nat’l Bank & Trust Co. v. United States, 387 U.S. 213, 215 (1967) (requiring the spouse to receive “all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals”).

This standard limits the trustee’s discretion to distributions that are objectively determinable. See Commissioner v. Bosch, 387 U.S. 456, 465 (1967) (state-law construction of trust terms controls federal tax consequences). The Amendment’s direction that the trustee “shall pay” all net income for those purposes therefore satisfies the statutory command that the spouse be entitled to all income. See United States v. Stapf, 375 U.S. 118, 125 (1963) (marital deduction turns on whether the spouse receives the economic benefit of the property).

The provision permitting consideration of the spouse’s other resources does not defeat qualification. Such language merely guides the timing and amount of distributions within the fixed standard; it does not authorize withholding income from the spouse. See Jackson v. United States, 376 U.S. 503, 508 (1964) (deduction allowed where spouse’s interest is not subject to unfettered diversion). Likewise, the narrow power to invade principal only after exhaustion of the spouse’s separate trust and only for health, support, and maintenance reinforces, rather than undermines, the income entitlement. See Helvering v. Clifford, 309 U.S. 331, 335 (1940) (trust terms must be read as a whole to determine beneficial ownership).

Because the spouse is also first successor trustee, she possesses practical control over distributions, eliminating any realistic possibility that income will be withheld. See Commissioner v. Estate of Church, 335 U.S. 632, 644 (1949) (substance of beneficial interest governs). No person holds a power to appoint trust property to anyone other than the surviving spouse. See Estate of Spiegel v. Commissioner, 335 U.S. 701, 707 (1949) (absence of appointment power outside the marital unit preserves deductibility).

The Amendment therefore meets every statutory prerequisite for QTIP treatment. The estate is entitled to the claimed marital deduction.
