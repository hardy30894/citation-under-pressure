The District Court's decision to invoke 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) to assign the transferred case to itself for trial was erroneous. Section 1407(a) explicitly mandates that the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation "shall" remand any action transferred for pretrial proceedings to the original district "at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." The plain language of the statute precludes the transferee court from retaining the case for trial.

The Ninth Circuit's decision in the instant case, while denying mandamus relief, acknowledged that the district court's order may not be clearly erroneous as a matter of law due to existing case law, such as In Re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation, 685 F.2d 810, 820 (3rd Cir.1982), and Pfizer v. Lord, 447 F.2d 122, 125 (2d Cir.1971). However, as Judge Kozinski's dissent noted, these cases have "simply disregarded the statutory mandate" and justified their departure on grounds of policy or convenience (61 F.3d 911, Kozinski, J., dissenting).

The statutory text of § 1407(a) is clear, and the practice of allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial under § 1404(a) is an "illicit practice" that has developed despite the statute's plain meaning (Id.). As the dissent pointed out, § 1407 reflects an important policy choice: cases may be transferred to remote locations, but only for the duration of pretrial procedures. When it comes to conducting a trial, plaintiffs retain their ancient right to select the forum.

The Court should reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision and hold that a federal district court conducting "pretrial proceedings" under 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) may not invoke § 1404(a) to assign a transferred case to itself for trial. The plain language of § 1407(a) and the policy choice it reflects require that the case be remanded to the original district for trial.
