**ARGUMENT**

The district court erred in dismissing this putative class action as moot based solely on Defendant’s unaccepted Rule 68 offer of full individual relief. A named plaintiff who diligently pursues class certification retains a sufficient personal stake in the certification decision, and the certification motion relates back to the filing of the complaint, preventing mootness. This Court reviews mootness *de novo*. *Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co.*, 590 F.3d 1134, 1138 (10th Cir. 2009).

**I. THE NAMED PLAINTIFF RETAINS A CONTINUING PERSONAL STAKE IN CLASS CERTIFICATION EVEN AFTER A FULL OFFER OF JUDGMENT.**

The Supreme Court has long recognized that a class-action plaintiff whose individual claim is satisfied may nonetheless pursue class certification. In *Deposit Guaranty Nat’l Bank v. Roper*, 445 U.S. 326 (1980), the Court held that a plaintiff who received a full offer of judgment could appeal the denial of class certification because “the interest in having the class certified is a sufficient personal stake to survive the satisfaction of the individual claim.” *Id.* at 339. Similarly, *U.S. Parole Comm’n v. Geraghty*, 445 U.S. 388 (1980), established that “a plaintiff who brings a class action presents a live controversy even if his own claim becomes moot” as long as the class certification issue remains justiciable. *Id.* at 399. The “right to have the class certified” is a separate legal interest that does not evaporate when the defendant attempts to buy off the named plaintiff. *Id.* at 403.

This principle directly controls here. Lucero filed a motion for class certification on February 22, 2010—before the district court ruled on Defendant’s motion to dismiss. He did so within the schedule set by the court and without undue delay. At that point, his personal stake in the certification decision was established, and Defendant’s earlier offer could not retroactively erase it. *See Sosna v. Iowa*, 419 U.S. 393, 402 n.11 (1975) (class certification may “relate back” to the filing of the complaint for mootness purposes).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT’S RELIANCE ON *CLARK* AND *REED* WAS MISPLACED.**

The district court invoked the general rule that “a suit brought as a class action must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified.” *Clark*, 590 F.3d at 1138 (internal quotation marks omitted). But that rule is not absolute. Both *Clark* and *Reed v. Heckler*, 756 F.2d 779 (10th Cir. 1985), involved plaintiffs who either accepted the offer or failed to move for class certification before the claim became moot. *See Clark*, 590 F.3d at 1137 (offer accepted); *Reed*, 756 F.2d at 782 (no certification motion filed). Here, Lucero neither accepted the offer nor delayed in seeking certification. The district court thus ignored the crucial distinction recognized in *Weiss v. Regal Collections*, 385 F.3d 337 (3d Cir. 2004), which held that “an unaccepted Rule 68 offer of judgment for the full amount of the named plaintiff’s individual claim does not moot a class action when the plaintiff has moved for class certification without undue delay.” *Id.* at 340.

**III. THE RELATION-BACK DOCTRINE PRESERVES JURISDICTION.**

The Supreme Court in *Sosna* explained that “the certification of a class may properly be deemed to relate back to the filing of the complaint so that the action is not rendered moot by the passage of time.” 419 U.S. at 402 n.11. This doctrine applies with full force where, as here, the named plaintiff files a certification motion before the defendant’s attempt to moot the individual claim. *See Geraghty*, 445 U.S. at 404 (relation back ensures that “mootness does not automatically deprive the plaintiff of the opportunity to litigate the class certification issue”). The Fifth Circuit applied this reasoning in *Zeidman v. J. Ray McDermott & Co.*, 651 F.2d 1030 (5th Cir. 1981), holding that “a named plaintiff’s claim is not moot when a motion for class certification is pending.” *Id.* at 1051. The Ninth Circuit reached the same conclusion in *Pitts v. Terrible Herbst, Inc.*, 653 F.3d 1081 (9th Cir. 2011), stating that “a defendant cannot moot a class action by tendering the full amount of the named plaintiff’s individual claim before the plaintiff has had a reasonable opportunity to move for class certification.” *Id.* at 1085.

**IV. DEFENDANT’S TACTIC SHOULD NOT BE REWARDED.**

Allowing Defendant to extinguish Lucero’s suit by a pre-certification Rule 38 offer would permit precisely the “pick-off” strategy that courts have uniformly condemned. *Weiss*, 385 F.3d at 343; *Zeidman*, 651 F.2d at 1051. The Supreme Court has warned against “permitting defendants to ‘buy off’ class actions by tendering judgments to the named plaintiffs before class certification.” *Roper*, 445 U.S. at 330 n.2. The district court’s ruling frustrates the purposes of Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23 and the FDCPA by allowing defendants to evade meaningful classwide liability.

**CONCLUSION**

This Court should reverse the dismissal and remand for consideration of Lucero’s class certification motion.
