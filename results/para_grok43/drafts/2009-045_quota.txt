ARGUMENT

Federal fee-shifting statutes permit enhancement of the lodestar in rare and exceptional cases where specific evidence demonstrates that superior performance produced exceptional results. Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 899 (1984), held that “the quality of representation” and “results obtained” may justify an upward adjustment when the fee applicant offers “specific evidence” that the services rendered were “superior to that one reasonably should expect in light of the hourly rates charged” and yielded “exceptional success.” The Court reiterated that “in some cases of exceptional success an enhancement award may be justified.” Id. at 901 (quoting Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424, 435 (1983)).

Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens’ Council for Clean Air, 478 U.S. 546 (1986), confirmed the same rule. The Court stated that “upward adjustments of the lodestar figure are still permissible … in certain ‘rare’ and ‘exceptional’ cases, supported by both ‘specific evidence’ on the record and detailed findings by the lower courts.” Id. at 565 (quoting Blum, 465 U.S. at 898-901). Although the lodestar ordinarily subsumes most factors, the opinion left open the possibility of enhancement when those factors are not fully reflected in the lodestar. Id. at 566.

The Eleventh Circuit has consistently applied this authority. Norman v. Housing Authority of Montgomery, 836 F.2d 1292, 1302 (11th Cir. 1988), recognized that “superior results coupled with superior performance can be the basis for an enhancement.” NAACP v. City of Evergreen, 812 F.2d 1332, 1336-37 (11th Cir. 1987) (per curiam), likewise held that evidence of exceptional success may support an upward adjustment.

Every other circuit to address the question has reached the identical conclusion. Geier v. Sundquist, 372 F.3d 784, 794-95 (6th Cir. 2004); Van Gerwen v. Guarantee Mutual Life Co., 214 F.3d 1041, 1046 (9th Cir. 2000); Quaratino v. Tiffany & Co., 166 F.3d 422, 425 (2d Cir. 1999); Forshee v. Waterloo Industries, Inc., 178 F.3d 527, 532 (8th Cir. 1999); Hyatt v. Apfel, 195 F.3d 188, 192 (4th Cir. 1999); Roe v. Cheyenne Mountain Conference Resort, Inc., 124 F.3d 1221, 1233 n.8 (10th Cir. 1997); Shipes v. Trinity Industries, 987 F.2d 311, 320 (5th Cir. 1993). Each decision requires the same showing of specific evidence that Blum and Delaware Valley I demand.

Here the district court made precisely the findings those precedents require. Its detailed, on-the-record observations of counsel’s performance and the unprecedented scope of relief obtained supplied the “specific evidence” and “detailed findings” that permit enhancement. Because the award rests on that evidentiary foundation, it is consistent with the governing Supreme Court authority and with the uniform view of the courts of appeals.
