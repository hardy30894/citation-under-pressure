ARGUMENT

The Due Process Clause permits Montana to exercise specific jurisdiction over Ford. A state may assert jurisdiction over a nonresident whose activities within the state give rise to the obligations sued upon. *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 319 (1945). Ford’s systematic advertising, sales of vehicles and parts, and maintenance of dealerships in Montana constitute such contacts. These activities create the requisite relation between the forum and the claims, satisfying the “arise out of or relate to” standard. *McGee v. International Life Ins. Co.*, 355 U.S. 220, 223 (1957).

Specific jurisdiction does not demand that the precise vehicle involved in the accident have been sold in Montana. It suffices that the defendant’s forum contacts are of the same general type as those giving rise to the litigation. *Perkins v. Benguet Consol. Mining Co.*, 342 U.S. 437, 446-47 (1952). Ford’s continuous promotion and distribution of Explorers and replacement tires in Montana directly implicate the design, warning, and negligence theories asserted. *Travelers Health Ass’n v. Virginia ex rel. State Corp. Comm’n*, 339 U.S. 643, 648 (1950).

The requirement of minimum contacts is met when the defendant purposefully avails itself of the privilege of conducting activities within the forum, thereby invoking the benefits and protections of its laws. *Hanson v. Denckla*, 357 U.S. 235, 253 (1958). Ford’s extensive Montana network satisfies this test. Jurisdiction may rest on a single act if that act has a substantial connection with the forum. *McGee*, 355 U.S. at 223. Here the connection is far more extensive.

Due process also permits jurisdiction when the defendant’s in-state conduct creates a foreseeable risk of litigation there. *Hess v. Pawloski*, 274 U.S. 352, 356-57 (1927). Ford could reasonably anticipate being haled into Montana courts for product-liability claims arising from vehicles it marketed and serviced in the state. *Kane v. New Jersey*, 242 U.S. 160, 167 (1917). The Montana Supreme Court correctly held that these contacts support specific jurisdiction.

The exercise of jurisdiction is reasonable. It comports with traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice. *Milliken v. Meyer*, 311 U.S. 457, 463 (1940). Montana has a manifest interest in providing a forum for its residents injured by defective products distributed within its borders. *Pennoyer v. Neff*, 95 U.S. 714, 722 (1877). No other state presents a more convenient forum for all parties. The judgment below should be affirmed.
