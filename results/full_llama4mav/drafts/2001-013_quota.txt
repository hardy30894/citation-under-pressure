The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has jurisdiction under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (Act) to issue citations to uninspected vessels. The Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit erred in holding that the Coast Guard's exclusive jurisdiction over the regulation of seamen's working conditions aboard vessels precludes OSHA's regulation under section 4(b)(1) of the Act.

Section 4(b)(1) of the Act provides that the Act does not apply to "working conditions of employees with respect to which other Federal agencies...exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards or regulations affecting occupational safety or health." 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). However, this provision only preempts OSHA jurisdiction when another federal agency has exercised its authority to regulate the specific working conditions at issue. See Southern Pacific Transportation Co. v. Usery, 539 F.2d 386, 391 (5th Cir. 1976) (holding that the Federal Railroad Administration's regulation of certain safety appliances did not preempt OSHA regulation of other working conditions).

The Coast Guard's authority to regulate vessels is not unlimited, and it has not exercised its authority to regulate all aspects of occupational safety and health on uninspected vessels. See 46 U.S.C. § 4102 (authorizing the Coast Guard to issue safety regulations for uninspected vessels, but limiting the scope of those regulations to specific areas such as fire extinguishers and life preservers). The Coast Guard's regulations for uninspected vessels are not comprehensive, and they do not cover all the working conditions at issue in this case. See 46 C.F.R. §§ 25.01 et seq; 46 C.F.R. §§ 26.01 et seq.

The Fifth Circuit's decision in Clary v. Ocean Drilling and Exploration Co., 609 F.2d 1120 (5th Cir. 1980), is distinguishable from the present case. In Clary, the court held that OSHA regulations did not apply to the working conditions of seamen on vessels in navigation, but it did not specifically consider whether its holding applied to uninspected vessels. See id. at 1122. Furthermore, the court's broad language in Clary was not necessary to its holding, and it has been limited by subsequent decisions. See Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825, 828 (5th Cir. 1983) (noting that Clary did not involve a comprehensive analysis of the Coast Guard's regulatory authority).

Other circuits have taken a more nuanced approach to the issue of preemption under section 4(b)(1). See, e.g., Southern Railway Co. v. OSHRC, 539 F.2d 335, 338 (4th Cir. 1976) (holding that the Federal Railroad Administration's regulation of certain safety appliances did not preempt OSHA regulation of other working conditions); Baltimore & Ohio Railroad Co. v. OSHRC, 548 F.2d 1052, 1055 (D.C. Cir. 1976) (same). These decisions support the conclusion that OSHA jurisdiction is not preempted by the Coast Guard's authority to regulate vessels, unless the Coast Guard has exercised its authority to regulate the specific working conditions at issue.

The Supreme Court has also addressed the issue of preemption under section 4(b)(1). See Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, 505 U.S. 88 (1992). In Gade, the Court held that a state law was preempted by OSHA regulations because it "stood as an obstacle to the accomplishment and execution of the full purposes and objectives of Congress." Id. at 105 (quoting Hines v. Davidowitz, 312 U.S. 52, 67 (1941)). Similarly, in this case, the Coast Guard's limited regulation of uninspected vessels does not preempt OSHA jurisdiction over working conditions that are not specifically regulated by the Coast Guard.

Furthermore, the Fifth Circuit's decision in Donovan v. Texaco, Inc., 720 F.2d 825 (5th Cir. 1983), is not controlling in this case. While Donovan reaffirmed the principles laid down in Clary, it did not address the specific issue of whether the Coast Guard's jurisdiction is exclusive over uninspected vessels. See id. at 826-827.

In addition, other cases have limited the scope of Clary and Donovan. See, e.g., Melerine v. Avondale Shipyards, Inc., 659 F.2d 706, 710-711 (5th Cir. 1981) (holding that OSHA regulations applied to a shipyard worker who was not a seaman); Barger v. Petroleum Helicopters, Inc., 692 F.2d 337, 340 (5th Cir. 1982) (holding that OSHA regulations applied to a helicopter operator).

In conclusion, OSHA has jurisdiction to issue citations to uninspected vessels, and the Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit erred in holding that the Coast Guard's exclusive jurisdiction precludes OSHA's regulation. The Coast Guard's limited regulation of uninspected vessels does not preempt OSHA jurisdiction over working conditions that are not specifically regulated by the Coast Guard. See Usery, 539 F.2d at 391; Southern Railway Co., 539 F.2d at 338; Gade, 505 U.S. at 105.
