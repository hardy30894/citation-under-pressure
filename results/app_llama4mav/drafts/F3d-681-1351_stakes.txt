ARGUMENT

I. THE BOARD ERRED IN GRANTING HARARI THE BENEFIT OF THE '566 APPLICATION'S FILING DATE

The issue before this Court is whether the Board correctly determined that Harari's '880 application is entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date. To resolve this issue, we must examine whether the intervening applications in the priority chain - specifically, the '838 and '768 applications - sufficiently incorporated the '579 application by reference, thereby satisfying 35 U.S.C. § 120's continuity requirements.

The Federal Circuit has established that to incorporate a reference by reference, the host application must clearly identify the incorporated reference. See Advanced Display Sys., Inc. v. Kent State Univ., 212 F.3d 1272, 1282 (Fed. Cir. 2000) ("To incorporate material by reference, the host document must identify with detailed particularity what specific material it incorporates and clearly indicate where that material is found in the various documents incorporated."). The incorporation by reference doctrine is strictly construed, and the material to be incorporated must be clearly and unambiguously identified. See id.

In this case, the incorporation statement in the '566 application, which was copied into the subsequent applications, referred to a copending application "filed on the same day as the present application" by Sanjay Mehrotra and Dr. Eliyahou Harari. J.A. 1204. Although this Court previously held that a reasonable examiner would understand that this language referred to the '579 application in the context of the '880 application, see Harari I, 602 F.3d at 1353-54, the issue now is whether the same language in the intervening '838 and '768 applications sufficiently incorporated the '579 application.

The Board erred in concluding that it did. The '838 and '768 applications were filed years after the '566 application, and the '579 application's status as a copending application "filed on the same day" as the '566 application was no longer accurate. As a result, the incorporation statement in these applications was ambiguous and did not clearly identify the '579 application.

This Court's decision in Harari I does not control the outcome here. In Harari I, the Court applied a narrow rule for reviewing incorporation by reference statements at the "initial filing stage." See Harari I, 602 F.3d at 1352-53. However, the issue now is whether the incorporation by reference was sufficient to satisfy 35 U.S.C. § 120's continuity requirements, not whether the preliminary amendment was new matter. The standard for evaluating incorporation by reference under § 120 is more stringent, requiring clear and unambiguous identification of the incorporated material. See Advanced Display Sys., 212 F.3d at 1282.

Because the '838 and '768 applications did not sufficiently incorporate the '579 application by reference, the '880 application is not entitled to the benefit of the '566 application's filing date. The Board's contrary conclusion was erroneous, and this Court should reverse.

II. CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the Board's decision granting Harari the benefit of the '566 application's filing date.
