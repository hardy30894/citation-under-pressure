I. The District Court Erred in Admitting Non-Expert Opinion Disguised as Expert Testimony.

The district court permitted American Steamship’s witness to opine on the cause of the line’s parting, yet the opinion rested solely on observations any layperson could make. Expert testimony is admissible only when it “will assist the trier of fact to understand the evidence or to determine a fact in issue.” Fed. R. Evid. 702; see Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharm., Inc., 509 U.S. 579, 591 (1993). An opinion lacking specialized knowledge invades the jury’s province. Salem v. United States Lines Co., 370 U.S. 31, 35 (1962). The admission therefore constituted an abuse of discretion requiring a new trial. See Frye v. United States, 293 F. 1013, 1014 (D.C. Cir. 1923).

II. The District Court Erred in Denying Judgment as a Matter of Law on Unseaworthiness.

A vessel is unseaworthy when its equipment is not “reasonably fit for its intended use.” Mitchell v. Trawler Racer, Inc., 362 U.S. 539, 550 (1960). The duty is absolute and non-delegable. Mahnich v. Southern S.S. Co., 321 U.S. 96, 100 (1944). Here the record established that the line had been stored in a heated compartment yet was rigged in sub-zero conditions without inspection for ice or fatigue; the boom bearings contained frozen summer grease; and the master curtailed the usual rigging time. These facts left no genuine issue that the vessel and its appurtenances were unfit. See Seas Shipping Co. v. Sieracki, 328 U.S. 85, 94 (1946). The court therefore should have directed a verdict on unseaworthiness. See Pope & Talbot, Inc. v. Hawn, 346 U.S. 406, 409 (1953).

The denial of judgment as a matter of law on comparative negligence was not error, because the evidence permitted a rational jury to find that Cook’s use of the torch near the rigged line created a jury question. See Atlantic & Gulf Stevedores, Inc. v. Ellerman Lines, Ltd., 369 U.S. 355, 360 (1962). The erroneous admission of the non-expert opinion and the erroneous denial of judgment on unseaworthiness nevertheless require vacatur and remand for a new trial.
