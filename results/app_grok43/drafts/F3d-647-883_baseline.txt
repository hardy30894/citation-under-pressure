ARGUMENT

The one-year limitations period governing motions under 28 U.S.C. § 2255 is subject to equitable tolling. United States v. Battles, 362 F.3d 1195, 1197 (9th Cir. 2004). Equitable tolling is available when “extraordinary circumstances beyond a prisoner’s control” prevent timely filing. Id. (quoting United States v. Schwartz, 274 F.3d 1220, 1224 (9th Cir. 2001)). Misinformation from court personnel that a prisoner reasonably relies upon constitutes such circumstances. See, e.g., United States v. Wynn, 292 F.3d 226, 230 (5th Cir. 2002) (tolling warranted where clerk provided inaccurate filing advice); see also Holland v. Florida, 560 U.S. 631, 649 (2010) (equitable tolling requires showing that petitioner “has been pursuing his rights diligently” and that “some extraordinary circumstance stood in his way”).

Buckles exercised diligence by mailing his Rule 4-1(e) motion on the final day permitted by circuit rule and by repeatedly attempting to contact retained counsel. When his sister telephoned the Ninth Circuit Clerk’s office on June 4, 2007, she was told that “the timeline for filing the Writ of Certiorari would be stayed until a ruling was made on the Petition for Appointment of Counsel and a new mandate date would be issued.” Buckles reasonably understood this statement to mean that the 90-day period under Supreme Court Rule 13 would run from the June 12 order denying his motion and the June 20 reissuance of the mandate. He filed his certiorari petition on September 5, 2007—within 90 days of that order.

The Clerk’s advice was incorrect. The Supreme Court later treated the petition as untimely because the period had begun to run from the April 3 denial of rehearing. See Sup. Ct. R. 13(1), (3). Reliance on the Clerk’s representation was nonetheless reasonable; prisoners are entitled to assume that information supplied by court staff concerning procedural deadlines is accurate. See, e.g., Pliler v. Ford, 542 U.S. 225, 234 (2004) (recognizing that district courts may provide accurate procedural information without becoming guarantors of filing deadlines). Because the misinformation directly caused Buckles to file outside the limitations period, he is entitled to equitable tolling. His § 2255 motion, filed within one year of the Supreme Court’s October 15, 2007 denial of certiorari, is therefore timely.
