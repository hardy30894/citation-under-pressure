**I. The District Court’s failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was harmless error.**

The Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure require that a district court advise a defendant of his right to appeal. *United States v. Hare*, 99 F.3d 1131, 1134 (4th Cir. 1996). The purpose of this rule is to ensure that the defendant is aware of his right to appeal. Id. When a defendant is already aware of this right, the rule’s purpose is served, and the error is harmless.

Under the standard established by the Supreme Court, the government must show that the error was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. *United States v. Olano*, 507 U.S. 725, 734 (1993). The record in this case demonstrates that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing. The District Court found that although the court failed to advise Peguero of his right, he knew of his right when the sentencing hearing occurred. Because the rule’s purpose was served, the error did not affect the outcome of the proceeding.
