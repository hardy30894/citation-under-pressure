ARGUMENT

FOIA mandates disclosure unless material falls within one of nine “explicitly exclusive” exemptions. U.S. Dep’t of Justice v. Tax Analysts, 492 U.S. 136, 151 (1989) (quoting Adm’r FAA v. Robertson, 422 U.S. 255, 262 (1975)). These exemptions “are to be interpreted narrowly.” Lahr v. NTSB, 569 F.3d 964, 973 (9th Cir. 2009). Exemption 2 shields only matters “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). The Ninth Circuit’s adoption of a “predominantly internal” gloss that reaches any document whose disclosure might risk circumvention of agency regulation exceeds both the statutory text and controlling precedent.

The Supreme Court has instructed that Exemption 2 covers “those matters which are not of legitimate public interest” and “relate only to trivial administrative matters.” Dep’t of the Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S. 352, 369-70 (1976). Hardy v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 631 F.2d 653 (9th Cir. 1980), permitted withholding of law-enforcement manuals whose release would risk circumvention, but limited the holding to materials “concern[ing] internal personnel practices.” Id. at 656. Maricopa Audubon Society v. United States Forest Service, 108 F.3d 1082 (9th Cir. 1997), likewise treated the “law enforcement” route as sufficient but not necessary, and rejected data that did not “relate ‘solely,’ or even predominantly, ‘to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.’” Id. at 1085 (quoting 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2)).

The ESQD arc maps and related documents do not satisfy this threshold. They quantify blast effects on surrounding communities, prescribe minimum distances from civilian areas, and guide facility siting that directly affects public safety. Commander Whitbred’s declaration confirms the data are used “to design, array, and construct ammunition storage facilities” and to mitigate risk to “the surrounding areas.” Such information is not “related solely to the internal personnel rules and practices of an agency.” 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(2). It is operational data with immediate external consequences.

Even under the D.C. Circuit’s “predominantly internal” formulation, see Crooker v. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco & Firearms, 670 F.2d 1051, 1074 (D.C. Cir. 1981) (en banc); Schwaner v. Dep’t of Air Force, 898 F.2d 793, 795 (D.C. Cir. 1990), the maps fail. They are planning tools whose disclosure would inform the public about hazards the Navy itself has not classified under Exemption 1. U.S. Dep’t of State v. Ray, 502 U.S. 164, 173 (1991) (agency bears burden of justifying nondisclosure). The Ninth Circuit’s expansion of Exemption 2 to any document that is “predominantly” internal and whose release might “risk circumvention” effectively creates a new, atextual exemption untethered from Rose or the statute.

The Navy’s affidavits do not demonstrate that the maps were compiled for law-enforcement purposes, nor that their release would endanger life or physical safety within the meaning of Exemption 7(F). Minier v. CIA, 88 F.3d 796, 800 (9th Cir. 1996). Because the documents fall outside both Exemption 2 and Exemption 7(F), they must be disclosed.
