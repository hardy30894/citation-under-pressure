**ARGUMENT**

I. **The Amended Trust Qualifies for the QTIP Marital Deduction Under I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)**

The Tax Court erred in denying the marital deduction for the trust income passing to Mrs. Davis under § 2056(b)(7). The Amended Trust plainly grants Mrs. Davis a qualifying income interest for life, as required by the statute.

A. **Mrs. Davis is Entitled to All Trust Income for Life**

Section 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii) provides that a surviving spouse has a “qualifying income interest for life” if she is “entitled to all the income from the property, payable annually or at more frequent intervals.” The Amended Trust satisfies this requirement. Paragraph 2 of the Amendment states that the trustee shall pay to Mrs. Davis “all of the net income from the trust estate ... in quarter annual or more frequent installments.” This language mirrors the statutory standard. *See Estate of Nicholson v. Comm’r*, 94 T.C. 666, 672 (1990) (holding that a trust requiring quarterly distributions of all income to the surviving spouse qualified under § 2056(b)(7)).

The Commissioner argues that the trustee’s discretion to determine what is “proper for the health, education, or support, maintenance, comfort and welfare” of Mrs. Davis injects impermissible discretion that disqualifies the interest. This contention misreads the statute. The Treasury Regulation clarifies that a surviving spouse’s right to all income is not defeated by a trustee’s discretion to determine the *amount* of distributions so long as the spouse is entitled to *all* income that the trustee deems appropriate under the standard. Treas. Reg. § 20.2056(b)-7(d)(2). *See also Estate of Powell v. Comm’r*, 101 T.C. 489, 495 (1993) (upholding QTIP treatment where trustee had discretion to distribute income based on the surviving spouse’s needs).

B. **No Power to Appoint Trust Property to Others Exists**

Section 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii) further requires that “no person has a power to appoint any part of the property to any person other than the surviving spouse.” The Amended Trust contains no such power. The trustee’s discretion is limited to distributions for Mrs. Davis’s benefit, and the remainder passes to the decedent’s daughters and their descendants. *See Estate of Spencer v. Comm’r*, 43 T.C. 298, 305 (1964) (finding no prohibited power where trustee could invade principal only for the surviving spouse’s support and the remainder was fixed in others).

The Commissioner contends that the trustee’s power to invade principal under Paragraph 5 violates this requirement because it could benefit someone other than Mrs. Davis. This argument conflates the *source* of the distribution (principal) with the *beneficiary* of the distribution. The statute prohibits powers that allow appointment to *persons other than the surviving spouse*, not powers that allow distributions from sources other than income. *See I.R.C. § 2056(b)(7)(B)(ii); Treas. Reg. § 20.2056(b)-7(d)(3)*. The invasion power here is exercisable solely for Mrs. Davis’s benefit, and thus does not offend the statute.

II. **The Tax Court’s Construction of the Trust Terms Was Erroneous**

The Tax Court improperly relied on *Estate of Vissering v. Comm’r*, 99 T.C. 606 (1992), to deny the deduction. In *Vissering*, the Tax Court denied QTIP treatment where the surviving spouse’s income interest was subject to a condition precedent that she survive the decedent by 60 days. The Amended Trust contains no such condition. The right to income vests immediately upon Mr. Davis’s death and continues for Mrs. Davis’s lifetime. *See also Estate of Nicholson*, 94 T.C. at 673 (distinguishing *Vissering* where the surviving spouse’s interest was not conditioned on survival beyond the decedent’s death).

The Commissioner’s reliance on *Estate of Chamberlain v. Comm’r*, 65 T.C. 674 (1975), is similarly misplaced. In *Chamberlain*, the Tax Court denied QTIP treatment where the surviving spouse’s income interest was subject to a support standard that allowed the trustee to consider the spouse’s other resources. The Amended Trust contains an identical provision, and the Tax Court in *Chamberlain* expressly approved such language. *Id.* at 680. The regulation explicitly permits such considerations. Treas. Reg. § 20.2056(b)-7(d)(2).

**Conclusion**

The Amended Trust plainly satisfies the requirements of § 2056(b)(7). The Tax Court’s contrary conclusion was based on misreadings of the statute and inapposite precedent. This Court should reverse the Tax Court’s decision and remand with instructions to allow the marital deduction in full.
