ARGUMENT

I. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN DENYING JOANN PARKER'S MOTION TO DISMISS THE INDICTMENT BECAUSE SHE LACKED THE AUTHORITY TO GRANT OR DENY BENEFITS, AND THUS, COULD NOT HAVE COMMITTED PUBLIC BRIBERY UNDER 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C).

The indictment charging Joann Parker with conspiracy to commit public bribery and public bribery under 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C) was deficient because she lacked the authority to grant or deny benefits. However, the statute does not require that the public official have the authority to perform the act in question. Instead, it prohibits public officials from being induced to do or omit any act in violation of their official duty. 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C).

The term "official act" is broadly defined in 18 U.S.C. § 201(a)(3) as "any decision or action on any question, matter, cause, suit, proceeding or controversy, which may at any time be pending, or which may by law be brought before any public official, in such official's official capacity, or in such official's place of trust or profit." This definition reflects Congress' intent to "include any decision or action taken by a public official in his capacity as such." S.Rep. No. 87-2213, (1962), reprinted in 1962 U.S.C.C.A.N. 3852, 3856.

The Supreme Court has held that official acts that violate an official's duty are not limited to those within the official's specific authority or those proscribed by statutes and written rules and regulations. United States v. Birdsall, 233 U.S. 223, 231 (1914). Instead, they may also be found in "established usage" and "settled practice." Id. at 231. The Sixth Circuit has also held that an official act is not limited to those within the official's specific authority. United States v. Gjieli, 717 F.2d 968, 972 (6th Cir.1983).

In this case, Joann Parker's actions, although outside her specific authority, were still within her official capacity as a clerk to ALJ Aronson. Her duties included assisting ALJ Aronson before and during hearings, and typing and mailing his decisions. By using her position to facilitate the fraudulent approval of benefits and demanding payment for her services, Joann Parker committed acts that were in violation of her official duty.

Therefore, the district court did not err in denying Joann Parker's motion to dismiss the indictment, as her actions were within the scope of 18 U.S.C. § 201(b)(2)(C).

II. THE CONVICTIONS OF RALPH PARKER SHOULD BE REVERSED BECAUSE THE GOVERNMENT FAILED TO PROVE THAT HE WAS A PUBLIC OFFICIAL OR A PERSON WHO AIDED AND ABETTED JOANN PARKER IN THE COMMISSION OF PUBLIC BRIBERY.

Although the brief does not develop this argument, it is worth noting that the same statutory provisions apply to Ralph Parker. As the facts indicate, Ralph Parker was involved in the scheme and took $500 from Simmons along with Joann Parker. However, the record does not clearly establish that Ralph Parker was a public official. The Government's evidence and the indictment should be scrutinized to determine whether Ralph Parker's involvement was sufficient to sustain his convictions under 18 U.S.C. §§ 201(b)(2)(C) and 371.

In conclusion, the convictions of Joann Parker and Ralph Parker should be reviewed in light of the applicable law and the facts presented in the record.
