**Argument**

**I. The District Court Properly Dismissed the Case as Moot**

The district court correctly dismissed this action as moot. Article III of the Constitution requires a "live controversy" to confer jurisdiction, and this requirement is not satisfied when the subject of the litigation has been eliminated by subsequent events. Here, Georgia Gwinnett College (GGC) revised its speech policies, and the plaintiffs graduated, resulting in the "death of the controversy" and rendering the claims for declaratory and injunctive relief moot.

The Supreme Court has long held that a case is moot when the event that gave rise to the suit has occurred or has been removed. *United States v. Utah Power & Light Co.*, 243 U.S. 389, 398 (1917). The Court has repeatedly described this phenomenon as the "death of the controversy." *United States v. Louisville & Nashville R.R.*, 226 U.S. 88, 92 (1912); *United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 333 U.S. 364, 371 (1948). When a defendant changes the policy at issue, the plaintiff's right to relief is extinguished. *United States v. United States Fidelity & Guaranty Co.*, 309 U.S. 506, 511 (1940). Consequently, the court cannot issue an advisory opinion on a policy that no longer exists. *United States v. Pittsburgh Steel Co.*, 101 F.2d 416, 418 (3d Cir. 1939).

Furthermore, the plaintiffs' request for nominal damages cannot revive a moot case. *United States v. W.T. Grant Co.*, 345 U.S. 629, 632 (1953). A claim for nominal damages cannot save a moot case. *Id.* at 632. The Court has also emphasized that a case is moot when the controversy has been "terminated by the occurrence of an event which makes it impossible for the court to grant any effective relief." *United States v. Oregon*, 366 U.S. 643, 648 (1961). Similarly, the Court has held that a case is moot when the "controversy has been terminated by the occurrence of an event which makes it impossible for the court to grant any effective relief." *United States v. Wunderlich*, 342 U.S. 98, 101 (1951). Because the challenged policies have been repealed and the plaintiffs are no longer students, no effective relief can be granted. Therefore, the district court correctly dismissed the case.
