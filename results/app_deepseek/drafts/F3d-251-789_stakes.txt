**ARGUMENT**

The district court erred in denying the petition for return of Danelsy to Panama. The Hague Convention, as implemented by the International Child Abduction Remedies Act, 42 U.S.C. §§ 11601-11610, establishes that a child wrongfully removed or retained must be returned to her country of habitual residence unless the respondent proves an affirmative defense by clear and convincing evidence. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 11603(e)(2)(A). Here, the district court’s finding that Gonzalez-Caballero consented to the removal and retention of Danelsy is clearly erroneous and, in any event, insufficient to defeat the petition.

**I. The District Court Clearly Erred in Finding Consent**

A finding of fact is clearly erroneous when the reviewing court is left with a definite and firm conviction that a mistake has been committed. *Anderson v. City of Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 573 (1985). The record does not support a finding that Gonzalez-Caballero freely and voluntarily consented to Mena’s permanent removal of Danelsy from Panama.

Gonzalez-Caballero’s testimony, though inconsistent, consistently reflects that any agreement was conditional. She stated that she allowed Mena to take Danelsy for two weeks until she could obtain the proper paperwork to join her daughter. Alternatively, she claimed that she agreed to Mena’s removal of Danelsy only if he helped her immigrate to the United States, at which point she would reassume custody. These conditions are fundamentally inconsistent with the notion of unconditional consent. *See Friedrich v. Friedrich*, 78 F.3d 1060, 1067 (6th Cir. 1996) (consent must be “clear and unequivocal”).

Moreover, Mena’s own conduct undermines any claim of consent. He purchased a one-way ticket for Danelsy but a round-trip ticket for himself, suggesting he never intended to return Danelsy to Panama. He also failed to call Gonzalez-Caballero upon arrival in the United States, as promised, and later conditioned her reunification with Danelsy on her ability to immigrate through her sister. These actions are inconsistent with a good-faith belief that Gonzalez-Caballero had consented to permanent removal. *See Baxter v. Baxter*, 423 F.3d 363, 371 (3d Cir. 2005) (consent must be “voluntary, knowing, and unequivocal”).

The district court’s reliance on Gonzalez-Caballero’s provision of Danelsy’s legal documents and her accompaniment to government offices is misplaced. These actions are equally consistent with a mother temporarily entrusting her child to the other parent for a brief visit, not with permanent relinquishment of custody. *See Mozes v. Mozes*, 239 F.3d 1067, 1076 (9th Cir. 2001) (ambiguous conduct does not establish consent).

**II. Even If Consent Existed, It Was Revoked**

The Hague Convention requires return of a child if the petitioner did not consent to the removal or retention. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 11603(e)(2)(A). Consent, once given, may be revoked before the removal or retention becomes wrongful. *See Friedrich*, 78 F.3d at 1067. Here, Gonzalez-Caballero contacted Panamanian authorities and the police the day after Mena and Danelsy left, demonstrating that she revoked any purported consent immediately upon realizing Mena’s intentions. Mena’s retention of Danelsy in the United States thereafter was wrongful.

**III. The District Court’s Findings Are Insufficient to Overcome the Convention’s Presumption of Return**

The Hague Convention’s primary purpose is to secure the prompt return of children wrongfully removed or retained. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 11601(b)(4). The affirmative defense of consent must be established by clear and convincing evidence. *See* 42 U.S.C. § 11603(e)(2)(A). The district court’s findings, based on ambiguous testimony and conduct, do not meet this high standard. *See Miller v. Miller*, 240 F.3d 392, 399 (4th Cir. 2001) (consent defense requires “clear and convincing evidence” of “unequivocal” consent).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court’s denial of the petition for return of Danelsy to Panama should be reversed, and the case remanded with instructions to order Danelsy’s return to her habitual residence in Panama.
