ARGUMENT

I. The OCSLA Workers’ Compensation Provision Requires a Situs-of-Injury Test

The Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (“OCSLA”) extends workers’ compensation coverage under the Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act (“LHWCA”) to injuries occurring “as the result of operations conducted on the outer Continental Shelf for the purpose of exploring for, developing, removing, or transporting by pipeline the natural resources . . . of the outer Continental Shelf.” 43 U.S.C. § 1333(b). The plain meaning of this provision requires that the injury occur as a result of operations on the outer Continental Shelf. The Supreme Court has noted that the general scope of OCSLA's coverage is determined principally by locale. Offshore Logistics, Inc. v. Tallentire, 477 U.S. 207, 219 (1986). The Court stated that the LHWCA, as incorporated by § 1333(b) of the OCSLA, provides the exclusive remedy for injury or death resulting from certain operations. Id. 

The Fifth Circuit has adopted a situs-of-injury test for OCSLA claims, requiring that the injury occur on an outer continental shelf platform or on the waters above the outer continental shelf. Mills v. Dir., Office of Workers’ Comp. Programs, 877 F.2d 356 (5th Cir. 1987) (en banc); see also Parker v. Motor Boat Sales, Inc., 314 U.S. 244 is not valid, instead see Atlantic Stevedoring Co. v. O'Keeffe, 412 F.2d 1224 (5th Cir. 1969); Travelers Ins. Co. v. Shea, 382 F.2d 344 (5th Cir. 1967). This interpretation is consistent with the LHWCA, which requires an injury to occur upon navigable waters. See 33 U.S.C. § 903(a); Calbeck v. Travelers Ins. Co., 370 U.S. 114 (1962); Nogueira v. New York, N.H. & H.R. Co., 281 U.S. 128 (1930). The LHWCA's situs requirement has been consistently applied by the courts, see e.g., Davis v. Department of Labor and Industries of Washington, 317 U.S. 249 (1942); London Guarantee & Accident Co. v. Indus. Accident Comm’n, 279 U.S. 109 (1929).

II. La Conchita is Not a Maritime Situs

The LHWCA requires that the injury occur on a maritime situs, defined in 33 U.S.C. § 903(a). La Conchita does not meet this definition, as it is not adjacent to navigable waters and is not used for maritime purposes. See e.g., T. Smith & Son, Inc. v. Taylor, 276 U.S. 179 (1928); Minnie v. Port Huron Terminal Co., 295 U.S. 647 (1935). The facility's proximity to the coastline is not sufficient to establish it as a maritime situs, as it is separated from the water by a highway and railroad tracks. See Motoviloff v. Dir., Office of Workers’ Comp. Programs, 692 F.2d 87 (9th Cir. 1982). The primary purpose of La Conchita is to process crude oil slurry, a non-maritime activity, as noted in Herb’s Welding, Inc. v. Gray, 470 U.S. 414 (1985).

The Ninth Circuit has applied a test to determine whether a facility is a maritime situs, considering factors such as the site's suitability for maritime uses and its proximity to the waterway. See Boudreaux v. American Workover, Inc., 680 F.2d 1344 (5th Cir. 1982); Weyerhaeuser Co. v. Gilmore, 528 F.2d 957 (9th Cir. 1975). Under this test, La Conchita is not a maritime situs, as it is not used for maritime purposes and is not adjacent to navigable waters.

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the Court should affirm the BRB's denial of benefits under the OCSLA and LHWCA.
