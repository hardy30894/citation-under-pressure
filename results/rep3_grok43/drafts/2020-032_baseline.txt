ARGUMENT

The Eleventh Circuit correctly concluded that the case became moot once Georgia Gwinnett College repealed the challenged policies. A case is moot when “the issues presented are no longer ‘live’ or the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome.” Already, LLC v. Nike, Inc., 568 U.S. 85, 91 (2013) (quoting Murphy v. Hunt, 455 U.S. 478, 481 (1982) (per curiam)). Petitioners’ graduation and the college’s superseding policies eliminated any claim for declaratory or injunctive relief, leaving only a request for nominal damages.

That request does not preserve a live controversy. In Flanigan’s Enterprises, Inc. of Georgia v. City of Sandy Springs, 868 F.3d 1248 (11th Cir. 2017) (en banc), the en banc court held that a prayer for nominal damages alone will not save an otherwise moot challenge to a repealed ordinance. Id. at 1260. The same principle applies here. Petitioners never alleged any actual injury—monetary loss, emotional distress, or reputational harm—required for compensatory damages under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. See Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247, 266 (1978) (compensatory damages must be “proved” and “based on actual injury”).

Voluntary cessation doctrine does not alter the result. Although a defendant’s abandonment of challenged conduct ordinarily does not moot a case, City of Mesquite v. Aladdin’s Castle, Inc., 455 U.S. 283, 289 (1982), that exception presupposes a continuing controversy over some form of meaningful relief. Once the policies were repealed and no compensable injury was pleaded, no Article III case or controversy remained. See Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona, 520 U.S. 43, 71 (1997) (nominal damages insufficient to avoid mootness where no other relief is available).

Petitioners’ belated request for leave to amend was properly denied. A request for amendment embedded in an opposition memorandum is not a proper motion under Rule 15. See, e.g., Cita Tr. Co. AG v. Fifth Third Bank, 879 F.3d 1151, 1157 (11th Cir. 2018). The district court acted within its discretion in refusing to permit an eleventh-hour clarification that would have added new factual allegations never presented below.

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
