**ARGUMENT**

The Due Process Clause permits a state court to exercise specific personal jurisdiction over a nonresident defendant when the plaintiff’s claims “arise out of or relate to” the defendant’s contacts with the forum. *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255, 262 (2017) (quoting *Goodyear Dunlop Tires Operations, S.A. v. Brown*, 564 U.S. 915, 919 (2011)). This “arise out of or relate to” requirement is satisfied here.

Ford’s Montana contacts are extensive and continuous. Ford advertises, sells, and services vehicles in Montana through dozens of dealerships; it distributes replacement parts there; and it maintains a substantial service network. These activities are not isolated or sporadic. *See International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 320 (1945) (specific jurisdiction proper where defendant’s “activities [are] neither irregular nor casual” but instead “systematic and continuous”).

The claims at issue relate directly to those contacts. Respondent alleges that a Ford Explorer was defectively designed and manufactured, causing injury in Montana. Ford’s Montana marketing and sales of Explorers and comparable vehicles, together with its distribution of parts and provision of service, create the necessary affiliation. *See Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, 471 U.S. 462, 472 (1985) (jurisdiction proper where defendant “purposefully directed” activities at the forum and the litigation “arise[s] out of or relate[s] to” those activities). The same model line and component parts that Ford promotes and supports in Montana are the subject of the suit.

Ford’s contrary rule—that the precise vehicle must have been sold by Ford in Montana—finds no support in precedent. The Court has never required that the plaintiff’s claim be the direct result of a single, in-forum transaction. *See World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980) (jurisdiction turns on whether the defendant’s contacts make it reasonable to anticipate being haled into court, not on the precise chain of title). Requiring such a causal link would immunize manufacturers whose nationwide distribution networks place their products in every state while they simultaneously cultivate substantial in-state markets.

Because Ford’s Montana contacts relate to the claims asserted, the exercise of specific jurisdiction comports with the Due Process Clause. The judgments below should be affirmed.
