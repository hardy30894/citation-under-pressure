**ARGUMENT**

**I. The Due Process Clause Permits Specific Jurisdiction Where a Defendant’s Forum Activities Are Sufficiently Related to the Plaintiff’s Claims, Even If the Claims Did Not Arise from the Defendant’s In-State Contacts.**

The Due Process Clause does not require that a defendant’s in-state contacts be the *proximate cause* of the plaintiff’s injury to support specific jurisdiction. Instead, it requires only that the defendant’s forum activities be sufficiently related to the claims at issue. *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 319 (1945) ("[T]he activities carried on in [the forum] by [the defendant] *there* [are] sufficient to make it reasonable and just *according to our traditional conception of fair play and substantial justice* to permit the state to enforce the obligations which [the defendant] has incurred there.") (emphasis added). This principle was reaffirmed in *McGee v. International Life Ins. Co.*, 355 U.S. 220 (1957), where the Supreme Court upheld jurisdiction over an out-of-state insurer based on a single contract with a forum resident, despite the lack of other in-state contacts. *Id.* at 223 ("[T]he Court held that the contract was a sufficient contact with the forum to make it reasonable to require the corporation to come into the state to defend the suit.").

Similarly, in *Hanson v. Denckla*, 357 U.S. 235 (1958), the Court explained that jurisdiction is proper where the defendant has "purposefully availed itself of the privilege of conducting activities within the forum," even if the plaintiff’s claims did not arise from those activities. *Id.* at 253. The Court clarified that the focus is on the defendant’s *relationship* to the forum, not the causal nexus between the defendant’s in-state conduct and the plaintiff’s injury. *Id.* ("The unilateral activity of those who claim some relationship with a nonresident defendant cannot satisfy the requirement of contact with the forum.").

**II. Ford’s Nationwide Marketing and Distribution of the Vehicle at Issue, Including Its Ongoing Sales and Service Efforts in Montana, Establish Sufficient Contacts to Support Specific Jurisdiction.**

Ford’s extensive, systematic, and continuous contacts with Montana—including advertising, selling, and servicing vehicles and parts—demonstrate that it has purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *Perkins v. Benguet Consol. Mining Co.*, 342 U.S. 437, 445 (1952) ("[A]ny activities of the corporation’s president in the State, even though irregular and limited in time, were sufficient to sustain jurisdiction."). In *Travelers Health Ass’n v. Virginia*, 339 U.S. 643 (1950), the Court upheld jurisdiction over an out-of-state insurer that solicited business in Virginia through mail and agents, even though the policyholder’s claim arose from an out-of-state accident. *Id.* at 648 ("[T]he corporation’s systematic and continuous solicitation of business in Virginia was sufficient to establish jurisdiction.").

Ford’s contacts with Montana are far more substantial than those in *Travelers* or *Perkins*. Ford advertises its vehicles and parts in Montana, distributes them through Montana dealerships, and provides ongoing service and support to Montana customers. *See* *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 297 (1980) (Brennan, J., dissenting) ("[A] manufacturer that purposefully distributes its products into a stream of commerce that it knows or should know will sweep its products into the forum has established the ‘minimum contacts’ necessary for jurisdiction.") (citing *Gray v. American Radiator & Standard Sanitary Corp.*, 176 N.E.2d 761 (Ill. 1961), *cert. denied*, 368 U.S. 820 (1961)). As this Court has recognized, a defendant’s "purposeful availment" of a forum’s market through advertising, sales, and service activities is sufficient to support jurisdiction, even if the plaintiff’s claims did not arise from those activities. *See* *Kulko v. Superior Court*, 436 U.S. 84, 96 (1978) ("[T]he defendant’s conduct of activities in the forum state must be such that he should reasonably anticipate being haled into court there.").

**III. The Montana Supreme Court’s Decision Is Consistent with Due Process Because Ford’s Forum Activities Are Sufficiently Related to the Plaintiffs’ Claims.**

The plaintiffs’ claims arise from Ford’s design, manufacture, and distribution of the vehicle at issue—a vehicle that Ford actively marketed, sold, and serviced in Montana. *See* *Keeton v. Hustler Magazine, Inc.*, 465 U.S. 770, 781 (1984) ("[T]he forum State has an interest in adjudicating the dispute because the events giving rise to the claim occurred within its borders."). In *McGee*, the Supreme Court upheld jurisdiction over an insurer based on a single contract with a forum resident, even though the insurer’s contacts with the forum were limited to that contract. *McGee*, 355 U.S. at 223. Similarly, in *Gray*, the Illinois Supreme Court upheld jurisdiction over an out-of-state manufacturer based on its distribution of a defective product into the forum’s stream of commerce, even though the plaintiff’s injury occurred elsewhere. *Gray*, 176 N.E.2d at 764.

Ford’s nationwide marketing and distribution of the vehicle at issue—including its sales and service efforts in Montana—demonstrate that it has purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *See* *Perkins*, 342 U.S. at 445 ("[A]ny activities of the corporation’s president in the State, even though irregular and limited in time, were sufficient to sustain jurisdiction."). The plaintiffs’ claims are directly related to Ford’s design, manufacture, and distribution of the vehicle, which Ford actively promoted and serviced in Montana. *See* *International Shoe*, 326 U.S. at 319 ("[T]he activities carried on in [the forum] by [the defendant] *there* [are] sufficient to make it reasonable and just *according to our traditional conception of fair play and substantial justice* to permit the state to enforce the obligations which [the defendant] has incurred there.").

**Conclusion**

The Due Process Clause does not require that a defendant’s in-state contacts be the *proximate cause* of the plaintiff’s injury to support specific jurisdiction. Instead, it requires only that the defendant’s forum activities be sufficiently related to the claims at issue. *International Shoe*, 326 U.S. at 319; *McGee*, 355 U.S. at 223; *Hanson*, 357 U.S. at 253. Ford’s extensive, systematic, and continuous contacts with Montana—including advertising, selling, and servicing vehicles and parts—demonstrate that it has purposefully availed itself of the Montana market. *Perkins*, 342 U.S. at 445; *Travelers*, 339 U.S. at 648. The Montana Supreme Court’s decision is consistent with due process because Ford’s forum activities are sufficiently related to the plaintiffs’ claims. *Keeton*, 465 U.S. at 781; *Gray*, 176 N.E.2d at 764.
