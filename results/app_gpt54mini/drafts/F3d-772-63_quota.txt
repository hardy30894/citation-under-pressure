**ARGUMENT**

The district court’s dismissal should be reversed. At the pleading stage, Foley needed only “a short and plain statement of the claim showing that [he] is entitled to relief.” *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544, 555 (2007). A complaint survives if it pleads facts that make liability plausible, not merely possible. *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009). Here, Foley alleged that Wells Fargo was contractually required to consider him for a settlement-created mortgage modification before foreclosing, yet instead misled him for months, failed to process his application, and moved toward foreclosure. Those allegations state claims under Massachusetts law and are not preempted.

I. **Foley plausibly alleged a breach of contract and covenant claim.**

A settlement agreement is enforceable as a contract by intended beneficiaries. *Local No. 93, Int’l Ass’n of Firefighters v. City of Cleveland*, 478 U.S. 501, 519 (1986); *United States v. ITT Continental Baking Co.*, 420 U.S. 223, 236–37 (1975). Massachusetts likewise recognizes that a nonparty for whose benefit a contract is made may sue for breach. *Choate, Hall & Stewart v. SCA Services, Inc.*, 378 Mass. 535, 540, 392 N.E.2d 1045 (1979). Foley alleged that the Pick-a-Payment settlement required Wells Fargo first to consider Settlement Class B members for HAMP, then for MAP2R, before foreclosure. He further alleged that Wells Fargo did not do so and instead sent him on a months-long procedural detour while scheduling foreclosure. Those facts plausibly plead breach.

The implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing also supports relief. Massachusetts law implies in every contract a covenant that neither party will do anything that destroys or injures the other’s right to receive the fruits of the bargain. *Anthony’s Pier Four, Inc. v. HBC Assocs.*, 411 Mass. 451, 471–72, 583 N.E.2d 806 (1991); *Onesti v. Thomson*, 289 Mass. 413, 418, 194 N.E. 339 (1935). Where a party has discretion, it must exercise that discretion honestly and in good faith. *Uproar Co. v. National Broadcasting Co.*, 81 F.2d 373, 377 (1st Cir. 1936). Foley’s allegations that Wells Fargo ignored the settlement’s required modification process while assuring him that an application was forthcoming easily state a covenant claim.

II. **Foley plausibly alleged unfair and deceptive acts under Massachusetts law.**

Massachusetts General Laws chapter 93A reaches unfair or deceptive acts in trade or commerce. Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A, §§ 2, 11; *Kattar v. Demoulas*, 433 Mass. 1, 12, 739 N.E.2d 246 (2000). Conduct may be deceptive when it has “the capacity or tendency to deceive,” even absent proof of actual reliance. *Aspinall v. Philip Morris Cos.*, 442 Mass. 381, 394, 813 N.E.2d 476 (2004). And a pattern of evasive or misleading communications in the mortgage-servicing context can be actionable. *Morris v. BAC Home Loans Servicing, L.P.*, 775 F. Supp. 2d 255, 261–62 (D. Mass. 2011).

Foley alleged that Wells Fargo repeatedly told him HAMP was the only modification available, delayed sending an application for seven months, later claimed not to have received it, and then sent letters about short sale or deed in lieu while foreclosing. Those allegations describe conduct that would permit a trier of fact to find deception, unfairness, and an “immoral, unethical, oppressive, or unscrupulous” practice. *PMP Assocs., Inc. v. Globe Newspaper Co.*, 366 Mass. 593, 596, 321 N.E.2d 915 (1975). Dismissal was therefore improper.

III. **The claims are not preempted.**

Wells Fargo’s preemption argument fails because Foley’s claims are grounded in general state contract and consumer-protection law, not in a state attempt to impose substantive terms on a federally regulated mortgage product. Federal banking law does not preempt generally applicable state laws of contract and fraud absent a clear conflict. *Barnett Bank of Marion County, N.A. v. Nelson*, 517 U.S. 25, 31 (1996); *Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.*, 505 U.S. 504, 518 (1992). The First Circuit has likewise recognized that traditional state-law claims are not lightly displaced. *SPGGC, LLC v. Ayotte*, 488 F.3d 525, 533–34 (1st Cir. 2007).

Foley seeks enforcement of a settlement-required modification review and redress for allegedly deceptive servicing conduct. Such claims do not require the court to impose a new underwriting regime or interfere with federal mortgage standards. Because Wells Fargo identifies no “clear and manifest purpose” to displace these traditional state-law remedies, preemption does not apply. *Wyeth v. Levine*, 555 U.S. 555, 565 (2009).

IV. **At minimum, Foley was entitled to proceed past the motion-to-dismiss stage.**

A complaint may be dismissed only where the plaintiff can prove no set of facts entitling him to relief under the governing theory. *Conley v. Gibson*, 355 U.S. 41, 45–46 (1957), abrogated on other grounds by *Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544. Under the modern standard, Foley’s detailed factual allegations—settlement membership, mandatory consideration for modification, misleading communications, application delays, and foreclosure scheduling—more than satisfy plausibility. *Twombly*, 550 U.S. at 570; *Iqbal*, 556 U.S. at 678.

The judgment dismissing Counts I, II, and III should be reversed, and the case remanded for further proceedings.
