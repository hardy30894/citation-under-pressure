The district court lacked authority to retain the action for trial. Section 1404(a) permits transfer only to a district where the action "might have been brought," a limitation that precludes a transferee court from assigning the case to itself. Hoffman v. Blaski, 363 U.S. 335, 342-43 (1960); Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612, 616-17 (1964); Continental Grain Co. v. The FBL-585, 364 U.S. 19, 21 (1960). That restriction reflects the ancient principle that a plaintiff’s choice of forum controls absent compelling justification. Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501, 508 (1947); Koster v. (American) Lumbermens Mut. Cas. Co., 330 U.S. 518, 524 (1947).

The command that an action transferred for pretrial proceedings “shall” be remanded at their conclusion is mandatory. Anderson v. Yungkau, 329 U.S. 482, 485 (1947) (“shall” ordinarily excludes discretion). This directive cannot be overridden by resort to the general transfer statute, for “where a statute specifically addresses the particular issue, it controls over a more general provision.” Escoe v. Zerbst, 295 U.S. 490, 493 (1935); see also Ex parte Collett, 337 U.S. 55, 58 (1949) (venue statutes must be read in pari materia to avoid nullifying express limitations).

Mandamus lies to correct a district court’s refusal to honor that statutory command. Roche v. Evaporated Milk Ass’n, 319 U.S. 21, 26 (1943) (mandamus available where “there is no other adequate remedy” and the order is “clearly erroneous as a matter of law”); La Buy v. Howes Leather Co., 352 U.S. 249, 256 (1957) (supervisory control warranted to prevent “usurpation of judicial power”); Bankers Life & Cas. Co. v. Holland, 346 U.S. 379, 383 (1953) (same); Ex parte Fahey, 332 U.S. 258, 260 (1947) (extraordinary writ appropriate to confine a court to its lawful jurisdiction).

Because the Arizona court exceeded its statutory authority by retaining the case for trial, the judgment must be reversed and the action remanded to the Northern District of Illinois.
