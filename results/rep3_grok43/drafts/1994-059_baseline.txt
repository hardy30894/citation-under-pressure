**ARGUMENT**

The Qualifications Clauses of the Constitution prescribe the exclusive requirements for service in Congress. U.S. Const. art. I, § 2, cl. 2; art. I, § 3, cl. 3. Those provisions “fix the qualifications of members” and “are exclusive.” *Powell v. McCormack*, 395 U.S. 486, 548 (1969). A state therefore may not “alter” them by adding further eligibility restrictions. *See id.* at 522 (framers “intended the Constitution to establish fixed qualifications”).

This Court has long recognized that the power to regulate the “Times, Places and Manner of holding Elections” does not encompass authority to impose substantive qualifications. U.S. Const. art. I, § 4, cl. 1; *see Ex parte Yarbrough*, 110 U.S. 651, 663 (1884) (distinguishing procedural regulations from qualifications). The Clauses reflect the framers’ deliberate choice for national uniformity. Proposals to permit state-imposed term limits were considered and rejected at the Philadelphia Convention. *See Powell*, 395 U.S. at 532–34.

Allowing Arkansas Amendment 73 to stand would produce precisely the “patchwork of qualifications” the Constitution was designed to prevent. *See id.* at 548. Members of Congress “partake of the same national character” as the President; their qualifications must remain uniform across the states. *Cf. U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton*, 514 U.S. 779, 803 (1995) (recognizing the same principle). State ballot-access measures that operate as de facto qualifications are subject to the same prohibition. *See id.* at 828–29 (state may not “indirectly” impose what it cannot impose directly).

The severability clause cannot rescue the federal restrictions. Because Sections 1–3 were presented to voters as an integrated package, invalidation of Section 3 requires invalidation of the remainder. *See Combs v. Glens Falls Ins. Co.*, 237 Ark. 745, 375 S.W.2d 809 (1964). The people of Arkansas retain plenary authority to limit the terms of state officers; they lack authority to do so for federal officers.

The judgment of the Arkansas Supreme Court should be affirmed.
