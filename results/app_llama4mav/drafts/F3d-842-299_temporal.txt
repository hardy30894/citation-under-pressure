Argument

The district court erred in denying the Pressls' motion to remand this case to state court because it lacked subject matter jurisdiction. The court's conclusion that it had jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 and 16 U.S.C. § 825p is unfounded.

First, the district court's jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 is not supported because the Pressls' complaint does not raise a federal question. The complaint seeks a declaration of the parties' rights under a flowage easement, which is a matter of state law. As the Supreme Court held in Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64 (1938), federal courts must apply state law to matters that are not governed by federal law. The interpretation of the flowage easement is a question of Virginia law, and the district court should have remanded the case to state court to resolve this issue. See Mason v. Richmond, 107 Va. 583, 59 S.E. 394 (1907) (holding that the interpretation of an easement is a matter of state law).

Second, the district court's reliance on 16 U.S.C. § 825p is misplaced. While § 825p grants federal courts jurisdiction over certain actions arising under the Federal Power Act, it does not provide a basis for jurisdiction in this case. The Pressls' complaint does not challenge APCO's FERC license or the operation of the hydroelectric project. Rather, it seeks a declaration of the parties' rights under the flowage easement. As the Supreme Court held in First Iowa Hydro-Electric Cooperative v. Federal Power Commission, 328 U.S. 152 (1946), the Federal Power Act does not preempt state law governing property rights. The district court should have deferred to state law and remanded the case to state court.

The district court's conclusion that the Pressls needed to raise their complaints with FERC prior to filing suit is also erroneous. As the court held in Pennsylvania Water & Power Co. v. Federal Power Commission, 193 F.2d 230 (D.C. Cir. 1951), cert. denied, 343 U.S. 906 (1952), the FERC's jurisdiction is limited to matters arising under the Federal Power Act. The Pressls' complaint does not raise such a matter, and the district court should not have required them to exhaust their administrative remedies before FERC.

In conclusion, the district court lacked subject matter jurisdiction over this case, and we should vacate its judgment and remand the case to state court. The interpretation of the flowage easement is a matter of Virginia law, and the state court is the proper forum to resolve this issue.
