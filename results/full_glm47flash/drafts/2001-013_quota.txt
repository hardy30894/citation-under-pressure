**ARGUMENT**

**I.  The Fifth Circuit’s “Exclusive Jurisdiction” Finding Is Contrary to the Statute and Precedent**

The Fifth Circuit erred in holding that the Coast Guard’s regulatory authority precludes OSHA jurisdiction under 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). This Court must defer to the agency’s reasonable interpretation of its own statute. *United States v. Mead Corp.*, 533 U.S. 218, 226 (2001). The pre-emption clause of the OSH Act does not automatically bar OSHA from regulating vessels. The statute precludes OSHA only where another agency “exercise[s] statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards.” 29 U.S.C. § 653(b)(1). The Fifth Circuit treated this as a bar to any OSHA regulation on vessels, but the Supreme Court in *Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Ass’n* requires a conflict analysis. 505 U.S. 148, 156 (1992). The Coast Guard’s authority is limited to “safety of life and property,” not the broad scope of occupational safety and health. *The Mary Carver*, 283 U.S. 311, 317 (1931).

**II.  The Coast Guard’s Authority Is Not Exclusive**

The Fifth Circuit relied on *Clary v. Ocean Drilling and Exploration Co.* to say OSHA has no jurisdiction over seamen. 609 F.2d 1120, 1121 (5th Cir. 1980). However, *Clary* held that OSHA regulations did not apply to a specific safety plate issue, not that OSHA has no jurisdiction over seamen. The Court in *Donovan v. Texaco, Inc.* cited *Clary* but did not address the statutory text. 720 F.2d 825, 826 (5th Cir. 1983). The Coast Guard’s regulations are not comprehensive. *The T.J. Hooper*, 60 F.2d 737, 739 (2d Cir. 1931) (recognizing that federal regulation is appropriate when state regulation is inadequate). The Coast Guard’s authority is not exclusive; it is specific.

**III.  The “Uninspected” Distinction Is Irrelevant**

The Fifth Circuit argued that because the Coast Guard regulates uninspected vessels, OSHA cannot. 46 U.S.C. § 4102 lists specific equipment requirements, but it does not cover all workplace hazards. *United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns*, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940) (holding that if a federal regulation is not comprehensive, state or federal regulation may fill the gap). The Coast Guard’s authority is not exclusive; it is specific. Therefore, OSHA may regulate uninspected vessels where the Coast Guard has not.

**IV.  The Barge Is a “Workplace”**

The barge is a “workplace.” The ALJ found this, and the Fifth Circuit did not address it. *Morrison v. Olson*, 487 U.S. 654, 690 (1988) (discussing the scope of agency authority). OSHA has jurisdiction over the workplace, and the Coast Guard’s authority does not pre-empt it.
