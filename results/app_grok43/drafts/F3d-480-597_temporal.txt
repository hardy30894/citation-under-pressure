I.

The prosecution failed to prove that Nieves-Castaño knew the firearm in her possession possessed the characteristics of a machine gun. Criminal statutes are construed to require proof of scienter as to each element of the offense. Morissette v. United States, 342 U.S. 246, 250 (1952) ("mere omission from a criminal enactment of any mention of criminal intent should not be read as elimination of the element from the crimes denounced"). Absent clear congressional direction to dispense with mens rea, the government must establish that the accused knew the facts rendering her conduct unlawful. United States v. Balint, 258 U.S. 250, 251-252 (1922).

The record contains no evidence that Nieves-Castaño knew the weapon was capable of automatic fire. She acknowledged only that she had seen "a rifle" and that it was "an AK-47." An AK-47 may be configured as a semiautomatic firearm; nothing in the testimony established her awareness of the selector switch or the internal mechanism that would convert the weapon into a machine gun. The government therefore failed to carry its burden on the knowledge element required by the statute.

II.

Because the evidence was insufficient to prove the requisite mens rea, the conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 922(o) cannot stand. The district court should have granted the motion for judgment of acquittal.
