The District Court's failure to advise Manuel Peguero of his right to appeal, as required by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(a)(2), does not entitle him to habeas relief because he was aware of his right to appeal and elected not to exercise it. The Court of Appeals correctly held that a Rule 32 violation is subject to harmless-error review.

As this Court has recognized, not every error in the administration of the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure requires automatic reversal or collateral relief. See United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725, 731-32 (1993). Rather, the harmless-error doctrine applies to Rule violations where the error does not affect the defendant's substantial rights. Id. at 734.

In this case, the District Court's failure to advise Peguero of his right to appeal was a technical violation of Rule 32(a)(2). However, the court's subsequent finding that Peguero was aware of his right to appeal at the time of sentencing renders the error harmless. As the Court of Appeals noted, the purpose of Rule 32(a)(2) is to ensure that defendants are aware of their appellate rights. See United States v. Deans, 436 F.2d 596, 599 (3d Cir. 1971). Since Peguero was already aware of his right to appeal, the District Court's failure to advise him did not prejudice him.

The Ninth Circuit's decision in United States v. Drummond, 903 F.2d 1251 (9th Cir. 1990), is distinguishable. In Drummond, the court held that a Rule 32 violation required automatic reversal, regardless of whether the defendant was aware of his right to appeal. Id. at 1253. However, this Court has since clarified that the harmless-error doctrine applies to Rule violations, and that automatic reversal is not always required. See Olano, 507 U.S. at 734.

In this case, the District Court's error was harmless because Peguero was aware of his right to appeal and chose not to exercise it. Therefore, the Court of Appeals correctly affirmed the denial of habeas relief. The judgment should be affirmed.
