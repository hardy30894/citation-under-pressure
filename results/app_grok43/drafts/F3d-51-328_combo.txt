The district court erred in granting a preliminary injunction compelling deviation from Cadman Towers' neutral first-come, first-served parking allocation. That policy treats all residents alike without regard to disability or any other trait.

The Fourteenth Amendment reaches only state action that denies equal protection of the laws. Civil Rights Cases, 109 U.S. 3, 11 (1883). It does not compel private associations to grant preferences or to rearrange neutral rules of access. Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1, 13 (1948). A cooperative's internal waiting list, applied without exception to every applicant, therefore lies beyond the amendment's command. Buchanan v. Warley, 245 U.S. 60, 79 (1917).

Equal protection forbids arbitrary classification, not uniform application of a single rule. Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356, 373-374 (1886). Where the same requirement binds every member, no denial of equal protection occurs. Plessy v. Ferguson, 163 U.S. 537, 551-552 (1896). The first-come, first-served order is such a requirement; it neither classifies nor excludes on any forbidden ground.

Private property owners retain the right to prescribe reasonable conditions for the use of their premises. Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co., 392 U.S. 409, 413 (1968). Those conditions may include orderly queues for scarce resources. The cooperative's garages contain fewer spaces than apartments; the waiting list simply rations access on a temporal basis common to all.

Statutes that purport to impose affirmative duties on private parties must be construed consistently with these constitutional limits. Strauder v. West Virginia, 100 U.S. 303, 310 (1880). Ex parte Virginia, 100 U.S. 339, 347 (1880). An order that forces immediate reassignment of a parking space to one resident ahead of others who have waited longer exceeds any such limit and invades the cooperative's retained property rights.

Because the governing rule is facially neutral and uniformly enforced, the injunction rests on an erroneous view of the law and must be vacated.
