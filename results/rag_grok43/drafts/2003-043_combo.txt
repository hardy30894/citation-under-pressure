**ARGUMENT**

The Internal Revenue Code requires that an assessment identify the separate taxpayer whose liability is recorded. 26 U.S.C. § 6203. Because each partner is a distinct taxpayer from the partnership, an assessment against the partnership alone does not extend the ten-year collection period to the partners. Francis v. McNeal, 228 U.S. 695, 699 (1913) (“the partnership and individual estates both were to be administered”); Meek v. Centre County Banking Co., 264 U.S. 499, 501 (1924) (partnership and partners remain separate subjects of adjudication).

This Court has long recognized that a partnership is “a distinct thing from the partners themselves.” Forsyth v. Woods, 78 U.S. 484, 488 (1871). Consequently, a timely assessment against the firm does not operate as an assessment against any partner. Liberty Nat. Bank of Roanoke, Va. v. Bear, 276 U.S. 215, 221 (1928) (partnership property and individual property are administered separately); Heiner v. Mellon, 304 U.S. 271, 274 (1938) (each partner’s distributive share is computed individually).

The same separation governs tax liability. In family-partnership cases the Court has held that a partner’s tax obligation arises only from that partner’s own interest and conduct. Commissioner v. Tower, 327 U.S. 280, 289 (1946); Lusthaus v. Commissioner, 327 U.S. 293, 297 (1946); Commissioner v. Culbertson, 337 U.S. 733, 742 (1949). An assessment that names only the partnership therefore leaves the partners’ three-year limitation period untouched.

Nor does state-law joint-and-several liability dispense with the need for an individualized assessment or judgment. Guaranty Trust Co. v. Commissioner, 303 U.S. 493, 498 (1938) (partnership accounting does not alter the separate tax obligations of the partners). The Bankruptcy Act itself treats partnership and individual estates as distinct, confirming that collection from one does not automatically reach the other. Meek v. Centre County Banking Co., 268 U.S. 426, 429 (1925).

Because the IRS assessed only the partnership and obtained no judgments against the individual partners within the applicable period, the claims filed in the bankruptcy proceedings are unenforceable. The judgments below should be affirmed.
