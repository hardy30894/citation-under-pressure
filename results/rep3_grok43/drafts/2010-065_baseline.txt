**ARGUMENT**

Bond lacks standing to challenge her conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 229 on the ground that the statute exceeds Congress’s enumerated powers and violates the Tenth Amendment. In *Tennessee Electric Power Co. v. Tennessee Valley Authority*, 306 U.S. 118 (1939), the Court held that private parties “absent the states or their officers, have no standing in this suit to raise any question under the [Tenth] Amendment.” *Id.* at 144. That holding controls here.

The Tenth Amendment reserves to the States powers not delegated to the United States. U.S. Const. amend. X. A claim that federal legislation invades this reserved domain therefore asserts an injury to the sovereignty of the States themselves. *See also* *Massachusetts v. Mellon*, 262 U.S. 447, 485-86 (1923) (States alone may vindicate their sovereign interests against federal overreach). An individual criminal defendant suffers no distinct, particularized injury from any such structural violation; her grievance is “undifferentiated” and “common to all members of the public.” *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife*, 504 U.S. 555, 575 (1992) (quoting *United States v. Richardson*, 418 U.S. 166, 177 (1974)).

The Third Circuit correctly applied these principles. *Bond v. United States*, 581 F.3d 128, 137-38 (3d Cir. 2009). Five other circuits have reached the same conclusion. *See United States v. Hacker*, 565 F.3d 522, 525-27 (8th Cir. 2009); *Oregon v. Legal Servs. Corp.*, 552 F.3d 965, 971-72 (9th Cir. 2009); *Brooklyn Legal Servs. Corp. v. Legal Servs. Corp.*, 462 F.3d 219, 234-35 (2d Cir. 2006); *Medeiros v. Vincent*, 431 F.3d 25, 33-36 (1st Cir. 2005); *United States v. Parker*, 362 F.3d 1279, 1284-85 (10th Cir. 2004). Allowing a convicted defendant to litigate the federal-state balance would convert the Tenth Amendment from a structural safeguard for the States into a personal defense available to every criminal accused—an expansion unsupported by precedent or constitutional text.

Bond’s reliance on *Missouri v. Holland*, 252 U.S. 416 (1920), is misplaced. That decision addressed the validity of treaty-implementing legislation; it did not confer standing on private litigants to assert Tenth Amendment claims. *See id.* at 432-33. Because Bond cannot establish the requisite standing, this Court need not reach the merits of her constitutional challenge. *Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Env’t*, 523 U.S. 83, 94-95 (1998).

The judgment of the court of appeals should be affirmed.
