**ARGUMENT**

The preliminary injunction should be vacated because the Fair Housing Amendments Act does not require Cadman Towers to abandon its neutral, first-come/first-served parking policy and immediately reassign a preferred space to Shapiro. A plaintiff seeking a preliminary injunction must show a likelihood of success on the merits and irreparable harm. *Winter v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 555 U.S. 7, 20-22 (2008); *Sampson v. Murray*, 415 U.S. 61, 88-90 (1974). On this record, Shapiro cannot make that showing because the Act requires only a reasonable accommodation, not the creation of a special entitlement to the best parking space available. 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B); *Alexander v. Choate*, 469 U.S. 287, 301-02 (1985).

I. **The Act does not require Cadman Towers to reallocate scarce parking on demand.**

The Fair Housing Amendments Act makes it unlawful to “refus[e] to make reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services, when such accommodations may be necessary to afford” a disabled person “equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling.” 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B). But “reasonable accommodation” does not mean a modification that fundamentally alters the nature of the housing provider’s program or imposes undue burdens. *Southeastern Community College v. Davis*, 442 U.S. 397, 410, 413-14 (1979); *Alexander v. Choate*, 469 U.S. at 300-01. Cadman Towers’ parking system is a limited-capacity allocation scheme serving 423 apartments with only 202 indoor spaces, and it has long been administered by a first-come/first-served waiting list. Requiring the cooperative to displace that system whenever a resident asserts a disability would convert a neutral allocation rule into a guarantee of priority access, contrary to *Davis* and *Choate*.

The district court’s order also disregards the statutory focus on accommodations that are “necessary” to provide equal use and enjoyment. 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B). Necessity requires a causal link between the requested change and the disabled person’s ability to live in the dwelling on equal terms. *Bryant Woods Inn, Inc. v. Howard County, Maryland*, 124 F.3d 597, 603-04 (4th Cir. 1997); *Hovsons, Inc. v. Township of Brick*, 89 F.3d 1096, 1105-06 (3d Cir. 1996). Shapiro has not shown that only an immediate ground-floor garage space, as opposed to other available accommodations, is necessary to permit her equal access to her apartment. The cooperative offered to consider accommodating her when she became entitled to a space in the ordinary course, which is precisely the kind of incremental adjustment the statute contemplates.

II. **Shapiro has not shown that she was denied equal housing opportunity; Cadman Towers treated her like all other residents under a neutral policy.**

The Act targets discrimination against persons with disabilities, not all inconveniences associated with disability. *Alexander v. Choate*, 469 U.S. at 295-97. A housing provider does not discriminate by maintaining a facially neutral rule that applies equally to residents, absent a showing that a requested accommodation is required for meaningful access. *Lapid-Laurel, L.L.C. v. Zoning Board of Adjustment of Scotch Plains*, 284 F.3d 442, 457-58 (3d Cir. 2002); *Smith & Lee Associates, Inc. v. City of Taylor*, 102 F.3d 781, 794-95 (6th Cir. 1996). Cadman Towers’ policy is not exclusionary; it merely allocates a scarce resource by seniority on a waiting list. Shapiro remains free to use her apartment, the building, and the parking garage once she reaches her place in line.

The district court relied on the fact that Cadman Towers has made exceptions to its parking policy, including grandfathered multiple spaces and employee spaces. But isolated exceptions do not eliminate the legitimacy of a generally applicable rule, nor do they create a duty to prefer every disabled resident ahead of all others. *Bangerter v. Orem City Corp.*, 46 F.3d 1491, 1503-04 (10th Cir. 1995). The question is not whether some exceptions exist, but whether the requested exception is reasonable and necessary. 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B).

III. **The injunction is overbroad because it grants Shapiro the very relief that must be proved on a full record.**

A preliminary injunction is an extraordinary remedy and should not issue on an incomplete record when the requested relief effectively decides the merits. *Munaf v. Geren*, 553 U.S. 674, 689-90 (2008); *Tom Doherty Associates, Inc. v. Saban Entertainment, Inc.*, 60 F.3d 27, 33-34 (2d Cir. 1995). The order below compels immediate assignment of a specific parking space, yet the record reflects disputed issues about the necessity of that accommodation, the availability of alternatives, and the burdens imposed on other residents. Those issues should be resolved after a full evidentiary hearing, not by mandatory interim relief.

Because Shapiro has not established a likelihood of success under 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B), the injunction should be vacated. At minimum, the matter should be remanded for further proceedings on whether any accommodation short of immediate priority parking would afford her equal opportunity to use and enjoy her dwelling.
