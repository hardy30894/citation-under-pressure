**Argument**

**I.  The Supreme Court’s Decision in *Johnson v. United States* Does Not Apply Retroactively to Cases on Collateral Review**

Petitioner argues that the Supreme Court’s decision in *Johnson v. United States* requires the vacatur of his sentence. However, *Johnson* announced a new constitutional rule that does not apply retroactively to cases on collateral review. The Supreme Court established the general rule of non-retroactivity in *Linkletter v. Walker*, 381 U.S. 618 (1965). There, the Court held that "new rules for the conduct of criminal trials are not applied to cases that have become final before the new rules are announced." *Id.* at 624. This rule applies unless the new rule is a "watershed" rule of criminal procedure implicating the reliability of the trial process itself. *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218, 229 (1967).

The void for vagueness rule announced in *Johnson* is not a watershed rule. *Johnson* held that the residual clause of the Armed Career Criminal Act was unconstitutionally vague. This ruling concerns the interpretation of a statute, not the fairness of the trial process itself. In contrast, the right to counsel established in *Gideon v. Wainwright*, 372 U.S. 335 (1963), and the right to a jury trial in *Duncan v. Louisiana*, 391 U.S. 145 (1968), are watershed rules that apply retroactively because they are fundamental to the fairness of the trial. *Gideon*, 372 U.S. at 343; *Duncan*, 391 U.S. at 155.

Furthermore, the vagueness doctrine itself, as applied in *Lanzetta v. New Jersey*, 306 U.S. 451 (1939), requires that laws give fair notice. *Johnson* merely clarified that the ACCA failed to provide such notice. It did not change the fundamental nature of the trial process. *Stovall v. Denno*, 388 U.S. 293 (1967), further supports the principle that new rules do not apply retroactively unless they are essential to the fairness of the trial. Because *Johnson* is a rule of statutory construction rather than a rule of criminal procedure implicating the reliability of the trial, it does not apply retroactively to petitioner’s sentence.
