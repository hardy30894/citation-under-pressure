**ARGUMENT**

**I. Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial**

Petitioner respectfully submits that the text and structure of § 1407(a) compel remand to the Northern District of Illinois for trial. Section 1407(a) provides that when the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation (“Panel”) transfers actions for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings, it “shall” remand each action “at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings.” 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) (emphasis added). This mandatory language leaves no room for a transferee court to retain the case for trial under § 1404(a). *Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach*, 523 U.S. 26, 33 (1998) (recognizing that § 1407(a) “requires remand” and that the Panel “shall remand” the action). The Supreme Court has emphasized that § 1407(a) is “a limited grant of authority” confined to pretrial coordination. *Id.* at 34 (quoting *In re Diamond Mortg. Corp. of Ill.*, 855 F.2d 1531, 1535 (11th Cir. 1988)).

The Ninth Circuit’s contrary conclusion in *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810 (3d Cir. 1982), and its progeny, misreads § 1407(a). Those cases erroneously rely on Rule 14(b) of the Panel’s Rules, which permits a transferee court to “transfer” a case to itself for trial under § 1404(a). *See id.* at 820. However, the Panel’s rulemaking authority is limited to procedural matters and cannot override the clear statutory command of § 1407(a). *See Sibbach v. Wilson & Co.*, 312 U.S. 1, 10 (1941) (holding that the Supreme Court’s rulemaking power under the Rules Enabling Act does not authorize rules that abridge, enlarge, or modify substantive rights). The Panel’s rule cannot vest a transferee court with authority Congress did not grant.

**II. Transferee Courts Lack Authority to Invoke § 1404(a) to Retain Cases for Trial**

The Ninth Circuit’s reliance on *Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122 (2d Cir. 1971), is misplaced. In *Pfizer*, the Second Circuit acknowledged that § 1407(a) “requires remand” but justified the transferee court’s retention of the case for trial as a matter of judicial efficiency. *Id.* at 124-25. This reasoning contravenes the statutory text and legislative intent. *See Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 35 (rejecting the argument that § 1404(a) can be used to circumvent § 1407(a)’s remand requirement).

Other circuits have similarly recognized that transferee courts cannot use § 1404(a) to retain cases for trial after pretrial proceedings conclude. *See In re Air Crash Disaster Near Chicago, Ill.*, 644 F.2d 594, 600 (7th Cir. 1981) (“The plain language of § 1407(a) requires remand to the transferor district for trial.”); *In re Corrugated Container Antitrust Litig.*, 614 F.2d 958, 961 (5th Cir. 1980) (“The statute’s command is clear: after pretrial proceedings, the case must be remanded.”). These decisions correctly interpret § 1407(a) as a strict limitation on the Panel’s authority, not an invitation for transferee courts to expand their jurisdiction.

**III. The Panel’s Rule 14(b) Cannot Override the Statutory Mandate**

The Panel’s Rule 14(b) purports to authorize transferee courts to “transfer” cases to themselves for trial under § 1404(a). *See* J.P.M.L. Rule 14(b). However, this rule cannot override the clear statutory command of § 1407(a). *See Sibbach*, 312 U.S. at 10. The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized that rules of procedure cannot abridge substantive rights or expand judicial authority beyond that granted by statute. *See id.*; *United States v. Shimer*, 367 U.S. 374, 381-82 (1961). Rule 14(b) is therefore invalid to the extent it purports to authorize a transferee court to retain a case for trial.

**IV. The Legislative History of § 1407(a) Confirms the Mandatory Remand Requirement**

The legislative history of § 1407(a) confirms that Congress intended to limit the Panel’s authority to pretrial proceedings. The statute was enacted to address the problem of duplicative discovery in complex, multi-district litigation. *See* H.R. Rep. No. 88-1130 (1964), *reprinted in* 1964 U.S.C.C.A.N. 3386, 3387. Congress explicitly stated that the Panel’s authority was “limited to pretrial proceedings” and that cases were to be remanded “for trial.” *Id.* at 3388. This history underscores that the Panel’s role is temporary and that remand to the transferor district is mandatory.

**V. The Ninth Circuit’s Decision Undermines the Statutory Scheme**

The Ninth Circuit’s decision in this case undermines the statutory scheme by allowing transferee courts to retain cases for trial, contrary to the clear command of § 1407(a). This practice frustrates Congress’s intent to limit the Panel’s authority to pretrial coordination and deprives plaintiffs of their right to select the forum for trial. *See Lexecon*, 523 U.S. at 35 (recognizing that § 1407(a) “protects the transferor court’s right to try the case”). The Ninth Circuit’s decision should be reversed to ensure that § 1407(a) is enforced as written.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully submits that the Ninth Circuit’s decision affirming the district court’s retention of the case for trial under § 1404(a) is erroneous and should be reversed. The text, structure, and legislative history of § 1407(a) compel remand to the Northern District of Illinois for trial. The Panel’s Rule 14(b) cannot override this statutory mandate.
