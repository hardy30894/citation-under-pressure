## ARGUMENT

The district court erred by dismissing this class action as moot solely because Defendant served an unaccepted Rule 68 offer of judgment for the full amount of Plaintiff’s individual claim. An unaccepted offer—one that has expired by its own terms—does not extinguish a named plaintiff’s personal stake in the litigation, and even if it did, the class-certification question remains justiciable under the “relation back” doctrine and well-settled mootness exceptions for class actions.

### I. An Unaccepted Rule 68 Offer Does Not Moot a Class Action

A case becomes moot only when “the parties lack a legally cognizable interest in the outcome.” *Sosna v. Iowa*, 419 U.S. 393, 402 (1975). An unaccepted offer of judgment is not a judgment; it is a “tactical device” that “can be withdrawn at any time before acceptance.” *Pitts v. Terrible Herbst, Inc.*, 653 F.3d 1081, 1091 (9th Cir. 2011). Because BCR’s offer expired, there has been no satisfaction of Lucero’s claim, no entry of judgment, and no cessation of the controversy. *See, e.g., Weiss v. Regal Collections*, 385 F.3d 337, 344-45 (3d Cir. 2004) (holding that an unaccepted Rule 68 offer does not moot a class action). The district court’s contrary conclusion allows defendants to “pick off” named plaintiffs before class certification—a practice courts have uniformly condemned. *See Deposit Guar. Nat’l Bank v. Roper*, 445 U.S. 326, 339 (1980).

### II. Even if the Individual Claim Became Moot, the Class-Certification Question Is Not Moot

The Supreme Court “has held that an action brought on behalf of a class does not become moot upon expiration of the named plaintiff’s substantive claim.” *U.S. Parole Comm’n v. Geraghty*, 445 U.S. 388, 394 (1980). In *Geraghty*, the Court explained that a named plaintiff retains a “personal stake” in the class-certification decision—a “procedural” right separate from the merits—that survives the mootness of his individual claim. *Id.* at 402-03. That principle applies here: Lucero filed his class-certification motion, and the district court vacated the hearing only because it erroneously deemed the entire action moot. Under *Geraghty*, the class-certification question remains live.

### III. The Class-Certification Motion Relates Back to the Filing of the Complaint

Alternatively, this Court should adopt the “relation back” doctrine articulated in *Sosna v. Iowa*, 419 U.S. 393. Under *Sosna*, “the timing of the certification motion” is not dispositive; rather, the relevant inquiry is whether the named plaintiff’s claim was “inherently transitory” or whether the defendant manipulated mootness. *Id.* at 402 n.11. Here, BCR served its Rule 68 offer only 43 days after filing the answer and before the parties had completed any class-certification discovery. The district court’s own scheduling order set a class-certification deadline of February 23, 2010—almost eight months after the complaint was filed. Lucero moved for certification on that exact date. To allow BCR to moot the action by making an early offer that expires before the court can rule frustrates the very purpose of Rule 23. *See Roper*, 445 U.S. at 339 (characterizing such tactics as “procedural frustration of the class action device”).

### IV. The District Court’s Reliance on *Clark v. State Farm* Was Misplaced

*Clark v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co.*, 590 F.3d 1134 (10th Cir. 2009), stated that a class action “must be dismissed for mootness when the personal claims of the named plaintiffs are satisfied and no class has been properly certified.” *Id.* at 1138 (internal quotation marks omitted). But *Clark* addressed a situation where the named plaintiffs *accepted* a settlement offer—a completely different scenario. Here, Lucero never accepted BCR’s offer; it expired. The general language in *Clark* does not govern this case, and the district court erred by treating it as dispositive.

### Conclusion

The district court should have denied the motion to dismiss because: (1) the unaccepted Rule 68 offer did not moot Lucero’s individual claim; (2) even if it did, the class-certification issue survives under *Geraghty*; and (3) the class-certification motion relates back to the complaint’s filing. Allowing BCR to “pick off” the named plaintiff before a reasonable opportunity for certification would subvert the policy of Rule 23 and encourage strategic gamesmanship. This Court should reverse and remand for further proceedings consistent with class-action principles.
