ARGUMENT

I. The District Court Erred in Dismissing Moll's Hostile Work Environment Claim

The district court incorrectly concluded that Moll's hostile work environment claim was premised solely on allegations of "sexually offensive acts." However, this Court has held that a sex-based hostile work environment claim can be supported by both facially sex-neutral incidents and "sexually offensive" acts. Alfano v. Costello, 294 F.3d 365, 375 (2d Cir. 2002). In Alfano, this Court emphasized that "the fact that the evidence in [a hostile work environment] case is not explicitly or overtly sexual is not dispositive." Id. at 374. Rather, the court must consider "the totality of the circumstances" and "all the incidents alleged" to determine whether a hostile work environment existed. Id. at 373-74.

Moll's complaint alleged a range of incidents, including Irving's refusal to permit her to communicate with him by email or telephone, his denial of her requests to work from home, and his exclusion of her from work-related social events. These incidents, while not necessarily "sexually offensive," are relevant to Moll's hostile work environment claim. See id. at 375. The district court erred in failing to consider these allegations in their totality, and its dismissal of Moll's hostile work environment claim was therefore improper.

II. The District Court Erred in Granting Summary Judgment on Moll's Hostile Work Environment Claim

The district court's grant of summary judgment on Moll's hostile work environment claim was also flawed. As discussed above, the court failed to consider the totality of the circumstances alleged by Moll. Moreover, the court erred in applying the "sham issue of fact" doctrine to disregard the testimony of non-party witnesses that conflicted with their prior statements. See generally Hayes v. N.Y.C. Dep't of Corr., 84 F.3d 614, 619 (2d Cir. 1996) (holding that the "sham issue of fact" doctrine applies to a party's contradictory testimony, not to non-party witnesses).

In light of these errors, the district court's grant of summary judgment on Moll's hostile work environment claim was improper. This Court should vacate the judgment and remand for further proceedings.

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should vacate the district court's judgment insofar as it granted in part Verizon's motion for summary judgment and remand for further proceedings.
