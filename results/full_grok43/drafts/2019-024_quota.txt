ARGUMENT

Section 1113(2) bars any action under section 1104 commenced more than three years after “the earliest date on which the plaintiff had actual knowledge of the breach or violation.” 29 U.S.C. § 1113(2). The Ninth Circuit erred in holding that this period does not run until the plaintiff also knows the acts were imprudent. That construction contradicts the statute’s text and this Court’s precedents.

“Actual knowledge” requires awareness of the facts constituting the breach, not a legal conclusion that those facts violate ERISA. In Blanton v. Anzalone, 760 F.2d 989 (9th Cir. 1985), the court held that trustees “had actual knowledge of the transaction at the time it took place” because they “actually made the decision,” and the statute “is triggered by the [plaintiff’s] knowledge of the transaction that constituted the alleged violation, not by their knowledge of the law.” Id. at 991-92. Meagher v. International Ass’n of Machinists & Aerospace Workers Pension Plan, 856 F.2d 1418 (9th Cir. 1988), applied the same rule: receipt of reduced checks gave the plaintiff “knowledge of the transaction, though he may not have known at the time that the reduction in benefits was unlawful under ERISA.” Id. at 1423. Ziegler v. Connecticut General Life Insurance Co., 916 F.2d 548 (9th Cir. 1990), confirmed that actual knowledge exists once the plaintiff learns the material facts of the challenged conduct. Id. at 552.

Phillips v. Alaska Hotel & Restaurant Employees Pension Fund, 944 F.2d 509 (9th Cir. 1991), reinforced the point: the limitations period begins on “the ‘earliest date’ on which [the plaintiff] knew of the breach,” measured by awareness of the underlying acts. Id. at 520. The Court has repeatedly distinguished actual knowledge from constructive knowledge, the latter having been repealed in 1987. See Lockheed Corp. v. Spink, 517 U.S. 882, 887 (1996) (describing fiduciary duties under section 1104); Tibble v. Edison International, 135 S. Ct. 1823, 1828 (2015) (same).

Sulyma received Fund Fact Sheets, Summary Plan Descriptions, and website disclosures detailing the precise allocations and strategy he now challenges. Those documents supplied the facts of the transactions. Under Blanton, Meagher, and Ziegler, that information constituted actual knowledge. The Ninth Circuit’s additional requirement—that Sulyma also appreciate imprudence—imports a legal-knowledge element the statute does not contain and that Congress eliminated when it removed the constructive-knowledge provision.

The same analysis governs the derivative claims. Phillips holds that the earliest date any breach is known starts the period for related claims. 944 F.2d at 520. Because Sulyma possessed actual knowledge of the investment decisions more than three years before suit, section 1113(2) bars all counts.

Reversal is required.
