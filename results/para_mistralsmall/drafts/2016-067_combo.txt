**ARGUMENT**

Petitioners’ attempt to redefine “debt collector” under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) contravenes the plain meaning of the statute and this Court’s longstanding precedent. The FDCPA regulates only those who collect debts *for others*; it does not reach creditors collecting their own debts. *Heintz v. Jenkins*, 514 U.S. 291, 293 (1995) (quoting 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6)). Santander, having purchased the defaulted loans for its own account, acted as a creditor—not a debt collector—when it sought repayment. The Fourth Circuit correctly held that the default status of a debt at the time of acquisition is irrelevant to this analysis. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6); *S. Rep. No. 95-382, at 3-4* (1977).

### I. THE FDCPA DOES NOT REGULATE CREDITORS COLLECTING THEIR OWN DEBTS

The FDCPA’s definitional structure distinguishes between “debt collectors” and “creditors.” A “creditor” is one to whom a debt is owed, while a “debt collector” is one who regularly collects debts *owed to another*. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(4), (6). This Court has long recognized that the Act’s primary concern is abusive practices by third-party collectors, not creditors acting in their own right. *See, e.g., Bassett v. Stolper, Koritzinsky, Brewster & Neider, S.C.*, 111 F.3d 1322, 1324 (7th Cir. 1997) (citing *S. Rep. No. 95-382*).

Santander’s purchase of the defaulted loans transformed it into the creditor to whom the deficiency balances were owed. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(4) (excluding from the definition of “debt collector” those who receive an assignment of a debt *solely for the purpose of facilitating collection for another*). Because Santander collected these debts for itself—not for CitiFinancial Auto—it was not acting as a “debt collector” subject to the FDCPA. *Cf. United States v. Nat’l City Lines*, 334 U.S. 573, 584 (1948) (holding that a party acting in its own interest is not a “collector for another”).

### II. THE DEFAULT STATUS OF A DEBT AT ACQUISITION IS IRRELEVANT

Petitioners erroneously argue that the default status of a debt at the time of purchase determines whether an entity is a “debt collector.” This misreads the statute. The FDCPA’s exclusion for servicers of non-defaulted debts, 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6)(F)(iii), applies only to those collecting *for others*; it does not convert a creditor into a debt collector. *See S. Rep. No. 95-382, at 3-4* (explaining that the exclusion was intended to protect loan servicers, not purchasers of defaulted debt).

This Court has repeatedly emphasized that the FDCPA’s reach turns on *who* the collector is acting for, not *when* the debt went into default. *See, e.g., United States v. Turkette*, 452 U.S. 576, 580 (1981) (interpreting statutory terms by their plain meaning). The Fourth Circuit correctly applied this principle in *Henson v. Santander Consumer USA, Inc.*, 814 F.3d 131, 135 (4th Cir. 2016), holding that Santander’s status as a creditor—not its acquisition of defaulted debt—determined its obligations under the FDCPA.

### III. PETITIONERS’ INTERPRETATION WOULD CREATE AN UNWORKABLE LOOPHOLE

Petitioners’ theory—that any purchaser of defaulted debt is automatically a “debt collector”—would eviscerate the FDCPA’s creditor exemption. *See* 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6). Under their logic, a bank that purchases a mortgage in foreclosure would become a debt collector, subject to the Act’s strictures, despite acting as the creditor. This contradicts the statute’s purpose. *See S. Rep. No. 95-382, at 3* (explaining that the FDCPA targets “abusive debt collection practices by debt collectors,” not creditors).

Moreover, this Court has long rejected efforts to expand the FDCPA’s scope beyond its text. *See, e.g., Bassett*, 111 F.3d at 1324 (rejecting an overbroad interpretation of “debt collector”). The Fourth Circuit’s decision aligns with this precedent.

### CONCLUSION

Santander, having purchased the defaulted loans for its own account, acted as a creditor—not a debt collector—when it sought repayment. The Fourth Circuit correctly affirmed the dismissal of petitioners’ FDCPA claims. This Court should do the same.
