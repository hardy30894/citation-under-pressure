**Argument**

**I. ANILCA § 103(c) is unambiguous and grants the NPS authority over all lands and waters within the boundaries of National Park System units in Alaska, regardless of underlying ownership.**

The text of ANILCA § 103(c) is clear and unambiguous. The statute provides that NPS regulations "shall apply to all lands and waters within the boundaries of the National Park System in Alaska." 16 U.S.C. § 3113(c). The phrase "all lands and waters" is not qualified by "federal" or "public" ownership. Under the plain meaning rule, "words are to be understood in their ordinary sense." *Klein v. United States*, 80 U.S. 277, 283 (1871). The ordinary meaning of "all lands and waters" encompasses the entire geographic area defined by the unit's boundaries, irrespective of the title to the underlying land. *United States v. Mead Corp.*, 533 U.S. 218, 226 (2001). Therefore, the NPS has the authority to regulate activities on state, private, and Native lands that lie within the boundaries of a National Park System unit.

**II. Sturgeon’s interpretation is contrary to the statute’s plain meaning and the NPS’s long-standing authority to manage units of the National Park System.**

Sturgeon argues that the NPS lacks authority over state-owned lands and waters within a National Park System unit. This argument ignores the statutory text and the NPS’s authority to manage the unit as a whole. The NPS’s authority to regulate the unit is not dependent on the ownership of the land beneath the waters. As the Supreme Court has held, "words are to be read in their ordinary sense." *United States v. Utah*, 436 U.S. 1, 18 (1978). The phrase "all lands and waters" encompasses the entire geographic area defined by the unit's boundaries. Consequently, the NPS may regulate activities on state-owned navigable waters that flow through a National Park System unit, just as it may regulate activities on federal lands within the unit. *United States v. Causby*, 328 U.S. 256, 261 (1946) (federal power extends to navigable airspace over private land). The NPS’s regulation of hovercrafts on the Nation River is a valid exercise of this authority.
