**ARGUMENT**

**I. The School District’s Facilities Constitute a Limited Public Forum Open to All Nonreligious Speech, and the Denial of Access to Lamb’s Chapel Based on the Religious Content of Its Speech Violates the First Amendment’s Free Speech Clause.**

The Second Circuit erred in concluding that the School District’s facilities were a “limited public forum” closed to religious speech. *See* Perry Educ. Ass’n v. Perry Local Educators’ Ass’n, 460 U.S. 37, 45 (1983) (explaining that a limited public forum is one “opened for a particular purpose” but not “indefinitely”). The District’s policy and practice of allowing secular community groups to use its facilities—including the Southern Harmonizers’ musical performance and the “Psychology and the Unknown” lecture series—demonstrates that the forum was open to a broad range of expressive activity. *See Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Def. & Educ. Fund, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 788, 802 (1985) (holding that a forum’s boundaries are defined by the government’s policy and actual practice).

The District’s refusal to allow Lamb’s Chapel to show religious films while permitting secular films on similar topics constitutes impermissible viewpoint discrimination. *See Rosenberger v. Univ. of Va.*, 515 U.S. 819, 829 (1995) (“[T]he government must abstain from regulating speech when the specific motivating ideology or the opinion or perspective of the speaker is the rationale for the restriction.”). The District’s policy barring “religious purposes” is a content-based restriction that disfavors religious speech. *See id.* at 828–29. Such discrimination is permissible only if it survives strict scrutiny, which it cannot here. *See id.* at 831.

**II. The District’s Policy Violates the Free Speech Clause by Discriminating Against Religious Speech in a Public Forum.**

The District’s policy is not viewpoint-neutral. *See* Rosenberger, 515 U.S. at 830 (holding that a university’s refusal to fund a religious student publication while funding secular ones was not viewpoint-neutral). The District allowed secular films on family values—such as those addressing humanism or societal decline—while banning religious films on the same subject. This selective exclusion demonstrates that the policy targets religious speech based on its content. *See id.* at 829–30.

The Second Circuit’s reliance on *Widmar v. Vincent*, 454 U.S. 263 (1981), and *Board of Educ. of the Westside Cmty. Schs. v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226 (1990), is misplaced. Those cases involved student-initiated religious speech in forums already open to student groups. *See Widmar*, 454 U.S. at 267 (finding a university forum open to student groups); *Mergens*, 496 U.S. at 235 (applying the Equal Access Act to a high school forum open to noncurriculum-related student groups). Lamb’s Chapel, by contrast, is an outside organization seeking access to a forum open to secular community groups. The District’s policy is thus more akin to the unconstitutional exclusion in *Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free Sch. Dist.*, 508 U.S. 384 (1993), where this Court held that a school district’s refusal to allow religious films while permitting secular films violated the First Amendment.

**III. The District’s Policy Does Not Violate the Establishment Clause.**

The District argues that its policy avoids an Establishment Clause violation by excluding all religious speech. *See* *Lemon v. Kurtzman*, 403 U.S. 602, 612–13 (1971) (articulating the three-part *Lemon* test). However, the Establishment Clause does not permit the government to suppress speech merely to avoid an Establishment Clause concern. *See Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 844 (holding that the government may not “suppress private speech in order to pursue a goal of neutrality”). The District’s policy is not neutral; it singles out religious speech for disfavored treatment. *See id.* at 835–36.

Moreover, the District’s policy does not advance a secular purpose. *See Lemon*, 403 U.S. at 612. The District’s asserted purpose of avoiding an Establishment Clause violation is circular and disingenuous. *See Rosenberger*, 515 U.S. at 840 (rejecting the argument that funding a religious publication would violate the Establishment Clause). The policy’s true purpose is to suppress religious speech, not to maintain government neutrality. *See id.* at 844.

**CONCLUSION**

The Second Circuit’s decision upholding the District’s policy violates the First Amendment’s Free Speech Clause and should be reversed. The District’s facilities constitute a limited public forum open to secular speech, and the denial of access to Lamb’s Chapel based on the religious content of its speech constitutes impermissible viewpoint discrimination. The policy also fails strict scrutiny and violates the Establishment Clause by suppressing religious speech under the guise of neutrality.
