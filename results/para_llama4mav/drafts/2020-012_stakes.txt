ARGUMENT

The Railroad Retirement Board's ("the Board") decision not to reopen Manfredo Salinas's 2006 disability annuity application is a "final decision" subject to judicial review under 45 U.S.C. § 355(f). Although the Fifth Circuit has previously held that it lacks jurisdiction to review such decisions, see Roberts v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 346 F.3d 139 (5th Cir. 2003), this Court should reconsider its precedent in light of the D.C. Circuit's well-reasoned decision in Stovic v. Railroad Retirement Board, 826 F.3d 500 (D.C. Cir. 2016).

The Supreme Court's decision in Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977), does not preclude judicial review of the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's claim. While Sanders held that a decision not to reopen a claim is not reviewable under the Social Security Act, the Court explicitly distinguished the Social Security Act from other statutes that "expressly authorize[ ] judicial review of a refusal to reopen a prior final decision." Id. at 108. The Railroad Retirement Act ("RRA") is one such statute, as it incorporates the judicial review provisions of the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act ("RUIA"), 45 U.S.C. § 355(f), which provides for review of "final decisions" of the Board.

The RRA's statutory scheme and legislative history support the conclusion that the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's claim is a "final decision" subject to judicial review. See Szostak v. R.R. Retirement Bd., 370 F.2d 253, 254-55 (2d Cir. 1966) (holding that a decision not to reopen is reviewable under the RRA). The D.C. Circuit's decision in Stovic, which carefully analyzed the RRA's statutory text and legislative history, is persuasive authority that this Court should follow.

Reconsidering Roberts in light of Stovic and the relevant statutory authority, this Court should hold that it has jurisdiction to review the Board's decision not to reopen Salinas's 2006 application. The rule of orderliness does not preclude this Court from reconsidering Roberts en banc or, alternatively, certifying the question to the full Court for review. See Vaughan v. Anderson Reg'l Med. Ctr., 849 F.3d 588, 591 (5th Cir. 2017) (noting that a panel may not overrule a prior panel decision, but may certify a question to the full Court or suggest en banc review).

For these reasons, this Court should reverse the Fifth Circuit's dismissal of Salinas's petition and hold that the Board's decision not to reopen his 2006 application is subject to judicial review.
