ARGUMENT

The district court erred in granting a preliminary injunction that compels deviation from Cadman Towers' established first-come, first-served policy for allocating parking spaces. A cooperative apartment corporation holds authority to adopt and enforce reasonable rules governing use of its common facilities, and those rules bind all shareholders as contractual obligations. See Trustees of Dartmouth College v. Woodward, 17 U.S. (4 Wheat.) 518, 651 (1819) (the charter constitutes "a contract between the government and the corporation").

Judicial alteration of the allocation order to favor one shareholder would impair the settled expectations of others who have complied with the waiting-list procedure. See Fletcher v. Peck, 10 U.S. (6 Cranch) 87, 136 (1810) (a law "impairing the obligation of contracts" is void). Equity will not intervene in the internal management of a corporation unless fraud or bad faith is shown. See United Copper Securities Co. v. Amalgamated Copper Co., 244 U.S. 261, 263 (1917) ("courts of equity will not interfere with the management of a corporation by its directors").

Cadman Towers applied its policy uniformly and without exception to all residents seeking garage space. A preliminary injunction that overrides that policy therefore exceeds the proper scope of equitable relief. See, e.g., Irwin v. Gavit, 268 U.S. 161, 167 (1925) (equity acts only to prevent irreparable injury to a clear legal right). The order should be reversed.
