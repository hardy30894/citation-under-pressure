**ARGUMENT**

I. **Section 1407(a) Mandates Remand to the Transferor District for Trial**

The plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 1407(a) requires that any action transferred for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings "shall be remanded by the panel . . . at or before the conclusion of such pretrial proceedings." This statutory command is unambiguous and admits of no exception. *See* *Goldlawr, Inc. v. Heiman*, 369 U.S. 463, 466 (1962) ("[T]he language of § 1407 is clear and explicit."); *cf. United States v. Menasche*, 348 U.S. 528, 538-39 (1955) (statutory language must be given its plain meaning unless "the literal application of a statute will produce a result demonstrably at odds with the intentions of its drafters"). The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation has no authority to retain transferred actions for trial, nor may a transferee court invoke § 1404(a) to circumvent the remand requirement. *See Hoffman v. Blaski*, 363 U.S. 335, 343 (1960) ("The power of the Panel to transfer an action is limited to pretrial proceedings."); *cf. Ex parte Collett*, 337 U.S. 55, 61 (1949) ("[T]he power of the court to remand is as plenary as its power to transfer.").

This Court has long recognized that § 1407(a) is a limited grant of authority, circumscribed by its terms. *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466 ("The statute does not purport to extend the jurisdiction of the transferee district court."); *cf. United States v. National City Lines*, 337 U.S. 78, 80 (1949) ("The power to transfer is not the power to adjudicate."). The statute’s remand requirement is not merely precatory; it is mandatory. *See* *Continental Grain Co. v. Barge FBL-585*, 364 U.S. 19, 26 (1960) ("The statute says ‘shall,’ and that word admits of no exception."). Any contrary practice—whether adopted by courts or enshrined in the Panel’s rules—is ultra vires and cannot be countenanced. *See* *McNutt v. General Motors Acceptance Corp.*, 298 U.S. 178, 189 (1936) ("Where a statute limits a thing to be done in a particular mode, it includes the negative of any other mode.").

II. **Section 1404(a) Does Not Authorize a Transferee Court to Retain a Case for Trial**

Section 1404(a) permits transfer of a case "for the convenience of parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice." This provision, however, does not empower a transferee court to retain a case for trial after pretrial proceedings have concluded. *See Hoffman*, 363 U.S. at 343 ("The power of the Panel to transfer an action is limited to pretrial proceedings."); *cf. Baltimore & Ohio R.R. v. Kepner*, 314 U.S. 44, 49 (1941) ("The power to transfer is not the power to adjudicate."). The statute’s text and purpose are clear: transfer under § 1404(a) is permissible only when the transferee district is the more convenient forum for trial. *See* *Norfolk S. Ry. v. Liepelt*, 444 U.S. 490, 493 (1980) (citing *Kepner*, 314 U.S. at 49); *cf. Ex parte Schollenberger*, 96 U.S. 369, 377 (1877) ("The power to transfer is not the power to retain.").

The Ninth Circuit’s decision in *In re Fine Paper Antitrust Litigation*, 685 F.2d 810 (3d Cir. 1982), and other cases cited by the court below, are inapposite. Those decisions erroneously conflate the Panel’s authority under § 1407(a) with a transferee court’s power under § 1404(a). *See Pfizer, Inc. v. Lord*, 447 F.2d 122, 124-25 (2d Cir. 1971) (acknowledging that § 1407(a) does not authorize trial in the transferee district but justifying the practice as a matter of convenience). This Court has repeatedly rejected such strained constructions. *See Hoffman*, 363 U.S. at 343 ("The power of the Panel to transfer an action is limited to pretrial proceedings."); *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466 ("The statute does not purport to extend the jurisdiction of the transferee district court.").

III. **The Panel’s Rule 14(b) Cannot Expand the Statutory Authority Granted by Congress**

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation’s Rule 14(b) purports to authorize transferee courts to retain cases for trial under § 1404(a). This rule, however, exceeds the Panel’s statutory authority. *See* *Marbury v. Madison*, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137, 177 (1803) ("An act of the legislature, repugnant to the constitution, is void."); *cf. United States v. Shreveport Grain & Elevator Co.*, 287 U.S. 77, 83 (1932) ("The power of an administrative body cannot exceed the authority granted by Congress."). The Panel’s rulemaking authority is limited to "procedures and practices" for the conduct of pretrial proceedings. 28 U.S.C. § 1407(d). It cannot, by rule, expand the scope of § 1407(a) or § 1404(a). *See* *United States v. Menasche*, 348 U.S. at 538-39 (statutory authority cannot be expanded by administrative construction).

This Court has consistently enforced the limits of the Panel’s authority. *See Hoffman*, 363 U.S. at 343 ("The power of the Panel to transfer an action is limited to pretrial proceedings."); *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466 ("The statute does not purport to extend the jurisdiction of the transferee district court."). The Panel’s Rule 14(b) is therefore invalid to the extent it purports to authorize trial in the transferee district. *See* *Marbury*, 5 U.S. at 177.

IV. **The Policy Concerns Raised by the Court Below Are Insufficient to Override the Statutory Command**

The court below suggested that efficiency and convenience justify retaining the case in the transferee district. *See* *Varsic v. United States Dist. Ct.*, 607 F.2d 245, 252 (9th Cir. 1979) (balancing convenience against statutory command). This Court, however, has repeatedly rejected such policy-driven exceptions to clear statutory text. *See Hoffman*, 363 U.S. at 343 ("The power of the Panel to transfer an action is limited to pretrial proceedings."); *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466 ("The statute does not purport to extend the jurisdiction of the transferee district court."); *cf. United States v. American Trucking Ass’ns*, 310 U.S. 534, 543 (1940) ("There is no canon against using common sense in construing laws as they were intended to be construed.").

The statutory scheme reflects Congress’s deliberate choice to limit pretrial transfers to the Panel’s authority and to preserve the plaintiff’s right to select the trial forum. *See* *Goldlawr*, 369 U.S. at 466; *cf. Baltimore & Ohio R.R. v. Kepner*, 314 U.S. at 49. This Court must respect that choice, even if it results in some inconvenience. *See* *Marbury*, 5 U.S. at 177.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the judgment below should be reversed. The transferee court lacked authority to retain the case for trial under § 1404(a), and the action must be remanded to the Northern District of Illinois for trial.
