## ARGUMENT

The district court erred in denying Floyd Perkins's petition for a writ of habeas corpus by requiring him to demonstrate reasonable diligence in addition to making a credible claim of actual innocence. This Court has previously held that a credible claim of actual innocence is sufficient to warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA's statute of limitations. See Souter v. Jones, 395 F.3d 577, 602 (6th Cir. 2005). The Supreme Court's decision in Holland v. Florida, 130 S. Ct. 2549 (2010), reaffirmed that equitable tolling is available to a petitioner who demonstrates "(1) that he has been pursuing his rights diligently, and (2) that some extraordinary circumstance stood in his way." Id. at 2562 (quoting Pace v. DiGuglielmo, 544 U.S. 408, 418 (2005)). However, the Court in Holland did not overrule Souter, and the actual innocence exception remains a standalone basis for equitable tolling.

The Warden's argument that Pace and Holland impose a diligence requirement on actual innocence claims is unpersuasive. The Supreme Court in Pace addressed the general principles of equitable tolling, not the specific context of actual innocence claims. See Pace, 544 U.S. at 418. The Court in Holland similarly did not address the actual innocence exception. See Holland, 130 S. Ct. at 2562-63. In the absence of explicit guidance from the Supreme Court, this Court's precedent in Souter controls.

Perkins has made a credible claim of actual innocence, supported by new evidence that was not available at trial. The affidavits from Ronda Hudson, Demond Louis, and Linda Fleming provide substantial evidence that Damarr Jones, the prosecution's key witness, was involved in Henderson's murder. This new evidence is not merely cumulative or impeaching, but rather provides a new narrative that undermines the prosecution's case. See Schlup v. Delo, 513 U.S. 298, 324 (1995) (requiring "new reliable evidence" to support an actual innocence claim).

Under Souter, Perkins's credible claim of actual innocence is sufficient to warrant equitable tolling of AEDPA's statute of limitations. The district court's requirement of reasonable diligence is an additional burden not supported by this Court's precedent. Therefore, this Court should reverse the district court's judgment and remand for consideration of Perkins's habeas corpus petition on the merits.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, this Court should reverse the district court's denial of Floyd Perkins's petition for a writ of habeas corpus and remand for further proceedings.
