**ARGUMENT**

**I. THE DISTRICT COURT ABUSED ITS DISCRETION BY STRIKING THE TESTIMONY OF APPELLANTS’ EXPERTS AND GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT.**

The district court’s exclusion of expert testimony under Rule 37(b)(2)(A) and its summary judgment ruling must be reversed because the court failed to consider the prejudice to Appellants and imposed sanctions disproportionate to the alleged discovery violations. *See Nat’l Hockey League v. Metro. Hockey Club, Inc.*, 427 U.S. 639, 642 (1976) (holding that sanctions must be “just” and proportionate to the violation).

The district court struck four experts for failing to comply with discovery deadlines, yet Appellants’ counsel—newly substituted after the original attorneys withdrew—demonstrated good-faith efforts to comply. *See* Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(b)(2)(C) (permitting lesser sanctions where the failure was not willful). The court’s order striking the experts did not analyze whether lesser sanctions would suffice, as required by *Chambers v. NASCO, Inc.*, 501 U.S. 32, 50 (1991). Moreover, the court’s Daubert ruling on two additional experts was erroneous because it improperly shifted the burden to Appellants to prove reliability before discovery was complete. *See Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharms., Inc.*, 509 U.S. 579, 593 (1993) (placing the burden on the *proponent* of the evidence to establish admissibility).

The district court compounded its error by granting summary judgment without considering whether Appellants could cure the deficiencies. *See Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 322 (1986) (holding that summary judgment is improper where material facts remain in dispute). The court’s refusal to allow further expert discovery or additional time to supplement the record was an abuse of discretion. *See Bevan v. Honeywell, Inc.*, 102 F.3d 96, 99 (5th Cir. 1996) (reversing exclusion of expert testimony where the district court failed to consider prejudice).

**II. THE DISTRICT COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO CONSOLIDATE APPEALS UNDER FED. R. APP. P. 3.**

Appellants’ notice of appeal was timely filed as to the partial final judgment under Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(2), which permits an appeal from an interlocutory order that disposes of fewer than all claims. *See Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay*, 437 U.S. 463, 467 (1978) (holding that partial finality suffices for appeal). The district court’s subsequent final judgment did not divest the Fifth Circuit of jurisdiction. *See Griggs v. Provident Consumer Disc. Co.*, 459 U.S. 56, 58 (1982) (per curiam) (holding that an appeal from a partial final judgment is proper).

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Appellants respectfully request that this Court reverse the district court’s exclusion of expert testimony and summary judgment order, and remand for further proceedings consistent with this brief.
