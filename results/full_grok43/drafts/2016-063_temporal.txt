The Merit Systems Protection Board decision is subject to review in district court. Federal courts possess authority to examine administrative dispositions that implicate rights secured by the Constitution or statutes. Marbury v. Madison, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137, 177 (1803) ("It is emphatically the province and duty of the judicial department to say what the law is.").

When an employee alleges that an adverse action rested on prohibited discrimination, the claim presents a federal question properly cognizable in the first instance by a district court. See Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954) (declaring state-imposed segregation unconstitutional because it denies equal protection). Jurisdictional rulings by the agency do not alter this allocation, for the presence of a discrimination allegation keeps the controversy within the district court's original authority. Osborn v. Bank of the United States, 22 U.S. (9 Wheat.) 738, 819 (1824) (federal question jurisdiction extends to every case in which a federal title or right is an ingredient).

A contrary rule confining review to a single appellate forum would deny the employee the opportunity for plenary consideration of the discrimination component in the tribunal Congress has designated for such matters. The statutory scheme therefore places review of the Board's jurisdictional dismissal in district court.
